# Domestic expansion: precedents and historical boundaries

This note covers the later economic, party-administration, diplomatic-service and successor-policy additions in `tools/north_korea_1970/expansion.py`. It records institutions and constraints that make the proposals credible; the focuses are alternate-history choices, and their modifier values are gameplay abstractions. Existing party, defence and patron precedents are covered in [PARTY_PRECEDENTS.md](PARTY_PRECEDENTS.md), [DEFENCE_PRECEDENTS.md](DEFENCE_PRECEDENTS.md) and [HISTORICAL_SOURCES.md](HISTORICAL_SOURCES.md).

## Coal, chemicals, power and freight

The DPRK entered the 1970s with a substantial heavy-industrial base. A declassified CIA statistical table estimates 1971 output at 17 billion kWh of electricity, 23 million tonnes of anthracite, 2.6 million tonnes of crude steel and 400 million linear metres of textiles. These figures are contemporary estimates, not audited DPRK accounts, but caution against presenting the programme as building industry from nothing. The Fifth Party Congress in November 1970 adopted the Six-Year Plan; its official report emphasized modernization, technical improvement, heavy industry and simultaneous attention to light industry and agriculture. [CIA, *Economic Intelligence Statistical Handbook 1974*](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp85t00875r001500200012-7); [Kim Il Sung, report to the Fifth WPK Congress, in *Works*, vol. 25](https://bannedthought.net/Korea-DPRK/KimIlSung/KimIlSung-Works-25-OCR.pdf).

Coal was an energy source, export commodity and industrial feedstock. A 1970s study of Juche industrial policy describes reliance on domestic anthracite for power and synthetic chemicals, and electrification of railways as a way to reduce imported liquid-fuel dependence. The later transport snapshot in a U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency handbook estimates about three-quarters of the railway network electrified, while describing a limited, terrain-constrained network and weaknesses in rolling stock and traffic control. This is a later observation, so it should not be backdated as an exact 1970 share. [Taylor, “North Korean Chuch’e: The Political Economy of Independence”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/14672715.1975.10406372); [DIA, *North Korea: A Country Study*, transportation chapter](https://irp.fas.org/dia/product/knfms/knfms_chp4a.html).

These precedents support the proposed **Deeper Coal Seams**, **Chemical Inputs**, **Fertilizer Recovery** and **Electric Traction** policies, but they also expose their trade-offs: more coal extraction brings safety and maintenance costs; fertilizer and chemical production compete for energy and feedstocks; electrification shifts transport demand onto an unreliable grid. The mutually exclusive **Diesel Flexibility** route is a plausible resilience policy where power supply is uneven, but it should carry a liquid-fuel/import burden rather than imply that diesel was the network's dominant traction system. The proposed freight dispatch, repairs, storage and county distribution improvements are management responses to these connected bottlenecks, not claims that a specific historical upgrade programme existed.

Production and plan targets also need careful wording. A 1984 CIA assessment described extensive delays in completion of imported plant projects and a 1970s external-debt crisis after rapid purchases of Western equipment and technology. Archival scholarship on the Eurocurrency period documents arrears, rescheduling negotiations and foreign-creditor concern about short-term plan targets. Accordingly, **Investment Accounts**, **Balance the Accounts**, **Industrial Reserves** and **Rapid Reinvestment** are credible policy alternatives, but should not be described as a proven historical reform package. [CIA Reading Room, “North Korea: A New Direction in Economic Policy”](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP84S00553R000100030005-1.pdf); [Seung Woo Kim, “A Brief Encounter: North Korea in the Eurocurrency Market, 1973–80”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14682745.2023.2184805).

One actionable measurement caution concerns fertilizer: official plan claims and retrospective production/use series report figures on inconsistent bases. Avoid precise production totals or assertions that fertilizer capacity was absent; frame **Fertilizer Recovery** as reconditioning, reliable feedstock allocation and seasonal delivery. Likewise, treat ports, cold storage, safety teams, construction brigades and apprenticeships as proposed capacity improvements. The documented constraints justify their benefits and costs, but do not establish these exact programmes at the named sites.

## Party oversight, specialization and local administration

North Korean management doctrine placed party leadership inside economic administration. Kim Il Sung's January 1964 speech “Some Tasks of the Machine Industry” described Taean management as higher units helping lower units solve production problems and supply materials; it also criticized material-supply officials who lacked current information on factories' needs and stocks. The design implication is institutional complementarity: **Party Inspection**, **County Reports** and **Institutional Records** can strengthen monitoring, while **Specialist Appointments**, **Ministry Schools** and **Evaluate the Plan** can improve technical capacity. The evidence does not support depicting the historical choice as “party or experts”; party supervision and specialist execution were expected to coexist, although their balance is a reasonable alternate-history choice. [Kim Il Sung, *Works*, vol. 18, “Some Tasks of the Machine Industry”](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kim-il-sung/cw/18.pdf); [Kim Il Sung, *Works*, vol. 24](https://www.marxists.info/archive/kim-il-sung/cw/24.pdf).

Local responsibility was bounded and sector-specific. Official accounts describe central ministries directing major economic bodies while local power organs guided services and commerce; the Taean system placed supply obligations on higher units. Thus **County Reports**, **County Care Network**, **County Distribution** and **Public Maintenance** can plausibly improve accountability and delivery, but broad county discretion over industrial inputs would be a counterfactual departure from the historical command structure. The reciprocal cost of more reporting, staffing and service provision fits the administrative burden rather than implying autonomous local government. [Kim Il Sung, *Works*, vol. 27](https://proletarianlibrary.online/public/download.php?file=Kim+Il-Sung+Collected+Works_+Volume+27.pdf); [South Korean Ministry of Unification, “Economic Management”](https://nkinfo.unikorea.go.kr/nkp/pge/view.do?menuId=EC202).

## Commercial missions and professional staffing

North Korea expanded external contacts during the 1970s and sought machinery, export markets, credit and technical knowledge. Archival research on the Foreign Trade Bank shows a London trade representative office, a proposed consortium-bank relationship and repeated negotiations over overdue bills. It documents both the possibility of professional commercial diplomacy and the consequences of weak credit management and inexperienced engagement with Western banking practice. This supports **Consular Service**, **Commercial Missions**, **Shipping Arbitration**, **Translation Institutes** and **Reciprocal Standards** as proposed institutional investments. Their credibility comes from documented demands for export promotion, contract handling and specialist knowledge; the sources do not verify these exact consular or standards programmes. Commercial contacts should have costs for staff and a risk of political or financial exposure, not grant effortless foreign-market access. [Kim, “A Brief Encounter: North Korea in the Eurocurrency Market, 1973–80”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14682745.2023.2184805).

## Jong-il and local discretion

The proposed **Local Discretion** versus **Central Allocations** choice is best treated as an alternate policy path for an accelerated Kim Jong Il succession, not as a summary of a documented 1970s reform. In historical sequence, a major, explicitly documented expansion of enterprise and local managerial authority came with the July 2002 economic-management measures, after the 1990s crisis weakened central supplies and enterprise backstopping. The measures allowed bounded autonomy and goods exchanges while retaining central control over strategic sectors; they did not establish general local sovereignty or a market economy. [South Korean Ministry of Unification, “Changes in North Korean Economic Policy”](https://nkinfo.unikorea.go.kr/nkp/pge/view.do?menuId=EC204); [South Korean Ministry of Unification, “Economic System”](https://nkinfo.unikorea.go.kr/nkp/pge/view.do?menuId=EC200); [Korea Development Institute, 1990 study of North Korean economic management](https://www.kdi.re.kr/research/reportView?pub_no=8492).

This history gives the focus pair a useful design distinction: **Local Discretion** can trade central political control for faster procurement and distribution; **Central Allocations** can protect priority industries and military supply while increasing civilian scarcity. For historical fidelity, present either as a contested choice rather than the actual course of policy, and keep its strongest precedent tied to the post-1990s crisis and 2002 measures. **Enterprise Returns** and **A Ministry Compact** can be framed as the proposed institutional bridge between central priorities, managerial responsibility and reliable reporting.

## Design boundary

The cited sources establish industrial constraints, planning and Taean management principles, foreign-trade difficulties, and much later bounded decentralization. They do not establish the exact named programmes, effect magnitudes or alt-history cabinet arrangements in this expansion. Those remain gameplay proposals; keep player-facing text focused on their institutional choices, benefits and costs.
