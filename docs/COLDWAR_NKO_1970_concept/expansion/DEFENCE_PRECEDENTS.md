# North Korea 1970 defence expansion: historical precedents

The added programmes treat the 1970 KPA as a large, politically privileged force whose modernization competed with civilian investment. This is grounded in contemporary published state priorities and archival assessments; the particular ministries, sequencing, costs and alternate-history choices in the tree are design proposals, not claims that these exact programmes existed.

## Historical basis

- **Defence and industrial investment were already linked.** The National History Compilation Committee of Korea's history database describes the 1966 Workers' Party decision to strengthen defence even if the People's Economy's development rate had to be moderated. It reports defence allocations rising from 19% of the budget in 1960 to 31.3% during 1967–69 and 43% in 1970, while heavy industry took the dominant share of industrial construction investment. This supports the expansion's explicit opportunity costs on industrial output, energy, education and public spending. [National History Compilation Committee, *The National History DB*, vol. 70, "The Socialist Construction Period"](https://db.history.go.kr/diachronic/level.do?levelId=kn_070_0070_0030_0030)
- **Artillery, fortification and air defence had concrete precedents.** A 1974 interagency National Intelligence Analytical Memorandum assessed North Korean strategy as primarily defensive, with strong forward defences on both coasts and the DMZ, distributed firepower, and dependence on allied logistical support in a prolonged conflict. [FRUS, E–12, doc. 255, 15 July 1974](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve12/d255)
- **Defence industry and maintenance capacity were credible policy concerns.** A declassified CIA assessment reports that the defence industrial buildup began in earnest around 1970, with Chinese and Soviet equipment and technical aid, including Soviet assistance for a tank plant and Chinese help for submarine construction. It also describes fortified underground facilities and imported Soviet air-defence systems. These assessments justify arsenal workshops, vehicle repair, coastal services and air-warning institutions as plausible policy directions; no factory or weapon is granted by the research focuses themselves. [CIA, *North Korea: The Military Balance and Defense Industries* (released under FOIA; document CIA-RDP84S00553R000300010002-4)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP84S00553R000300010002-4.pdf)
- **Civilian industrial modernization relied on imported technical machinery.** A contemporaneous CIA report on North Korean machine-tool imports states that domestic production remained inadequate for industrial-military modernization and that the Seven-Year Plan had been extended through 1970. This supports the recurring trade-off between military workshops, civilian factories, specialist training and scarce imported machine tools. [CIA, *North Korea: Machine Tool Imports and Industrial Modernization* (document CIA-RDP85T00875R001600030142-1)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T00875R001600030142-1.pdf)
- **Military research is separated from a completed weapons programme.** The tree uses the existing MD categories `CAT_nuclear_reactors` and `CAT_nuclear_weapons` only for single-use research bonuses. The history supports scientific and military-industrial competition; the exact Academy branches are alternate-history institutional proposals. The civilian route remains gated by active bilateral cooperation and safeguards. The strategic route carries higher budget, training and diplomatic costs and does not create uranium production, mines, reactors, fissile material or finished weapons.

## Design trade-offs

The four KPA continuations model distinct priorities: artillery and munitions, vehicle sustainment, integrated air defence, and coastal readiness. Each is institutionally plausible given the historical emphasis on artillery, military-industrial expansion and defensive deployment; the precise internal focus names and package effects are balancing choices. All four services remain investable. Each has an internal exclusive policy pair: massed gunfire versus rocket batteries, recovery battalions versus parts standardization, fighter instruction versus dispersed bases, and patrol refits versus littoral exercises. Two carefully guarded building records represent a single additional arms factory and a single dockyard, each accompanied by one shared slot and restricted to owned, controlled suitable NKO states.

Research bonuses are finite, single-use advantages in existing MD categories. They are not research grants that finish a technology and they never bypass the nuclear programme's normal development, material or construction requirements.
