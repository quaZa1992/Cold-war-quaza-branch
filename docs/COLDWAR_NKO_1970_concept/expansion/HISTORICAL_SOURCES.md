# DPRK Patron Branches: historical precedents and design notes

Research note for the proposed 1970–1990 NKO expansion. It distinguishes recorded precedents from branch ideas that would be alternate-history player choices. Treaty clauses and diplomatic ties establish a basis for negotiation; they do not establish that every proposed shipment, exercise, or programme actually happened.

## Recorded precedents

### China

- The **1961 PRC–DPRK Treaty of Friendship, Cooperation and Mutual Assistance** called for consultation on major shared questions and for all possible economic and technical assistance, along with continuing scientific and cultural cooperation. It is a sound basis for bilateral offers and recipient-controlled decisions, but does not require bloc membership. [Treaty text, World and Japan Database](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/docs/19610711.T1E.html); [UN Treaty Series, vol. 420](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20420/volume-420-I-6045-English.pdf).
- A contemporaneous U.S. diplomatic telegram records the ROK foreign minister's allegation that a **Chinese–North Korean military-assistance agreement was signed in September 1971**. This is a report of an allegation, not the text of that agreement. [FRUS, 1969–1976, vol. XIX, document 109](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v19p1/d109).
- A declassified CIA assessment says China supplied North Korea with **170 F-6 fighters between 1972 and 1978**, and notes the aircraft were still sent to China for overhaul. The report also considers Chinese grant aid and assistance to North Korea's aircraft-production effort; these are contemporary intelligence judgments, not Chinese/DPRK public accounting. [CIA Reading Room, “North Korea: Jet Fighter Production” (April 1984)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T00310R000100060006-4.pdf); [CIA Reading Room, “North Korea: New Weapons in the Military Forces” (October 1985)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T01058R000201940001-0.pdf).
- China publicly backed Pyongyang's peaceful-unification programme during the 1971–72 opening of inter-Korean talks. The July 1972 joint communiqué itself committed the two Korean governments to exchanges in multiple fields and a hotline/coordination committee, offering another credible diplomatic avenue beside patron ties. [FRUS, 1969–1976, vol. E–13, document 56](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve13/d56); [FRUS, vol. XIX, document 151](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v19p1/d151).

### Soviet Union

- The **1961 USSR–DPRK treaty** likewise provided for political consultation, economic and cultural assistance, and mutual aid; its Korea clause framed unification as peaceful and democratic. It supports proposals and reciprocal protocols rather than automatic Soviet control of Pyongyang. [UN Treaty Series, vol. 420](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20420/volume-420-I-6045-English.pdf).
- A 1968 internal Soviet Foreign Ministry report records Soviet machinery and technical assistance for 27 industrial enterprises, 250 Soviet specialists working in the DPRK, and an intergovernmental commission on economic, scientific and technical questions. It also notes Pyongyang's requests for goods including coking coal and petroleum products, plus a logging arrangement that employed Korean workers in the Soviet Far East. [UMD Digital Archive, translated Soviet MFA report](https://digitalarchive.umd.edu/document/far-east-department-ussr-ministry-foreign-affairs-domestic-situation-and-foreign-policy).
- The Russian federal-archives bulletin indexes Soviet–DPRK trade/economic plans for **1971–75 and 1976–80**, and a 1972 decision granting Pyongyang a deferment on trade debt. These records support long-term planned trade and credit negotiations while flagging repayment as a real burden. [Federal Archives of Russia, declassified-documents bulletin](https://portal.rusarchives.ru/secret/bul10/garf8.shtml).
- A contemporaneous CIA report says Soviet oil purchases were tied to North Korea's ability to pay through exports: planned crude deliveries were about one million tons annually from 1973, while actual 1980–83 deliveries fell to roughly 600,000 tons. It also reports tougher Soviet loan terms in 1983 and Moscow's caution about aid without political returns. This is useful evidence against treating energy aid as unlimited or free. [CIA Reading Room, “Soviet–North Korean Relations in the 1980s” (June 1984)](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T00313R000100150007-0.pdf).
- The same CIA account describes no specific new assistance agreement announced at Kim Il Sung's 1984 visit, while observing renewed economic and security cooperation. A CIA 1985 assessment confirms Soviet delivery of MiG-23 fighters and SA-3 surface-to-air missiles that year. A distinct **late-1980s military upgrade** is therefore better grounded than an unrestricted Soviet arms stream from 1970 onward. [1984 CIA report](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T00313R000100150007-0.pdf); [1985 CIA military assessment](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP85T01058R000201940001-0.pdf).
- Civilian atomic work has a separate precedent: the USSR agreed in 1985 to assist with a DPRK nuclear power plant, while earlier Soviet help supported reactor/science infrastructure and personnel training. Keep this distinct from weapons assistance and use safeguards, trained staff and recurring programme costs as the political/economic trade-offs. [U.S. Department of Justice-hosted report summarizing the 1985 agreement](https://www.justice.gov/file/277546/dl?inline=); [CIA Reading Room, 1986 assessment of DPRK capabilities](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0000835124.pdf).

## Candidate reciprocal packages

Treat the following as proposed game branches, not assertions that these exact packages or manoeuvres occurred. Both patrons should receive a reply event/decision; either side can decline or request terms. The suggested effect keys already appear in current_design.json (including its Soviet and China agreement spirits), so they fit the design vocabulary without implying a new engine mechanic.

Build on the existing Border Cooperation Protocol; focus on fertilizer, farm machinery, rail/customs capacity, and barter for minerals/cement rather than another generic grant. | Agricultural productivity and supply use, with productivity growth on an upgrade. Existing keys: agricolture_productivity_modifier, supply_consumption_factor, productivity_growth_modifier. | DPRK makes regular export commitments, pays a weekly trade/administration contribution, and accepts some foreign-influence-defense loss; China gains trade opinion and a modest revenue/administration benefit. It can accept a tax_gain_multiplier_modifier or resource_export_multiplier_modifier burden and expose itself to interruptions if it misses delivery terms. USSR receives financial/export commitments and reciprocal access for advisers. Avoid free units, stockpiles, or claims of established recurring joint manoeuvres. |

These branches should make balancing the two patrons a meaningful DPRK choice: China's best-supported distinct role is frontier exchange and airframe support/maintenance; the USSR's is heavy industrial and power engineering, conditional energy trade, then a credible but late military-modernization option. An explicit autonomy route can reject both patrons and pay for domestic research, with slower progress and lower external leverage.

## Existing local design hooks

- tools/north_korea_1970/current_design.json already has consent-based china_border and soviet_industry agreements, 90-day recipient decisions, expiry/refusal resubmission and reciprocal GDP commitments. The new branches can deepen or upgrade these protocols instead of presenting aid as automatic.
- Existing Soviet upgrades are soviet_training (technical placements), while the China upgrade is border_trade (border logistics). The China agricultural/logistics package therefore needs a more distinct military or aircraft-maintenance counterpart; the Soviet industrial/power agreement needs its own late military tier.
- Effect examples above come from modifier keys in the current design; validate any proposed grant, import, equipment transfer or agreement logic against the existing generator/runtime before implementation. The note makes no claim that an effect key alone transfers actual goods.

## Historical boundary on exercises

The primary sources assembled here establish treaty consultation, technical/military assistance, advisers or specialists, and arms/equipment support. They do **not** verify that the DPRK and either patron held a named recurring joint field-exercise series during 1970–1990. If the expansion offers military exercises, present them as a player-negotiated alternate-history programme—such as annual border-defence command-post manoeuvres—and price political exposure, fuel, logistics and specialist time. Do not narrate them as established historical fact.
