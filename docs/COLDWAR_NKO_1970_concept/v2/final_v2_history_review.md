# Historical and localization review: NKO v2

**Overall:** The design’s core historical framing is sound. It separates the 1970 Kim Il Sung government from counterfactual alternatives, makes bilateral offers recipient-controlled, and presents the nine packages as proposals rather than already signed new accords. The biggest terminology correction is the starting spirit titled **“Byungjin: Economy and Defence.”**

## Findings

- **1970 language:** The historical substance of a defence burden competing with civilian investment is appropriate. The line adopted in 1962 and reaffirmed in the 1970 Fifth Congress was described contemporaneously as carrying on economic construction simultaneously with defence building. “Byungjin” is liable to read as the familiar Kim Jong Un-era economy-and-nuclear-weapons policy, announced in 2013. Rename the starting spirit “Economic and Defence Construction” or “Simultaneous Economic and Defence Construction”; retain the existing trade-off effects. North Korea’s retrospective account itself calls the earlier line a precursor to its 2013 nuclear Byungjin policy, which supports distinguishing the period vocabulary. [Ministry of Unification, military policy](https://nkinfo.unikorea.go.kr/nkp/pge/view.do?menuId=MENU_17); [KCNA’s 2013 announcement](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/dprk/2013/dprk-130402-kcna02.htm); [KINU analysis of the 2013 line](https://repo.kinu.or.kr/retrieve/2003).
- **Starting conditions:** The ration spirit describes uneven supply and explicitly says it is not the 1990s famine; this is the right distinction. No starting spirit imports the famine or mature Songun system into 1970. The industrial bottleneck, plan delay, and defence-versus-civilian investment trade-offs fit the period’s established industrial state.
- **Leadership:** Kim Il Sung remains the starting leader. The design correctly places heir cultivation after 1974 and the Sixth Congress confirmation in 1980. The news event is dated **8 July 1994**, and its guard replaces only the incumbent Kim Il Sung regime with Kim Jong Il; alternate governments remain in office. “Assumed the leadership” is acceptable as a broad description of de facto authority. The explicit note that Kim Jong Il is not automatically President or WPK General Secretary in 1994 prevents a false title claim: he became General Secretary in October 1997. [U.S. National Security Archive, 9 July 1994 intelligence summary](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB421/docs/19940709.pdf); [KBS leadership-post timeline](https://world.kbs.co.kr/special/northkorea/contents/archives/supreme_leader/kim_jong_il.htm).
- **Bilateral offers and upgrades:** The nine packages—USSR industrial cooperation, PRC border/agricultural exchange, ROK inter-Korean exchange, Soviet civilian atomic cooperation, GDR machinery/materials, Bulgarian engineering, Romanian exchange, Egyptian technical cooperation, and Japanese fisheries/commerce—are phrased as proposals and require a recipient response. Their paired spirits and persistent duration are gameplay abstractions; do not describe them in explanatory prose as historical treaties or as historically perpetual commitments. Existing 1961 Soviet/Chinese treaty relationships and the 1969/1983 Egyptian cooperation framework are useful precedents, not proof that these exact game packages were signed. The 1972 Joint Statement and 1980 confederal plan likewise support an optional offer, not a completed integration.
- **Partner-specific caution:** Change **“Repair Ties with Beijing”** if concise accuracy is desired; “Propose Chinese Agricultural Exchange” avoids implying that relations had been severed. The Japan package is carefully framed as a new, government-authorized limited protocol without diplomatic recognition; retain that caveat because the cited 1977 fisheries arrangement was a private interim agreement, while Japan’s 1971 Bluebook explicitly says there were no diplomatic relations. Chongryon trade is correctly a separate domestic/diaspora spirit, not a Japan-government agreement. [Japan MFA 1971 Bluebook](https://www.mofa.go.jp/mofaj/gaiko/bluebook/1971/s46-2-1-1.htm); [Japan MFA 1980 Bluebook](https://www.mofa.go.jp/mofaj/gaiko/bluebook/1980/s55-2010101-3.htm).
- **Dates for precedent-inspired upgrades:** The GDR 1978–84 cooperation framework, Bulgarian 1972 credit and later 1986–90 programme, Egypt’s 1983 broad accord, and the 1985 NPT accession are correctly identified in concept material as historical precedents. If the corresponding offers can be made before those dates, their proposal wording already marks them as alternate-history; date-gating or a short counterfactual label would make that distinction stronger. The design’s date guard for the NPT stage is the clearest instance of good practice. [Wilson Center, GDR–DPRK agreements](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/publication/CWIHP_Bulletin_14-15.pdf); [Wilson Center, Bulgarian archives](https://www.wilsoncenter.org/publication/korea-the-bulgarian-archives-1945-1995-introduction); [Egyptian State Information Service, agreement record](https://sis.gov.eg/en/international-relations/bilateral-relations/north-korea/); [IAEA DPRK safeguards report](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/dprk.pdf).
- **Funeral image:** The cited Daily Herald page verifies the July 1994 funeral procession image as released by KCNA, distributed by Korea News Service, and credited to the Associated Press. The design’s source credit and “AI color restoration for concept review” note accurately distinguish the altered preview from the archival source. The AP source image remains copyrighted; a concept preview citation does not itself grant permission for distribution or game use. [Daily Herald/AP image caption](https://www.dailyherald.com/20111226/news/what-to-look-for-at-north-koreas-funeral-for-kim-jong-il/).

**Review scope:** Historical and English-language concept text only. This review makes no edits to the design JSON, preview, or game files.
