## Volunteer aid: supported levers and hardcoded gaps

The valid volunteer modifier family is:

- `send_volunteers_tension`: reduces the world-tension threshold; native tooltip at `localisation/english/MD_dm_modifiers_l_english.yml:371`.
- `send_volunteer_size`: adds integer volunteer capacity; tooltip at line 286.
- `send_volunteer_divisions_required`: reduces the share of the sender's army it must retain; tooltip at lines 73-74. MD examples use -5%/-15% (`common/national_focus/05_armenia.txt:6786-6788,7523-7524`).
- `send_volunteer_factor`: scales volunteer capacity; native use +5% in the same Armenia focus (`05_armenia.txt:6787`).

These are actual idea/dynamic-modifier keys: the SOV land dynamic modifier maps all four (`common/dynamic_modifiers/99_SOV_dynamic_modifiers.txt:267-271`); Armenia maps and changes them through dynamic variables (`99_ARM_dynamic_modifiers.txt:117-120`, with native effect examples above). A country-level `can_send_volunteers = yes` rule is a separate permission switch, not a numeric modifier; an MD event sets it directly (`events/05_north_korea_events.txt:3556`). The global game rule defaults to LIMITED (`common/game_rules/00_game_rules.txt:1361-1379`), and `DIPLOMACY_SEND_VOLUNTEERS_ENABLE_TRIGGER` handles free/limited/same-ideology/blocked modes (`common/scripted_triggers/00_game_rule_triggers.txt:666-704`). In SAME_IDEOLOGY mode it checks `has_government = FROM`; LIMITED does not impose that extra ideology check.

The MD scripted trigger contains no explicit same-faction or target-at-war test, and a source search found no separate `can_receive_volunteers` trigger. Those diplomatic-action constraints are engine-side, not proven by these MD files. The stock 1970 alignment setup in the local mod creates NATO under USA and adds GRE/TUR, but not CYP (`common/scripted_effects/IMP_1970_world_politics_effects.txt:1205-1252,1393`; CYP's initializer is `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CYP_1970_effects.txt:21-56`). Thus GRE and CYP are not same-faction at the 1970 start; a simple same-faction ban does not block GRE-to-CYP aid. However, after Turkish intervention CYP may be fighting NATO member TUR while GRE remains in NATO. The action's treatment of volunteers supporting a country at war with the sender's faction ally is not script-defined here and must be checked in game. A focus can ease the cap/threshold; it does not itself send volunteer divisions. If the action rejects this ally-versus-recipient situation, the proposed easy-volunteers branch is not available as written.

## NATO exit and restoration

The NATO member idea is the authoritative membership receipt: its `on_add`/`on_remove` maintains `global.nato_members` (`common/ideas/Factions.txt:5-25`). `NATO_currently_exists` reads that array and `can_join_NATO` checks membership eligibility (`common/scripted_triggers/00_NATO_scripted_triggers.txt:1-22`).

Use the native scripted helpers rather than manually removing ideas or calling raw faction effects:

- `NATO_leave` (`common/scripted_effects/01_NATO_effects.txt:166-218`) removes the member idea and NATO tech sharing, transfers leadership first if the leaving country is leader, leaves its current faction, removes NATO military-access/opinion links from other current members, adds `left_nato` opinion penalties, and announces the exit. Since GRE/TUR are not the USA faction leader at scenario setup, their departure does not transfer US leadership. Do not dismantle the NATO faction.
- `NATO_join` (`01_NATO_effects.txt:64-120`) requires `can_join_NATO`, restores opinion and military-access links, adds the membership idea and tech-sharing group, then joins a current NATO faction leader found in `global.nato_members`. With USA still the current leader this preserves USA leadership. GRE and TUR both satisfy the scripted eligibility geography/exceptions.

Important lifecycle detail: `NATO_leave` is much broader than flipping a flag. Save a separate country flag for each participant that actually had `NATO_member` immediately before exit; call `NATO_join` only for those saved tags after this conflict ends and NATO still exists. This prevents granting NATO to a tag that had already left for another reason. `NATO_join` does not explicitly remove the `left_nato` opinion modifier applied on departure, so decide whether that diplomatic consequence should persist or be cleaned up when temporary membership is restored. Do not clear/rebuild the whole `global.nato_members` array, tech-sharing group, or USA faction.

## War identity and completion detection

The script API exposes pairwise `has_war_with`, not a native scriptable war ID. The core on-actions include `on_war_relation_added` and `on_peaceconference_ended` (`common/on_actions/00_on_actions.txt:205,228`); I found no generic `on_war_ended`/`on_war_relation_removed` hook. Therefore, tie membership snapshots to a unique GRE-TUR intervention flag and require only that pair's `has_war_with` relation to end; do not use a generic `has_war = no`, which can be held open by unrelated wars. Use peace-conference completion for a targeted immediate check where available, plus a low-cost fixed-tag monthly fallback for unhooked peace endings. Prevent a second GRE-TUR intervention from starting until the first marker is cleared, so a new conflict cannot be mistaken for the old one. Do not make the marker global or scan every country.

For Turkish refusal, a named 180-day `Angry Army` timed idea is mechanically supported. Treat “coup risk” as a scripted crisis variable/threshold plus a guarded finite event or permitted decision, not a presumed native domestic-coup modifier. MD's Thailand example keeps a 0-100 `SIA_coup_risk` variable, clamps it and swaps visible risk-tier ideas (`common/scripted_effects/SIA_thailand_effects.txt:290-344`; `common/ideas/Thailand.txt:481-504`). The idea's `influence_coup_modifier` is not itself a domestic coup trigger. A single delayed 180-day event can check refusal marker, current government, existing coup/crisis state and once-per-crisis receipt; cancel or no-op it if those conditions changed. Avoid a new daily observer.
