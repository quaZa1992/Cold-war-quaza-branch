# COLDWAR UAE: British protection and independence branch

Research date: 19 October 2026. Scope: the historical basis and limits of a British protected-state branch before sovereign UAE formation. Research and design constraints only; no generator or loaded game file changed.

## Historical finding

**A January 1970 British-subject abstraction is defensible for gameplay, provided the text calls the arrangement special treaty protection rather than a British colony.** Remaining under that protection beyond 1971 is a plausible negotiated alternative, not the historical outcome. Independence and federation formation must be separate changes: the British treaty relationship ended before the sovereign UAE was proclaimed.

## 1. What the status actually meant in 1970

The seven Trucial States were separate sheikhdoms in special treaty relations with Britain, with their own rulers. Britain controlled the conduct of external relations and carried defence responsibilities. An MD puppet relationship compresses those treaty restrictions into one game mechanic; it is not evidence that Britain owned the territory, that local rulers were ordinary colonial governors, or that a unified UAE state already existed.

This distinction is unusually well evidenced. In *Buttes Gas and Oil v Hammer*, the House of Lords reproduced the Foreign Secretary's certificate for 1969–71: Britain recognised Sharjah as a sovereign state and Khalid as its sovereign ruler, held responsibility for foreign relations and defence, and did not claim sovereignty over Sharjah. British jurisdiction nevertheless extended to specified persons and matters. This is a description of historical status, not advice about present law. [House of Lords judgment transcript, 1982 AC 888, pp. 927–928](https://www.uniset.ca/other/css/1982AC888.html)

Use **British protection**, **Protected States**, **special treaty relations**, or **Trucial States under British protection** in player-facing text. “Protectorate” is an understandable shorthand, and the termination instruments themselves refer to protectorate treaties, but “British colony of the UAE” is misleading. The current British-subject tag represents several emirate treaty relationships at once. [UK Treaty Series No. 34 (1972), exchanges of notes terminating special treaty relations](https://treaties.fcdo.gov.uk/data/Library2/pdf/1972-TS0034.pdf)

## 2. The withdrawal timetable was already policy, not a future surprise

On **16 January 1968**, Harold Wilson announced that British forces would withdraw from the Persian Gulf by the end of **1971**. His statement connected the decision with spending, overseas commitments and Britain's economic position. Therefore a January 1970 branch begins after the withdrawal announcement, with the question of how to replace or renegotiate protection already urgent. [Wilson's original statement, Hansard, 16 January 1968, especially paragraph 12](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1968/jan/16/public-expenditure)

Do not portray 1971 as a sunset clause that had always been written into the nineteenth-century agreements. It was the announced termination timetable, implemented by new diplomatic instruments. “The 1971 withdrawal timetable” is more precise than suggesting that an untouched 1892 treaty simply aged out.

The actual sequence was:

| Date | Historical change |
|---|---|
| January 1968 | British withdrawal announced for completion by the end of 1971. |
| July 1970 | The new Conservative government publicly pursued consultations on Britain's future Gulf role. |
| 1 March 1971 | Britain confirmed replacement of the protected relationships by new arrangements and a friendship treaty, rather than retaining the old operational commitment. |
| 1 December 1971 | Special treaty relations terminated with all seven Trucial States. |
| 2 December 1971 | Six emirates proclaimed the sovereign UAE; Britain signed a Treaty of Friendship with the new union. |

The last two steps are explicitly distinguished in the Foreign Secretary's report to Parliament. **Ras Al Khaimah also left the British relationship on 1 December despite not joining the UAE on 2 December.** Independence therefore did not require prior federation membership. [Douglas-Home, Hansard, 6 December 1971](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/dec/06/the-gulf)

## 3. Is a loyal retained-protection route plausible?

Yes, as a labelled alternative requiring a British decision. Some rulers preferred continued British protection and offered to help meet the military costs. Contemporary parliamentary debate specifically records Abu Dhabi's offer, although individual speeches reflect partisan argument and do not establish a binding accepted price. A protection proposal with negotiated cost sharing, access rights and retained restrictions has historical roots; a guaranteed purchase of British protection for a fixed sum does not. [Hansard, 25 January 1968, debate on Gulf defence and financial offers](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1968/jan/25/foreign-affairs)

The Conservative government elected in 1970 genuinely reconsidered the future British role through consultations. On 6 July, Douglas-Home said that Britain would consult regional rulers about stability and did not want to impose its presence where unwelcome. That creates a credible counterfactual opening in 1970, rather than requiring an invented British policy out of nowhere. It does not establish that London historically accepted indefinite protection. [Hansard, 6 July 1970, Gulf-policy passage](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1970/jul/06/foreign-affairs)

Specific constraints:

- A local “loyalty” focus can **request** an extension and offer terms. It cannot bind the UK to retain forces or assume new obligations.
- Remaining a subject until the existing withdrawal date is continuation of the starting arrangement. Retaining that status **beyond** the historical termination requires an explicitly accepted alternative compact.
- Costs, British access, advisory appointments, limits on diplomacy and renewal arrangements should be terms of that compact. Proposed numerical game terms are balance choices, not documented historical treaty prices.
- Britain's consent is substantive. London must be able to prefer its withdrawal policy, propose a shorter extension, or offer independent friendship/cooperation instead.
- If Britain declines continued protection, the branch must offer a route back to withdrawal negotiations. Local willingness to remain protected did not historically prevent British departure.

## 4. Independence did not end British cooperation

The March 1971 British offer included consultation in time of need, transfer of the Trucial Oman Scouts, loaned personnel, training teams, exercises, naval visits and assistance with equipment and policing. The union would finance its own forces. Britain expected continued aircraft overflight and staging arrangements. The Foreign Secretary clarified that a continuing British personnel presence would serve liaison and training roles, not preserve the old operational force commitment. This supports meaningful cooperation on the independent route as well as the retained-protection alternative. [Hansard, 1 March 1971](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/mar/01/persian-gulf-states)

The **2 December 1971 Treaty of Friendship** must not automatically be labelled an unconditional military guarantee or continued puppet status. Its signature is separately documented in the UK treaty register, and the UAE National Archives hold a photograph of Zayed signing it. [UK parliamentary treaty listing, TS 035/1972, Cmnd 4937](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200506/ldselect/ldconst/236/236ii.pdf), [UAE National Archives: treaty signing](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/treaty-of-friendship-between-united-arab-emirates-and-united-kingdom/QgGQKTL0JTsuug)

## 5. Constraints for the requested branch

| Requested feature | Historically coherent treatment |
|---|---|
| British puppet starting branch | Identify the MD subject state as a mechanical representation of the seven protected treaty relationships. |
| Loyal protected route | Request a renewed protection compact. Show that continued external restrictions buy negotiated security cooperation; UK acceptance is required. Mark post-1971 continuation as alternative history. |
| Request independence focus | Send Britain a termination/release proposal. The proposal itself changes no sovereign status. Historical default concerns an agreed late-1971 handover; an earlier release is an accelerated alternative. |
| British acceptance | End the existing British-subject relationship once, through an explicit accepted release. Record that the release has happened. |
| Main withdrawal/formation focus | Require actual game independence before the sovereign proclamation completes. It should settle federal institutions, identity and leadership; it must not release the country a second time. |
| Union preparation while protected | Negotiations, development cooperation and draft charters can occur before independence. The final sovereign declaration remains distinct. |
| UK refusal or counter-offer | Preserve diplomatic options. Refusing early release is different from refusing all future withdrawal. An indefinite post-1971 refusal is itself a counterfactual British choice. |
| A player seeking war | Keep war an explicit escalation outside the historical peaceful transition. Neither an independence request nor its refusal historically creates an automatic war. Peace terms, recognition and control require their own resolution. |

There is no verified historical independence purchase fee in this research. An administrative transition cost may be proposed for balance, but it must not be described as the actual price Britain demanded for releasing the emirates.

## 6. Security claims to avoid

- Continued British protection must not guarantee that Iran abandons its island claims. Iran landed on the islands on 30 November 1971, before the Trucial treaty relationships ended. The Abu Musa arrangement and the Tunbs seizure followed different negotiations and events. [US State Department, 1 December 1971](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d110)
- A retained British garrison cannot automatically force Britain into every war begun by the local government. Commitments and command restrictions require actual terms.
- Independence must not erase all foreign advisers, training links, commercial relationships or access arrangements.
- A loyal British path should not be called the historical UAE founding route after 1971. The actual outcome was sovereign federation plus continuing cooperation.

## Recommended narrative boundary

The opening choice is **seek a renewed British protection compact** or **seek the termination of the special treaty relationship**. Both begin from real 1970 anxieties and institutions. The historically faithful independent outcome is a negotiated British release followed by sovereign federation; the retained-subject outcome is a consensual alternative requiring London to reverse or extend its withdrawal policy.

## Additional note: paired treaty spirits and the agreed date

A treaty spirit shown to **both Britain and the Trucial/UAE player** can represent the same accepted agreement. Both tooltips should identify the parties, their own obligations, the other party's commitments, and the same agreed date. The spirit records an actual accepted compact; an unanswered proposal is not yet a treaty.

For a negotiated handover, distinguish **termination of special treaty relations on 1 December 1971** from **UAE founding on 2 December 1971**. These were separate historical acts. A game compact accepted in 1970 and displaying that handover date is a proposed interface model of the negotiated transition; it must not be presented as a newly discovered, historically named 1970 independence treaty or proclamation. Any different accepted date is an explicit alternative. [Foreign Secretary's contemporary account, 6 December 1971](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/dec/06/the-gulf)

The paired handover spirits may explain the agreed timetable, transitional security/advisory responsibilities, access terms and the transfer of external responsibilities. Only obligations included in the accepted offer should appear. Continued training or friendship after release is a separate surviving agreement where applicable, not proof that the subject relationship continues.

For the loyal retained-protection branch, show an agreed **review date**, **extension end date** or **termination terms**, according to what Britain and the local rulers accepted. A review date is not automatically an independence date. No historical evidence here establishes routine five-year renewals; any such term must be labelled as part of the proposed alternative compact. The two spirits should agree on what occurs at review, expiry, renegotiation or accepted termination, without inventing historical automatic renewals.
