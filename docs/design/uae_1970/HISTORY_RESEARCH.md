# COLDWAR UAE / Trucial States: historical evidence report

Research date: 19 October 2026. Scope: January 1970 through 1990, with a limited 1991–2000 horizon. This is historical research for the concept stage, not an implemented focus tree or a claim that any proposed game effect works.

## 1. The January 1970 starting situation

The independent United Arab Emirates did **not** yet exist. The seven Trucial sheikhdoms had individual rulers and British treaty relationships. There was an existing Trucial States Council and development machinery, alongside negotiations for a larger union. The treaties constrained foreign relations and Britain supplied the external security framework. Withdrawal had been announced in 1968 for completion by the end of 1971. This was a negotiated transfer of responsibilities, not a British colony becoming a single pre-existing UAE administration. [UAE National Library and Archives: British Era](https://www.nla.ae/en/our-history/historical-periods/british-era/)

Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan had ruled Abu Dhabi since 6 August 1966; Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum ruled Dubai. Their February 1968 agreement supplied the political nucleus of union. Zayed was already directing oil income toward shared development. His position in the proposed union must be distinguished from his later office as president of the sovereign UAE. [UAE National Library and Archives: Formation of the Federation](https://www.nla.ae/en/our-history/historical-periods/the-formation-of-the-federation/)

The January 1970 rulers were:

| Emirate | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Abu Dhabi | Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan |
| Dubai | Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum |
| Sharjah | Khalid bin Mohammed Al Qasimi |
| Ras Al Khaimah | Saqr bin Mohammed Al Qasimi |
| Ajman | Rashid bin Humaid Al Nuaimi |
| Umm Al Quwain | Ahmad bin Rashid Al Mualla |
| Fujairah | Mohammed bin Hamad Al Sharqi |

These are the founding-ruler cohort, rather than modern rulers projected backward. In particular, Sultan bin Mohammed Al Qasimi was not yet Sharjah's ruler. Rashid of Dubai must not be confused with his son Maktoum, the first UAE prime minister. [UAE government: Founders of the Union](https://u.ae/en/about-uae/founders-of-the-union), [UAE government: Cabinet history](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-uae-government/the-uae-cabinet)

The Council was a cooperative institution, not a strong federal executive. Its chairmanship had passed from the British political agent to the rulers in 1965. Saqr of Ras Al Khaimah was its first elected chairman, serving until October 1968; this does not establish that he remained chairman in January 1970. Its Development Fund supported roads, agriculture, schools and health services. [Ras Al Khaimah Department of Antiquities and Museums](https://rakheritage.rak.ae/chapters/the-beginnings-of-the-uae/)

## 2. Starting difficulties, with evidence and limits

| Historical problem | What the evidence supports | What should not be asserted |
|---|---|---|
| Fragmented authority | Separate emirate administrations, local interests and an unfinished common government. | An already consolidated federal state in 1970. |
| Unequal resources | Abu Dhabi oil wealth and Dubai trade stood apart from the much poorer northern emirates. | Every emirate being equally rich because oil existed somewhere in the territory. |
| Limited administrative capacity | Finance was easier to obtain in Abu Dhabi than skilled planners, officials and technicians. | Oil revenue immediately translating into efficient institutions. |
| Thin services and infrastructure | Rapid expansion was underway, but healthcare, education, water, roads and power had large deficits. | An entirely empty country without existing schools, towns, trade or development projects. |
| Small skilled national workforce | Imported workers filled construction jobs and many professional/public-service positions. | A large citizen military manpower pool created by population growth alone. |
| Vulnerable security | British withdrawal, separate armed forces, foreign expertise and stronger neighbours. | Military parity with Iran or Saudi Arabia. |

A contemporary British Treasury file describes the sharp resource inequality and identifies fragmented planning and shortage of skilled manpower as obstacles. Its background material estimates Abu Dhabi oil revenue in 1968 at approximately £68 million, versus combined revenues of only about £0.3 million for the five poorer states. These figures illustrate disparity; they are neither a January 1970 budget nor values ready for direct game conversion. [TNA T 317/1455 via Arabian Gulf Digital Archive](https://agda.ae/en/archives/TNA/T/317/1455)

A World Bank mission brief reports that the main service indicators in Abu Dhabi and Dubai more than doubled during 1971–73, while labour demand exceeded the small local skill base. Construction relied heavily on workers from Pakistan, India and Iran; administration and social services used many Arab expatriates. This supports a shortage-and-rapid-growth starting picture, while its observations are from after federation and should not be presented as exact 1970 statistics. [World Bank contemporary UAE mission files](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/763391399316505980-0560011975/original/WorldBankGroupArchivesFolder1772922.pdf)

The 1970–71 school population was only about 16,400, including approximately 900 secondary pupils, according to the Dubai government's retrospective account. This is stronger support for an education bottleneck than an invented national illiteracy percentage. [Dubai Government Media Office: Armed Forces unification speech](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2021/May/05-05/Mohammed-bin-Rashid-speech)

In January 1970, even the important Dubai–Ras Al Khaimah trunk road was still being completed: a contemporary road-system account dates completion of the 102 km route through Sharjah, Ajman and Umm Al Quwain to spring 1970. [Contemporary Ras Al Khaimah road-system account, Exeter Digital Archive](https://humanities-collections.exeter.ac.uk/dame/files/original/6a30292f01debe6b7cccab149d3b997e30970554.pdf)

## 3. Federation was a choice, not an inevitable scripted merger

The proposed union included **Bahrain and Qatar as well as the seven Trucial States**. Discussions had made progress and encountered severe differences by late 1969, but the nine-state option was still alive in 1970. The Geoffrey Arthur archive contains Qatar's provisional constitution of 2 April 1970, correspondence with Zayed in October/November 1970, and memoranda concerning Qatar's membership and Bahrain's demand for representation proportional to population. This is direct evidence against treating Bahrain and Qatar as irreversibly gone on the scenario's first day. [Oxford Middle East Centre: Geoffrey Arthur collection inventory](https://www.sant.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/gb165-0430-sir-geoffrey-arthur-collection.pdf)

Constitutional representation, control of common institutions and protection of individual rulers' sovereignty were serious issues. Bahrain's Iranian claim also had to be resolved: the UN good-offices exercise and Security Council endorsement came in May 1970. A nine-state federation is consequently a credible counterfactual diplomatic outcome; effortless incorporation of Bahrain and Qatar is not historical. [TNA FCO 8/1372, Iranian claim to Bahrain](https://agda.ae/en/archives/TNA/FCO/8/1372)

Six emirates established the sovereign UAE on **2 December 1971**. Ras Al Khaimah acceded on **10 February 1972**. The first FNC session followed on **13 February 1972**. The FNC was an advisory institution rather than an elected sovereign parliament. [UAE government: History](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/history), [WIPO-hosted constitutional text and accession instruments](https://wipolex-res.wipo.int/edocs/lexdocs/laws/en/ae/ae030en.html), [UAE government: FNC history](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-uae-government/the-federal-national-council-)

The federal settlement retained substantial emirate authority. Natural resources belonged to the respective emirates; Abu Dhabi's petroleum was not automatically a common federal asset. Each ruler sat on the Supreme Council. Substantive decisions required five votes including **both Abu Dhabi and Dubai**, giving the two large emirates a constitutional veto. Islam and Arab identity were integral to the settlement, and presidential election occurred among rulers, not by popular national vote. [UAE Cabinet: Constitution, Articles 7, 23, 46, 49 and 51](https://uaecabinet.ae/en/the-constitution)

Historical tension lay in building a more effective federation while preserving consent. The 1976 and 1979 constitutional crises, including conflict over military integration and demands for a permanent constitution, make this an enduring national issue rather than a single founding-day problem. A 1979 cabinet compromise placed Rashid of Dubai in the premiership. The claim that the historical solution was simply absolute Abu Dhabi centralisation is false. [Contemporary-institutional study citing official gazettes, Warwick thesis, chapter four](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/36095/1/WRAP_THESIS_Jakka_1993.pdf), [Frauke Heard-Bey, Statehood and Nation-Building](https://www.cooperative-individualism.org/heard-bey-frauke_the-united-arab-emirates-2005-summer.pdf)

## 4. Islands, borders and regional security

On **30 November 1971**, Iranian forces landed on Abu Musa and the Greater and Lesser Tunbs. The mechanisms differed. Sharjah had accepted a British-mediated arrangement allowing Iranian occupation of a defined part of Abu Musa while retaining civil administration elsewhere. Ras Al Khaimah refused a comparable concession concerning the Tunbs, where the Iranian landing met resistance. Sovereignty remained contested; present-day UAE and Iranian claims should be labelled as claims, not an internationally adjudicated conclusion. [US Department of State, memorandum of 1 December 1971](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d110)

A contemporary US intelligence account records that Iran treated control of the islands as a condition of accepting a union and describes the asymmetry between Iranian power and the small sheikhdoms. This makes negotiation under pressure, resentment and security dependence authentic issues. A guaranteed easy reconquest is not supported. [FRUS, Helms report on the Gulf](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d101)

The Saudi–Abu Dhabi territorial dispute was another obstacle to regional cooperation, independent of the islands. The **Treaty of Jeddah, 21 August 1974**, was an actual border settlement, with later Emirati reservations. Its existence should not be simplified into Saudi patronage having no diplomatic price or an entirely uncontested permanent solution. [UN Treaty Series registration and text](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=08000002800b448c)

British withdrawal did not remove every British officer. Advisers, contractors and seconded personnel continued serving. The 1972 Sharjah coup was suppressed through cooperation between the Abu Dhabi Defence Force and the Union Defence Force. Relations with Iran were pursued despite the island dispute. A pro-Western security orientation therefore coexisted with regional diplomacy. [US State Department assessment, 28 December 1972](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d36)

The legal unification of armed forces occurred on **6 May 1976**, after a period of distinct emirate forces. This should be understood as a major integration decision, not proof that all organisational differences disappeared overnight. [Dubai Government Protocol Department: Armed Forces Unification Day](https://www.protocol.dubai.ae/en/media-listing/news-events/mohammed-bin-rashid-decision-to-unify-uae-armed-forces-pivotal-moment-in-uaes-history/)

The GCC Charter was signed in Abu Dhabi on **25 May 1981**, by the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait. Its stated objectives cover economic, legislative, social and technical cooperation as well as shared security concerns. The attachment's description as solely an anti-Iran military alliance is too narrow. British contemporary records explicitly connect its formation with the Iran–Iraq War and distinguish bilateral aid to Iraq from GCC decisions. [GCC founding Charter](https://www.gcc-sg.org/en/AboutUs/Pages/default.aspx), [TNA FCO 8/4281](https://agda.ae/en/archives/TNA/FCO/8/4281)

The USSR relationship also needs nuance. UAE foreign ministry history dates diplomatic relations to 8 December 1971, with the Soviet embassy opening in Abu Dhabi in 1986 and the UAE embassy in Moscow in 1987. Diplomatic or commercial contact with Moscow was thus not equivalent to communist accession. [UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs: Moscow bilateral relations](https://www.mofa.gov.ae/en/Missions/Moscow/UAE-Relationships/Bilateral-Relations)

## 5. Oil, savings and commercial diversification

Oil exports predated federation: Abu Dhabi from 1962 and Dubai from 1969. The two emirates already had different economic structures; Dubai's entrepôt trade was not invented after 1979. ADNOC was founded in 1971 as an Abu Dhabi institution. Its historical timeline also places the first LNG cargo in 1977 and the gas-processing organisation in 1978. Hydrocarbon development included processing and domestic energy supply, not only crude export. [National Library and Archives: British Era](https://www.nla.ae/en/our-history/historical-periods/british-era/), [ADNOC historical timeline](https://reports.adnoc.ae/e/advancing-towards-net-zero)

Government participation developed through negotiated purchases: a February/March 1973 World Bank brief reports Abu Dhabi's initial 25% participation in ADPC and ADMA production facilities, effective January 1973. This offers a concrete historical distinction between commercial participation and wholesale uncompensated expropriation. [World Bank UAE mission brief, 1973](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/984811391204517823-0560011973/original/WorldBankGroupArchivesFolder1772673.pdf)

The national currency was introduced in **May 1973** by a Currency Board. The immediate predecessors were the **Bahraini dinar in Abu Dhabi** and the **Qatar–Dubai riyal elsewhere**, not a still-general Gulf rupee. The full central bank dates to 1980. [CBUAE institutional history](https://centralbank.ae/en/about/about-cbuae/our-history/), [IMF, Currency Arrangements and Banking Legislation in the Arabian Peninsula, 1975](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/024/1975/002/article-A009-en.xml)

The 1973 Arab oil embargo was a real political choice made alongside production cuts and price increases. It should not be described simply as a universal OPEC embargo: Iran was an OPEC member and not an Arab embargo participant. Abu Dhabi appears in contemporary US documentation concerning the embargo coalition. [FRUS, December 1973 Arab oil policy report](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v36/d268), [US congressional record, State Department testimony](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1973-pt29/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1973-pt29-8-3.pdf)

ADIA was created in March **1976** for the government of **Abu Dhabi**, building on a Financial Investments Board established in **1967**. A saving-and-investment institution therefore need not be invented from nothing in 1976, and its assets should not be described as automatically belonging equally to all emirates. [ADIA 2016 review](https://www.adia.ae/en/pr/2016/index.html), [ADIA 2009 history](https://www.adia.ae/media/azure/adia/media/annual_review_website2009.pdf)

The commercial milestones in the attachment are broadly correct: Mina Rashid commenced operations in **1972**, Jebel Ali Port opened in **1979**, and Jafza began in **1985**, initially with 19 businesses. Avoid a misleading Jafza English marketing sentence that confuses construction and operating dates; DP World's own historical timeline explicitly distinguishes 1979 port opening from 1985 free-zone formation. [DP World historical timeline](https://www.dpworld.com/uae/-/media/project/dpwg/dpwg-tenant/mea/uae/media-files/arabic-publications/381022017104804pm531-dp-world-uae_oct.pdf?rev=d01b28c6fc89434490c35b30dc22d078), [Jafza establishment history](https://www.jafza.ae/resource-centre/guides/where-is-jafza-located/)

Emirates' first commercial flights took place on **25 October 1985**, using a Boeing 737 and Airbus A300 wet-leased from Pakistan International Airlines. Its own account records $10 million seed funding and a commercial viability mandate. It was a Dubai venture, not an already global federal airline at the 1970 start. [Emirates corporate history](https://www.emirates.com/us/english/about-us/timeline/)

Oil did not make fiscal constraints disappear. The 1986 price collapse sharply reduced petroleum income; IMF historical analysis identifies prolonged budget pressure and public-investment cuts in regional exporters, including UAE deficits through much of the following period. This undercuts the attachment's suggestion of permanent unlimited wealth after 1973. [IMF Regional Economic Outlook, October 2015, historical discussion](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/reo/2015/mcd/eng/pdf/mreo1015.pdf)

## 6. Historical politics versus explicit alternative history

The federation was governed through ruling families and institutions, not competitive national parties. Later official country research also records the prohibition of political parties. Any MD party slots represent a game abstraction; they must not be retroactively described as real 1970 electoral parties. [US Library of Congress country profile](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/copr/UAE.pdf)

| Political direction | Evidential status |
|---|---|
| Dynastic federation with Zayed-led shared development | Historical core. Negotiated authority, local rulers and Abu Dhabi's financial contribution are central. |
| Dubai-centred commercial autonomy | Historical policy emphasis within the federation; not a documented liberal opposition party. |
| More effective federal administration and military integration | Historical objective, contested over pace and powers. |
| Abolition of the emirates under a unitary monarch | Explicit counterfactual. It would overturn the actual founding bargain. |
| Elected constitutional federation / liberal reform coalition | Explicit counterfactual expansion of consultative institutions. No verified national liberal party or competitive 1970 election mandate. |
| Arab-nationalist political challenge | Genuine regional and local historical influence. Its transformation into a national republic is counterfactual. |
| Socialist or communist republic | Higher-divergence counterfactual influenced by regional revolutionary movements; no verified strong UAE communist governing party in January 1970. |
| Sunni social-reform association evolving into politics | Has a later historical organisational basis in Islah; a national elected government remains counterfactual. |
| Shia revolutionary or Salafi-jihadist national government | No verified mass Emirati governing movement in this research. Muslim country status alone does not establish a credible national path. |

The important local nationalist hook is **Saqr bin Sultan Al Qasimi**, the former ruler of Sharjah, rather than an invented UAE Baath party. His removal in 1965 was connected to the conflict over Arab League assistance and British influence. His later attempted return must not be re-labelled as proof of a fully formed republican programme. [Sharjah ruler's official site: account based on My Early Life](https://sheikhdrsultan.ae/Portal/en/media-center/ruler-in-their-pens/28/10/2025/%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%A1%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B1-%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%B7%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A7%D8%B3%D9%85%D9%8A.aspx)

The attempted restoration on **24–25 January 1972** killed Sharjah's ruler Khalid and failed after federal intervention. Sultan bin Mohammed succeeded. For general design, the January timing is secure; use caution with exact hour, force size and death date, since popular accounts conflict. A contemporary US assessment confirms the ruler's death and successful joint response. [FRUS, December 1972 assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d36)

**Al-Islah was licensed in 1974 as a civil-society association**, with a long initial period of government cooperation. It was not an established national party in January 1970. A Sunni educational, welfare or moral-reform current is historically grounded later in the period; projecting the post-2011 confrontation backwards would be misleading. [UK House of Commons Library, SN06619](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/SN06619/SN06619.pdf)

PFLOAG, South Yemen and the Dhofar war supplied real revolutionary regional pressures. They do not establish that the Trucial States had a mass communist electorate. British and American intelligence fears are evidence of what officials feared, not neutral measurements of popular support. Similar caution applies to interpreting expatriate Arab teachers or Palestinian residents as automatic revolutionaries. [FRUS, Helms report](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v24/d101), [TNA FCO 8/2134, ADDF and regional security](https://agda.ae/en/archives/TNA/FCO/8/2134)

## 7. 1990–2000 horizon and attachment corrections

The UAE participated in the coalition that liberated Kuwait in **1991**. This is suitable historical continuation, but it is beyond a strict 1990 endpoint. The substantial formal US defence-cooperation agreement dates to **1994**, providing for exercises, prepositioning and equipment cooperation. It should not be presented as an already-existing 1970 alliance treaty. [Emirates News Agency: international military missions](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/hszr6im8-uae-armed-forces%E2%80%99-international-efforts), [UAE Embassy: security cooperation history](https://www.uae-embassy.org/uae-us-cooperation/security)

The constitution became permanent and Abu Dhabi became the permanent capital in **1996**. The original constitution instead envisaged a purpose-built capital, **Al Karama**, between Abu Dhabi and Dubai, with Abu Dhabi as temporary seat. This supplies an authentic unfulfilled institutional proposal. Modern consolidated constitutional pages mix later amendments into the founding text, so their present wording is not proof of original 1971 wording. [WIPO constitutional collection, historical Article 9 and 1996 amendment](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/17028)

Burj Al Arab opened in **1999**. Calling this the completion of all diversification would be an interpretation, not an established economic fact. It is a symbolic late-horizon tourism milestone. [Jumeirah corporate history](https://mediahub.jumeirah.com/25-years-of-redefining-luxury-celebrating-the-legacy-of-jumeirah-burj-al-arab/?lang=eng)

**F-16 Block 60 aircraft are anachronistic as a historical 1970–1990 delivery.** The manufacturer's record dates their first flight to December 2003 and the first UAE arrivals to May 2005. A 2000 procurement horizon can foreshadow the programme; it cannot honestly grant historically delivered Block 60 fighters during the Cold War. [Lockheed Martin, 4 May 2005 release](https://investors.lockheedmartin.com/node/8191/pdf)

## 8. Source cautions and unresolved precision

- Primary constitutional and treaty texts outrank modern promotional summaries for legal dates and powers. The accession instrument supports 10 February 1972 for Ras Al Khaimah; popular pages sometimes give adjacent dates.
- The GCC Charter itself supports 25 May 1981. Some biographies use 24 May; do not prefer that over the signed instrument.
- AGDA catalogue entries include modern machine-generated summaries. Where a claim depends on exact details, inspect the scanned document or OCR, not just the catalogue summary. British records reflect their authors' policy outlook and colonial language.
- No precise January 1970 national population, literacy rate, oil budget or citizen manpower figure has been established here to a level suitable for unqualified numerical localisation.
- No named national liberal, Baathist, communist, Salafi or Shia party roster was verified for January 1970. Any such names in existing mod politics require a separate code-and-history audit.
- The broad Dubai–Abu Dhabi tension is well established, but assigning every later policy to one emirate in a rigid binary would oversimplify considerable cooperation and changing bargains.
