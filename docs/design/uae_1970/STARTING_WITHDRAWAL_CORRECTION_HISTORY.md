# COLDWAR UAE: correction to the starting British withdrawal arrangement

Research date: 19 October 2026. Bounded recheck of four contemporary parliamentary records. Historical interpretation and concept constraints only; no generator or loaded files edited.

**The user is correct: withdrawal was already announced before the January 1970 start. Britain and the Trucial States should both start with a visible withdrawal spirit. The default historical handover must not depend on a new Emirati independence petition or another British approval.** Those are appropriate only for changing the existing policy, accelerating its timetable, or agreeing continued protection.

This corrects the earlier modelling suggestion in `BRITISH_PATH_HISTORY.md` wherever that suggestion would require a newly accepted treaty before the already-announced historical withdrawal can occur. The distinction between ending British protection and subsequently proclaiming the federation remains valid.

## What was announced, and what was agreed later?

| Date and primary evidence | Established fact | Limit on the claim |
|---|---|---|
| 16 January 1968: Harold Wilson's public-expenditure statement, paragraph 12 | Britain had decided to withdraw its forces from the Persian Gulf **by the end of 1971**. The statement also says relevant governments had been informed. | This is a British policy announcement. It does not establish a jointly signed 1968 UAE independence treaty or a precise 1 December handover date. |
| 4 March 1970: Defence Secretary Denis Healey's defence debate | The government expected to terminate its defence treaty obligations to the Lower Gulf states **before the end of 1971**. | This confirms an existing programme, not a proposal first created by a future Emirati focus. It is a March 1970 reaffirmation, not an event already held on 1 January. |
| 1 March 1971: Foreign Secretary Alec Douglas-Home's Gulf statement | He explicitly attributes the announced intention to terminate the protected relationships and withdraw forces to **January 1968**. The new government still stated an end-1971 deadline. | New friendship, personnel, exercise and access arrangements were proposals under discussion. When asked whether they were agreed, he said they were not yet agreed. |
| 6 December 1971: Douglas-Home's report of the completed transition | Britain had terminated special relations with all seven Trucial States on **1 December 1971**; the sovereign UAE was established and a friendship treaty signed on **2 December**. Ras Al Khaimah also left British protection despite not joining the federation that day. | This establishes the eventual precise date. It does not retrospectively prove that 1 December had been bilaterally agreed by January 1970. |

Sources: [Wilson, 16 January 1968](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1968/jan/16/public-expenditure), [Healey, 4 March 1970](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1970/mar/04/defence-1), [Douglas-Home, 1 March 1971](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/mar/01/persian-gulf-states), [Douglas-Home, 6 December 1971](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1971/dec/06/the-gulf).

The historically precise starting distinction is therefore **an announced British withdrawal commitment with an end-1971 horizon**, alongside **still-operative protected-state treaty responsibilities**. It is neither a blank diplomatic slate nor proof that every final handover term was already signed.

**Britain — “The Announced Gulf Withdrawal”**

“In January 1968, the government announced that British forces would leave the Gulf by the end of 1971. Existing treaty responsibilities continue during the transition, while the protected states prepare to assume their external affairs and defence.”

**Trucial States — “The Announced British Withdrawal”**

“Britain has announced its departure from the Gulf by the end of 1971. The rulers must prepare for the end of special treaty protection and settle their common institutions. A different timetable or renewed protection requires a new agreement.”

Do not label these starting spirits as an already signed new bilateral independence treaty. Nor should they invent new Emirati financial, basing or military obligations agreed in 1968. The existing treaty responsibilities and the announced future withdrawal are sufficient content.

## How to handle the precise date honestly

The evidence checked supports **end of 1971** at the scenario start, and **1 December 1971** as the actual later treaty-termination date. Two honest presentation options exist:

1. Start with the announced end-1971 deadline. A later handover settlement records the exact day; the historical scenario default resolves on 1 December 1971.
2. If the preview must show the exact scheduled game date immediately, label it **“Historical scenario handover: 1 December 1971”**, alongside **“Announced timetable: by the end of 1971.”** This discloses the scenario's use of hindsight. Do not call that exact day a date already jointly agreed in January 1970.

The federation's founding date remains **2 December 1971**, a distinct institutional act. Final federal proclamation may require actual game independence; British release must not require completion of that proclamation first.

## Default withdrawal and alternative consent

The historical route carries forward a decision already made before the start. It should proceed without requiring the UAE to spend political power petitioning for basic release or requiring the British player to click a fresh approval merely to honour the existing policy. Transition and administrative preparation can still have meaningful work, costs and consequences.

A request for **earlier independence**, a **new date**, or **continued British protection** changes the default and requires explicit bilateral acceptance. Merely choosing a loyal focus, sending an unanswered extension offer, or allowing an event to time out must not cancel the original schedule. Only a completed alternative agreement overrides it, with the replacement terms visible to both players.

The normal scheduled handover can remove or replace both withdrawal spirits together. A retained-protection compact can instead replace them with matching spirits showing its accepted review/termination terms. A review date is not automatically an independence date, and routine five-year renewals are not established by this research.

Britain historically reconsidered aspects of its future role and then maintained the withdrawal timetable. The counterfactual retained-protection route remains credible, but the consent gate belongs to **that deviation**, not to the announced historical departure. An attempt to reverse withdrawal unilaterally would need its own explicitly labelled alternative political action; silence or failure to petition is not such an action.

## Boundary of this correction

The default schedule is a historical scenario rule, not a claim that a policy speech alone legally replaced every treaty instrument in 1968. The later formal termination and friendship arrangements still matter. This correction simply prevents the playable January 1970 setup from forgetting a major decision already announced two years earlier.
