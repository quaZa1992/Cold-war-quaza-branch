# Iraq: historical policy-choice audit, 19 October 2026

Read-only design audit. No loaded game files changed. The eight pairs below are **proposals**, not approved values or historical claims that the policy would have produced a given percentage. Exact MD rewards need the companion mechanics/reference audit. The canonical input examined was `tools/iraq_1970/approved_iraq.json`, with the current Iran/Gulf implementation in `runtime_design.py` and `gulf_module.py` checked where relevant.

The strongest improvement is to rebuild existing forks into distinct programmes, merging their small follow-up rewards. Adding more icons with the same +2–4% reward does not make a better choice. Exclusion should usually mean competing investment priorities for the next several years; it should not imply that Iraq could never operate both sectors or ever change a contract. Paid policy reviews may change a current priority without replaying factories, cash or training grants.

## 1. Nationalize the IPC / settle a new participation contract

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_oil_nationalize` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_oil_renegotiate`; bundle the respective `oil_state_company` / `oil_concession_delivery` follow-up into a clearly advertised two-focus programme. Preserve the common engineering and maintenance continuation.

**Current weakness:** the concession entry gives no immediate mechanical benefit. The nationalized route eventually removes the whole starting oil burden while the concession keeps a foreign share. A player sees an inferior long-term concession unless the early advantage is concrete.

**A — Nationalize the IPC:** the larger long-run Iraqi oil-revenue share and direct control over development, plus an Iraqi engineering programme. **Downside:** a bounded, temporary marketing disruption and compensation/settlement costs; no permanent invented inability to operate oil wells.

**B — A participation and marketing settlement:** earlier reliable export receipts, a funded infrastructure/technical package and lower immediate public cash outlay. **Downside:** a smaller continuing Iraqi revenue share or a real purchase obligation to the partner. Counterparty consent remains real.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** ownership and the same concession's revenue contract cannot simultaneously be fully nationalized and foreign-operated on the proposed terms. Both routes can share training and export infrastructure. Renegotiation later must pay to change the contract, not stack both revenue shares.

**Historical basis:** nationalization occurred on 1 June 1972; the immediate CIA assessment emphasized marketing rather than insurmountable production problems. Its pessimistic forecast was a contemporary assessment, not the eventual outcome. The March 1973 settlement reopened opportunities. Sources: [CIA memorandum, June 1972, paragraphs 5–12](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve04/d311), [Baghdad assessment, 31 March 1973, paragraphs 5–6](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d208).

## 2. Basic industries first / consumer production first

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_industry_heavy` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_industry_light`; merge `industry_steel` / `industry_consumers` into the chosen priority's delivery rather than presenting almost identical rewards twice.

**Current weakness:** both routes ultimately buy one civilian factory. Their differences are +3% maximum efficiency versus +3% efficiency growth, with small generic negatives. The names promise a much larger contrast than the result.

**A — Basic industries and machine tools:** a concentrated industrial complex/workshop investment, a meaningful production-efficiency ceiling and an appropriate engineering bonus. **Downside:** higher initial cash requirements and a temporary consumer-goods cost while the project is built.

**B — Food processing, textiles and consumer production:** a civilian factory plus a useful reduction in the existing household/consumer burden and faster recovery of ordinary production efficiency. **Downside:** a smaller benefit to military/heavy production capacity. Do not grant a permanent all-factory output penalty merely because textiles exist.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** scarce trained labour, imported machinery and the first major investment tranche go to one priority. They can rejoin at electricity and transport; the special priority bonus remains exclusive. Later capital expansion need not be prohibited forever.

**Historical basis:** Iraqi planning sought integrated domestic production, diversified non-oil activity and development of food, chemicals and textiles alongside oil. Capital alone did not eliminate implementation and technical constraints. [CIA study, November 1976, Economic and Social Transformation](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d317).

## 3. Cooperative equipment pools / guaranteed smallholder credit

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_agri_cooperatives` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_agri_smallholders`; combine each with its existing credit follow-up. Common water inspection, extension, roads and clinics stay available.

**Current weakness:** +5% agriculture versus +4%, followed by the same +3%, feels like a minor tax-accounting choice. Both should alter the rural economy in a visible way.

**A — Cooperative mechanization:** the larger productivity improvement and one actual agricultural-district/equipment investment, tied to shared machinery and procurement. **Downside:** a continuing administrative cost and larger up-front mechanization expense.

**B — Smallholder guarantees:** earlier rural-burden relief, a smaller productivity gain and cheaper first delivery. **Downside:** a limited treasury cost/credit subsidy and less immediate scale for mechanization. Neither option should be the only way to fix the starting agrarian penalty.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** the same initial rural-credit budget and machinery pool cannot be spent twice. This is a funding priority, not a claim that private holdings and cooperatives never coexisted. Shared river management and extension join the routes naturally.

**Historical basis:** contemporary reporting described private holdings, cooperatives, collectives and state farms operating alongside one another, and questioned the effectiveness of collective policy despite high investment. [Baghdad assessment, 31 March 1973, paragraph 6](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d208).

## 4. A concentrated Republican Guard / strengthen the regular army

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_saddam_guard` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_saddam_regular_army`; merge `saddam_guard_supply` / `saddam_staff_college` into branch-defining packages. The shared `state/professional_command` and `defence_professional` must be checked so three programmes do not erase each other's choice.

**Current weakness:** the Guard gives +3% nationwide organization and +8% personnel cost; the regular army gives +4% organization and +5% training time. The Guard can look like a worse version of the regular army. The nationwide bonus also fails to express concentrated privilege.

**A — Concentrated Guard priority:** a costed ready reserve or equipment/training package and better regime reliability. **Downside:** a noticeable continuing personnel bill and opportunity cost to ordinary-force expansion. Use real named formations or native training/equipment mechanics if available; a national modifier must be described honestly as national.

**B — General Staff and regular formations:** a broader organization/recovery and doctrine-training improvement, with wider force readiness. **Downside:** slower training or a bounded loss of presidential political control, rather than an equally large continuing cost.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** the scarce officer training, modern kit and premium pay allocation is concentrated or distributed. Logistics infrastructure can be common; the two military priority packages cannot stack. A later wartime review can replace the current priority at a real cost.

**Historical basis:** party control over appointments conflicted with professional military independence; the Guard had a distinct place in regime security and later combat operations. Do not give January 1970 Iraq the fully expanded late-1980s Guard. [CIA study, November 1976, military section](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d317), [US Army history, Republican Guard in the 1990 invasion](https://history.army.mil/Portals/143/Images/Publications/ArmyHistoryMag/pdf/AH115.pdf?ver=VntIs7J8S5lwIyUeRuiy6Q%3D%3D).

## 5. A revenue-sharing autonomy bargain / central oil administration with local guarantees

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_north_talks` and `autonomy_guarantees` as the shared gateway; `kdp_oil_status` and `kdp_shared_revenue` as the Kurdish-negotiation interface. Do **not** hide the Iraqi government's main settlement behind the KDP governing Baghdad. A new pair can replace two minor account-keeping foci, rather than adding another full northern tree.

**A — Revenue-sharing settlement:** the larger relief of the northern stability and garrison burden, paid regional services and a meaningful local development asset. **Downside:** lower central oil/tax retention and a continuing regional budget transfer.

**B — Central oil administration:** Baghdad retains the larger central revenue share, while offering language, municipal-budget and local-policing guarantees. **Downside:** smaller initial pacification relief and a continuing security/administrative bill until an accepted settlement is implemented. It must still offer the Kurdish counterparty meaningful benefits; no guaranteed consent.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** oil-revenue retention and authority over Kirkuk are competing terms of one constitutional contract. Both can reach common schools, roads and civilian protections. If no agreement is reached, the existing conflict continues; repression is not a free third reward package.

**Historical basis:** the March 1974 autonomy breakdown concerned both limited internal authority and exclusion of oil-rich Kirkuk. These were substantive disputes, not merely a choice between friendship and irrational hostility. [Colby memorandum, 21 March 1974, including contemporary explanatory note](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d243).

## 6. Standardized Soviet supply / a diversified procurement network

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_defence_soviet_standard` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_defence_mixed_supply`; bundle `defence_soviet_offer` / `defence_maintenance_manuals` with them. Keep `defence_logistics` as a shared continuation.

**Current weakness:** -4% supply versus +3% maximum production efficiency is poorly matched to arms procurement. A mixed foreign inventory does not automatically make domestic factories much more productive.

**A — Standardize the inventory:** a substantial logistics/spares saving and a Soviet training or licensed-equipment opportunity. **Downside:** a concentration of supply and a real diplomatic or purchasing commitment. Acceptance by a Soviet player is required for transfers.

**B — Diversify suppliers:** access to a second actual arms/training offer and a bounded aircraft/technical improvement. **Downside:** a smaller but meaningful spares/logistics cost and more up-front cash. The alternative must offer capability or access that A cannot obtain, rather than merely another opinion modifier.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** one budget and maintenance doctrine emphasizes interoperability or supplier resilience. Iraq may buy from several countries historically; the exclusive focus is the priority subsidy and training system, not a permanent ban on trade. Supplier-specific grants never replay when policy changes.

**Historical basis:** Iraqi dependence on Soviet supply coexisted with resistance to Soviet political leverage and greater Western technical contacts. [CIA study, November 1976, military and East/West relations sections](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d317). Contemporary CIA arms assessments also describe diversification; their indexed PDFs currently redirected during verification, so their dollar figures were not used as audited facts.

## 7. Concentrate for a short campaign / prepare a war of endurance

**Reuse:** `COLDWAR_IRQ_iran_short_war` / `COLDWAR_IRQ_iran_sustained_war`. Preserve their native finite offensive mechanics, the actual-war gate and severe escalating war cost. Consolidate the extra small preparatory focuses into one legible programme per choice.

**A — Short campaign:** a powerful finite opening offensive, a real fuel/equipment reserve and rapid deployment preparation. **Downside:** a smaller prolonged-war production base; after the opening period, it no longer supplies that combat benefit. The short-war clock should start with the actual Iranian war, not be wasted while awaiting another player's reply.

**B — Endurance:** a larger sustainable production/reserve organization bonus and logistics preparation. **Downside:** a moderate civilian-research/consumer cost and no equivalent opening burst. It must not permanently amplify the already extreme automatic war bill until the player cannot use it.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** scarce reserves and industrial effort go to forward concentration or replacement capacity. Both can enter common campaign accounts and reconstruction; the opening package cannot be rearmed by reviewing policy. War is still a player choice and is not imposed in 1970.

**Historical basis:** Iraq's initial offensive did not produce the intended rapid decision; the war became a long conflict requiring major military expansion. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, Iran–Iraq War, 2006 article](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iraq-vii-iran-iraq-war/). This is a scholarly secondary synthesis. Exact durations and percentages are gameplay proposals, not historical laws.

## 8. Gulf liquidity / a domestic war levy

**Reuse:** the existing `COLDWAR_IRQ_iran_gulf_credit` and `iran_mobilisation_base` as the gateways; place the **financing priority** at their common strategic level. The Iran branch's own direct credit gateway sits only on its short-war leg. A separate working route exists through `COLDWAR_IRQ_saddam_credit` → `COLDWAR_IRQ_decision_gulf_credit` → `COLDWAR_IRQ_gulf_unlock_credit` (`decisions_module.py`, lines 301–305); a long-war player with that access is not barred from Gulf loans. The proposed change improves visibility and leadership-independent access within the Iran branch. Existing creditor consent, real transfers and debt ledger must survive.

**A — Negotiate Gulf liquidity:** the larger immediate war budget when an Arab player actually accepts, allowing normal civilian projects to continue longer. **Downside:** actual debt and continuing creditor leverage; loan refusal does not block the war tree.

**B — A domestic war levy:** a smaller reliable budget from native taxation/mobilization, available without foreign consent, and less new external debt. **Downside:** a bounded consumer-goods tax/cost increase and social strain during the emergency. It must not create money from an arbitrary repeated event.

**Why exclusive / where to join:** the exclusive benefit is the subsidized financing priority, not a historically false prohibition on ever combining tax and borrowing. Both receive ordinary financing tools; one gets better external terms, the other a stronger internal revenue package. Both reach a real post-war debt/reconstruction review.

**Historical basis:** Gulf credit financed Iraq's war and refusal to forgive loans later contributed to Saddam's coercion of Kuwait. This is a motive, not legal entitlement to Kuwait or a reason to make war automatic. [US State Department historical account, The Gulf War, 1991](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1989-1992/gulf-war). The stronger invasion incentives requested by the user can coexist with donor refusal, coalition response and expensive integration.

## Historical and structural things that look wrong

- **Uniform party templates:** governing-party and leader trees repeatedly use two small forks, an account/review join and three later spurs. Retain relevant parties but merge duplicate account foci into programme effects. Programme depth should come from actual policy consequences and decisions.
- **Duplicate Communist strategic decisions:** `icp_front_strategy` / `icp_independent_strategy` and `aziz_negotiated_road` / `aziz_opposition_road` repeat near-identical strategic decisions. Choose one strategic ownership point; use Aziz's branch for distinctive implementation and leadership decisions. National Front participation did not historically make the ICP the ruling party. Keep opposition/front decisions reachable while Baath governs; reserve sovereign government programmes for actual accession. The conditional front was historically contentious inside the ICP. [CIA study, November 1976, Communist and National Front sections](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d317).
- **KDP ending contradicts its own opening:** `kdp_autonomy` excludes `kdp_federal_republic`, yet the common final focus `kdp_common_republic` is titled **Federal Republic**. This is a confirmed naming/choice-presentation contradiction; its current small stability/PP package does not itself prove that the constitution is changed. Rename the common capstone to a neutral agreed-state settlement and preserve the selected constitution in effects.
- **Industry themes with identical deliveries:** heavy workshops and light consumer industries both grant one civilian factory. Different names and small efficiency shifts cannot carry this central economic choice.
- **The Iran branch's direct Gulf-credit gateway lives under the short-war leg:** a separate Saddam decision route can unlock the same credit system, so this is not a universal long-war financing lockout. The direct Iran-branch presentation still misleadingly ties finance to short-war doctrine. Make finance orthogonal to short/endurance operational doctrine and retain the existing alternate gateway.
- **National Guard bonus ambiguity:** a nationwide organization modifier described as an elite-formation effect overstates selectivity. Use the actual native scope or write the national abstraction honestly.
- **Rapid cores are alternate history:** Khuzestan and Kuwait integration are user-requested conquest systems. Show administrative, compliance, financial and security conditions; do not describe cores as a historically rightful restoration or universal popular demand.
- **1991 US/Kurdish chapter:** retain it as a conditional post-war continuation beyond the nominal 1970–1990 horizon. US support should respond to the actual crisis and recipient consent. UN resolution 688 addressed repression and humanitarian access; it did not itself create a geographically enforced flight-ban mechanic or a US-created Kurdish state. [UN Security Council resolution 688, 5 April 1991](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/110659?ln=en).

All sources accessed 19 October 2026. Contemporary diplomatic/intelligence sources contain their authors' judgments and acknowledged gaps; their observations support the tensions, not deterministic outcomes or proposed modifier numbers.
