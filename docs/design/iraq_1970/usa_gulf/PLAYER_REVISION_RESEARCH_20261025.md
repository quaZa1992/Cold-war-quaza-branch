IMPERIA COLD WAR: USA Gulf player revision research

25 October 2026. Independent historical research and design recommendations for the newly authorized revision. No gameplay, generator or immutable proposal was changed. Dates below describe history, not a proposal to restore arbitrary calendar locks on player programmes. Contemporaneous Presidential papers, declassified memoranda and Security Council texts establish policy; official military histories provide retrospective operational chronology.

## Historical distinctions that should govern the branch

**Clandestine support existed before 1991.** Kissinger's 24 June 1974 memorandum states that US financial and material support to Barzani began in July 1972. It records approval of $8.06 million for FY1975, partly for a continuing cash subsidy and partly for arms. This was support for an existing organized movement, not the creation of a recognized Kurdish state. Those are nominal historical annual dollars, not an MD weekly budget. [FRUS XXVII, document 254](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d254)

The July 1972 proposal preferred a limited, defensible guerrilla position, existing compatible weapons, Iranian delivery and restricted exposure. It rejected the scale of a much larger offensive scheme and warned about Soviet escalation, Turkish sensitivities and Kurdish factional divisions. This supports a meaningful liaison/logistics institution with recurring costs and limits, not a guaranteed march on Baghdad. [FRUS E-4, document 321, main memorandum and attachments](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve04/d321)

**External sponsorship could be interrupted by diplomacy.** Colby's 4 June 1975 termination memorandum states: "all our aid to the Kurds was channeled through the Iranians." Iran stopped its aid after the 6 March 1975 Algiers agreement with Iraq; the Kurdish resistance collapsed and the US programme ended. An Iraqi diplomatic agreement with a route state can therefore be real counterplay. It must actually affect that state's consent or supply route, rather than magically deleting an independently organized movement. [FRUS XXVII, document 289](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d289)

**Bush's appeal was broad political encouragement.** On 15 February 1991 he addressed the Iraqi military and people and urged them to "take matters into their own hands" against Saddam. He also connected an end to coalition operations with compliance over Kuwait. The speech did not name a specifically Kurdish uprising order or promise an American arms package for rebels. [Bush Presidential Library, Raytheon remarks, 15 February 1991](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/finding-aid/public-papers/remarks-raytheon-missile-systems-plant-employees)

**The uprisings were actual Iraqi events in March.** The official Army history places the southern and northern rebellions after the Gulf War cease-fire; Canadian defence history describes Kurdish territorial gains in March, Iraqi counterattacks and subsequent refugee flight. Political encouragement and expectations of help mattered, but the movement's own decisions and Iraq's postwar vulnerability remain necessary distinctions. [US Army CMH, *Between Desert Storm and Iraqi Freedom*, printed p.35](https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/57-1-1.pdf), [Canadian Department of National Defence operational history](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/past-operations/middle-east/provide-comfort.html)

On 3 April Bush still stated, "we are not there to intervene," refusing to commit US forces to Iraq's internal civil war. Thus limited war without initial military aid to the rebellion is a documented 1991 policy, not an empty or ahistorical refusal branch. Expanded rebel arming can be offered as a clearly identified alternative-RP escalation. The earlier 1972-75 programme is a separate precedent; it does not prove that Washington organized or armed the initial 1991 uprising. [Bush Presidential Library, Hobe Sound exchange, 3 April 1991](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/finding-aid/public-papers/question-and-answer-session-reporters-hobe-sound)

## Shield, mandate and humanitarian chronology

| Historical stage | Verified distinction | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 August 1990: Iraq invades Kuwait | This is the aggression to which the deployment responded. | Iraq can avert this particular coalition route by not invading, or change it through an actual withdrawal. |
| 7 August: Desert Shield deployment orders | Immediate purpose was Saudi defence and deterrence; Saudi consent mattered. This preceded Resolution 678. | Defensive deployment and paid host access should be available independently of a later offensive mandate. |
| 29 November: Resolution 678 | Authorized cooperating member states, unless Iraq fully complied by 15 January 1991, to use all necessary means to implement the Kuwait-related resolutions and restore regional peace. | A focus can seek a mandate; it should not fabricate a Security Council result or convert it automatically into annexation/regime change. |
| January-February 1991: Desert Storm; March: internal uprisings | The military campaign and subsequent rebellion are distinct events. | A US encouragement focus can influence opportunity; it must not guarantee a revolt, local control or victory. |
| 5 April: Resolution 688 and Bush's relief announcement; operational relief followed | Repression, displacement and immediate humanitarian access are the subject. | Provide Comfort should respond to actual need and remain independent of whether the USA encouraged rebellion. |
| 16 April: expanded protected encampments announced | Bush described temporary sites protected by air/ground forces and aircraft restrictions north of the 36th parallel. | Safe-haven protection is a further funded commitment with actual access, presence and exposure, not instant sovereign independence. |

Deployment references: [official US Navy operational history](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/u/the-united-states-navy-and-the-persian-gulf.html), [US Army CMH operations catalogue and dates](https://history.army.mil/Publications/Publications-Catalog/Operations-DESERT-SHIELD-and-DESERT-STORM/). The full contemporary mandate is [Security Council Resolution 678, paragraph 2, reproduced by UNHCR Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/legal/resolution/unsc/1990/en/8997).

Resolution 688 demands an end to repression, humanitarian access and cooperation, while reaffirming Iraqi sovereignty and territorial integrity. Its text does not establish a no-fly zone, recognized Kurdish autonomy, independence or an automatic territorial transfer. Bush presented the expanded operation as consistent with it; that presidential position should not be rewritten as an explicit flight-ban clause in the resolution. [Resolution 688, preamble and paragraphs 1-7](https://www.refworld.org/legal/resolution/unsc/1991/en/113183)

Bush's 16 April statement distinguishes the 5 April relief announcement from the expanded protected-camp programme, calls the latter interim, and rejects occupying Baghdad. Canadian operational history dates Provide Comfort's launch to 6 April; first airdrops and subsequent ground protection are different milestones. Use the primary 5 April date for Resolution 688: some service summaries conflate it with other April UN action. [Bush, 16 April statement](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/finding-aid/public-papers/remarks-assistance-iraqi-refugees-and-news), [Canadian operational chronology](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/past-operations/middle-east/provide-comfort.html)

## Recommended player choices and Iraqi counterplay

These are proposed causal rules, not claims that history supplied exact game modifiers or that they are already implemented.

- **Limited War:** make Kuwait's liberation, credible withdrawal and the coalition's limited objectives a useful outcome. A national readiness/diplomatic or budget-recovery benefit can make restraint attractive. Avoid representing the historical choice as a blank reward while expanded arming gets every benefit.
- **Expanded Arms:** retain it as an explicit stronger intervention in a 1991 context. Require an actual willing local actor or organized underground, real funding/equipment and a viable consented route. Rewards should improve local capability, not automatically release a country or guarantee regime collapse. Existing 1972-style clandestine assistance can use different context from a new overt 1991 commitment.
- **Iraqi political counterplay:** credible autonomy implementation, agreed local administration and genuine protection/aid access should reduce relevant grievances and displacement. A paper promise alone should not erase an armed crisis. Paid public services, negotiations and limited security commitments can trade money, political authority or central control for stability. These are alternatives to escalating repression, whose civilian harm should not become a free administrative bonus.
- **Iraqi diplomatic counterplay:** a real agreement with the external route/sponsor state can suspend that route, as the Iranian channel demonstrates. Preserve already delivered assets and independent local agency. Renewed external intervention requires its actual conditions and consent again.
- **Kuwait counterplay:** recheck actual occupation and compliance when escalating from defensive deployment to offensive action. Withdrawal should alter the specific liberation justification. A decision to continue towards imposed regime change is a separate alternative escalation with coalition, diplomatic and fiscal consequences.
- **Relief and protection:** aid access, host basing, protected camps and armed cover are separate transactions. Let Iraq cooperate with monitored relief, negotiate arrangements or contest an expanded foreign commitment. Acceptance of aid must not silently accept US tutelage or a new border. A successful Iraqi settlement can remove the underlying emergency, rather than leaving every foreign programme permanently active.

For the obsolete contacts card, either reuse its existing ID as substantive **Kurdish Liaison**, with a real paid preparation benefit, or keep the legacy node out of the live route and put meaningful preparation in the existing appeal/fork. Do not add an empty authorization toll. Earlier clandestine liaison should not be described as a new relationship created by the February 1991 speech. If retained between the appeal and fork, its description should explain a new liaison mandate, rather than the first-ever US contact with Kurdish actors. Provide Comfort must remain a separate crisis entry.
