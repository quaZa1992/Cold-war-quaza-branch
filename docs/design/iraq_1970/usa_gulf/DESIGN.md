## Historical basis

On 15 February 1991 Bush encouraged the Iraqi population and armed forces to remove Saddam. This was broader political encouragement, not a specifically Kurdish uprising order or an unconditional military guarantee. The US Army history describes a locally initiated northern revolt; coalition forces initially did not give it military assistance. The refugee crisis then prompted relief and protection. Bush's 16 April statement described protected camps and aircraft restrictions, while emphasizing humanitarian purposes. Regional elections on 19 May 1992 and a local administration developed under the resulting conditions.

Sources: [Bush's February appeal](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/finding-aid/public-papers/remarks-raytheon-missile-systems-plant-employees), [US Army history, pp.29–30](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D101-PURL-gpo5268/pdf/GOVPUB-D101-PURL-gpo5268.pdf), [Bush's April protection statement](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/finding-aid/public-papers/remarks-assistance-iraqi-refugees-and-news), [Kurdistan Parliament](https://www.parliament.krd/english/about-parliament/history-of-parliament/).

[UN Resolution 688](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/110659?ln=en) condemned repression and required humanitarian access. It did not explicitly create Kurdish autonomy, authorize a no-fly zone or grant independence. A historical focus must not claim that it did.

Direct US arms assistance in this reactive 1991-style chapter is an **alternate stronger intervention**. Earlier 1972–75 covert assistance is a separate historical episode, not proof that Washington organized the 1991 revolt.

## Structure and agency

- Desert Storm remains a direct crisis entry, fourteen days, with a proposed ninety-day liberation war goal against Iraq. Desert Shield and Coalition Mandate are optional preparation roots.
- Appeal to Iraq → Kurdish Contacts → the mutually exclusive Limited War / Arm the Front choice. A real military opening, unresolved local grievances and a local decision are required for a rising. A respected autonomy agreement suppresses the corresponding grievance route.
- Provide Comfort and Northern Access are independent crisis entries. Humanitarian protection does not require the USA to have encouraged rebellion, and access is not lost merely because Kuwait was liberated before Desert Shield finished.
- Relief AND access unlock Safe Haven and Northern Air Shield. Safe Haven → Regional Assembly → Baghdad Accord or, with the air commitment, Guarantee Self-Rule. The final recovery focus accepts either route, but delivery requires a real settlement or viable protected local administration.
- A rejected Baghdad proposal cannot veto an already existing de facto enclave. The USA focus cannot create local control remotely. Kurdish actors may negotiate, rise, remain neutral or refuse a US offer.

The candidate northern envelope is states 164, 633, 630 and 1078, assessed separately. Kirkuk, state 165, requires a separate disputed-territory settlement or actual military outcome. Do not release every transnational Kurdish core. Preserve Iraqi legal claims/cores when modelling de facto separation.

## Benefits, liabilities and status

The alternative arms route offers one total $0.25bn/1,000-item package from actual US funds and `infantry_weapons_1` stock. A funded, accepted ninety-day adviser contract grants the recipient +8% defence against Iraq through native `targeted_modifier = { tag = IRQ defense_bonus_against = 0.08 }`. This targets Iraq everywhere, not only northern states. Its $0.05bn weekly support rate and Turkish/Iraqi reactions are explicit costs.

Relief, access and protection have their own spending agreements. Northern Air Shield instead uses existing native military-cost modifiers: one timed USA air-readiness package and accepted recipient air-cover ideas. Normal expenses of actual deployed forces continue. Guarantee Self-Rule maintains these existing commitments instead of duplicating them. Permanent paid construction is not removed when America withdraws.

For Baghdad the principal consequence is actual loss of direct regional control, revenue and recruitment where local self-government genuinely takes over; the concept does not substitute a fictional country-wide tax penalty. For Washington the reward is an accepted regional partner, influence and a means of constraining renewed Iraqi coercion, balanced against expenditure and military/diplomatic exposure.

A recognized settlement is **internal Iraqi autonomy**, not a US subject. Proposed minimum properties: IRQ is the legal sovereign; local government administers the agreed region; no automatic 75% civilian/military factory transfer; no forced deployment of all local units to Baghdad; budget, security and outside guarantees are explicit negotiated terms. This requires a new reviewed `COLDWAR_` arrangement. De facto protection remains a distinct control/protection outcome when Baghdad refuses recognition. These statuses do not stack or replay grants when recognition changes.

## Revision: an underground movement before a Kurdish country exists

The existing fifteen-focus geometry, icons, titles and prerequisite graph are unchanged. `Arm the Front` now has an absent-country variant using native forced core resistance. `Kurdish Contacts` explains the local political actors, and Branch Decisions exposes eight additional US/shared/Iraqi contracts. Four Iraqi responses become visible from the live northern crisis, independently of US focus completion.

The initial US package can commit money and actual equipment to an underground network rather than targeting an absent country. In at most two organized, eligible states, resistance is enabled against **IRQ**, bound to a valid local KUR/PUK core tag and seeded once to a 60% minimum. Paid continued support contributes **+25 points to the resistance target** for ninety days and at most one +10-point pulse per thirty days. The pulse cannot itself push current resistance above 80, but this is not an engine cap. A paid Iraqi security operation blocks this automatic pulse; native growth and paid counter-effects remain active. Expansion costs another $0.10bn and thirty days, and cannot reseed an already touched state.

Emergency Security Appropriation costs 2% of frozen Iraqi annual GDP, then applies a ninety-day **+35% army-personnel / +20% equipment-upkeep** package. The larger operation costs 4% of GDP with **+60% / +35%**. These are country-wide native financial modifiers, additive to existing component modifiers; they are not purchase-price increases or a geographically exact northern invoice. Choose one programme per operation cycle, never both. After thirty funded days with a viable actual force commitment, the smaller programme reduces resistance by fifteen points and its target contribution by twenty; the larger uses twenty-five/thirty-five points. The target reductions last for the remaining term. Ending early removes the continuing programme, retains sunk preparation costs and preserves its cooldown.

Two eligible states remaining at least 80% resistant for thirty days, an organized supplied movement and an actual intervention/military opening can start a visible forty-five-day warning. Falling below the two-state condition pauses it; fifteen consecutive days below reset it. A functioning political settlement cancels it. Ignoring the crisis can produce a Kurdish rising, with finite northern strongholds and forces; Iraq can oppose it militarily or negotiate. Actual local self-rule then feeds the existing US protection/autonomy branch.

The native implementation must be coordinated: `force_enable_resistance` explicitly supports own cores, and `start_resistance = KUR/PUK` can bind a valid core country without it already owning land. Native MD already imposes severe controller supply/movement and attrition effects at 75% resistance. Its generic 90% core-group hook can create a country, transfer grouped states, spawn equipped units and declare war. The dedicated northern crisis therefore **requires a reviewed coordination guard and geographical isolation**; a target value or a daily clamp is not proof of safe threshold ordering. No automatic Kirkuk or transnational-core transfer is authorized. Halabja's PUK binding requires that movement actually to have formed; no PUK is invented in January 1970.

Seed markers, operation/pulse cooldowns and cache balances survive renewals and government changes. Aid is consumed once on a valid rising or accepted lawful handover; a defeated/resolved underground writes off its undelivered balance without refund or later duplication. Ending US funding removes only its identified target contribution, not all local resistance or unrelated native protests. Actual core-state occupation law/garrison UI, threshold ordering, resulting country identity and save/reload require engine tests before implementation is considered ready.

Historical support for funding a movement without a state is explicit in [Kissinger's June 1974 memorandum](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d254), which confirms US financial/material support from July 1972. This does not establish that America organized or initially armed the 1991 revolt. All proposed numerical resistance levels and financial packages are balancing choices, not historical measurements.
