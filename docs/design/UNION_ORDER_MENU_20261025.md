IMPERIA COLD WAR

Annual Union order register. Decision-menu and multiplayer design, 25 October 2026. The core register is now implemented by `tools/union_orders_1970/compile.py`; this document retains the design contract. See that module's README and current validation reports for actual implementation and evidence limits.

The user explicitly rejected a new menu/window. Everything stays in the existing Events and Decisions screen, with native decision categories, decision rows, descriptions, tooltips and timed missions. Do not build a new scripted GUI, overlay, text-input widget or event-based configuration menu. There are Soviet and republican views of one shared register. Neither ideology nor current governing party substitutes for actual Union membership. The interactive conversation preview illustrates decision categories and their changing rows; it is not a game screenshot or proof of native scripting.

Three collapsible categories keep the screen bounded:

- Annual Union Orders: the current recipient, obligation family, quantity, fixed period and support terms in the category description. Visible preparation decisions change one field at a time. Moscow sees player SSRs by default and can separately reveal AI advisory programmes. The republican version prepares only that republic's own proposal.
- Republican Submissions: pending named proposals. Moscow selects a proposal, sees its complete unchanged terms and chooses Approve or Reject. The proposing SSR can inspect or withdraw its own pending submission. These are decisions, not an event-dialog menu.
- Annual Obligations: actual timed missions for the selected republic/family, with quantities and remaining days. A Soviet filter decision selects the republic to inspect; republics see only their own obligations. AI/neutral programmes are clearly separate non-punitive records. Complete outcomes remain in native descriptions/tooltips.

Preparation is progressive disclosure inside the same category. Selecting Change Republic shows only eligible republic choices and Back; selecting Change Family shows a short sector-group choice followed by that group's families. Adjust Quantity shows four increment choices, +1/+10/+100/+1000, followed by Increase, Decrease and Done using the selected increment. These are repeatable native decisions, not a numeric input field. Decrease cannot go below one. Support selection similarly shows GDP-percentage choices and Back. Review returns to a concise set of editing decisions plus Issue or Submit. No money is charged merely for preparation; discarded drafts have no effect. No entire multi-family catalogue is displayed alongside all adjustment actions.

The category description and final commitment tooltip contain the obligation type, actual equipment/building family, editable quantity, fixed 365-day period, measurement method and optional support expressed as a percentage of Soviet GDP. They show complete proposed funding and signed success/failure effects before submission or approval. Missile, aircraft and ship options need actual supported equipment identifiers; naval obligations require naval production access. Narrow production-family proof remains subject to the previously recorded native-counter limitations. A main-battle-tank order must show production-board certification unless finer automatic proof is demonstrated. Broad native categories must carry their honest category names. Delivery remains a different obligation from new production.

Authority and simultaneous requests:

1. Moscow's first valid direct issue immediately commits an annual obligation. A human SSR receives notification and its mission; it cannot decline that internal order unilaterally.
2. A human SSR's first valid submission instead reserves the same family slot as a pending proposal. Moscow receives an approval/refusal decision with the immutable submitted terms and can inspect it in the register. No production baseline, deadline or payment starts yet.
3. Approval rechecks both countries, actual membership, authority, family reservation, human/AI responsibility, funding and current proposal identity. It commits those exact terms, the baseline and a full 365-day deadline once. Modified terms require a new visible proposal. Rejection, withdrawal or a 30-day unanswered-proposal lapse releases only the pending reservation and carries no penalty.
4. The shared execution transaction resolves simultaneous clicks in the game's processed order. The first successful reservation wins, including in co-op. A second request sees the existing proposal/order and cannot overwrite it, debit money or open a duplicate timer. Client UI selection, client time and local view state are not authority. Native country-level preparation choices are shared by co-op controllers; the visible summary must reflect that shared draft. Do not claim independent per-client drafts without a proven native mechanism.
5. Different families may run in parallel. The annual reservation belongs to the underlying equipment/obligation family, including overlapping broad and narrow targets; changing a dropdown label, production versus delivery mode, or exact model cannot create a second conflicting baseline. Early fulfilment does not release the original annual window. Support transfers and assets have immutable per-order receipts.

Responsibility and settlement:

Every quota-related success or failure programme lasts six months, represented as 180 native days, following the user's latest instructions. This includes the successful production-cost reduction, failure cost penalty and Consumer Goods burden. The obligation itself still has a full 365-day period. AI/neutral orders receive no quota result award or failure debuff. Successful and unfulfilled spirits identify the affected production programme in their names.

| Case | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Human SSR at commitment and settlement, still responsible | Normal evidence-based fulfilment or published failure terms. |
| AI SSR from the start | Advisory programme only. No punitive mission, no failure entry and no invented success. Ordinary AI development stays separate. |
| Unfinished human order changes to actual AI administration | Mark neutral once. It may lapse harmlessly at its original deadline. No new failure consequences or success award. |
| A human later takes an AI or neutral republic | Existing advisory/neutral orders stay non-punitive. No inherited nearly expired obligation. Its next binding annual window has a full 365 days. |
| Human order already fulfilled or failed before a later AI switch | Keep the settled historical result. Country-control switching does not rewrite paid rewards or erase a valid settled penalty. |
| All relevant orders were AI/neutral | Exclude them from collective scoring. No empty-set collective reward and no failure. |

A neutralised annual family slot stays reserved until its original end. It cannot mint another support payment or restart the quota simply by changing control. Assistance already delivered is a paid transaction, not a penalty, and is not automatically refunded. A temporary disconnect matters only if the country actually changes to AI control; a paused session does not consume game days. Human-to-human co-op/controller changes do not by themselves create an AI transition.

The responsibility guard must cover every consequence path, not only the mission timeout: shortfall counters, consumer-goods and equipment-cost spirits, notices, cohesion/loyalty, rankings, any competition gate and delayed events. A cancelled AI/neutral order is never counted as a fulfilled order to make up the numbers. Outstanding human obligations continue to count normally, and earned shared rewards may still benefit AI members. Neutralisation does not erase independent, previously earned effects from other human orders.

Pre-implementation contrast: the legacy `COLDWAR_UNI_ai_floor` gave an AI republic a 75% chance of a scheduled fulfilment event. Its failure helpers did not independently guard human responsibility. The implemented register retires those entries and guards queued legacy outcomes while enabled. Already-settled legacy expiry events remain valid so historical costs cannot become permanent. The current source tests and isolated native fixture are recorded separately from this design contract.

Persistent state includes order/proposal identity, actual origin and recipient, canonical family reservation, submitted immutable terms, issue/approval dates, original deadline, opening measurement baseline, human-responsibility state, support receipt, evidence/certification receipt and terminal result. Save/reload, rehost and reopening the menu must preserve them. UI-only cached selections cannot authorise execution. Independence cancels internal authority and needs the separately designed funding settlement; it is not an AI-control exception.

Performance and review: process pending and active orders through bounded queues. Reconcile controller state for the fourteen eligible republics and recheck it at issue, approval, completion, timeout and delayed effects. Decision visibility and tooltip calculation must not scan every world country or apply rewards. Native validation must cover staged visibility, first/last/empty target sets, invalidated selections, co-op shared drafts and duplicate submissions, cross-client confirmation, mixed human/AI scoring, disconnect with and without AI takeover, human return, exact evidence, queue expiry, independence and save/rehost. The interactive preview exercises the proposed interaction locally; it is not evidence of engine or multiplayer execution.
