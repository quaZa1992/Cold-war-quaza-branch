## Editorial and historical review

The five new entries have concrete Cuban institutions and policy tensions rather than generic filler. Their descriptions and reward rows follow the main-MD presentation pattern: identify the institution, explain a decision and its trade-off, then show the named numerical outcome in the effect panel.

Current main MD Russia examples:

- `SOV_2011_healthcare_reforms` uses a named reform, 5 focus cost (35 days), a political gate, modest stability/party effects, and an actual healthcare-budget increase (`common/national_focus/05_russia.txt:1553–79`).
- `SOV_expand_welfare_programs` is a 3-cost (21-day) example that combines +45 political power with a Treasury cost and a native social-spending action (`05_russia.txt:6161–88`).
- `SOV_support_workers_rights` frames a concrete labour-institution change through a conditional national-spirit replacement and political effects (`05_russia.txt:6240–73`).

The Cuba additions match the relevant presentation grammar while staying specific to the 1970–1990 programme:

| New focus | Editorial and historical assessment |
| --- | --- |
| The Family Code | The Federation of Cuban Women and the redistribution of domestic labour ground the focus in a specific institution. Childcare, maternity protection, and access to study/work explain why legal equality requires continuing public provision. +2% workforce and the higher welfare cost make the trade-off visible, alongside a one-time Treasury charge. February 1975 correctly precedes the December 1975 First Congress; its root branch starts beside the existing alternatives rather than being forced after the Congress. |
| Family Doctor Teams | The description identifies the Lawton experiment, community teams, and links to polyclinics; it also shows workforce efficiency against higher health spending and a health-budget investment. This is appropriately specific. The national doctor-and-nurse programme launched in 1984, so the concept correctly labels earlier introduction as alternate history. The current branch position is after Family Code but alongside First Congress; exact 1984 enactment is not enforced by the preview layout. |
| The Biological Front | The focus distinguishes civilian bioscience coordination from an unsupported biological-weapons claim. CIB/CIGB links, imported equipment, protected research funding, and competing public services give the branch a distinct institutional purpose. The reward bundle includes a one-time Treasury cost, Havana industrial capacity, and a named genetics research bonus, with a recurring education-budget burden. Its historical status records the 1981 start and later 1982/1986 institutions. |
| Peasant Markets | The surplus-sale condition after state procurement quotas is accurate and gives the market mechanism a clear limit. Agricultural productivity is balanced by reduced district tax income and stability, plus a one-time Treasury cost. The description's food-supply/inequality tension is stronger than an unqualified market reward. The historical status correctly marks the free-market experiment as 1980–86 and earlier adoption as alternate history. |
| Revive Microbrigades | Workplace teams, housing/childcare/neighbourhood works, and labour diverted from ordinary production make this a specifically Cuban collective construction policy. The construction-speed reward is offset by lower industrial productivity and higher welfare expenditure, with a Treasury charge and a Havana infrastructure grant. The status accurately distinguishes early-1970s operation from the 1986–87 revival. |

The historical chronology is supported by the following references: the UN lists Cuba's Act No. 1289 as the 1975 Family Code ([UN document](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/703927/files/CRC_C_CUB_Q_2_Add.1-EN.pdf)); WHO records the Family Doctor and Nurse Programme as launched in 1984 ([WHO case study](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/primary-health-care-conference/workforce.pdf)); WHO's local-production history traces Cuban biotechnology to interferon work beginning in 1981 ([WHO report](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/336685/9789241509718-eng.pdf)); the free peasant market experiment ran from 1980 through closure in 1986 ([Rosenberg, *Cuba's Free-Market Experiment*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/latin-american-research-review/article/cubas-freemarket-experiment-los-mercados-libres-campesinos-19801986/0ABAE18F9DB8D77884B68F0F9DCA018E)); and the 1970s workplace microbrigade pattern is documented in a housing-policy study ([University of Chile study](https://revistainvi.uchile.cl/index.php/INVI/article/download/62305/65946?inline=1)).

One point remains for content—not layout—signoff: the concept flags Family Doctor Teams, Biological Front, and Peasant Markets as earlier-than-historical introductions, but their actual focus prerequisites are political and do not themselves enforce the named years. This is clearly disclosed as alternate history in the concept status. If the eventual emitted tree must reserve these for their historical dates, that is a separate date-gating/content decision; the current approval concept is internally explicit about the alternate timeline.
