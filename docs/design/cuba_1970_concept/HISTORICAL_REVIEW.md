# Cuba 1970 concept: historical and editorial review

Reviewed `tools/cuba_1970_concept/content.py` as an English concept draft. This review addresses historical claims, institution names, chronology and political plausibility; balance modifiers are not validated here. The ten party slots are a coherent scope provided every non-PCC government is clearly marked as a counterfactual transition rather than a legal Cuban party waiting in 1970.

## Changes recommended before treating copy as historically clean

1. **`washington_talks`; contract `us_normalisation` — distinguish the 1970–90 executive embargo from later statutory controls.** The U.S. embargo then rested on presidential action under authority Congress had delegated in 1961; a 1976 Commerce Department transition report states explicitly that the executive branch could “alter[] or end[]” it unilaterally. A bilateral accord still requires an independent U.S. executive decision, and other restrictions remain distinct: notably the Trade Act of 1974's terms for nondiscriminatory tariff (MFN) status and Eximbank/Commodity Credit Corporation credits. Do not demand congressional repeal of the embargo in the scenario's 1970–90 timeline. The legal boundary changed with the 1996 LIBERTAD/Helms–Burton Act, which codified the embargo then in effect and placed statutory conditions on termination; that later law must not be projected backward. For this scenario, distinguish executive suspension or repeal of the embargo from separate trade, credit, claims, migration, and Guantánamo settlements. Sources: [Ford Presidential Library, 1976 transition report, Appendix I](https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0067/7773983.pdf); [State Department archive, Cuba: Helms-Burton Act](https://1997-2001.state.gov/regions/wha/cuba/helms.html); [State Department archive copy of the act, §102(h)](https://1997-2001.state.gov/regions/wha/cuba/helms-burton-act.html). The USA must still be an active negotiating party rather than a passive object of a Cuban decision.

2. **`moscow_guarantee` — the title implies a formal defence guarantee that was not in place.** Soviet fuel, arms, credits and technical assistance are well grounded; Moscow's influence over those flows is a real constraint. Rename this “Soviet Assistance” or “Moscow's Support” unless the alternate-history event explicitly negotiates a new mutual-defence treaty. The focus and `soviet_assistance` contract otherwise read as alternate negotiated aid, not a false claim that it was automatically owed.

3. **`cuban_liberation` and `group("alpha", ...)` — soften the asserted Alpha 66 doctrine.** Alpha 66 was a real, militant, Miami-based exile action organization that carried out raids and planned armed overthrow; it was not a Cuban party or government with a settled governing programme. Its membership and political outlook were not reducible to a single social platform. “Rejects … the restoration of Batista's personal order” is too definitive without a solid source for a formal anti-Batista platform. Describe the group as anti-Castro and armed; present a nationalist, anti-Batista-led constitutional direction as a possible coalition platform after victory, not Alpha's established doctrine. Keep `status="Counterfactual governing role for a real militant exile movement"` prominent. The game choice can remain, but should show the legitimacy and internal-coalition problem this creates.

4. **`anap_cooperatives` — distinguish the 1970 small-farmer cooperative forms from later CPAs.** ANAP and small-farmer cooperation predate 1970. Early *Cooperativas de Crédito y Servicios* shared credit, equipment and services while members retained private land and production. The voluntary Agricultural Production Cooperatives (CPAs) began in 1975 and expanded after the 1975–77 policy turn. Current wording is broad enough to be read as the later collective-land model being available at the 1970 start. Name the early CCS/voluntary service model, or date-gate/identify any production-cooperative version as a later institutional choice. The producer-retained-income trade-off is apt for the small-holder branch.

5. **`air_defence` — remove the implication that Cuba once had its own strategic nuclear weapons.** The clause “not the return of strategic nuclear weapons to the island” can suggest Cuban-held weapons were previously deployed. The USSR deployed missiles in 1962 and removed them in the crisis settlement; do not imply a Cuban arsenal. Suggested copy: “The proposal concerns training and a limited radar installation; no strategic nuclear deployment is part of it.” Soviet assistance and radar work in Cuba are plausible.

## Chronology and contextual labels to preserve

- **`zafra_campaign`; `finish_zafra`; `restore_sectors`; `STARTING_SPIRITS` resolution.** The copy correctly says the campaign is unfinished on 1 January 1970. The 1969/70 crop was a record, about 8.54 million tonnes, but missed the political 10-million target. The result was acknowledged in Fidel's 19 May speech; cane cutting continued into July, and his 26 July speech reviewed the effort and its consequences. Keeping the starting spirit active until the July review is defensible; do not mark the harvest already failed in the January setup. In the later event, distinguish the May admission/shortfall from the completed July review. [FRUS's 1969 estimate](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d207) is a forecast, not final production; the [University of Florida archive](https://cubanstudies.history.ufl.edu/gems-of-the-archive/the-ten-million-ton-harvest-of-1970/) and [ASCE Cuban sugar study](https://ascecubadatabase.org/asce_proceedings/cubas-sugar-industry-in-the-1990s-potential-exports-to-the-u-s-and-world-markets/) support the record ~8.5/8.54m result.
- **`popular_power`; `first_congress`; `socialist_constitution`; `sdpe_continuity`; `division_labour`; `rectification`.** People’s Power was developed first in Matanzas (1974–75), then nationalised under the 1976 Constitution. The First PCC Congress was in December 1975; the Constitution took effect in February 1976; Cuba joined CMEA in 1972; the SDPE came in from 1976. The 1986 rectification rejected/cut back SDPE mechanisms and material incentives. Their `history=` text correctly permits early enactment as an explicit alternate timeline, but **future events** need to establish the actual historical milestones and reconcile/replace any already-active early-reform effects rather than award the same institution twice.
- **`african_mandate`; `expeditionary_forces`; `mission_schools`; `overseas_mission`.** Cuban military and revolutionary activity in Africa predates 1970 at smaller scale, but the major Angola deployment began in 1975 and Ethiopia deployment in 1977. The mandate/request language is appropriately consent-based. Keep country-specific military commitments dated and require an actual recipient/war context; do not let the generic “African Mandate” read as a 1970 Angolan war or as a focus authorizing a third-party intervention. “Expeditionary contingent” is more accurate than equating Cuban regular troops in Angola with private or volunteer fighters.
- **`thaelmann_name`; `caribbean_treaty`; `playa_rda`; `development_protocol`; island contracts.** This is the best-handled history in the draft. Fidel's June 1972 promise/map and symbolic naming were publicised; the August ceremony/report performed the symbolism. The island stayed Cuban: Cuban and German authorities later described the “gift” as symbolic, not a transfer of ownership. Keep June for the naming/promise, optionally August for the ceremony, and keep the `island_cession` contract explicitly counterfactual with real acceptance, compensation and transfer checks. Rename node key/title `caribbean_treaty` / “A Caribbean Treaty”: the counterparty is the GDR, not a Caribbean state. “Cayo Blanco Agreement” or “East German Transfer Agreement” is clearer.
- **`old_party`; `group("orthodox",...)`.** The Microfracción around Aníbal Escalante was defeated and purged in 1968. The current copy correctly calls slot 6 a surviving PSP/planning tradition and alternate post-purge coalition, not an intact rival party. Preserve that caveat in any visible branch name/description. “Orthodox Planning Cadres” avoids falsely claiming that the 1970 organization named “Microfracción” was functioning openly.
- **`unfinished_revolution`; `moral_incentives`.** Che died in Bolivia in 1967. The draft correctly uses his “New Man” as a surviving political ideal and does not resurrect him. Moral incentives and voluntarism, however, were broader PCC/Castro policy, not exclusive property of a separate Guevarist faction. Keep “Guevarist” as an interpretation/current within the ruling coalition, not a literal independent Guevarist party apparatus.
- **`far` group; `professional_revolution`; `orderly_succession`.** Raúl was defence minister and the FAR was an unusually strong institution in 1970; the U.S. 1969 assessment described the military officer corps as the principal organized power after Fidel. A FAR-led successor regime is still counterfactual; the metadata marks that. Avoid changing “professional military current” into an asserted historical coup faction. [FRUS 1969 assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d207).
- **`authentic_republic`; `independent_republic`; `civic_reconciliation`; `courts_factions`; `exile_directorate`.** Auténtico, Ortodoxo, Christian Democrat and constitutionalist traditions are genuine, but they did not operate as open domestic parties in 1970. Their routes are properly described as constitutional alternatives after a negotiated opening or rupture. Keep no domestic-majority or spontaneous-election claim. The 1978 prisoner/family dialogue, not an assumed church-led transfer, is a plausible mechanism for contact and a limited opening.
- **`liberal` branch.** The 1940 Constitution is a credible banner. “Guarded restitution” and “claims bonds” offer meaningfully different remedies to competing property claims; do not imply that the party had a single agreed property-restitution plan or that every exile estate could be returned without displacement. Keep compensation and return conditional on a transition settlement.
- **`militia_logistics`.** Cuba's National Revolutionary Militias existed in the early revolution, so generic local reserves are possible. If this is meant to evoke the later Territorial Troops Militia, that name/institution would be anachronistic for 1970; keep the current wording generic or specify revolutionary reserve/local defence.
- **`havana_ports`.** Havana is a major trade gateway, but “foreign trade depends on a small number of harbours” overstates the concentration. The local investment programme is plausible; say “Havana is a major gateway” instead.
- **`island_diplomacy`; `caribbean_neighbours`; `havana_voice`; `berlin_havana`.** These are intentionally policy choices rather than literal 1970 treaties. In future event copy, Cuban nonalignment should be described as a diplomatic claim/coherent platform alongside deep Soviet dependence, not neutrality or freedom from the USSR. Cuba was a founding participant in the NAM in 1961 and chaired the 1979 Havana summit; it only opened Interests Sections with the U.S. in September 1977. The missing NAM landmark is the 1979 summit, not accession; do not make “independent diplomacy” an achieved status on 1 January 1970.

## Planned historical events

Future implementation should keep the specified milestones distinct and state-sensitive: the 1970 Zafra ends with a record crop but missed target; 1976 institutions and limited SDPE appear on their actual timeline; 1978 dialogue enables prisoner releases/family visits without automatic regime change; 1980 Mariel begins with the Peruvian Embassy sequence, guard withdrawal on 4 April and Mariel opening on 20 April; 1986 rectification reverses SDPE tendencies; and 1990 is an emergency contingency whose severity depends on Soviet trade/oil exposure, not an inevitable state collapse. The underlying January 1970 status should include both social-service legitimacy and real consumer, agricultural, fuel, transport, skill and external-credit pressures.
