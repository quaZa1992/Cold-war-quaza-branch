## What to borrow from the references

Use Cuba for country-specific subject matter, icon style, and small-state economic/military vocabulary. Its existing tree is a sprawling modern-day tree, not a 1970 layout template: 313 focuses, with three major roots spread across x=20 (`CUB_cuban_politics`, line 41), x=60 (`CUB_army_reorganisation`, line 134), and x=84 (`CUB_economic`, line 2615). The economic and political branches assume post-1990 realities (dual currency, tourism, foreign investment, Diaz-Canel and modern party alternatives); do not transplant their branches or historical framing into a 1970 design.

Use Russia for native political-programme construction and values. Its 806-focus tree is a great-power outlier with six focus shortcuts and extensive shared focus content, but the local Putin spine gives a legible example: root `SOV_reign_of_yeltsin` at `(62,0)` (line 51), `SOV_putin` at relative `(13,1)` (line 108), and same-height branches from `SOV_putin` at `x=-2,+2` (line 186/220). The tree sets `initial_show_position = { x = 2 y = 0 }` and uses shortcuts for widely separated military, economy, and party programmes (lines 13–47). For a small 1970 Cuba tree, one entry viewport and roughly 3–5 purposeful shortcuts should suffice if the final canvas warrants them; do not imitate Russia's number or width.

## Native geometry, costs, and labels

Main MD focus grid spacing in the installed `interface/nationalfocusview.gui` is 96 px horizontally by 130 px vertically (`positionType` `focus_spacing`, lines 574–577); centers and connector positions are separately defined at lines 579–595. These are rendered pixel spacings, not the source coordinates themselves. Typical code relationships in the inspected Cuba and Russia branches use one row per step (`y=1`), same-column spines (`x=0`), paired alternatives around an anchor (`x=-1,+1` or `-2,+2`), and wider siblings when their follow-ups need more footprint (`x=±2` or larger). Native relative anchors (`relative_position_id`) are common; use them to hold sections together. Russia frequently places separate prerequisite blocks on a focus, making each block an AND condition; several `focus =` entries inside the *same* prerequisite block are OR alternatives. The three-way Russia economic-policy verdict illustrates visible exclusive choices: `SOV_state_corporatism`, `SOV_oligarchic_republic`, and `SOV_combined_economic_system` share two prerequisites and exclude one another (lines 521–674). Preserve that choice grammar when adapting a branch.

Typical main-MD duration is 35 days for `cost = 5` (one cost point is seven days); 10-cost focuses are 70 days. Cuba's current tree ranges from cost 2 to 10, most commonly cost 5; Russia's ordinary branch commonly costs 5, with 1–2 cost entry focuses. For a compact country programme, use 5 as the normal policy step and reserve 7–10 for substantial finales or unusually large undertakings; short 2–3 cost focuses fit transition/entry steps. This is a scale reference only, not a balance prescription.

The native focus card (`nationalfocusview.gui:463–493`) is 165×128 px; its title is centered in a 147×20 px field using `hoi_16mbs`. The focus detail panel is 550×550 px, with a 450 px title field, 485 px description, and 455 px reward text area (`:599–742`). Keep focus names compact—2–4 words and around 24 characters where practical—then use the detail description for the full institution name. The 24-character target is editorial guidance; actual fit depends on glyph widths.

- The communist political chain `CUB_castro` → `CUB_friends_in_power` is at lines 6073–6176. Both use `cost = 5`, a one-row spine, and explicit availability for the ruling communist outlook. The text in current English localisation (`MD_focus_CUB_l_english.yml:514–539`) is brief, but sometimes generic; take the institution/theme specificity from Russia's improved descriptions rather than its sentence length alone.
- `CUB_the_new_castro` (line 6310) forks from `CUB_castro`, and `CUB_start_reformism` continues from it at 10 cost (line 6355): the design shows how an internal leadership fork can own distinct policy follow-through.
- Economy starts at `CUB_economic` (lines 2615–2642), with two immediate siblings `CUB_allow_foreign_inv` (x=-5, cost 5) and `CUB_legalize_dollar` (x=+1, cost 5), then a Russian investment vs EU investment exclusive pair (`CUB_ask_russia` line 2757; `CUB_ask_EU` line 2829). This local investment fork is a strong layout motif but its modern partners and macroeconomic assumptions do not fit 1970.
- `CUB_ask_russia` (lines 2757–2827) demonstrates the diplomacy pattern: a real opinion gate, country-existence and war conditions in `available`, an appropriate bypass, an event request, and a conditional acceptance tooltip. Avoid making a focus promise a reward that depends on another player's event choice; phrase and display it as a proposed agreement.

## Russia reward patterns and values

The compact Putin spine is the better pattern for an institution-focused tree than its huge total canvas. At `05_russia.txt:278–316`, `SOV_stabilisation_fund` costs 5, unlocks a named decision, adds a persistent idea, deducts 155 treasury units, and calls the native treasury effect. At lines 388–421, `SOV_putinism` costs 5 and bundles 50 PP, +2% stability, a named governing idea, and three persistent programme modifier deltas (+0.03 communism drift, +0.07 PP factor, +0.03 stability factor), displayed through native formatter rows. At lines 432–479, `SOV_empower_siloviki` includes a 5% stability gain, an intelligence programme's named deltas, +1% core defence, and a named stakeholder-opinion shift. These examples show how to combine a clear institutional outcome with a modest immediately legible reward; don't copy a superpower's values into Cuba.

For choice consequences, `SOV_state_corporatism` (line 553) and `SOV_oligarchic_republic` (line 601) have genuinely different endpoints and visible mutual exclusions. They require previous political/economic verdicts, so the choice reflects the branch's earlier policy decisions rather than appearing as a disconnected binary.

Russia English text examples are at `MD_focus_SOV_l_english.yml:32–43` (`SOV_stabilisation_fund`, `SOV_putinism`) and 1110–1111 (`SOV_oligarchic_republic`). These descriptions name the reserve's purpose, governing institutions, and the constituency behind oligarchic rule. Match that institutional clarity for Cuba's party, revolutionary state, sugar economy, land reform, and external security pressures.

## Recommended Cuba 1970 design scale

The best fit is a compact but substantive tree of about 55–80 focus nodes across four cohesive sections: (1) revolutionary consolidation and leadership/institutional choices, (2) agrarian and sugar-led development with a real planning-versus-incentives fork, (3) armed forces, militia/civil defence and Soviet/Non-Aligned security, and (4) Caribbean diplomacy and external guarantees. Keep one shared national entry and place party/leadership eligibility on the real relevant branches. A three-way fork is justified only if all options lead to differentiated consequences; otherwise make one substantial two-way fork and leave complementary economic/security work shared.

For a canvas, reserve approximately x=0–44 and y=0–18 grid units for the complete main programme (about 4,224×2,340 px between focus centers), then extend only if real follow-ups need it. Place the root near `(12,0)` with branches initially visible around x=4, 12, 20, and 30; leave a clear 2–4 grid-unit subsection break, while using roughly 1–2 x-units between small sibling alternatives only after checking label/card fit. A root-relative sibling gap of 2 is about 192 px between centers; one grid column is only 96 px, so preview at normal scale and widen siblings whose titles/cards collide. Keep the entry viewport near the opening branch and add a few native shortcuts only if a real distant programme justifies them.

"55–80" and the proposed coordinate envelope are design guidance, not quota: the tree should stop where historically meaningful 1970 programmes run out. Cuba has a small economy and a constrained military, so typical rewards should emphasize institutions, production composition, training, limited doctrine/research bonuses and diplomacy; save broad industrial grants for the few genuinely transformative packages. Teach costs and numeric effects in native tooltips, keep flavour descriptions about the Cuban policy problem and institutional stakes, and ensure choices impose believable opportunity costs.
