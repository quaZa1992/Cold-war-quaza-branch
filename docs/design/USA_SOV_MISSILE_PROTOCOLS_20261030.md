IMPERIA COLD WAR: USA-Soviet missile terms and treaty-maker help
=============================================================

Implemented revision: 30 October 2026. This supersedes the former treaty-system bomb-production restriction. Existing approval snapshots and older dated reports remain historical records.

## Actual MD equipment

The loaded equipment definitions provide six `nuclear_missile_equipment` models and five `nuclear_ballistic_missile_equipment` models inheriting the nuclear strike mission. Six ordinary ballistic and six guided/cruise models inherit conventional missions instead. The treaty catalogue now covers all 23 real models. The original fifteen frozen plan indices retain their original identities; additional models are appended. Newly negotiated papers carry a coverage version so an old signed instrument does not silently acquire extra equipment obligations.

Nuclear-capable reserves selects only the eleven nuclear-capable models. INF-range reserves selects the conventional ballistic, nuclear ballistic and cruise models whose actual loaded range is between 500 and 5,500 km. This currently means conventional ballistic 1-3, nuclear ballistic 1-2 and cruise 1-6. SAMs are not counted as strategic ABM installations.

Reduction removes the agreed, frozen quantities of real stored equipment once after actual consent. The optional aggregate ceiling is the agreed total remaining after that reduction. Modernisation between covered models is possible within the total. Deployed formations, launchers and separately attributed warheads remain outside this reserve protocol. The interface states that boundary explicitly.

## Historic proposals and own papers

Historical proposal opens a draft without sending, signing, charging or dismantling anything. Suggested commercial, investment and regional drafts keep their explicit existing protocols. Custom agreement opens its own twelfth instrument with empty terms. The user selects the missile scope, percentage, ceiling, commercial measures, term and participants before the other government reviews and signs. Programme clauses remain attached to their corresponding named instruments and US focus initiatives.

| Reference | Implemented opening proposal | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SALT I, 1972 | Stored nuclear ICBM freeze, no initial destruction, five-year term | Reserve-based adaptation of the historical launcher freeze, not a count of deployed launchers or warheads. |
| ABM Treaty, 1972 | Strategic-defence consultation | No invented ABM category or destruction of ordinary SAMs. |
| SALT II, 1979 | Five-year reserve freeze | Explicit adaptation; the historical signed treaty was not ratified. |
| INF, 1987 | 100% retirement of the actually loaded 500-5,500 km reserve models, continuing zero ceiling | Historical elimination covered ground-launched nuclear and conventional systems. Game retirement executes on signature and excludes deployed equipment. |
| START I, 1991 | 25% stored-ICBM reduction, remaining-reserve ceiling, fifteen-year term | The percentage is a gameplay adaptation, not the historical 1,600 delivery-vehicle or 6,000 warhead attribution. |
| Reykjavik, 1986 | 50% stored-ICBM reduction and continuing ceiling | Based on the deep reductions discussed; this framework was not historically concluded. |

The historical references do not add calendar gates. Both players may negotiate an earlier or alternative agreement, change its proposed terms or refuse it.

## Continuing observation and consent

Weekly inspections cache observance for actual signatories only. Exceeding a ceiling does not confiscate new equipment. It suspends the affected agreement's commercial and investment privileges, selected sanctions protection and the existing joint Cold War tension relief. Linked bilateral programmes require both core parties to observe their applicable treaty. Other independent agreements retain their own effects.

Restore the missile ceiling is a native recipient-owned decision. It recalculates actual excess at execution, dismantles only that excess with older loaded models first, and refreshes observance. Its prospective effect preview displays the real equipment losses. It neither repeats setup fees nor returns earlier reductions. A non-member cannot use it to dismantle another government's missiles.

Signing checks both frozen retirement quantities and the ability to honour the agreed ceiling before either core party's fees or assets change. Stock growth that exceeds the reviewed ceiling requires revised terms. Optional invitees review and accept their own reserve contributions. Programme-only addenda leave material commitments and original expiry unchanged.

Existing production spirits are removed without adding missile caps or destroying assets. An unsigned paper containing the superseded production restriction must be revised before signature. Counteroffers show updated missile terms and require fresh review. Existing signed consultation benefits are not treated as consent to new equipment obligations.

## Interface and validation

Every editor setting has an English hover explanation, current selection and relevant limits. Mission and invitation controls have separate explanations. Historical catalogue entries explain the actual precedent and adaptation; the question-mark control explains the full drafting and signature workflow. Intelligence pages retain their original source/help tooltip.

The catalogue and register now show all nine summary lines. The register uses four rows per page so its last entry, page controls, invitation and programme-amendment controls fit the existing 790 x 794 folder. No global font or donor-art change was made. Native-asset compositions are not live screenshots.

Canonical owners: `tools/superpower_dossier/treaties.py`, `missile_protocols.py`, `treaty_help.py`, `treaty_text.py`, `treaty_ui.py`, `transactions.py` and the existing programme/addendum modules. The American Arms-Control Talks description is updated in its own generator. Budget rates and accepted setup prices are unchanged.

The recurring additional inventory work is bounded by 12 slots, at most 8 members each and 23 fixed equipment tokens: at most 2,208 native reserve queries per weekly inspection, plus inexpensive cached observance tests. This is an operation bound, not measured engine timing. No per-frame country or stockpile discovery scan was added.

Source/reference checks, emitted-AST transaction tests and native-asset compositions are recorded by the owning tools. No HOI4 engine launch or live multiplayer synchronization, co-op or save/rehost certification was performed.
