IMPERIA COLD WAR

This file records the approved concept stage. For the installed game integration,
see [the implementation manual](../../../tools/usa_kissinger_implementation/README.md)
and [the final implementation report](IMPLEMENTATION.md).

Kissinger foreign-policy concept, 2026-10-28. Design and visualization only.

The proposal contains 41 new focuses: 13 general statecraft focuses, one Kurdish
chapter entry, eight Iraqi, six Iranian, seven Turkish and six Syrian focuses.
Fourteen existing visible Gulf cards are included as context; the fifteenth
legacy receipt remains hidden. No loaded game file, country generator,
equipment definition, approval snapshot or registered sprite is changed.

The broader programme combines triangular diplomacy, limited or expanded
commitments, regional guarantees, and political or proxy assistance. China and
Soviet agreements require acceptance. Angola and Chile are separate historical
theatres with their own local eligibility; they do not promise an automatic
coup, party replacement or transfer of territory.

The Kurdish chapter uses the existing Iraqi northern-operations system as its
mechanical donor. Implementation should extend that system with four separate
host and regional ledgers, rather than create a competing Iraqi engine. Transit
hosts and existing Kurdish RP players approve their own agreements. A targeted
government receives regional warnings and counterplay, rather than a veto over
hostile covert actions. It does not automatically learn classified sponsor
budgets or equipment caches before exposure.

Iraq is the historical core, including the documented 1972 assistance through
Iran. A separate proposed Turkish transit agreement supplies an alternative
accepted route. Starting operations against the Shah or against NATO Turkey is
explicitly alternative policy, with a partnership rupture and diplomatic costs.
The peaceful Turkish continuation can follow accepted alliance cooperation or
an independently established regional network. Syria begins with civic and
political contacts; an armed route needs an actual local movement and crisis.
No later PKK, PYD, YPG or SDF identity is imported into the January 1970 opening.

The existing Kuwait intervention stays independent of Kurdish support. An
unresolved Iraqi invasion includes occupation after Kuwaiti capitulation; it is
not limited to an ongoing IRQ-KUW war check. Kurdish organisation may make a
later Iraqi crisis harder, but neither the focus nor the model creates an
independent country automatically. Status changes require a successful local
struggle, actual Kurdish control of eligible territory and local political
consent. Existing Iraqi KUR and PUK identities are not overwritten by another
host's rebellion.

The revised campaign is a standing regional system without automatic expiry.
Sustained funded troop operations build stabilisation gains that remain after
withdrawal and decay gradually. Direct deployment effects end on withdrawal,
but the accumulated gains can preserve an extended period of calm. There is
no thirty-day deployment requirement or permanent immunity. The simple player
view has one regional unrest bar, three defender responses and one warning.
Detailed costs remain inspectable in an expandable section. The reserve is a
hidden balance input, rather than another bar the player must manage.
US aid and defender security funding continue weekly until changed or suspended,
replacing the draft's ninety-day support packages. Containment and an accepted
autonomy agreement do not delete the regional ledger or grant permanent immunity.

The interactive model advances four or fifty-two weeks at a time, with separate
country ledgers retaining unrest, warning progress and recurring spending.
Changing policy does not restart the campaign. The latest fifty-two weeks are
charted; the full campaign age and cumulative costs are retained without a time
limit. Two regions at 80 unrest, thirty mobilisation days, a subsequent forty-five
day warning and a fifteen-day warning reset remain escalation safeguards. They
are not a deployment duration or the lifetime of the regional system.
New pressure rates, development delays, prices and effects are balance proposals,
not measured engine behaviour. Stage quotes are derived from the selected
country focus data. Unsigned or suspended delivery channels do not transmit aid.
The preview accumulates recurring costs only; setup and equipment remain quotes.
Defence, accepted autonomy and sponsorship remain different inputs.

The new prewar aid quotes are proposals informed by the historical programme.
They do not replace the existing Gulf module's $0.25 Billion cache and
$0.05 Billion/week funding. A later implementation must reconcile stage costs,
previously delivered weapons and existing receipts in one account, avoiding
duplicate setup invoices or equipment grants. All recurring currency is shown
as Billion/week. Iranian, Turkish and Syrian locality labels are illustrative
communities, not a validated state/province release map.

`DESIGN.json` is the shared review data. `build_preview.py` creates it and embeds
the same data in the visual from `preview.html`. Proposed world coordinates are
recorded separately from the schematic section projections. Native frame, font,
shared-import offsets, troop-quality checks and all non-Iraqi borders require
implementation and engine review before release.
