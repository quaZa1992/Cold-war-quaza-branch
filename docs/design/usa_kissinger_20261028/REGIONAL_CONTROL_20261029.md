# Regional control, costly reconciliation and repression

Implemented follow-up to the approved continuing Kurdish campaign, 29 October

## Host access and ordinary containment

The regional decision category becomes visible to the host, USA and relevant
local countries when the American contacts programme first tracks that host.
Iraq, Iran, Turkey and Syria retain independent ledgers. The category remains
inspectable after containment and consolidation. Zero unrest is not deletion of
the ledger, and there is no thirty-day troop expiry.

Two deployed divisions and a paid security operation contain unrest and build
lasting stabilisation. Development is an alternative funded programme; respected
regional rights are a separate source of calm. Four divisions provide additional
direct containment. The existing actual-division-count model is preserved: it
does not measure field strength, equipment or supply quality.

## Ten years, or six through major reform

One successful regional week earns one week of sustained policy. It requires
respected local rights, funded development, two deployed divisions, unrest below
10, stabilisation of at least 80, peaceful full government control of all listed
contested states, no unresolved repression trauma and no active US escalation.
Failure removes two earned weeks per week, floored at zero. Paid reform credit
is retained, while sustained progress can be lost.

The final decision requires 520 progress weeks and at least 312 earned weeks of
sustained policy. Four ordered reforms can contribute 52 weeks each:

| Reform | Earned-policy milestone | GDP invoice | Political power | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entrench Regional Rights | 52 weeks | 10% | 100 | 52 weeks |
| Regional Revenue Settlement | 104 weeks | 12% | 100 | 52 weeks |
| Integrate Regional Public Institutions | 156 weeks | 15% | 100 | 52 weeks |
| Civilian Reconstruction Commission | 208 weeks | 18% | 100 | 52 weeks |

Each invoice uses current GDP and is guarded against repetition. At unchanged
GDP the four reforms cost 55% of annual GDP. Final consolidation costs another
10% and 200 PP. Ordinary programme costs continue separately. Four paid reforms
thus reduce the minimum from 520 earned weeks to 312, but cannot complete it at
311. The milestones concern earned regional policy, not fixed calendar dates.

Consolidation removes spontaneous unrest and resets the underground organisation
score. Its spirit grants +2% stability, +5% foreign influence defence and +1%
bureaucracy cost. Ordinary American agitation is contained by the standing
political settlement. Withdrawal of rights, territorial loss or renewed local
war can break the compact; it is not an irrevocable script immunity.

## American counterplay

An armed-stage American programme can fund **Intensify regional resistance**:
75 PP, 0.0025% of current US GDP and 1,000 real stockpiled infantry weapons, followed by
0.0002% of current US GDP/week for 26 active weeks, in addition to the ordinary armed
programme. It adds nine weekly unrest pressure and ten organisation, commits
the rifles to the finite recipient-approved cache and interrupts reconciliation.
Native cost and complete recipient-assistance previews remain visible.

It requires actual access, funding and confrontation. An American-guaranteed
settlement must first cease to be active; domestic rights require no American
permission to exist and no host approval of hostile sponsorship. Local-player
equipment, liaison and uprising consent remain separate.

Rights retain their calming effect during escalation, but cease to be an
absolute mobilisation veto. A defended compact withstands the tested pressure.
A neglected compact breaks only after 26 consecutive weeks above 19 unrest
under active foreign pressure. The ordinary warning and separate US mobilisation
decision still apply. Renewed intervention therefore creates a contest, not an
automatic release or war.

## Coercion and civilian consequences

Both harsh decisions require four real divisions, unrest above 39, treasury,
full control of a populated eligible state and no active emergency cooldown.
They are deliberate human RP choices; routine host AI has weight zero.

| Choice | GDP / PP | Actual civilian population loss | Immediate containment | Reconciliation setback | Trauma |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Impose Emergency Military Rule | 2% / 100 | 1,000 | -25 unrest, +10 stabilisation, -10 organisation | -52 earned weeks | 104 weeks, +0.75 unrest/week |
| Authorise Indiscriminate Repression | 4% / 150 | 10,000 | -50 unrest, +20 stabilisation, -20 organisation | -104 earned weeks | 156 weeks, +1.50 unrest/week |

These casualty figures are explicit fictional gameplay outcomes, not historical
death-toll claims for all four countries. One deterministic eligible state loses
the population through native state-scope `add_manpower`, not a cosmetic counter
or merely a national recruitable-manpower invoice. The same guarded transaction
records the losses. A shared 104-week cooldown prevents immediate repetition.

Emergency rule additionally costs 4% stability; indiscriminate repression costs
12%. Both end political rights and American settlement guarantees, remove any
consolidated compact and impose **Legacy of Regional Repression**: -5% stability,
-10% foreign influence defence, +5% police cost and +3% bureaucracy cost. Trauma
blocks reconciliation until it expires. The four large reforms cannot bypass it.

The host, USA and existing local authorities receive separately localised country
events in the native existing MD frame. Washington can publicly condemn the
crackdown for 25 PP, imposing a year of -10% foreign influence and mutual -50
opinion. Existing local authorities can document the victims for 25 PP. These
events do not impose automatic global sanctions or declare another player's war.

## Historical grounding and limits

Kissinger's April 1974 memorandum describes assistance intended to strengthen
Kurdish bargaining and tie down Baghdad; it also records the expiry of the
four-year 1970 truce. The 1975 termination memorandum documents the dependence
on Iranian access. They support reversible accommodation, costly interference
and supply constraints, not a guaranteed permanent Kurdish client state.

Sources: [FRUS 246](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d246),
[FRUS 289](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v27/d289).

The historical record includes Iraqi mass atrocities during Anfal in 1988 and
executions and civilian killings in Iranian Kurdistan after 1979. Those precedents
justify severe civilian and political consequences. They do not establish an
identical historical campaign in 1970 Turkey or Syria. The shared decisions are
alternative player choices; neither their casualty counts nor the six-/ten-year
balance thresholds are represented as historical statistics.

Sources: [Human Rights Watch, Anfal investigation](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1993/iraqanfal/ANFALINT.htm),
[UN Special Rapporteur findings on Iran](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/countries/iran/20240717-SR-Iran-Findings.pdf).
