IMPERIA COLD WAR

SSR and Soviet Union campaign-depth review, 25 October 2026.

This is a review and an unapproved development proposal. No focus rewards, Union rules, government checks or economic gameplay were changed. The installed scripts and their owning generators were read directly. Existing implementation reports were used for context, not treated as proof of campaign depth. No new native game process or multiplayer session was started. A separate audit chat has unrelated working-tree changes; they were preserved.

The current content has several substantive systems, but their integration is uneven. Repeated annual missions, false reporting, investigations, punishments and transferable honours already have consequences. The largest gaps are constitutional continuation, sector-specific economic development and the connection between republican specialisation and Union planning. Focus count is not a measure of how many meaningful decisions remain after several campaign years.

Material findings and their smallest useful changes:

1. Federation Council is an institutional endpoint. `COLDWAR_SOV_federation_council` sets a flag, removes republican seats and sends news. The flag disables the existing republican decision groups and dossier category. There is no corresponding Council voting provider. Relevant source: `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_scripted_effects.txt:1680` and `common/decisions/categories/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_decision_categories.txt:82`. Add a real successor institution before allowing this constitutional reform to retire the old one.

2. The economic-priority ballot is a confidence vote. `COLDWAR_SOV_set_plan_group_a` and the other priority decisions add their national idea before starting a ten-day ballot. A failed type-2 vote adds Defying the Politburo for 180 days, without reversing that priority. Source: `common/decisions/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_decisions.txt:728` and `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_scripted_effects.txt:1232`. This can be intentional executive authority, but it must not be described as a binding approval procedure. A development version could offer amendment, withdrawal or explicit enactment by decree.

3. Specialisation and annual planning are separate. `COLDWAR_UNI_issue_annual_target` uses the global economic-priority code and static regional substitutions. It never reads the selected SSR specialisation receipts. Source: `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_UNI_1970_scripted_effects.txt:234`. A republic specialising in aircraft therefore does not acquire an aircraft delivery target from that choice. Preserve every republic's access to all nine programmes, then derive optional contracts from the actual selected programme and existing assets.

4. The six enterprise sectors are mechanically interchangeable within each method. All six auctions use the same fees, proceeds, duration and modifiers; the same is true separately for worker buyouts, public retooling and liquidation. The immutable approved snapshot confirms six sectors but one numerical variant per method. Source of the original template: `tools/ssr_concept/market.py:19`; installed execution: `tools/ssr_1970/market.py:51`. Sector-specific results should be added as a separate approved revision, not by modifying the immutable snapshot.

5. Sovereign industrial recovery has no progression beyond the fixed legacy and general conversion focuses. Full programme benefits retain 20% of beneficial components, 75% of adverse components and half the weekly allocation after independence. General conversion can remove Unconverted Union Orders and add its separate benefits, but does not upgrade the specialisation legacy itself. Source: `tools/ssr_1970/lifecycle.py:93` and `:342`. Add a costly national continuation of the selected industry, retaining an initial major loss of Union advantages.

6. Union cohesion is predominantly centralised. Monthly drift reads Soviet stability, surrender progress and corruption. It does not aggregate current satisfaction, investment delivery or burden sharing across the fourteen republics. Source: `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_scripted_effects.txt:313`. Republican actions change cohesion directly, but there is no saved bilateral settlement record explaining each republic's ongoing consent.

7. A false-report correction does not fully reopen an already won annual plan. Exposure removes the republic's fulfilled flag and decrements the aggregate count. It does not clear `COLDWAR_UNI_year_won`; settlement subsequently counts a good year from that flag. Thus a year can remain won even when an exposed report supplied the decisive ninth completion. Relevant source: `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_UNI_1970_scripted_effects.txt:506`, `:519` and `:580`. This is a confirmed source-level inconsistency, not a newly reproduced native-game result. Re-evaluate quorum after audits and define whether already paid collective rewards are provisional or final.

8. Constitutional autonomy is not independence. Declaring sovereignty removes a republic from the Union Plan while its actual Soviet subject relation remains. The newer SSR provider consequently retains SSR-status content. The later referendum offers Moscow a new treaty, release, or refusal. Source: `common/decisions/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_decisions.txt:1848`, `events/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_events.txt:510`, and `common/scripted_triggers/COLDWAR_SSR_lifecycle_triggers.txt:5`. Treat this intermediate state deliberately rather than labelling it independent.

9. The five-year calendar stops advancing after Plan XII. The recurring session event advances the plan in January 1971, 1976, 1981 and 1986 only. Annual targets continue afterwards, but no equivalent Plan XIII transition resets the economic-priority choice or the per-plan directive quota in 1991. Source: `events/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_events.txt:190` and `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_SOV_1970_scripted_effects.txt:1702`. Add a repeatable subsequent-plan framework while preserving the original IX-XII focus rewards. This is a source-level continuation gap, not evidence that annual missions stop in 1991.

The recommended development order is four connected packages, with no new giant parallel focus tree.

1. Make the existing planning loop consequential over several years. At the start of a plan, Moscow proposes a programme and a republic selects ordinary obligations, an ambitious development contract or a funded specialisation contract. Capture the payer, recipient, target, current-GDP setup budget, weekly allocation, deadline, promised support and failure terms. Include annual reviews in the same contract. Meeting an intermediate target can improve later terms; an unfunded Soviet commitment should reduce what can reasonably be demanded from the republic. Keep genuinely binding votes separate from confidence votes. Correct the exposed-report accounting first.

2. Introduce real constitutional alternatives. A centralised Union can concentrate procurement and investment while retaining larger contributions and stronger central appointment powers. A negotiated federation can trade part of that control for fiscal autonomy, local administration and an actual republic chamber. A looser common market can preserve selected economic arrangements while making common procurement and defence agreements dependent on explicit commitments. Determine actual constitutional membership separately from ideology. If a country is sovereign, independent content applies; an economic treaty must not silently restore full SSR benefits. These are alternate-history game arrangements, not three historical constitutions assumed to coexist in January 1970.

3. Extend each selected specialisation into a development cycle. The common sequence is founding institutions, production scale, technical renewal and conversion. Aircraft can choose concentrated Union orders versus a broader maintenance and civil-aviation base. Armour can choose long production runs versus expensive modernisation and quality control. Agriculture can choose mechanisation and lower agricultural labour requirements versus lower energy dependence and broader food supply. All nine options remain available to every republic, subject to real geography and existing resources. New regional examples explain consequences, not nationality locks. Retain weapon-specific production-cost reductions and existing output bonuses. Never use a penalty to total workforce as a substitute for technical shortages.

4. Continue sovereign political economy after the first emergency. Keep the original one-time 730-day reform window. Follow it with sector-specific decisions on competition, public procurement, investment, industrial debt and household protection. Food processing should affect agricultural supply and energy demand; repair engineering should affect maintenance and technical renewal; building materials should affect construction; industrial suppliers should affect conversion and equipment production. A public owner needs funding and accountability; a private owner needs viable customers, financing and regulation. Ownership changes should have consequences after the initial receipt. No permanent annual growth bonus or new 730-day windfall on a government switch.

An illustrative aircraft-republic campaign:

Moscow offers a three-year procurement and tooling programme. The republic chooses either a larger dedicated production line with stronger dependency on Union orders, or a smaller mixed production and maintenance network with lower dependency. Annual reviews compare actual agreed deliveries and Soviet funding. Missing funding permits an extension or renegotiation, rather than automatic republican punishment. Technical renewal competes with consumer investment. On independence, the approved major loss of Union benefits still occurs. The country then chooses a paid clearing and maintenance arrangement with its former partners, an export-certification programme with a consenting new partner, or domestic conversion. These choices change the liabilities and reachable industrial continuation; they are not an immediate restoration of full Union advantages. Equipment transfers require real stock and supported native transfer effects. Nothing here is implemented by this review.

Historical basis for development, with limits:

- The February 1970 Kissinger memorandum records the contemporary US assessment of slowing productivity, simultaneous consumer and defence claims, and the tension between reform and central control. It is an external contemporary assessment, not a neutral measurement of every Soviet republic. [FRUS, document 124](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v12/d124).
- Japan's 1991 diplomatic account records competing gradual and rapid reform proposals, republican taxation disputes and negotiations over a new Union Treaty. It supports making constitutional and economic authority interact, without prescribing inevitable dissolution. [Diplomatic Bluebook 1991, Soviet Union](https://www.mofa.go.jp/policy/other/bluebook/1991/1991-4-4.htm).
- The IMF's retrospective account of the 1991-93 ruble area describes changing choices over monetary arrangements and inter-state financing. It supports negotiated clearing and separate currency choices after sovereignty, rather than one universal automatic collapse modifier. Search-index text was available; the full article and PDF returned HTTP 403 during this review. [IMF Working Paper 01/101](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2001/101/article-A001-en.xml).

These sources justify policy tensions and historical precedents. The proposed programme lengths, recovery steps and eventual numerical balance remain game design, not historical contract quotations.

Implementation boundaries for any later approval: reuse supported MD finance and production consumers; show concrete costs and effects before consent; preserve exact ruling-party activation with visible branches; keep short COMECON contracts and Union membership distinct; use bounded records for actual participating republics; put private native tests and archives on E:. Source checks, native calendar execution, reload/rehost and live multiplayer are separate verification stages. This review does not certify them.
