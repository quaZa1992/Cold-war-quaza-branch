IMPERIA COLD WAR

Union production orders and common development programmes. Design record, 25 October 2026. The decision-only annual order register is now implemented by `tools/union_orders_1970`; the additional common-tree development branches below remain proposals. Check the implementation README and current reports before attributing a draft feature to runtime code.

This extends the campaign-depth review with the user's requests for freely configured production obligations, civilian-industry alternatives to agricultural expansion, equipment-specific common consequences and a larger joint Union tree. Existing approved SSR design snapshots are unchanged. The initial proposal did not rebuild gameplay files. The later authorised implementation installs the decision-only register and its narrow legacy integrations, documented in `tools/union_orders_1970/README.md`.

The Union requirement remains actual membership, regardless of Soviet or republican ideology. The fourteen original republics retain every specialisation choice. Common programme outcomes apply to the Soviet Union and current participating SSRs; material grants go to the agreed actual republican sites. Moscow directs internal Union programmes. Sovereign foreign partners require their own agreements and are not subject to these orders.

Production orders

Moscow chooses the republic, equipment family, quantity, optional financial support and the published common success/failure terms. Every building and production quota has a full 365-day annual accounting period, as explicitly requested by the user. Quantities are editable integers, with native-style increment controls for 1, 10, 100 and 1,000; shorter quota deadlines are not offered. There is no two-orders-per-five-year-plan restriction. Several equipment families may be commissioned in parallel; a republic has one current annual order per family so the same family does not have several competing baselines. Early fulfilment does not shorten or restart that year's accounting period. A midyear order receives a full year rather than only the remaining calendar months.

The user subsequently requested two entry routes: Moscow can issue a binding annual order, or a player republic can submit its own proposed quantity and support terms for Soviet confirmation. All interaction must stay in the existing Decisions menu: no new window or custom scripted GUI. Native decision categories and conditionally visible preparation decisions provide recipient, family, quantity and assistance selection. The first valid submission reserves that republic's obligation-family slot; a proposal has no binding quota, money transfer or opening baseline before approval. Moscow must accept or reject the reserved proposal rather than silently overwrite it. Confirmation starts the full 365 days. The republic can withdraw before confirmation; unanswered proposals lapse after 30 days without consequences. That review window is not a shortened production deadline. The authoritative execution guard, not local button state or a client timestamp, decides simultaneous submissions. See `UNION_ORDER_MENU_20261025.md` for the menu and lifecycle design.

Multiplayer responsibility is restricted to human-controlled republics. AI-only programmes never produce shortfalls, Consumer Goods penalties, equipment-cost penalties, political penalties or collective failure entries. A registered transition from human to actual AI control neutralises an unfinished binding order irreversibly for that annual window, without a success award or automatic refund of assistance already paid. It cannot block a collective completion threshold. A later human does not inherit the old deadline or reactivate that order. The original family window remains reserved until its original end, preventing control changes from repeatedly restarting assistance and rewards; subsequent binding obligations receive a fresh full year. Completed outcomes are not retroactively rewritten by a later control change. All final settlement effects recheck current human control and the order's responsibility state. Excluded AI/neutral orders cannot manufacture collective success; zero eligible human orders gives neither a collective reward nor a failure. This replaces the present probabilistic AI-floor approach in the proposed new mechanic, rather than treating random fabricated fulfilment as safe AI support.

The catalogue must cover small arms, C&C equipment, utility and heavy vehicles, main battle tanks, light tanks, APCs, IFVs, towed artillery, self-propelled artillery, MLRS, portable and vehicle air defence, anti-tank equipment, fighters, strike/CAS aircraft, transport aircraft, helicopters, convoys, escorts, submarines and major warships. Missile orders can use the actual supported categories and researched equipment. A naval programme needs real naval production access. Equipment names and targets come from the active MD equipment definitions, including its designer duplicates.

Two different obligations are available and must remain clearly named:

| Obligation | What fulfils it |
| --- | --- |
| Production quota | Newly produced equipment after the saved opening baseline. Existing reserves and imports are not a substitute for this measurement. |
| Delivery order | Actual agreed equipment handed over from available stock. This can be useful for Soviet procurement, but it does not prove new production. |

Native production counters are available, but their MD grouping is a real limitation. The installed documentation exposes `total_equipment_produced_armor`, `_artillery`, `_motorized`, `_fighter`, `_cas`, `_air_transport`, `_screen_ship`, `_capital_ship`, `_submarine` and other equipment-type counters. Save the appropriate cumulative value at issue and compare later production against it. Equipment already deployed, consumed or exported need not disappear from a cumulative production result.

Those counters are not an exact universal equipment-family API. MD's `heavy_tank_chassis` is used for helicopters while belonging to `armor`; APCs and other designer duplicates also enter armour-related groups. Towed artillery is both `infantry` and `artillery`; C&C and small arms share `infantry`. Thus an armour-class total cannot honestly certify 300 main battle tanks, and an infantry-class total cannot certify rifles alone. Generic documentation also does not establish exact producer/model filtering. Narrow categories require a proven finer native measurement, an honestly labelled delivery obligation where supported, or explicit production-board certification by the Soviet RP player. That certification is a human adjudication, not automatic production proof. A game implementation must expose the actual verification method and test it before promising exact automatic counts. Do not retype MD equipment families merely to obtain a counter; that can alter unrelated equipment bonuses and game behaviour.

Illustrative configured annual order: 300 main battle tanks in 365 days. Completion gives all current Union members a named successful equipment programme with -8% main-battle-tank production cost for 180 days. Failure gives +8% production cost for that same equipment for 180 days and +3% Consumer Goods Factor for 180 days. The full-year quota period and six-month success/failure durations, represented as 180 native days, are the user's final requirements. A 1,000-artillery annual order is another configuration, not a separate hardcoded mechanic. Other equipment families use matching rewards and penalties. Outcome durations are separate from the mandatory 365-day production period.

Use removable country ideas with equipment-specific `equipment_bonus`, `build_cost_ic` and `instant = yes`, following the existing SSR specialisation pattern. Positive cost changes mean more industrial work per unit; a generic national factory-output penalty would affect unrelated production. Keep a single common outcome state per actual equipment target rather than stacking repeated identical rewards. Use the strongest current quota-related Consumer Goods burden rather than duplicating that national burden for every failed republic. Overlapping equipment categories need explicit aggregation. A new order's baseline starts on issue and survives reload; the same receipt cannot complete twice. For real production quotas, the existing Storm the Plan and fabricated-report flags must not silently count as physical production. They can remain separate political reporting actions.

The Soviet government may make an ambitious or deliberately difficult annual demand. The interface should show quantity, the 365-day period, required average daily output and recent measured output where that history exists. It must not manufacture a capacity estimate when there is no production history. Difficulty guidance does not veto a manually chosen RP target. Material support and quantity amendments remain explicit changes to the current order without restarting its annual clock.

Building orders and cotton processing

The current one-building objective in 300-321 days is not uniformly difficult. Large republics and Soviet-assisted recipients can often fulfil it easily; a small republic with little uncommitted construction can struggle. The new design gives building orders the same full 365-day period as production orders. Building type, quantity, support and consequences remain configurable. The annual accounting pipeline must close the old period before resetting receipts or opening its successor, rather than cancelling a still-running 365-day target at the old January settlement.

White Gold gains two separate fulfilment routes: additional agricultural districts, or civilian industrial capacity representing ginning, spinning and textile processing. Store both opening counts and test the selected route. The ordinary version needs one added level of the chosen type; an ambitious version can require two. A generic CIV is an explicit abstraction of processing capacity, not evidence that the native factory actually manufactures cotton textiles. The proposal applies the same principle to food processing and fruit/wine processing where appropriate, without turning every agricultural project into the same industrial reward.

Common Union tree

First expansion: 24 additional focuses in six substantive branches, bringing the existing 17-focus common tree to 41 focuses. Each branch has a funding/institutional focus, a material programme and two mutually exclusive follow-up policies. Complementary industry, energy and transport programmes remain compatible; the forks govern how each programme operates. Large programmes are initiated by Moscow, with republican proposals routed through existing institutions. They must not let a republic silently charge another human government's treasury.

| Branch | Material programme | Illustrative common benefit after commissioning | Continuing trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Union Industrial Programme | One civilian factory in each participating SSR, each with its extra shared slot | +5% civilian factory productivity | +3% energy use; financed construction and programme administration |
| Unified Thermal Power Programme | One `fossil_powerplant` in each participating SSR, each with its extra shared slot | +5% fossil power generation | Actual native fuel consumption and staffing demand, plus programme funding |
| Union Freight Corridors | One infrastructure level in each participating SSR, each with its extra shared slot | -5% supply consumption and +10% infrastructure construction speed | +2% bureaucracy costs and continuing transport funding |
| Food and Processing Programme | An agricultural district or civilian processing combine in each participating SSR, with its extra shared slot | Agricultural route: +8% agricultural productivity. Processing route: +4% agricultural productivity and +4% civilian factory productivity | +3% energy use and the agreed investment budget |
| Union Scientific and Technical Network | Shared standards, training and research cooperation | +3% research speed and +5% production-efficiency growth | +5% bureaucracy costs and a weekly research budget |
| State Defence Orders | The configurable production and procurement register, with funded technical assistance | Equipment-specific common production-cost programmes based on actual results | Published failure terms and material-support costs |

These are draft programme values. They are not already active spirits. Each actual building level grants its extra slot first in the same owned and controlled eligible state and guarded transaction. Construction must respect native capacity. The beneficiaries and sites are reviewed before commitment. Fourteen factories or power stations are a financed development programme, not an immediate gift from one short focus.

Plausible policy forks include:

- Concentrated Combines: +8% civilian productivity with +5% energy demand, versus Regional Workshops: +5% civilian productivity and +5% infrastructure construction speed with +3% bureaucracy costs.
- Maximum Thermal Output: +10% fossil generation and +10% fossil fuel consumption, versus Efficient Turbines: +5% fossil generation and -10% fossil fuel consumption.
- Raw-Material Expansion: stronger agricultural output and sector labour savings, versus Processing at Home: industrial value added and a broader customer base.
- Standardised Production Runs: greater equipment-specific cost savings, versus Design-Bureau Cooperation: stronger research and technical renewal.

These describe alternative final programme variants. They replace their base variant instead of granting the listed base bonus again on top. Agricultural labour savings affect native agricultural requirements, never total national workforce. Each material branch can later open another fully funded development round, while cooperative spirits do not stack copies on every round.

Draft financing for review uses current Soviet GDP, not fixed dollar amounts. One possible initial calibration is an industrial setup contribution of 0.2% of Soviet GDP per funded republic plus 0.003% per week per funded republic during 730 funded days. With fourteen republics this is 2.8% upfront and 0.042% per week. A thermal programme could use 0.15% upfront and 0.002% per week per funded republic, giving 2.1% upfront and 0.028% per week for fourteen. Those are explicit new balance proposals; the old individual Union-project helper charges 2% of Soviet GDP for one CIV or power station. Bulk programme prices require economic testing, not merely a syntax pass. Subsequent operating budgets are separate from construction contributions, and optional republican cofinancing must be agreed before charging that republic. Weekly source literals must respect the five-fractional-digit rule.

Native MD's fossil plant adds nominal 2 GW and consumes 20 fuel per hour, or 480 per day. Fourteen additional plants therefore add nominal 28 GW and 6,720 daily fuel consumption before modifiers and operating constraints. This is fossil/thermal generation in MD's abstraction, not a guaranteed oil-only technical specification or free fuel grant. Oil production, refining, supply and efficiency programmes become meaningful companions to the new plants.

Physical assets remain national property after independence. Membership-dependent cooperative spirits suspend outside the Union and are reconciled on re-entry without repeating assets or setup fees. A funded order and unpaid liabilities need an explicit exit settlement; independence must not be used to make the same equipment receipt pay out again. The existing substantially reduced specialisation legacy remains a separate system. Common civilian development does not force every republic into the same mutually exclusive specialisation.

Layout feasibility was checked from actual parsed coordinates. The current Soviet tree has 856 local focuses with bounds x=0..426, y=0..18. The existing common block occupies x=346..365, y=8..11, and no Soviet local focus lies within x=340..370 at y>=7. In the SSR view its root is at 70,0; the specialisation row begins at y=14, leaving space below the existing common content for a compact extension. This establishes candidate space, not finished connector or native text-layout approval. The generic host and every conditional country view still require their own complete geometry and icon review before installation. All proposed cards must remain clickable and inspectable, including unavailable choices.
