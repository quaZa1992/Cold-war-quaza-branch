IMPERIA COLD WAR
# Argentina, January 1970: historical research for national programme concepts

Scope: Argentina from the January 1970 scenario through 1990. This is historical grounding for a multiplayer roleplay concept, not a claim that alternate outcomes occurred. The starting position must remain Onganía's authoritarian government in January 1970; branches and named later leaders should respect their actual dates. All effects suggested below are balance/design inferences, not historical facts.

## Starting brief: January 1970

Argentina in January 1970 is politically brittle, economically strained, and still a substantial industrial country. It is not yet the 1980s debt crisis. Onganía's "Revolución Argentina" had suppressed party politics and the Peronist movement remained proscribed. The 29 May 1969 Cordobazo, a mass worker-student uprising in industrial Córdoba, damaged the regime's legitimacy. Armed services were not a single-minded bloc: the 1960s Azules/Colorados conflict is relevant background, but the 1970 succession is more accurately described as competing paternalist, nationalist and liberal currents, plus career officers. Under the Azules' earlier approach, Peronism might be managed or selectively integrated, while hardliners sought its exclusion. [UNLP account of the 1966-73 regime](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/139143); [O'Donnell, *Bureaucratic Authoritarianism*, military currents and succession](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/bureaucratic-authoritarianism-argentina-1966-1973-in-comparative-perspective.html).

The credible baseline policy crisis is how to recover governability without surrendering the military's political role: coercive order and corporatist rule; a nationalist-industrial turn; or a controlled electoral opening. Onganía's position is already unsustainable in actuality: on 9 June 1970 the commanders removed him after he refused their phased return-to-elections plan, while labor conflict and the Aramburu kidnapping worsened security concerns. Levingston (1970-71) and Lanusse (1971-73) followed. Lanusse's opening culminated in the 1973 election and Peronist return. [FRUS, 10 June 1970, contemporaneous US record of the removal](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d63); [UNLP chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/139143).

Economic constraints should be real but period-appropriate. Inflation was chronic, but it was not yet the later triple-digit norm: the World Bank describes 1968-70 as one of only two postwar intervals with inflation below 20 percent, after Krieger Vasena's controls and fiscal adjustment held inflation down for nearly three years. The 1969 social disturbances weakened incomes policy, livestock-cycle weakness and political uncertainty complicated 1970, and sector capacity varied: some factories ran near full capacity while others had slack. The 1970 government targeted growth, an improved external balance and reserves, and a higher labor share. This gives a genuine choice between wage restraint/export competitiveness, demand and wage recovery, or investment-led industrial upgrading. [World Bank historical inflation review](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/756861468739164141/pdf/multi0page.pdf); [World Bank 1970 country analysis](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/852291468002635167/pdf/multi0page.pdf). The severe inflation break belongs later: 1975 inflation rose to 183 percent annually, and the March 1975-March 1976 price level quintupled amid devaluation and crisis; 1980s stagnation and near-100-percent debt/GDP are later outcomes, not January 1970 starting spirits. [World Bank, *Argentina: From Insolvency to Growth*](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/644441468002112850/pdf/multi-page.pdf); [World Bank inflation history](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/756861468739164141/pdf/multi0page.pdf).

## Political constituencies and credible programmes

- **Military government and services:** Onganía's paternalist/corporatist project can pursue administrative consolidation, order and industrial planning, but faces a legitimacy ceiling, strikes, insurgent violence and divided officer corps. A Lanusse-style negotiated exit trades direct military control for a chance to preserve institutional prerogatives through elections. The contemporaneous US cable explicitly records his proposed staged electoral transition. A nationalist Levingston-style industrial turn is also historically grounded, but should be presented as a contested 1970-71 alternative, not Onganía's January policy already accomplished. [FRUS, 10 June 1970](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d63); [World Bank 1971 country programme paper](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/088121924460069302-0560011971/original/WorldBankGroupArchivesfolder30275575.pdf).
- **Peronist movement and labour:** Peronism is a broad movement, not one tidy party current. Union leadership and orthodox Peronists can bargain for wages, union recognition and political legalization, but a deal risks labor discipline and leaves Perón's authority unresolved. The Peronist left and revolutionary Peronists gained relevance amid 1969-70; they are not interchangeable with the labor bureaucracy. The movement returned electorally in 1973, but the subsequent internal conflict and violence make any unity path conditional. Perón remained in exile until his 1973 return. [UNLP on proscription, union politics and 1973 return](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/139143); [FRUS, 13 March 1973 election summary](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve11p2/d2).
- **UCR:** Ricardo Balbín's UCRP-led constitutional opposition was a real national force. On 11 November 1970, Peronists, Radicals and smaller parties issued *La Hora del Pueblo*, demanding elections without exclusions and respect for minorities. This is a plausible early broad-democracy coalition. Raúl Alfonsín was not the January 1970 UCR leader or national president-in-waiting: his internal challenge emerged later, he won the 1983 election, and his government established CONADEP. Use Balbín early; Alfonsín's leadership/accountability programme belongs to the transition. [O'Donnell on the November 1970 coalition](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/bureaucratic-authoritarianism-argentina-1966-1973-in-comparative-perspective.html); [Argentine government on CONADEP's creation, 15 December 1983](https://cultura.gob.ar/que-es-la-conadep-9904/).
- **MID / developmentalists:** Frondizi and Rogelio Frigerio's Movimiento de Integración y Desarrollo offers a credible industrial-development, energy, domestic-capital and infrastructure programme, with the drawback of high capital requirements and dependence on foreign technology/financing. Use MID as its actual distinct political formation, not an UCR faction in 1970. Its later electoral alliance with Peronists is historical precedent for pragmatic coalition-building. Industrial decentralization has real antecedents: regional promotion began in the 1960s; Law 19,640 on Tierra del Fuego's special customs regime was enacted 16 May 1972, and a new industrial promotion law followed in December 1973. These are later unlockable policies, not current 1970 law. [World Bank on regional promotion and industrial capacity](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/500211468010241779/pdf/multi0page.pdf); [Law 19,640, 16 May 1972](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-19640-28185/texto); [Law 20,560, 10 December 1973](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-20560-37924/texto).
- **Liberal-conservative path:** Do not label a 1970 branch UCeDe: Álvaro Alsogaray founded it in 1982. A 1970 liberal current among military officers and technocrats is plausible, but must not be conflated with the later party. Alsogaray's UCeDe later entered a coalition with President Carlos Menem in 1989; Menem's market-oriented turn belongs to his 1989 government, after a Peronist electoral victory, rather than to the orthodox union wing at the start. [UCeDe party history](https://www.ucede.org/ucede); [primary-era account of the 1989 coalition](https://revistas.juridicas.unam.mx/index.php/derecho-electoral/article/download/20358/20650?inline=1).
- **Socialist and Communist options:** Socialist parties and the Communist Party existed and participated in opposition coalitions; they can support constitutional labour, education, public industry and non-aligned diplomacy as a minor coalition route. Do not make an electorally dominant communist route the default. Armed groups (Montoneros, ERP and others) are insurgent organizations, not substitutes for the Socialist or Communist parties. A plausible alternate history can have left parties gain legal, electoral influence following early liberalization, but a mass one-party revolution is a much larger divergence than a democratic labour-left alliance. [O'Donnell on La Hora del Pueblo signatories](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/bureaucratic-authoritarianism-argentina-1966-1973-in-comparative-perspective.html); [UNLP on armed groups and the political crisis](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/139143).
- **Catholic Church and social policy:** Catholic institutions mattered in state social policy and nationalist/corporatist thought, but the Church should be treated as internally diverse, not a monolithic ruling faction. A social Catholic/Christian-democratic route can build welfare, education, rural services and mediation. The trade-off is coalition tension between hierarchy, labor, student movements and rights advocates. Progressive clergy and lay networks can be distinct from the Church hierarchy. Do not imply a Catholic clerical party takeover occurred in January 1970. [CONICET study of Catholic networks and the Social Welfare Ministry, 1966-70](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/10784).
- **Provinces:** Córdoba deserves weight as a distinct industrial and labor center, not simply a Buenos Aires proxy. Regional incentives can create jobs and strategic presence, but risk costly tax privileges, weak linkages and uneven gains. A Tierra del Fuego exception was enacted in 1972 and pursued economic activity alongside territorial sovereignty, making it a date-appropriate later federal development choice. [Law 19,640's stated geography and rationale](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-19640-28185/texto); [World Bank on regional schemes](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/500211468010241779/pdf/multi0page.pdf).

## Institutions, rights and accountability

The security crisis is real in 1970: labor unrest, urban uprisings, the 29 May 1970 kidnapping of former president Pedro Eugenio Aramburu by Montoneros, and the spread of guerrilla violence. But state accountability should be an explicit policy choice and not a free stability bonus. Emergency rule, censorship and expanded security powers can yield short-term control while corroding legitimacy and legality. The 1976-83 state terror and disappearances are a subsequent historical escalation, not a January 1970 fait accompli. The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights documented disappearance and other violations for 1975-79 in its report published 11 April 1980. On 15 December 1983 President Alfonsín created CONADEP; its 1984 *Nunca Más* documented complaints and evidence. A democratic accountability branch can investigate, publish, prosecute and face military resistance; an amnesty/accommodation route may ease transition while injuring justice and public trust. [FRUS on 1970 security concerns](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d63); [IACHR report and recommendations, 1980](https://cidh.oas.org/countryrep/Argentina80eng/introduction.htm); [Argentine government on CONADEP](https://cultura.gob.ar/que-es-la-conadep-9904/).

## Industry, energy, science and procurement

National development was already a live objective. Industrial modernization, domestic ownership, oil and gas, steel, transport, agricultural exports and regional infrastructure can each be plausible. The tension is not simply state versus market: policy must decide who finances capital-intensive projects, how much foreign technology to accept, whether wage growth supports demand or erodes external balance, and which provinces get investment. Nuclear science is a credible long programme: Atucha I began operation in 1974; the 1970s/80s Argentine programme was explicitly civilian in its public orientation, while the sensitive fuel cycle also raised proliferation concern. Do not give free nuclear power or weapons in 1970. [IAEA, Argentina's nuclear development overview](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/P1475_web.pdf); [IAEA/ABACC overview notes Atucha I operation in 1974](https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/gc/gc56-17_en.pdf).

Argentina signed a peaceful-use nuclear cooperation agreement with Brazil on 17 May 1980. It is an excellent late-game choice between expensive sovereign technology, reciprocal inspection and trust-building. In 1990, the Foz do Iguaçu declaration affirmed the peaceful-use process and reciprocal facility visits; the bilateral accounting and control agency followed in 1991, outside this concept's cutoff. [Argentine Law 22,494 approving the 1980 agreement](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-22494-206224); [UN document on the 28 November 1990 declaration and earlier visits](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/104535/files/A_45_809-EN.pdf).

Arms acquisition should have supplier and political costs. Argentina pursued French Mirage aircraft in 1970, while British-origin naval and communications contracts remained important through the 1970s. The 1982 war exposed reliance on external spares, munitions and logistics and made postwar supply conditional. A domestic defense industry can reduce vulnerability but competes with civilian investment; US/French equipment is plausible, but do not depict either supplier as a guaranteed alliance. [1970 French-Argentine Mirage supply accord citation in contemporary treaty register](https://tratados.mrecic.gov.ar/tratado_ficha.php?id=mKSkmw%3D%3D); [UK Parliament, 14 July 1982, on equipment contracts signed since 1970](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/1982/jul/14/argentina-supply-of-military-equipment); [US contemporaneous 1982 briefing on routine support asymmetry](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v13/d65).

## Diplomacy and bilateral roleplay hooks

- **United Kingdom / Malvinas:** The 1971 joint statement is a useful cooperative but unresolved agreement. Talks 21-30 June 1971 established regular weekly air service and plans for passenger, cargo, mail, telephone, telegraph and postal links, within UN sovereignty negotiations; a sovereignty umbrella preserved both sides' legal positions. This supports an Argentine offer focused on connectivity, trade and family travel, with no sovereignty transfer. The relationship then diverged sharply: Argentina invaded on 2 April 1982, war ended with surrender on 14 June 1982, and relations were restored in stages: consular relations in 1989, full diplomatic relations in 1990. A postwar restoration deal is not a return to prewar trust. [UN record of the 1971 talks](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1293548/files/A_8369-EN.pdf); [Argentine Foreign Ministry timeline, 1971/1989/1990](https://cancilleria.gob.ar/es/politica-exterior/cuestion-malvinas/conversaciones-y-entendimientos-bilaterales-con-el-reino-unido); [FRUS volume on the 1982 conflict and postwar diplomacy](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v13/sources).
- **Chile / Beagle Channel:** The 1978 crisis brought the two countries close to war over islands and maritime claims south of the Beagle Channel. Argentina declared the arbitral award void in January 1978; papal mediation was accepted as a path to avert hostilities. The 1984 Treaty of Peace and Friendship resolved the immediate crisis after the democratic transition. An earlier Argentine opening could reduce conflict risk, but a deal would face nationalist opposition and require real concessions. [FRUS, 8 December 1978, near-war and papal mediation](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v24/d38); [UN treaty record, 1984 Treaty of Peace and Friendship](https://treaties.un.org/pages/showDetails.aspx?objid=08000002800c90e3).
- **Brazil:** Brazil is both a strategic competitor and the most consequential development partner. In 1980 the nuclear agreement offered technical cooperation while raising questions of safeguards, reciprocity and military confidence. A credible route can advance from trade/industrial talks to nuclear visits and inspections. Distinguish the 1980 agreement from later democratic integration: reciprocal safeguards and ABACC came in 1991, beyond the endpoint. [1980 Argentine approval law](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-22494-206224); [UN document on the 1990 bilateral declaration](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/104535/files/A_45_809-EN.pdf).
- **Soviet Union and United States:** Argentina's grain exports to the USSR were not just ideological alignment. After President Carter announced an embargo on 4 January 1980 following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, Washington pressed Argentina to restrict sales. Argentine officials argued that grain sanctions would be ineffective and that commercial ties mattered; US officials emphasized alliance credibility. This creates an excellent roleplay choice: honor US pressure at cost of export earnings, or preserve agricultural sales and accept friction. [FRUS, 6 May 1980 conversation with Economy Minister Martínez de Hoz](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v24/d108); [FRUS, 26 January 1980 report](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v24/d106).
- **France and the United States / procurement:** French Mirage procurement was underway in 1970; the US supplied other military support, while its willingness to supply combat aircraft and credit changed over time. The 1982 US tilt to the UK and French wartime embargo show that procurement does not equal a reliable defense guarantee. Use equipment access, credit, training and spare parts as distinct benefits/costs rather than an alliance switch. [Argentine treaty register](https://tratados.mrecic.gov.ar/tratado_ficha.php?id=mKSkmw%3D%3D); [FRUS, 6 April 1982 support memo](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v13/d65); [FRUS, 9 April 1982 communications support to UK fleet](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v13/d86).

## Branch and event concepts: design inference

1. **Order and the Revolution:** strengthen executive administration and security; benefits are short-run command capacity and investment coordination; costs are labor opposition, party exclusion and a recurring legitimacy crisis. Key event: 9 June 1970 commanders demand Onganía's resignation. An alternate Onganía victory can defer the coup, but should create a harder subsequent crisis, not erase the Cordobazo.
2. **National Development / Levingston-Ferrer option:** shift toward Argentine-owned industry, wage growth and domestic suppliers; benefits industrial capability and labor support; costs external-balance pressure, capacity bottlenecks and conflict with large firms/creditors. Make it available after the real June 1970 succession point, with its actual historical failure as the historical route's risk.
3. **Controlled opening / Lanusse:** bargain with party leaders, legalize competition and preserve defined military guarantees; benefits legitimacy and reduced insurgent isolation; costs uncertainty over Perón, union militancy and military autonomy. Anchor *La Hora del Pueblo* on 11 November 1970, the GAN in 1971 and elections in March 1973.
4. **Peronist return:** choose an orthodox union compact, left-national mobilization, or pragmatic institutional pact. Each must be a real policy distinction. Avoid importing Menem's 1989 market package into a 1973 union-led route without an explicit later political realignment.
5. **UCR constitutional route:** Balbín and broad opposition lead the earlier coalition; later Alfonsín can make democratic institutions, human rights accountability and civilian control central after the 1983 transition. The cost is confronting the armed forces and managing economic crisis.
6. **MID developmentalist route:** use national energy, industry, and selected foreign capital/technology; trade-off is financing needs and dependence. This can cooperate with Peronists while retaining distinct developmentalist ownership and planning goals.
7. **Late liberal-conservative route:** introduce UCeDe in 1982; its coalition with Menem in 1989 can support privatization, fiscal reform and market opening, at the cost of labor conflict, distributional pressure and dislocation of protected industry. This should be a 1980s/1989 alternative rather than a starting ideology.
8. **National projects and diplomacy:** regional industrial promotion and Tierra del Fuego from 1972; civilian nuclear capability through 1974 and later stages; Soviet grain trade in 1980; Brazilian nuclear confidence from 1980; Beagle treaty in 1984; UK communications restoration in 1971 and diplomatic normalization in 1989-90. These are dated milestones, not automatic outcomes.

## Main balance guidance

January 1970 needs an acute political legitimacy and labor problem, a serious but still manageable inflation/external-balance challenge, and existing industrial/scientific strength. Avoid starting with later foreign-debt crisis, 1975 hyperinflation, 1976-83 terror-state outcomes, 1980s deindustrialization or 1989 hyperinflation. Those should be contingent risks or later events. Useful trade-offs include real wage recovery versus export reserves; regional tax incentives versus revenue and efficiency; imports/foreign expertise versus domestic technological learning; energy and nuclear sovereignty versus capital costs and safeguards; security versus civil liberties; Malvinas pressure versus UK connectivity; US alignment versus grain-market access; and deterrence versus Brazil-Chile confidence-building.

## Political coverage audit: socialist identities and registry implications

The provisional programme spread is broadly defensible as alternate-history coverage if player access and historical incumbency are kept distinct. The 1970 state is an Onganía-led military government, not the representative of every institution listed. Peronists and Radicals were proscribed, and the socialist field was fragmented. Consequently, a full programme for a small party or organization represents a counterfactual route to government after political opening, not its real national weight or presence in the 1970 cabinet.

The socialist line needs dated identities rather than one continuous generic party. In 1958, the Socialist Party divided into the Partido Socialista Democrático (PSD), associated with Jacinto Oddone and later Juan Antonio Solari, and the Partido Socialista Argentino (PSA), associated with Ramón Muñiz. The PSD retained its programmatic line into the 1980s. The PSA itself fragmented: in April 1972 a PSA grouping led by Víctor García Costa and Jorge Selser joined MAPA, Grupo Evolución and Militancia Popular to found the Partido Socialista Popular (PSP). A separate PSA faction under Juan Carlos Coral joined PRT-La Verdad, formally creating the Partido Socialista de los Trabajadores (PST) in December 1972. These streams are not interchangeable. [UNLP dissertation on the 1958 split and political traditions](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.1788/te.1788.pdf); [UNLP/CLACSO research on PSP's 1972 founding coalition](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/4871/1/Un-nuevo-partido.pdf); [Library of Congress-hosted study on the Coral faction/PST](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/71/98/58/2020719858/2020719858.pdf).

A constitutional Socialist programme is therefore appropriate as a small alternate government path, especially after a negotiated opening. The most defensible identity is an explicit PSD or provisional constitutional-socialist alias, not CGTA. The PSD ran in the March 1973 presidential election and received 109,068 votes (0.91 percent); the Socialist Workers' Party received 73,796 (0.62 percent), while the Popular Front of the Left received 48,571 (0.41 percent). Those contemporary figures support a minor, coalition-dependent route and reject portraying it as the obvious governing mass movement. [Inter-Parliamentary Union, 1973 Argentine election summary](https://data.ipu.org/election-summary/PDF/ARGENTINA_1973_E.PDF).

For a single provisional neutral-social registry slot, keep the Socialist Party identity exclusive and unresolved pending the project's approved party alias. If the design chooses PSD, name it PSD and keep its democratic-parliamentary programme distinct. If it chooses a broader PSA-derived identity, name the 1972 successor PSP and do not imply it already existed in January 1970. Do not activate two overlapping socialist incumbents in the same slot or use CGTA as a party synonym. A 12-focus provisional concept can credibly cover constitutional rights, labor legislation, social insurance, public education, municipal and provincial organization, cooperative development and electoral coalition-building, with coalition dependence and limited initial support made explicit. This is a design inference from the historical parties' legal-electoral participation and programme traditions, not a claim that such a Socialist-led national government occurred.

CGTA means the Confederación General del Trabajo de los Argentinos, a labor confederation that split from the CGT in March 1968 under Raimundo Ongaro, alongside the Vandor-led CGT Azopardo. It is a worker-organizing and strike-capacity actor, not a political party or a Socialist government. A CGTA route may influence, ally with, or challenge governments; its identity should not replace the party that holds government. This matters especially if both CGTA and socialist content are represented: keep their incumbent checks mutually exclusive and use the union as a constituency or mobilization layer. [CEHTI's CGTA archive and historical note](https://cehti.org/cgt-de-los-argentinos/); [Argentine government overview of the CGT's union-centre history](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/capital-humano/cultura/monumentos/confederacion-general-del-trabajo).

Likewise, Montoneros and PRT-ERP are armed political organizations, not ordinary legal parties. A five-focus Montoneros concept should be a conditional insurgent/movement route linked to the Peronist left and armed-conflict escalation, never a routine incumbent label. A 19-focus PRT/ERP path is a much larger counterfactual divergence: distinguish PRT political organization from ERP armed wing, and make armed escalation costly and contested. If multiplayer play requires these as selectable governments, disclose that this is an alternate-history victory path. Avoid adding a Socialist programme by relabeling either guerrilla path. [UNLP-hosted study of PRT, electoral participation and the armed strategy](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/71/98/58/2020719858.pdf); [UNLP study of the armed organizations and 1970-73 political crisis](https://www.sociohistorica.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/article/download/SHe049/9425).

The wider registry also needs a one-line chronological guard: Menem's market turn and UCeDe alliance belong to 1989; UCeDe was founded in 1982. A PCI/UCeDe political-tradition continuation can be a clearly marked provisional design shorthand, but those exact party identities must not be collapsed or projected backward into 1970. The conservative-democratic Partido Demócrata, SRA-linked agrarian leadership, Catholic-democratic groups and UDELPA can support small coalition governments or institutional leverage; their independent national incumbency is a multiplayer counterfactual. The Socialist precedent is *La Hora del Pueblo*: parties including Peronists, Radicals and minor parties jointly demanded elections without exclusions on 11 November 1970, demonstrating cross-party constitutional cooperation without implying socialist electoral dominance. [O'Donnell on the coalition and its democratic commitments](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/bureaucratic-authoritarianism-argentina-1966-1973-in-comparative-perspective.html).
