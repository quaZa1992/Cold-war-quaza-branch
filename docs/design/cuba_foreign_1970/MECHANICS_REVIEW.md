# IMPERIA COLD WAR: Cuba Foreign Policy Concept Mechanics Review

Read-only review of the 25 foreign-policy focus proposals in `FOREIGN_POLICY_CONCEPT.json` and their source builder. This evaluates mechanic fit and proposed balance values. It does not claim that the concept values are implemented in runtime.

## Resolved concept findings and remaining implementation requirements

The regenerated concept now corrects Angola to native tag `AGL`; keeps Algeria at `ALG`; targets state 855 as Camagüey with a structured accepted effect for one shared building slot and one civilian industry; restores the one-use 25% `CAT_industry` reward to Division of Labour; and makes CMEA admission a membership credential with a named Soviet-side Cuban Coordination Commission spirit. The Soviet-side admission spirit proposes +2% foreign influence and +1% bureaucracy cost. These close the earlier data inconsistencies.

The volunteer focus no longer grants a redundant “deployment permitted” reward. Instead, it explicitly records that `send_volunteer_divisions_required` and `send_volunteers_tension` need a new Cuban modifier-provider integration. That is the right concept-stage statement: the modifier keys are supported by MD, but the current Cuban dynamic modifier reads only `send_volunteer_size`. The seven-division minimum is defined in `common/defines/MD_defines.lua:31`; -25% gives a nominal 5.25-division threshold before engine rounding, and -5% is a five-point lower tension threshold. The existing +2 expeditionary and +1 Guevarist volunteer-size contributions are in `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_effects.txt:1410,1790`. Integration must preserve these contributions and ensure the replacement branch does not double-count the old Expeditionary Forces and Mission Schools rewards.

The remaining design risk is the continuing military and training burden. Overseas Service adds +4% army personnel expense. Advisers bring the total to +9%; Expeditionary Brigades bring it to +16%, plus +5% supply use and +5% fuel use. Their one-time treasury costs ($0.20 billion for Overseas Service, then $0.15 billion for Advisers or $0.35 billion for Expeditionary Brigades) are separate from those ongoing costs. Industrial Credits adds +5% Cuban and +3% Soviet education costs. The concept descriptions identify training, supply and imported-parts obligations, so these values are legible. Keep them as recurring modifiers in any eventual implementation and tooltip.

## Stacking and small-state balance

The bilateral spirits are indefinite and independently suspend during war or mutual sanctions. Therefore their values stack across partners; they do not replace one another outside the paired alternatives. A high-end Cuban diplomatic stack can reach -43% foreign-influence defense if Cuba signs Soviet assistance and commodity terms, Mexico fuel, Spanish nickel, Canadian rail, US Interests plus Claims Settlement, and DDR island access. This is before other Cuban tree values. That weakens resistance to external influence substantially and deserves an explicit balance decision. The strongest positive trade stack is also broad: +30% resource-export multiplier with Soviet commodity, Spanish nickel, Canadian fisheries and US limited trade. These modifiers represent general income and defense, not actual bilateral volumes.

The overseas branch has a separate and potentially important cumulative bill. Adviser route: +1 volunteer size, +3% organization, +9% army personnel expense when including Overseas Service. Expeditionary route: +2 size, +6% organization, +3% special-forces cap, +16% personnel expense, +5% supply use and +5% fuel use. Because CUB starts with no fielded divisions, volunteer capacity alone creates no units and no immediate deployment. The proposal correctly avoids workforce bonuses and should continue to require actual divisions and recipient consent.

Most counterpart spirits remain net positive, with bureaucracy or education cost as their principal offset. This is acceptable for a small-country consent deal when the one-time partner contribution is charged only on first acceptance, but it can make long-running agreements a broad positive stack for every signatory. Keep one-time costs distinct from recurring modifier costs in the concept and tooltips. Do not model oil, railcars, fishing catch, nickel shipments or factories as automatic cash transfers.

The concept's partner-side reopening after refusal is feasible only as an explicit recipient-owned diplomacy action. A completed Cuban focus cannot be selected again to replay its offer. Refusal should leave the focus receipt intact, cost nothing, and allow the recipient to reopen only the exact Cuban proposal for a new consent window.
