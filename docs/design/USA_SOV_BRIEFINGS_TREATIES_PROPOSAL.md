IMPERIA COLD WAR: Rival Briefings and Treaty Creation
==================================================

Status: visual and design proposal only. User approval is reserved. This document and the separate interactive concept change no game scripts, generators, ideas, events or localisation. The earlier approved dossier reference is preserved. The current request adds participant spirits, withdrawal, international news and optional additional signatories to the proposed agreement system.

## What the player sees

USA receives a CIA executive file addressed to the President, titled **Soviet Affairs**. SOV receives a KGB registry file addressed to the General Secretary, titled **American Affairs**. Each uses its own seal, watermark, classification and folder. No date is shown anywhere in the file. Internal scheduling and saved transaction dates remain implementation details.

Three sections:

1. **Soviet activities / American activities**: only activities whose provider is the rival government. Each report says what it is doing, where, which evidence establishes it, and how it intersects with our actual commitments. The service's political assessment is separate from facts.
2. **Détente & agreements**: choose a treaty subject, inspect its proposed spirit and complete obligations, prepare a negotiating text, invite additional governments where appropriate, review the full effects and submit for the counterpart's signature.
3. **Treaties & commitments**: our participation, our treaty spirits, outstanding implementation and withdrawal. Our own support records are also here, rather than mixed into the rival briefing.

The catalogue uses **Arms control**, **Economic** and **Regional**. Historical outcomes, labels such as “historical agreement”, “later frustrated”, “never ratified” and predictions of failure do not appear in normal gameplay. Those facts belong only in this research appendix. The players decide the outcome of their campaign. SALT II and the Reykjavík framework are available as substantive negotiating choices without a scripted failure.

The concept's campaign records are fixtures, not live intelligence or a claim about the actual January 1970 situation. There are no forced historical incidents. Full country names and parties resolve from the current game. The sample Arab-Israeli conflict is an illustrative campaign war, not an automatic scenario event.

## Briefing rules

A lend-lease connection establishes a relationship, not delivered quantities, successful arrival or equipment composition. A volunteer record establishes a deployment, not division positions or casualties. An attaché is distinct from combat forces. An investment identifies its real project partner; a generic aggregate investment variable cannot identify its destination. Party backing uses the actual supported native party slot. The file must not infer covert payments from a popularity change.

For example, the President's brief can say Moscow maintains lend-lease to the United Arab Republic, which is at war with Israel, and that Israel also receives American lend-lease only if all those current facts have been verified. The General Secretary sees the corresponding American activity. Neither receives a list of its own sanctions as purported rival activity.

The report can identify an opposing recipient for a response only from the current campaign's real war and own support links. No hardcoded assumption that Egypt must fight Israel or Hanoi must fight Saigon survives a change in the campaign. Multiple possible response recipients require an explicit choice. The original recipient remains a valid destination for competing investment or aid.

Keep the already approved financial-access policy: 60% civilian intelligence on the provider or 50% network strength in the recipient. Public covers identify partners. Unknown sensitive fields are withheld; wholly undiscovered payments are absent before row counts and pagination. Old undiscovered payments do not become retroactively known after gaining access. Previously discovered correspondence remains in the record. No separate agency-strength statistic is introduced.

## Treaty creation and consent

The treaty's proposed text, participant list, role of each participant, full prospective effects, declared force basis and implementation requirements form one versioned instrument. USA and SOV are the core negotiating parties for this menu. Selecting a template or reviewing effects does not sign it.

The sender submits one exact revision. The counterpart may sign, decline or amend and return a counteroffer. An amendment invalidates signatures and invitation acceptances for the older terms. Neither silence nor opening the event counts as approval. Once the necessary core signatures exist, the agreement is concluded, the appropriate spirit is added once to each joining signatory, and one international signing announcement is issued.

Use a single pending negotiating instrument for the pair. This is a proposal simplification, not a new diplomatic currency or a fee. Existing native trade and investment offers retain their real authoritative consent and payment procedure. A generic treaty editor must not add a second acceptance fee or execute the same economic benefit twice. These templates wrap or adopt the canonical accepted provider; they do not mint a parallel trade agreement.

The complete acceptance page shows each party's exact spirit, native numeric effects, costs and actual obligations before signature. A named spirit alone is insufficient. Numerical force ceilings and their units must be explicit once actual accounting has been established. A declared current-capacity freeze must capture the real agreed baseline, not a mutable GUI variable. Narrative background stays outside the Effects field.

### Additional countries

The menu remains USA/SOV-only. Additional countries receive scoped country events or decisions to inspect, accept or decline the offered terms. They need no CIA/KGB menu. Their own withdrawal remains available through a normal treaty decision.

The core accord may be concluded while optional invitations remain unanswered or are declined. An invitee receives no spirit, effects, constraints or consent receipt until it has agreed and the underlying accord has been concluded. Acceptance before the core agreement waits for conclusion. Acceptance afterward is accession, with its own spirit grant and international announcement. Unanswered invitations remain attached to their agreement, even when a different negotiating paper is opened.

Each invitation specifies that country's real role and prospective effects. An accession to arms limits applies to the joining country's own covered forces. A regional invitation must name the invitee's own contribution or an explicit consultation/guarantor role; it cannot give France control of Soviet lend-lease or award a misleading copy of an American obligation. The concept demonstrates invitation consent with an arms-control text. The generic additional-party regional clause editor remains an implementation detail to resolve before shipping that invitation type.

If a clause requires a third country's own action, its consent is necessary for that clause. The core governments can instead remove that clause and submit a narrower revision. They cannot sign away another player's forces, territory, payments or war. Ending American/Soviet support does not itself require the supported country to sign, but ending that country's war would.

## Spirits and historical effects

Each concluded treaty has a named removable national spirit for each actual participant. The spirit is the active policy handle for its exact clauses. Effects may be supported native modifiers, existing bilateral economic effects or checks on the actual actions and assets covered by the agreement. They are not a fabricated trust meter, tension resource or automatic stability reward.

A spirit must do what its displayed effects say. A descriptive SALT spirit alone does not limit a launcher; an INF icon does not destroy missiles. Where a hard restriction cannot be implemented safely through the real provider, describe an observable obligation and record a substantiated breach instead of claiming the action has been disabled. Explicit RP-only consultation clauses must remain distinguishable from automatically verified reductions.

| Treaty spirit | Proposed concrete representation | Required integration boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SALT I Treaty | Separate agreed fixed-ICBM and SLBM launcher limits; actual expansion/replacement observance. | Authoritative launcher categories and deployment/construction accounting. It does not freeze warhead numbers. |
| ABM Treaty | Agreed strategic defensive areas and strategic interceptor limits. | A distinct strategic ABM representation must exist. Ordinary SAM sites cannot be restricted as a replacement. |
| SALT II Treaty | Strategic delivery-system ceiling and agreed sublimits, including MIRVed systems. | A common authoritative delivery-system and warhead-attribution mapping. |
| INF Treaty | Zero target for covered ground-launched ballistic/cruise missiles and their launchers; recorded elimination and non-reintroduction obligations. | Ground-launched 500-5,500 km classes, including conventional versions, reconciled across real stocks and deployed forces. |
| START I Treaty | Agreed phased reduction of accountable delivery systems and warheads through actual retirement. | Retirement must remove the real object without also counting its deployed copy or treating a launch/export as destruction. |
| Reykjavík Framework | Separately selected initial reductions, later elimination scope and strategic-defence terms. | Do not conflate eliminating ballistic missiles with eliminating every nuclear weapon. Each selected clause needs its own real provider or explicit RP verification. |
| Nuclear Risk Reduction Accord | Standing urgent-consultation access and a recorded restraint undertaking. | A consultation procedure, not automatic prevention of nuclear use or a fictitious combat modifier. |
| Bilateral Commercial Accord | Existing +100 trade opinion and +25 relations in each approved bilateral relationship, native influence calculation and conditional native embargo relief. | Existing trade provider is authoritative. Normal proposal and cancellation cost: 75 PP. Do not remove all Imperia sanctions implicitly. |
| Mutual Investment Accord | Existing +30 relations and +30 trade opinion in each approved bilateral relationship, native influence and reciprocal permanent investment targets. | Normal 75 PP proposal/cancellation. Preserve applicable native project cost/duration calculations; no free construction. |
| Regional Restraint Accord | Named own lend-lease withdrawals, and optional non-resumption. Other variants can specify real volunteer/attaché recalls. | Existing actual connections. Volunteer recall retains its game rule and 50 PP/15 CP native cost. Signing does not recall another player's troops. |
| Reciprocal Restraint Accord | A named sanction-class relief exchanged for a named own support withdrawal. | Native sanction relief: 35 PP per class. Verify each obligation separately. |

These are proposed effects and implementation requirements, not verified running features. Supported country modifiers exist, including nuclear production and military-spending factors, but their existence does not justify assigning arbitrary percentages to every treaty. In particular, a blanket nuclear-production penalty would misrepresent SALT I; an unconditional budget discount would not establish actual INF or START reductions. The existing Soviet focus spirits with generic spending reductions are not proof of actual force limits and are not copied into this system.

The current repository contains legacy missile arrays, native missile equipment, deployments and uranium/nuclear infrastructure. A common loaded-arsenal mapping and safe retirement path have not been established by this design exercise. Detailed enforceable SALT/INF/START/ABM effects are conditional on that work. The visual demonstrates the target signing/withdrawal lifecycle; it is not evidence that these adapters already exist. Where the final game cannot represent a clause, narrow the treaty text or mark that particular commitment as RP verified. Do not pretend it has an automatic effect.

Economic effects remain bilateral. Additional signatories do not grant opinion, trade access or resource flows with non-members automatically. Each actual new economic link requires its canonical consent and any real provider cost. The spirit identifies the approved treaty; economic effects are applied once through their real owner and are removed through that same owner.

## Withdrawal and news

Each participant has an explicit **Withdraw from treaty** action. Its review shows the exact spirit and ongoing effects removed, affected bilateral benefits and any real native cancellation cost. Confirmation rechecks membership and current agreement identity, removes that country's membership and spirit once, clears its ongoing treaty effects/obligations and publishes one international withdrawal announcement. No new exit fee or cooldown is proposed beyond actual existing provider costs.

Other participants do not have their spirits removed merely because someone else acts. Benefits with the departing country end. Remaining participants can continue their own obligations, amend the arrangement by consent or leave themselves. If only one signatory remains, display **Unilateral observance**, not a bilateral treaty falsely shown in force. No one is trapped: the remaining government has the same withdrawal action.

Withdrawal does not restore already dismantled weapons, recover completed payments, demolish completed construction or return spent political power. Those were real actions with real consequences. Signing again must not repeat rewards, repay funds or duplicate the same native agreement. Re-accession needs a fresh approval for the current instrument; an old invitation or old signature is insufficient.

International news covers conclusion, additional accession and actual withdrawal. Drafting, declining a proposal and opening an invitation do not announce a signed treaty. News text names the actual participants and departing country, without predetermined historical outcomes. One transition creates one news event for the world; it must not broadcast once per observer or once per signatory. News presentation must use the live native news donor, with a separate country-event donor for invitation/acceptance where applicable. The browser's bulletin is a concept clipping, not a native event-frame verification.

## Performance and RP multiplayer contract

Keep one authoritative agreement record with stable ID, revision, participant roles, signatures, active memberships, exact clauses and one-time notification receipts. A small fixed catalogue and bounded participant/active-agreement slots keep validation work finite. Proposed upper bounds: 8 participants and 6 clauses per instrument; one active instance of a template, with bounded correspondence/archive history. The limits and their UI treatment remain design choices, not measured performance claims.

Signature, accession and withdrawal are event-driven. Recheck the actual acting country, current revision, target existence, participant role, native requirements and execution receipt in the authoritative effect. Never use the currently selected report, a local country picker or a global delayed scratch packet as payment/signature authority. All participants must consent to the same exact text. Save all relevant pending and committed state for reload/rehost.

The existing observation cache retains its single weekly pass for the pair. Read report text from that disclosed cache. Selected-report context may examine the already cached own partner list on selection, rather than introduce a pairwise world sweep. No new per-frame country, state, fleet or division scans; no random discovery rolls. Treaty presence/sanction checks can be bounded by registered clauses and participants. Reuse valid existing aggregates/hooks for strategic forces once mapped, rather than scan the world's deployed forces for every treaty or GUI refresh.

GUI paging, hover, wording, preview perspective switches and invitation previews are display-only. Co-op players share country authority, so send/accept/withdraw must be safe against stale, repeated and simultaneous input. Two clients cannot both claim the same signature or charge the same concession. A participant's own withdrawal cannot silently withdraw another player. No player receives another agency's controls by changing a local display selection.

This is a synchronization-conscious design, not a desync guarantee. Before implementation can be called MP-ready, exercise USA/SOV and a consenting third player, same-country co-op, simultaneous signatures/withdrawals, old-revision responses, invitation refusal and late accession, already changed support, missing/annexed recipients, native economic affordability, save/load and rehost. Browser interaction checks establish only that the visual behaves as described.

## Research record, outside normal gameplay

Historical agreements and proposals supply the subject matter, not a predetermined campaign ending:

- [Office of the Historian: SALT I and II](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/salt): SALT I and ABM were signed in 1972. SALT II was signed in 1979, never ratified by the US and subsequently observed voluntarily for a period. This supports separate negotiation, signature, entry/status and observance rather than an inevitable failed path.
- [US Department of State: ABM Protocol](https://1997-2001.state.gov/global/arms/treaties/abm/abmprot1.html): the 1974 protocol reduced the original two defensive deployment areas to one. This supports selectable explicit ABM terms, not a generic SAM penalty.
- [US Department of State: INF text and narrative](https://2009-2017.state.gov/t/avc/trty/102360.htm): elimination of covered ground-launched 500-5,500 km missiles, launchers and associated equipment. This supports scope by actual weapon type and real elimination.
- [US Department of State: START central limits](https://1997-2001.state.gov/global/arms/factsheets/wmd/nuclear/start1/startcen.html): 1,600 accountable delivery vehicles and 6,000 accountable warheads with sublimits and phased reductions. The historical values are not automatically correct game balance.
- [FRUS: report prepared for Reagan at Reykjavík](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v05/d305) and [FRUS: NSDD 250](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1981-88v44p1/d152): substantial common ground and unresolved linkage to strategic defence, with differing elimination proposals. Basis for a negotiable alternative outcome, not “a completed zero-nuclear treaty”.
- [FRUS: the commercial opening and Jackson-Vanik dispute](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v38p1/d31): the trade opening was later rejected amid trade/emigration conditions. The gameplay Commercial Accord supplies usable native commercial choices without putting that outcome into its label or description.
- [FRUS: Prevention of Nuclear War agreement](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v15/d122): restraint and urgent consultation. It was not a general no-first-use guarantee. The proposed consultation accord preserves that distinction.

## IMPERIA COLD WAR: subsequent approved implementation

The preceding sections preserve the approved proposal and its verification scope. The subsequent user instruction authorized implementation, configurable treaty scopes and variants, real concessions, optional participants and individual withdrawal. The native implementation is now owned by tools/superpower_dossier/build.py and its treaty modules. Its current behaviour and limitations are documented in tools/superpower_dossier/README.md. The JSON reports in that directory distinguish source tests, native execution, visual compositions and multiplayer work still outstanding.

The implementation uses eleven fixed negotiating templates and explicit protocols. Reserve retirement removes selected actual stored missile models once; it does not claim historical launcher/warhead accounting or deployed-triad coverage. Production restraint uses the existing nuclear/thermonuclear production factors. The default ABM template is consultation because no distinct strategic ABM category was established. Ordinary SAMs are not substituted. Historical SALT/INF/START names therefore identify the negotiating subject; their actual selectable game obligations are displayed explicitly.

Trade and investment variants use actual bilateral opinion and native investment cost/duration calculations. The -5% investment precedent is the existing MD mutual investment provider; -10% is an explicit stronger negotiable game variant. The 10/25/50% production and 10/25/50/100% reserve choices are transparent gameplay scales, not researched historical percentages. No automatic weekly money subsidy, stability reward or invented agency statistic is attached to a treaty name.

Selected Imperia sanctions are lifted between the core countries using their original native bodies and 35 PP per existing class. Selected classes receive a bilateral moratorium. National embargoes remain separate. Regional terms name each core government's own attaché/volunteer recipient, retaining native recall conditions and costs. Optional countries consent only to their own applicable reserve/production/economic participation; they do not control another country's missions or war.

The existing Cold War tension system receives a reversible, capped participation adjustment. This represents standing jointly accepted restraint, not comprehensive automatic verification of every historical obligation. Supported governments and whole alliances are never bound without their own participation. International news reports the actual signing, joining or departing country, without predetermined historical outcomes.

The immutable approved browser artifacts remain unchanged. Current native asset compositions are separate verification artifacts and must not be described as live game screenshots. No multiplayer/desync guarantee follows from either those compositions or a single-client native probe.
