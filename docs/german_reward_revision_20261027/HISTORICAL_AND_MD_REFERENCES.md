IMPERIA COLD WAR

# BRD and DDR reward revision: historical and MD references

Research note for the 1970 Cold War roleplay trees. Historical claims are separated from proposed gameplay readings. The numbers below are verified examples from the installed main Millennium Dawn tree, not historically measured modifiers. They are scale and presentation references only.

## BRD: historically grounded alternatives

| Policy area | Historical precedent (fact) | Reward and trade-off reading (game-balance inference) |
|---|---|---|
| Social market and reform | The 1969 SPD-FDP government announced a reform program. It expanded educational and social opportunity, passed BAföG in 1971, supported university expansion, and broadened worker participation through the Works Constitution Act passed by the Bundestag on 10 November 1971, dated 15 January 1972 and published on 18 January 1972. The 1976 Co-determination Act was passed on 18 March and dated 4 May 1976. The earlier Montan co-determination framework dates to 1951, not 1971. The social-liberal coalition did not obtain every intended reform: federal-state authority, opposition and constitutional review constrained university reform, and parity co-determination was not achieved under the 1976 law. [Federal Law Gazette: Works Constitution Act (1972)](https://www.bgbl.de/xaver/bgbl/start.xav?start=%2F%2F%2A%5B%40attr_id%3D%27bgbl172s0013.pdf%27%5D); [Bundestag: 1976 Co-determination Act](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/kw10-kalenderblatt-mitbestimmungsgesetz-414734); [Bundestag: Montan co-determination (1951)](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/1951-04-10-montanmitbestimmung-205146); [LeMO: Social-liberal reforms](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/geteiltes-deutschland-modernisierung/bundesrepublik-im-wandel/sozialliberale-reformen.html) | A reform path can reward human-capital/research development and labor legitimacy while imposing fiscal or bargaining friction. A business-led alternative could emphasize investment and productivity with weaker labor approval or greater distributional tension. Do not make either path a free, universally better bonus. |
| Concerted Action and labor relations | Karl Schiller convened employers, unions, the Council of Economic Experts and government in 1967 to coordinate against recession. Under the Stability and Growth Act, participants exchanged indicative economic and income targets; these were coordination goals, not binding wage controls. [LeMO: 1967 chronology](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/jahreschronik/1967.html); [Bundesbank Annual Report 1967](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/690128/b256eb87a10c257f96cc8175f25a6805/mL/1967-geschaeftsbericht-data.pdf) | Productivity, stability or labor-business confidence can represent negotiated coordination; a weaker treasury/budget position or a risk of labor conflict can model the limits of consensus. A strike or failed concertation should be a credible alternative outcome, not a required historical event in 1970. |
| Energy: coal, oil and nuclear | Before the 1973 oil shock, imported petroleum had become important. The December 1973 European response called for domestic energy production, nuclear power, coal use and coal research to substitute for Arab oil. The 1974 federal energy program funded coal gasification/liquefaction, mining technology and energy efficiency; it also committed to domestic hard-coal generation. [Federal Cabinet Protocols, 1973](https://kabinettsprotokolle.bundesarchiv.de/resources/pdf/f8408ce9-79de-4c02-a82e-ac235671e1b4.pdf); [Cabinet Protocols, 1974](https://kabinettsprotokolle.bundesarchiv.de/resources/pdf/d0bd2649-4f29-4d07-9c2f-f682ffc01bc8.pdf) | In a 1970 start, later oil-shock policy is a plausible forward branch, not a completed 1970 fact. Import security can be traded against high capital cost, environmental impact and public opposition. Coal and nuclear should have different trade-offs; efficiency and diversified supply can be a slower, lower-pollution alternative. |
| Universities and electronics | West Germany faced overcrowded and under-equipped universities and a qualified-worker shortage concern. The Deutscher Bildungsrat began work in 1965; 1970s university and comprehensive-institution expansion proceeded, but federal jurisdiction and political/legal resistance limited structural reform. [LeMO: Educational crisis](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/geteiltes-deutschland-modernisierung/bundesrepublik-im-wandel/bildungsnotstand.html); [LeMO: Social-liberal reforms](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/geteiltes-deutschland-modernisierung/bundesrepublik-im-wandel/sozialliberale-reformen.html) | Research slots, limited-use electronics bonuses or offices can stand for education/research investment. A credible downside is delayed returns, public cost, or intergovernmental friction. Avoid claiming a single new university automatically produces a specific quantity of electronics output. |
| Bundeswehr armor, artillery, air and naval procurement | The Leopard 1 was the first post-war tank conceived and produced in West Germany; 1,845 were built for the Bundeswehr by February 1970. The Marder entered service in 1971. The F-4 Phantom entered Luftwaffe service from 1974. The Bundeswehr was adjusting to NATO's flexible-response strategy; material, personnel, finance and infrastructure remained constraints. [Bundeswehr: Leopard 1 chronology](https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/ausruestung-technik-bundeswehr/kampfpanzer-leopard-versionen-bundeswehr); [Bundeswehr: Marder](https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/meldungen/bundeswehrgeraet-tiernamen-5321614); [Bundeswehr: Luftwaffe history](https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/selbstverstaendnis/geschichte-bundeswehr/geschichte-luftwaffe); [Bundeswehr: Army history](https://www.bundeswehr.de/de/selbstverstaendnis/geschichte-bundeswehr/geschichte-heer) | Armor/mechanized readiness, artillery support, aircraft research and NATO interoperability are grounded reward themes. Large procurement should carry treasury/weekly maintenance, import dependence or opportunity cost against civilian investment. Use Marder and later F-4 as dated follow-through, not as already fielded at the January 1970 start. |

## DDR: historically grounded alternatives

| Policy area | Historical precedent (fact) | Reward and trade-off reading (game-balance inference) |
|---|---|---|
| Ulbricht's NÖS versus Honecker's Unity policy | The New Economic System introduced decentralization and profitability principles from July 1963. It brought economic successes and improved material living conditions, but supply shortages persisted and the 1966-70 perspective plan was missed. Honecker's 1971 Unity of Economic and Social Policy prioritized housing, wages, pensions, consumer supply and family support. [LeMO: Economic reforms](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/geteiltes-deutschland-modernisierung/reformversuche-im-osten/wirtschaftsreformen.html); [LeMO: NÖSPL directive record](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/bestand/objekt/druckgut_richtlinie-noespl.html); [bpb: Economic and social policy](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/ddr-kompakt/521894/wirtschafts-und-sozialpolitik/) | NÖS can grant productivity/efficiency or targeted industrial modernization while risking supply imbalance and tension with central planning. Unity policy can improve housing, welfare and stability, with the documented opportunity cost of weaker industrial R&D investment and debt pressure. Do not collapse the two approaches into one generic production bonus. |
| Housing, consumer goods, debt and exports | The 1971 program's housing and social package improved living standards, especially in the first half of the 1970s. It was financed in part through growing western debt and competed with productive investment. Export plans missed targets and the state faced continuing consumer shortages. [bpb: Economic and social policy](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-teilung/ddr-kompakt/521894/wirtschafts-und-sozialpolitik/); [bpb: DDR development to late 1980s](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/deutsche-teilung-deutsche-einheit/43675/entwicklung-in-der-ddr-bis-ende-der-80er-jahre/?p=all) | Housing levels and consumer supply can be substantial benefits. Pair them with a meaningful debt/treasury burden, reduced research investment or export capacity. Financing via exports should be a costly, uncertain route rather than an automatic cure. |
| Robotron and microelectronics | The DDR began a major microelectronics program in 1977 after earlier electronics R&D work had been cut back. The cited total program cost is 50 billion Mark. By the mid-1980s a domestically made 256 kB chip was listed at 534 Mark versus 4-5 Deutsche Mark on the world market, with 517 Mark of state subsidy per chip. These are later retrospective figures, not January 1970 conditions. [bpb: DDR economy figures](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/lange-wege-der-deutschen-einheit/47076/die-wirtschaft-in-der-ddr/?k=0&show=image&type=galerie); [bpb, APuZ: Microelectronics in the planned economy](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/apuz/archiv/538226/hochtechnologien-in-der-zentralplanwirtschaft-der-ddr-zum-dilemma-der-mikroelektronik-in-der-ddr-in-den-achtziger-jahren/) | Robotron/microelectronics can provide a major research or computing payoff if sustained, but should consume scarce investment and risk technology lag or subsidy cost. Any 1970 branch that chooses it is alternate-history acceleration; do not use the 1977 program's actual costs as 1970 opening data. |
| Lignite, oil and pollution | The DDR had limited access to imported fuels and relied heavily on domestic lignite. Following Soviet oil supply pressure, plants shifted from oil toward lignite. Lignite secured domestic energy supply but caused severe air pollution, water contamination and landscape damage. [Bundesarchiv: DDR and FRG environmental policy](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/themenbeitraege/umweltpolitik-in-der-ddr-und-der-bundesrepublik/); [bpb: Energy system](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/lange-wege-der-deutschen-einheit/504994/energiesystem-und-energiewende/) | Lignite is a defensible reliability/industrial-power choice with environmental and health costs. Oil imports or pipeline deals reduce some local pollution but create foreign-supply and hard-currency exposure. Nuclear is capital-intensive and not a cost-free escape route. |
| Agriculture and LPG | By 1960 the last independent family farms had joined collective production. LPG allowed pooled land, machinery and scale; land formally remained with the farmers who contributed it. Later assessments identify underinvestment, low productivity and environmental damage as recurring problems. [bpb: Agriculture in East Germany](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutsche-einheit/lange-wege-der-deutschen-einheit/47157/landwirtschaft-in-ostdeutschland-der-spaete-erfolg-der-ddr/); [bpb: LPG types](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/hintergrund-aktuell/292011/vor-60-jahren-gesetz-ueber-die-landwirtschaftlichen-produktionsgenossenschaften-in-der-ddr/) | Collective scale can support machinery, agricultural output and food supply; a trade-off can be low investment/productivity, environmental harm or rural political costs. Some capacity to coordinate is real, but avoid treating collectivization as an unconditional production increase. |
| NVA procurement and domestic production | The NVA was designed and equipped as a Warsaw Pact coalition force, predominantly with Soviet weapons and equipment. The DDR had a limited armaments industry, including selected electronics, vehicles, shipbuilding, small arms and repair capacity; it did not have broad autonomous production of major systems. [LeMO: National People's Army](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/geteiltes-deutschland-gruenderjahre/weg-nach-osten/nationale-volksarmee.html); [bpb: NVA](https://www.bpb.de/themen/militaer/deutsche-verteidigungspolitik/223787/die-nationale-volksarmee-der-ddr/) | Soviet deliveries/licensing can deliver combat power sooner and preserve Warsaw Pact compatibility, at the cost of dependence and hard currency/transfer constraints. Domestic production/repair can improve support capacity and technical skills, but should be limited to plausible sectors and absorb investment. |

## Installed main MD mechanics examples

Source files are from the installed Workshop tree `E:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/workshop/content/394360/2777392649/common/national_focus/`. The installed game file `E:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/Hearts of Iron IV/common/defines/00_defines.lua:3613-3615` sets `NFocus.FOCUS_POINT_DAYS = 7` and comments that each point takes a week. The project generator independently documents this in `tools/west_germany_1970/tree.py:284-287`. Thus duration is focus cost multiplied by 7 days: 4.3 = 30.1 theoretical days (about 30), 6 = 42, 6.5 = 45.5 theoretical days, 7 = 49 and 10 = 70. Whether fractional-day results are rounded up, down or handled internally has not been verified, so 4.3 and 6.5 remain approximate. Exact snippets are paraphrased below to keep this note compact.

| Focus ID | Cost, duration, gates | Concrete reward pattern |
|---|---|---|
| `GER_expand_mainstream_industry` (`05_germany.txt:498`) | 4.3 / about 30 days; requires `GER_deutschland_industrie` | Mixed industrial program: effect previews offer two civilian factories, two arms factories, two offices, two dockyards or two agriculture districts, with internal sector opinion changes; treasury charge is 26 for the office option. Useful example of a larger selectable package. |
| `GER_education_investments` (`05_germany.txt:5807`) | 6.5 / about 45.5 theoretical days; requires `GER_International_business` | Raises education spending if below `edu_05`; otherwise lowers education cost multiplier by 5%. Illustrates upgrade-aware rewards. |
| `GER_army_defence_exercise` (`05_germany.txt:7409`) | 6 / 42 days; requires `GER_Rapid_Response_supply` | +5% army defense, -5% attrition, +5% land reinforce rate. Training produces several distinct military outcomes. |
| `GER_Armored_Delay` (`05_germany.txt:7440`) | 6 / 42 days; requires both reserve reorganization and active-troop reorganization | +8% armor defense, +7% armor speed, -4% attrition. Example of a substantial combined-arms reward after multiple institutional prerequisites. |
| `GER_economic_growth` (`05_germany.txt:15405`) | 4.3 / about 30 days; requires three economic focuses and Social Democrats in power | Calls the economic-growth improvement effect. Strong party-gated economic result, but the named helper must be traced before copying values. |
| `GER_bundeswehr_reforms` (`05_germany.txt:21155`) | 6.5 / about 45.5 theoretical days; requires `GER_the_glorious_past`; nationalist monarchists in power | Raises military spending; when military-spending idea `defence_09` is already present, also reduces personnel cost multiplier 4%. Upgrade-aware party policy example. |
| `SOV_gen_3_nuclear_plants` (`05_russia.txt:480`) | 7 / 49 days; requires `SOV_empower_siloviki`; emerging reactionaries in power; blocked during bankruptcy mission | One random nuclear reactor and one 50% nuclear-reactor technology bonus. Example pairing a tangible asset with limited-use research and affordability gate. |
| `GENERIC_self_propelled_artillery` (`00_generic.txt:6312`) | 10 / 70 days; requires land-doctrine expertise and artillery experiments; requires more than 4 military factories | Two 50% self-propelled-artillery technology bonuses. `GENERIC_self_propelled_anti_air` (line 6351) follows at 10 / 70 days for two 50% anti-air bonuses. This demonstrates long, multi-step equipment research. |

Main MD examples demonstrate mechanics and presentation scale. They do not establish appropriate 1970 historical magnitudes. Adapt the type and cost of rewards to each country's institutions, actual capacity and route opportunity cost.
