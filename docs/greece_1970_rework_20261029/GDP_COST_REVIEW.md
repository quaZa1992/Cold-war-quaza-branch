# Greece programme costs: source and balance review

## Proposed price basis

The requested increase is mechanically coherent as a first balance pass: multiply one-time Greek-authored GDP charges by 3 and weekly charges by 10. Apply each factor to the owning charge once, replace existing prices on reused Greek-Cyprus actions, and show the revised value in the focus, event and recipient previews. Do not add a second fee around an existing helper.

| Greek-Cyprus action | Current price source | Suggested replacement | Why this scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Officer network (`gre_network`) | GRE pays 0.25% of CYP GDP once, plus 50 PP | Recommended: 0.75% of current GRE GDP once, plus 50 PP | Makes the Greek covert operation a material Greek commitment rather than a small-Cyprus-economy charge. Replace the existing helper's 0.25% charge; do not stack another 0.75%. This is a deliberate GDP-basis change as well as a ×3 rate change. If only strict ×3 cash scaling is intended, use 0.75% of CYP GDP instead. |
| Coup attempt (`gre_coup`) | GRE pays 0.5% of CYP GDP once, plus 100 PP | Recommended: 1.5% of current GRE GDP once, plus 100 PP | The decisive, one-use escalation costs more than sponsorship and is borne by the state directing it. Replace the existing 0.5% charge. This is a deliberate GDP-basis change; strict ×3 cash scaling would be 1.5% of CYP GDP instead. |
| Direct intervention (`gre_escalate`) | GRE pays 0.5% of GRE GDP once; new expedition package is 0.005% Greek GDP per week for 180 days | Replace the entry charge with 1.5% GRE GDP once; price the 180-day package at 0.05% GRE GDP per week | At about 26 weeks, the package costs about 1.3% GDP, or roughly 2.8% including setup. It is a substantial but finite wartime commitment. Charge the package only after successful native entry. |
| Volunteer mission (`gre_volunteers`) | 50 PP and 0.002% Greek GDP per week while active | Retain 50 PP and charge 0.02% Greek GDP per week while the real mission remains active | This is 1.04% GDP on a 52-week annualized basis. There is no setup GDP payment in the current actor, so do not invent a second launch charge unless separately approved. |

The percentage-to-runtime conversion matters. In a runtime multiplier against `gdp_total`, 0.75% is `0.0075`, 1.5% is `0.015`, 0.02% per week is `0.0002`, 0.03% is `0.0003`, and 0.05% is `0.0005`. Existing crisis code uses `0.00005` for 0.005% and `0.00002` for 0.002%. All proposed literals fit the five-decimal limit. Keep design percentages and runtime fractions clearly distinguished.

## Wider authored tree and cumulative cost risk

The current Greek generator applies setup prices across the shared economic, defence and party programmes. A mechanical ×3 applied to every GDP setup price would make illustrative 12-focus economic-group total 10.75% to 32.25% of Greek GDP if every setup-bearing node were taken; the defence group is 8.75% to 26.25% on the same deliberately broad sum. These are group-wide upper totals, not a single path forecast: mutually exclusive focuses and prerequisites reduce reachable totals. Still, a uniform multiplier risks making complete branches unaffordable. Recompute totals over actually reachable paths before freezing every focus price; reserve 3x for high-capital commitments and use lower multipliers for short institutional reforms if the route sum is excessive.

For temporary programme spirits, apply the ×10 weekly rate only to active temporary benefits. Suggested anchors are 0.02% per week for a staffed training or volunteer programme, 0.03% for the 180-day Cyprus operational initiative, and 0.05% for the direct-entry expeditionary command. The latter two cost about 0.78% and 1.3% GDP, respectively, over 180 days at 26 weekly charges. State the duration and expected total in previews as well as the weekly rate. If a programme has no temporary spirit or active service period, do not attach a recurring expense merely because its focus was completed.

## Native money path and lifecycle

`common/scripted_effects/00_money_system.txt:3730-3734` resets `additional_expenses_rate` and aggregates special expenses. Its GDP-based examples include 0.1% GDP each week for large-company and farmer subsidies and 0.2% for defence-industry subsidies (`00_money_system.txt:3842-3855`); maritime industry subsidies also use 0.2% (`00_money_system.txt:4160-4165`), while the Good Friday agreement uses 0.03% (`00_money_system.txt:4350-4356`). The Cyprus R2 stream is included in this native expense accumulator (`00_money_system.txt:4636-4639`), then flows into `display_expense` and `treasury_rate` (`00_money_system.txt:4756-4828`). These are weekly MD money units, not monthly budget rates.

The relevant stream computation runs in the native money calculation on country scope: `COLDWAR_CYP_R2_calculate_weekly_streams` is called from both the additional-income and additional-expense aggregation blocks (`00_money_system.txt:2546-2547,4636-4638`). The wiring recomputes the stream totals before summing them (`common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CYP_R2_wiring.txt:3-10`). A cost attached to a Greek temporary spirit therefore belongs in GRE's local money calculation, not in a monthly relationship pass. Each weekly calculation should reset its contribution to zero, then add it only when the local country has the exact temporary spirit and the unique active transaction/episode receipt. Expiration of the timed spirit must stop the charge at the next native money calculation. Do not depend only on a monthly cached amount or scan other countries from the weekly aggregator. Keep targeted monthly reconciliation for stale receipts and cleanup.

The present crisis stream instead gates Turkish intervention with `COLDWAR_CYP_R2_intervention_expense_cached` and volunteers with `COLDWAR_CYP_R2_volunteer_expense_active` (`common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CYP_R2_crisis.txt:692-704`). Those flags are managed in reconciliation; the volunteer branch checks the idea, actual deployed volunteer presence and war conditions there (`COLDWAR_CYP_R2_crisis.txt:600-632`). Preserve this targeted reconciliation, but have the weekly amount itself recheck the local mission idea and the active episode receipt. Expiry or an ended mission must not be billed through a stale flag.

The current Greek network and coup helpers calculate the budget from `CYP.gdp_total` and charge GRE via `modify_treasury_effect` in GRE scope (`COLDWAR_CYP_R2_crisis.txt:98-116,167-184`). Direct entry calculates from GRE GDP and charges once (`361-380`). The volunteer mission costs 0.002% weekly in the runtime stream (`692-704`). When revising network and coup to use Greek GDP, update the availability affordability triggers, freshly calculated values in apply, and native preview effects together. Keep the focus/helper one-time receipt; do not debit once in the focus and again in the old helper. Current GDP must be sampled at execution and affordability rechecked before charging, not taken from a monthly quote cache.

## Reciprocal transfers

Existing Cyprus agreements calculate an expense and matching recipient income separately. For example, Cyprus pays 0.002% of its GDP per week under the Greek civil programme while Greece receives a corresponding income stream (`common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CYP_R2_agreements.txt:915-916`). If a transfer rate is increased, raise both sides by the same agreed factor and keep payer and recipient amounts exactly matched. Prefer snapshotting one signed cash amount at agreement acceptance and using that same amount for payer expense and recipient income; independently multiplying each country's different GDP can make the two sides unequal. Update quote, acceptance, preview and stream formulas together. Do not confuse the Greek network/coup charges with bilateral transfers: those effects currently charge Greek treasury and do not credit Cyprus.

For that existing Greek-Cyprus weekly stream, strict ×10 gives `0.0002` as the runtime multiplier, or 0.02% of CYP GDP each week. Keep the same absolute CYP-GDP-based amount in Cyprus's expense and Greece's income calculation. The treaty setup payment is a distinct Cyprus expense (`COLDWAR_CYP_R2_agreements.txt:680-704`); do not turn it into a second Greek receipt when increasing the weekly stream.

## Historical and mechanical scale

The World Bank's Greece military-expenditure series is sourced from SIPRI and covers the 1970 observation; a SIPRI-derived historical series reports Greece at about 4.2% of GDP in 1970. This is an order-of-magnitude comparison for total annual national military expenditure, not cash available for a single focus. A 1.5% one-time Greek intervention charge plus an approximately 1.3% 180-day operational stream is a major alternate-history commitment but stays below that annual defence-spending scale. Annualized 0.02% and 0.05% weekly charges correspond to 1.04% and 2.6% of GDP, respectively. [World Bank indicator series](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/MS.MIL.XPND.GD.ZS?locations=GR), [SIPRI military expenditure database](https://www.sipri.org/databases/milex), [1970 observation](https://worldbody.org/data/MS.MIL.XPND.GD.ZS/greece/).

The political premise also supports a costly rather than routine operation: scholarship on the 1967-1974 military regime links its Cyprus policy to the effort to achieve Enosis, while the Security Council Report's history records the 1974 coup backed by the Greek military regime. The proposed 1970 coup path is alternate history, so the cost models the risk and resources of an intervention that had not yet happened at the start date. [Danopoulos, “The Greek Military Regime (1967-1974) and the Cyprus Question”](https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/pols_pub/12/), [Security Council Report, Cyprus history](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/research-reports/lookup-c-glkwlemtisg-b-4474149.php?print=true).

## Final fiscal revision recheck

The current `fiscal.py` matches the revised balance direction: most setup inputs are doubled, selected major capital programmes tripled, new temporary streams use their own issue receipt and deadline, the CYP_R2 actor charges are explicitly replaced at 0.75%, 1.5% and 1.5% of Greek GDP, and weekly bilateral money is repriced on both sides. Enosis is explicitly overridden at 5% + 0.03% weekly for full union and 2.5% + 0.015% weekly for the south route. The generated `concept.json` carries the same key changes. The temporary binding contract correctly requires the actual idea, active episode and original deadline in the local weekly money calculation; the relevant source calculation is the native weekly `additional_expenses_rate` path. No discrepancy found in these data-to-preview figures.

One remaining mismatch needs a design choice: `army_school` and `aegean_command` still have recurring 0.004% and 0.006% Greek-GDP-per-week rates in `fiscal.py`, but their focus rewards are only one-time army XP/doctrine and navy XP/research (`tools/greece_1970_rework_concept/design.py:54-55`). The current generated data labels both streams permanent (`maximum_days` is null), so payment persists after the one-time rewards have been consumed.

**Recommendation:** make both charges temporary 180-day funding periods attached to dedicated timed receipt spirits, with no added combat or economic modifier. Retain the one-time XP, doctrine and research exactly as designed. At 26 weekly charges, the funded periods cost about 0.104% and 0.156% Greek GDP after setup, instead of continuing indefinitely at about 0.208% and 0.312% per 52-week year. This binds each expense to a real timed native idea and one issuance receipt without inventing another positive buff. Add the two funding idea IDs to `fiscal.py`'s `binding()` map (or make the design spirit field use the actual idea ID), and ensure the idea definitions and effect previews show the finite 180-day obligation. Otherwise, a distinct permanent staff/command reward would be needed to justify permanent funding.

### Final recheck of the funding-spirit revision

The concept now implements the recommended data shape for both focuses: `Staff College Funding` and `Aegean Staff Funding` are 180-day temporary effects with empty modifier maps, and `fiscal.py` resolves them to `COLDWAR_GRE_R2_idea_staff_college_funding` and `COLDWAR_GRE_R2_idea_aegean_staff_funding` (`tools/greece_1970_rework_concept/fiscal.py:15-22,35-42`). Their recurring rates remain 0.004% and 0.006% per week and are tied to their active idea, distinct live episode, and original 180-day deadline. The concept specifies separate issue/expiry receipts, one original expiry event, zeroing and display-rate refresh on removal/expiry, and restoration of remaining duration only. `FISCAL_REVISION_CHECKS.json` includes active, removed-spirit/stale-cache, expired-deadline and cancelled-episode cases for all five fixed 180-day streams. It also reports compatible constant-GDP setup maxima of 19% for Economy and 14% for Defence; these are reachable setup sums, not cash-flow forecasts.

**Resolved in concept data:** the two permanent fees on consumed one-time rewards are now finite funding periods without added positive buffs. **Remaining before implementation:** the new idea definitions, `on_remove` hooks and original-deadline events are named contracts, not emitted runtime code here. Verify their refresh effects and save/rehost behavior in the native engine before release; this report does not claim that runtime behavior has been observed.
