# Greece rework concept: historical review

Scope: factual chronology, party and leader identity, and Cyprus/NATO framing in `concept-draft.json` and the sources attached to `tools/greece_1970_rework_concept/contracts.py`. This is not a balance review. Overall, the concept keeps the main January 1970 conditions separate from later developments. I found a few labels and descriptions that need explicit dating or tighter wording.

## Corrections and caveats

1. **The PAK label is a future political identity, not a January 1970 domestic party.** The party map calls slot 3 “Panhellenic Liberation Movement.” Andreas Papandreou founded PAK abroad after leaving Greece in January 1968; PASOK was founded only on 3 September 1974. The socialist branch's “Papandreou movement” can represent a later alternative government, but its 1970 starting identity should be described as an exile resistance network, not an operating national party. Keep PAK and PASOK distinct in any dated party names. [Official Greek Prime Minister biography](https://www.primeminister.gr/prime-minister/andreas-papandreou), [PASOK founding chronology](https://pasok.gr/history/).

2. **The royal branch should not imply that the monarchy had already been abolished.** Greece was still a kingdom in January 1970; Constantine II was king in exile and Zoitakis was Regent. The monarchy was abolished by the junta in 1973, then rejected again in the 1974 referendum. “A Constitutional Return for Constantine” is understandable as his return from exile, but the description's “restored institution” is anachronistic unless this focus follows an alternate abolition. “Royal Restoration Movement” likewise reads as a restoration party before abolition. Frame the 1970 option as the King's return or a royalist constitutional settlement; reserve restoration language for a post-1973/74 alternate path. [FRUS, 13 December 1967](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v16/d344), [1968 Constitution, Article 134](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1968-pt17/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1968-pt17-6-3.pdf), [FRUS, 22 March 1972](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v29/d333), [Greek Parliament, constitutional history](https://www.hellenicparliament.gr/en/Vouli-ton-Ellinon/To-Politevma/Syntagmatiki-Istoria/).

3. **The Cyprus response description conflates Turkey with Turkish Cypriots.** The `gre_response` text says “An actual Turkish-Cypriot war.” The 1974 war was between Turkey and Cyprus after the Greek-backed coup; Turkish Cypriots are a Cypriot community whose political agency should remain distinct from the Turkish state. “A war between Turkey and Cyprus” or “the Turkish operation in Cyprus” is historically clearer. UN reporting on the intercommunal talks treats Clerides and Denktaş as the two community interlocutors and records their sharply different positions. [UN Secretary-General report S/10199 (1970), paras. 59–71](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/490953/files/S_10199-EN.pdf).

4. **Four-guarantor consent is not a substitute for Turkish Cypriot consent.** The proposed settlement says that the four governments agree and that “both communities safeguards are accepted,” but it does not name an acceptance by the Turkish Cypriot community or its negotiator. The 1970 UN report records Denktaş negotiating for the Turkish Cypriot side, briefing the Turkish government separately, and arguing for a partnership arrangement and local self-administration; Makarios rejected federalization and cantonization. A four-state treaty or diplomatic agreement can be one layer of a settlement, but the concept should preserve a distinct, explicit Turkish Cypriot assent rather than treating Ankara's approval as that assent. The two community positions were not identical to those of Greece and Turkey. [UN report S/10199 (1970)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/490953/files/S_10199-EN.pdf), [UN report S/9233 (1969), joint Clerides–Denktaş statement](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/508826/files/S_9233-EN.pdf?ln=en).

5. **The 1970 Cyprus coup material is plausible as factional alt-history, not established Athens policy.** The March 1970 U.S. Embassy cable reports activity by some mainland and National Guard officers promoting double Enosis, while expressly saying it had no indication that the highest Greek government had adopted that course. The focus branch appropriately presents a player choice and a contest over the officer network. Preserve this uncertainty in any surrounding summary. EOKA-B must not appear as an existing 1970 actor: Grivas formed it after returning to Cyprus in 1971. The July 1974 coup was a later decision under Ioannidis, who had taken power in November 1973; it was not the inevitable outcome of the January 1970 situation. [FRUS, 17 March 1970](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v29/d352), [FRUS, July 1974 chronology](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v30/d148), [Library of Congress country study](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/frd/frdcstdy/cy/cypruscountrystu00sols/cypruscountrystu00sols.pdf).

6. **Make the NATO difference explicit in the Cyprus escalation description.** The focus effects disclose a full temporary Greece-and-Turkey Alliance exit and later return; this is an alternate-history mechanic. Historically, Greece's August 1974 action removed its forces from NATO's integrated military command, while Greece stayed in the North Atlantic Treaty and later rejoined the integrated command in 1980. Turkey did not withdraw. The current wording “Both prior NATO members leave” may be read as describing the historical 1974 event; label this as a full Alliance withdrawal in the alternate scenario. [NATO, Greece and the Alliance](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/nato-history/history-by-theme/my-country-and-nato/greece-and-nato), [FRUS record on the 1974 command withdrawal](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1977-80v21/d199).

7. **Keep Aegean procedures preventive at the 1970 start.** The concept presents them as a proposed restraint agreement, which is reasonable as an alternate policy. Avoid describing them as settling an already-developed Aegean continental-shelf crisis: the modern dispute over Turkish petroleum concessions and shelf delimitation emerged in late 1973. No historical 1970 bilateral pact is claimed by the proposal. [ELIAMEP, maritime dispute chronology](https://www.eliamep.gr/en/oi-ellinotourkikes-diafores-sto-thalassio-choro-apo-tin-optiki-tou-diethnous-dikaiou/).

## Points that are historically sound as written

- Papadopoulos is the January 1970 prime minister and regime centre; Ioannidis is presented as a possible later internal successor, not the starting ruler. The January 1970 U.S. ambassador's meeting identifies Papadopoulos as Prime Minister and records the regime's insistence on separating NATO ties from domestic reform. [FRUS, 26 January 1970](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v29/d268).
- Greece is not an EEC member in 1970. The concept describes the 1961 association as continuing in restricted form and labels its new terms as proposed, not as a completed historical contract. The `EU_member` starting leak is explicitly called out in the research record and should remain corrected in implementation.
- The two-party left framing does not invent a KKE Interior native party slot. The KKE split in 1968; the concept places cooperation with KKE Interior constituencies inside a broader communist-left programme. EDA, the pre-coup electoral vehicle associated with the communist movement, remains a useful historical predecessor to mention if more context is needed, but its absence as a 1970 legal party is correct.
- The French Mirage relationship is presented as a later precedent, not a 1970 contract or an automatic equipment grant. The sources list identifies 1974 as the purchase year.
- The contract source list marks bilateral terms as proposals subject to named recipient approval. Those are alternate-history offers, not claims that the listed agreements already existed in January 1970. The EEC accession offer correctly distinguishes membership from association and requires current member approvals.
- The Council of Europe, EEC and NATO timelines are separate in the research record: Greece notified withdrawal from the Council of Europe in December 1969, remained a member through 1970, was not an EEC member, and stayed in NATO in 1970.

## Coverage note

The six political programmes cover the junta, ERE/conservative, Centre Union, Papandreou's national-social current, communist left, and royalist constituencies. That is a defensible choice for the proposed tree. For historical completeness, the narrative may briefly identify EDA as the pre-coup communist-linked electoral vehicle and state that the Centre Union was a broad coalition. Avoid suggesting any of these banned parties operated legally in January 1970.

The concept is strongest when it keeps three things explicit: party traditions are historical constituencies rather than lawful 1970 organizations; Cyprus is an independent republic with two political communities; and later events such as EOKA-B, the Ioannidis coup, the Turkish operation, and Greece's NATO-command withdrawal are dated developments, not prewritten consequences.
