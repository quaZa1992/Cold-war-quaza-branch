# Japan reward revision: research and balance basis

## Scope

The revision is an overlay on the 425-focus Japan design. It changes reward payloads for 54 national and economic focus IDs, 69 effective party focus helpers, including six party-capital milestones, and 13 Article 9 programme steps. It retains each focus ID, name, description, duration, coordinate, prerequisite, exclusion, party requirement, constitutional approval requirement, and bilateral acceptance gate. Four recipient-selected development projects and three direct bilateral offer families receive expanded prospective effects. The machine-readable record in `JAP_reward_change_manifest.json` lists every national, party, and Article 9 ID with old and revised effects, modifier values, one-time costs, and route examples.

This is an authored source overlay. It has not been emitted into shared `common/` runtime files by this change. The root integration still needs to consume the writers listed in the handoff message and test the selected native transactions. The values are gameplay balance proposals, not historical measurements.

## Historical precedents and design use

The packages use existing MD energy modifiers and facilities; they do not create uranium production or mines. |

The 1973 oil share is used as a later vulnerability marker, not described as a January 1970 fact. The VLSI project is likewise a later institutional precedent that motivates a development path, not an instant capability.

## Reward scale and native MD calibration

The changes use varied packages instead of doubling every small bonus. Values of roughly 4 to 8 percentage points are reserved for substantial programme milestones, with specialized equipment-cost reductions, finite research or doctrine bonuses, and site-guarded assets added where the focus warrants them. Smaller connectors remain modest when the existing tree makes them intermediate steps. One-use research bonuses are specific to verified MD categories; military equipment-cost reductions target existing equipment families such as `infantry_weapons_type`, `L_AT_Equipment`, and `destroyer`, while retaining any accompanying production-output bonus.

The reference samples are actual MD 2000 focus rewards, not a universal conversion formula. Costs below are one-time treasury charges and duration is the focus's native `cost` multiplied by seven days. Gates are summarized from the live donor source:

Japan project assets use existing state IDs, real owner/control checks, native building-level caps, and the existing treasury helper. The 21 GDP-priced domestic policy costs use `cost_gdp_share` and MD's `treasury_change` / `modify_treasury_effect` path. Shares are deliberate policy prices sized to the asset and programme, rather than reproductions of nominal 2000-series prices in a different 1970 GDP economy. The 1970 GDP anchor of $221.798 billion is used only to show a comparison amount. Twelve capital prices were set to these shares: factory modernisation 1.25%, escort-yard modernisation 1.25%, public housing 0.75%, coal transition 1.25%, resilient energy 1.5%, nuclear expansion 2.25%, Shinkansen capacity 2%, financial centres 2%, knowledge economy 1.5%, microelectronics capacity 2.5%, precision tools 1.75%, and long-term credit 1.25%. At the anchor, these equal about $1.663 billion to $5.545 billion each. Their full before/after prices and 3-decimal anchor comparisons are listed in the manifest. The unconditional native `effect_tooltip` runs the same temporary GDP multiplication and treasury effect as the payout, so MD displays the exact current amount with the standard treasury formatter while the focus is locked. The reward and hidden focus-availability guards require GDP above zero, with no cash-on-hand affordability gate. Previously approved party fixed charges and accepted bilateral contract fees remain in place; six expanded party milestone packages add explicit GDP-share capital prices. Their per-focus share and anchor cost are listed separately in the manifest. Recurring budget effects remain weekly. No effect uses negative available-workforce size.

## Party capital milestones

Six party-programme follow-ups now convert the political branches into distinct, permanent institutional assets, while the party modifiers remain conditional on the actual ruling party. The LDP industrial-standards follow-up adds a Kanto factory, a finite 50% industry research bonus, and its former 25% bonus is replaced rather than stacked; its fee is the preserved $2 billion plus 0.75% GDP. Its rival market-credit follow-up builds two Kansai offices for 1% GDP. The DSP programme adds one Chubu factory and a finite 50% industry research bonus for the preserved $2 billion plus 1% GDP. The JSP adds a Kansai factory and Tohoku office for 1% GDP; JCP adds a Chugoku factory and Kansai office for its preserved $1 billion plus 1% GDP; Komeito adds a Kansai office and Tohoku factory for its preserved $3 billion plus 1% GDP. Their existing party-specific trade-offs remain: the packages do not erase each platform's costs to civilian output, research, naval experience, or public spending.

At the $221.798 billion 1970 GDP anchor, the six marginal transactions cost about $3.663b (LDP industrial), $2.218b (LDP market), $4.218b (DSP), $2.218b (JSP), $3.218b (JCP), and $5.218b (Komeito). These are individual focus prices, not an all-party sum. LDP A and B follow-ups are mutually exclusive. Building each level grants one extra shared state slot first. Execution and focus availability require Japan, the applicable actual ruling-party check, GDP above zero, the target state to be owned and controlled, and native state building-level headroom; the grant is one-time and durable. The focus preview uses the same concrete treasury, slot, construction and research effects unconditionally, so the numeric outcome remains inspectable while locked without granting it. The institutional differentiation follows the documented business-led growth and industrial-policy record, the DSP/JSP split and Komeito civic organization summarized above; these are game abstractions, not claims that the historical parties built these exact facilities.

## Modifier semantics checked against local MD sources

- `consumer_goods_factor` is the native **Tax Cost Factor**. The local display is `MODIFIER_CONSUMER_GOODS_FACTOR`; MD computes its effect against `consumer_goods_expected_value`. The overlay and generated national-preview label now say “Tax Cost Factor,” not consumer-goods demand.
- `energy_gain_multiplier` is the electrical power-generation factor; `renewable_energy_gain_multiplier` is the renewable power-generation factor. `fossil_pp_energy_generation_modifier` and `nuclear_energy_generation_modifier` refer to their corresponding facility power-generation modifiers.
- `energy_use_modifier_civs` and `energy_use_modifier_mils` refer to civilian- and military-factory power consumption. These are shown as power use, not generic fuel savings.
- `offices_productivity`, `civilian_factories_productivity`, and `country_productivity_growth_modifier` are registered in the MD money modifier definitions and are displayed through MD's native percentage tooltip keys. Credit/income entries use `tax_gain_multiplier_modifier` and the existing money helper.

## Source and integration order

The reward overlay is authoritative over the old `effect_inventory.json` and `party_map.json` snapshots. Use the effective accessors in `reward_revision.py`. Emit source in dependency order:

1. `generate_staged_effects.py` reads the effective general rewards and writes the national economy helpers, dynamic modifiers, calls, availability/building guards, localization, and native focus-preview registry. GDP-priced costs use the treasury formatter directly; there is no scripted-localization cost calculator.
2. Run `build_party_rewards.py`. It reads `effective_party_map()` and stages all party reward helpers, actual-ruling-party dynamic modifiers, and matching localization in `tools/japan_1970/staging/party/COLDWAR_JAP_1970_party_effects.txt`, `COLDWAR_JAP_1970_party_dynamic_modifiers.txt`, and `COLDWAR_JAP_1970_party_modifiers_l_english.yml`. `stage_focus_graph.py` separately reads `party_preview_overrides()` and `effective_party_map()` when it emits the 425-node `tools/japan_1970/staging/focus_tree.txt`, focus localization and complete party focus previews in `tools/japan_1970/staging/national_economy_l_english.yml`. Do not regenerate party rewards or previews from raw `party_map.json`.
3. `build_constitution_rewards.py` writes Article 9 focus helpers, timed ideas, and matching localization from the overlay. The shared Article 9 terminal ideas must also receive the overlay's final result values when the root integration emits them.
4. `stage_focus_graph.py` calls `native_foreign_previews.build_preview_source()` from the edited bilateral event, project decision, and helper-registry sources; it stages the prospective acceptance preview and writes all 425 graph nodes, focus localization, and party/constitutional/national preview blocks. Keep every numeric offer preview visible while locked or awaiting consent.
5. `build_diplomatic_runtime.py` then emits the recipient response reports and project decision runtime from the updated foreign transaction sources. Its reports are not a substitute for the focus's prospective effects. This writer is for the later root integration and was not run during the source-only pass.

Foreign source inputs changed for this overlay are `tools/japan_1970/staging/foreign/COLDWAR_JAP70_foreign_project_decisions.txt`, `bilateral_manifest.jsonl`, `foreign_helper_registry.json`, `COLDWAR_JAP70_bilateral_events.txt`, `COLDWAR_JAP70_bilateral_ideas.txt`, `COLDWAR_JAP70_bilateral_l_english.yml`, `COLDWAR_JAP70_multilateral_project_effects.txt`, and `foreign_presentation.py`. The graph writer emits the focus-side acceptance preview from these sources. The diplomatic runtime writer consumes the same files for recipient-side acceptance and project execution; do not run it until root's shared-runtime integration step.

The read-only source checks for this overlay report: 425 preserved IDs; 54 national reward entries, 69 effective party entries, 13 Article 9 records, and four reworked regional projects; zero overlay validation findings; 425 of 425 focuses mapped; and zero structural graph findings. Foreign script grammar parses cleanly. No writer in this review emitted runtime files or installed the staged source.
