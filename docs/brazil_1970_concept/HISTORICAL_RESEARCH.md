# IMPERIA COLD WAR — Brazil historical research, 1970 to 1990

Research note for a national focus tree concept. This is historical framing, not runtime design. Dates and institutional sequence should govern when parties, crises, and agreements become available. Historical branches should coexist with clearly marked alternate-history choices; no state scripted party conversion is implied.

## January 1970 starting position

Emílio Garrastazu Médici was president from October 1969. Brazil was under the military regime's 1967 Constitution as amended in 1969, the National Security Doctrine, and the exceptional powers of Institutional Act No. 5 (AI-5). Congress and elections existed, but direct presidential election did not. ARENA was the regime's governing party and MDB the sole permitted opposition party under the imposed two-party system. The opposition operated within restricted institutions and faced censorship, repression, and curtailed civil rights. A 1970 start should therefore represent military executive control, ARENA patronage and congressional machinery, and a legally constrained MDB. It should not start with a plural party system or Geisel's later liberalization policy. [FGV CPDOC, Tancredo Neves](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/tancredo-neves); [FGV CPDOC, Magalhães Pinto](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/magalhaes-pinto).

The economy was in the high-growth phase later called the Brazilian Miracle. The Central Bank's institutional history describes 1968 to 1973 as average GDP growth above 10 percent annually, with falling inflation and expanding credit. This was a real state capacity and industrial expansion, but not broad social inclusion. The IPEA account of Albert Fishlow's work notes that income concentration worsened from 1960 to 1970 even amid rapid growth. A credible strength and cost pair is therefore public investment, manufacturing and credit capacity alongside wage restraint, unequal gains, and weak political accountability. [Banco Central do Brasil, Working Paper 258](https://aprendervalor.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/WorkingPaperSeries/TD258.pdf); [IPEA, Fishlow and income inequality](https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/bitstream/11058/7052/1/Desafios_Desenvolvimento_v.10_n.79_2014.pdf).

## Political sequence and party reachability

- **1970 to 1973, Médici:** Keep ARENA and MDB as the only legal party choices. Do not offer a legal PT, PDT, PTB, PMDB, PDS, PFL, PCB, or PSDB yet. Political pluralism is constrained. An MDB parliamentary path should represent opposition, negotiation, and electoral campaigning inside a restricted system, not immediate democratic control.
- **1973 to 1974:** The oil shock belongs at the end of 1973, not at the January 1970 start. The MDB's 1973 anticandidacy of Ulysses Guimarães and the 1974 electoral breakthrough can open an opposition and regime legitimacy pathway. MDB won 16 of 22 Senate seats contested in 1974. [FGV CPDOC, Tancredo Neves](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/tancredo-neves).
- **1974 to 1979, Geisel:** The opening was a controlled, gradual regime project, not an immediate democratic transfer. Geisel's government negotiated with moderate MDB figures, pursued institutional liberalization while preserving military influence, and faced hardline resistance. In 1978 Tancredo advocated direct elections and amnesty; AI-5 was revoked at the end of 1978, with the revocation effective in 1979. A focus branch can make an opening possible while retaining an authoritarian veto and military faction tension. [FGV CPDOC, Tancredo Neves](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/tancredo-neves); [FGV CPDOC research on controlled opening](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/sites/default/files/brasilia/arquivos/Vera%20CHAIA%20-%20A%20dimensao%20politica%20de%20Brasilia.pdf).
- **1979 party reform:** The regime ended imposed bipartisanship and required party reorganization. ARENA became PDS and MDB became PMDB. The reform also permitted the formation of PTB, PDT, and PT. The PT emerged from labor and civil society organization; Brizola's faction formed PDT after losing the PTB name to Ivete Vargas's group. These should be distinct party formation or coalition choices, not government-mandated ideological switches. [TSE, 1980 party formation exhibit](https://www.tse.jus.br/institucional/catalogo-de-publicacoes/arquivos/portfolio-da-exposicao-o-voto-no-brasil/%40%40download/file/Expo-Voto-Brasil-Portifolio2.pdf); [FGV CPDOC, Leonel Brizola](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/leonel-brizola).
- **1982 to 1985:** The 1982 election consolidates the new party competition, while the debt crisis strikes in 1982. In 1984, the Diretas Já campaign mobilized mass support but failed to pass the amendment restoring direct presidential election. In 1985 Tancredo Neves won through the Electoral College with support from the PMDB and a PDS dissident group, the Liberal Front. PFL should emerge from that 1985 split, not exist in 1970. The civilian transition remained negotiated and indirect. [FGV CPDOC, Tancredo Neves](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/tancredo-neves); [FGV CPDOC, Magalhães Pinto](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/magalhaes-pinto).
- **1985 to 1990:** PCB's legal registration returned in May 1985 after its 1947 proscription. PCdoB is a separate communist party with distinct history and organization; do not use it as a renamed PCB or treat the two as interchangeable. PSDB formed in 1988 during the constituent process through a split from PMDB. The 1988 Constitution broadened the democratic and party framework; direct presidential elections returned in 1989. [FGV CPDOC, PCB historical entry](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/sites/default/files/verbetes/primeira-republica/Partido%20Comunista%20Brasileiro%20%28PCB%29%20-%20Primeira%20Rep%C3%BAblica.pdf); [TSE Digital Library, PSDB formation study](https://bibliotecadigital.tse.jus.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/48b6cd6a-f6f0-4e5d-8cb5-14348ec198fc/content); [TSE, historical judgments](https://www.tse.jus.br/jurisprudencia/julgados-historicos).

Do not create political content that assumes a Muslim sectarian landscape. Brazil's important social and religious institutions include the Catholic Church, including the CNBB, and an increasingly active labor movement and land reform debate. CNBB statements and pastoral bodies increasingly criticized repression and inequality from the early to mid-1970s. Those provide plausible civil society, human rights, labor, and rural policy themes, while state repression and Church-state tensions remain genuine constraints. [CNBB, Church and human rights in the dictatorship](https://www.cnbb.org.br/ao-celebrar-30-anos-a-semana-social-brasileira-reafirma-sua-luta-pelos-direitos-humanos/); [FGV CPDOC, Amazon land and labor discussion](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/sites/default/files/militares-amazonia/txt_Eugenio_Diniz.pdf).

## Development policy, strengths and costs

- **State-led industrialization and infrastructure:** Strong public banks, state enterprises, and national planning make heavy industry and infrastructure credible strengths. Preserve the costs of capital intensity, external borrowing, regional concentration, and distributional inequality. Avoid depicting the Miracle as either fictitious or universally beneficial. [Banco Central do Brasil, Working Paper 258](https://aprendervalor.bcb.gov.br/content/publicacoes/WorkingPaperSeries/TD258.pdf); [IPEA, Fishlow](https://repositorio.ipea.gov.br/bitstream/11058/7052/1/Desafios_Desenvolvimento_v.10_n.79_2014.pdf).
- **Oil import vulnerability:** Brazil imported more than 80 percent of its petroleum needs at the time of the first oil shock. The first shock followed the 1973 Arab-Israeli war, raising oil prices more than 300 percent between October 1973 and January 1974. The government chose to sustain investment with foreign borrowing rather than immediate contraction. This supports a real tradeoff: continued industrial capacity and energy substitution, purchased with foreign exchange exposure and future debt service. [Banco Central do Brasil, Pedro Malan interview](https://www.bcb.gov.br/historiacontada/publicacoes/hc_bc_volume_19_pedro_malan.pdf); [Banco Central do Brasil, external debt history](https://www.bcb.gov.br/htms/infecon/dividarevisada/prefacio_introducao.pdf).
- **Geisel's II National Development Plan and energy policy:** Post-1973 policy emphasized import substitution in capital goods and basic inputs, state investment, and alternative energy, but relied heavily on external finance. Proalcool began in 1975. Nuclear power expansion and the Brazil-West Germany agreement also belonged to this period. These policies need to unlock after the oil shock and under Geisel, with high investment costs, technological transfer goals, implementation risk, and debt exposure. [Banco Central do Brasil, external debt history](https://www.bcb.gov.br/htms/infecon/dividarevisada/prefacio_introducao.pdf); [National Archives, Ernesto Geisel](https://presidentes.an.gov.br/index.php/centro-de-referencia-de-acervos-presidenciais/assuntos/biografias/183-ernesto-geisel); [Brazil-West Germany nuclear agreement, Itamaraty treaty record](https://concordia.itamaraty.gov.br/ApiConcordia/Acordo/integra-acordo-html/2273).
- **Agricultural research and frontier development:** Embrapa was created in 1973 to develop tropical agricultural technology. The Cerrado programs from 1975 combined public credit, roads, storage, research, extension, and support for land conversion. Agricultural modernization and food production are valid strengths; land concentration, displacement, ecological costs, and uneven access to finance are material tradeoffs. Japan supported technical and financing cooperation, including the Cerrado program. [Embrapa, institutional history](https://www.embrapa.br/pt/visao-de-futuro/trajetoria-do-agro); [Embrapa Cerrados history](https://www.embrapa.br/web/cerrados/historia); [FUNAG, Brazil-Japan cultural and economic cooperation](https://funag.gov.br/loja/download/498-ensaios_sobre_a_heranca_cultural_japonesa.pdf).
- **Debt timeline:** External debt was about US$14.9 billion at the end of 1973 and US$85.5 billion by 1982; international interest rates and the second oil shock intensified the vulnerability. The debt crisis should be an earned 1982 event, not a starting debuff in 1970. The 1987 moratorium is later again. [Banco Central do Brasil, Pedro Malan interview](https://www.bcb.gov.br/historiacontada/publicacoes/hc_bc_volume_19_pedro_malan.pdf); [Banco Central do Brasil, 30 years of the Real](https://www.bcb.gov.br/controleinflacao/30anosreal?modalAberto=30anosreal_modal_jul94).

## Use and limits

This note supports historical plausibility and timing, not a claim that every policy had a single uncontested interpretation. The BCB, FGV CPDOC, TSE, federal archives, Embrapa, CNBB, and Itamaraty records are prioritized for institutional facts and dates. Broad effects should be translated into focus rewards only after balancing the documented tradeoffs. Where game mechanics differ from history, label the branch as alternate history and retain genuine political and institutional constraints.
