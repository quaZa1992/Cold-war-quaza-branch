### Remaining material historical limits

- **The French Mirage offer is a cooperative RP package, not the historical 1970 procurement contract.** The precedent is correctly dated: the contract was concluded on 15 May 1970 for 16 aircraft, with initial deliveries in 1972. The compiled offer describes training, maintenance, and technical cooperation, gives both countries setup and recurring costs, and gives both research rewards. Those terms are a proposed multiplayer arrangement and are not documented as the historical sale's financial terms. Keep the displayed contract clear that Brazil is not receiving the 16 historical Mirage aircraft through these research and training rewards. [Le Monde, 19 May 1970](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1970/05/19/le-bresil-achete-seize-avions-de-combat-mirage-iii.html); [SIPRI Yearbook 1969/70](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/SIPRI%20Yearbook%201969%E2%80%9370.pdf).
- **The Itaipu transfer is now honestly labelled as an RP financing payment.** It must remain understood as a game-specific support term, not the 1973 treaty's royalty stream. Historically the binational Itaipu entity paid equal royalties to both countries and separately remunerated energy ceded by one country to the other. The compiled concept expressly disclaims that formula, which resolves the earlier misstatement. [Itaipu Treaty, Article XV](https://www.itaipu.gov.br/sites/default/files/u13/tratadoitaipu.pdf).
- **Earlier River Consultations should be identified as an alternate timeline if completed before 1979.** The node is now explicitly bilateral and not the historical tripartite settlement. It can follow an active Itaipu offer, which itself may be marked as an earlier alternative. The treaty inspiration remains sound if the player understands that early bilateral consultation anticipates the later settlement rather than recreating it.
- **The 1980 Argentina nuclear-cooperation precedent and 1985 confidence-building precedent remain summarized in one offer.** This is directionally accurate, but a historical event chronology should distinguish the 1980 cooperation treaty from the 1985 Iguazu nuclear declaration. The 1986 industrial protocol is now independent of that offer, so this compression no longer gates economic integration.

No other material historical error is apparent in the reviewed party identities, 1970 starting spirits, or revised diplomatic prerequisites. Policy values and reciprocal GDP terms are proposed game balance, not measures claimed by the historical sources. Any subsequent change to `concept.json` requires a fresh hash and review.

### Remaining historical and diplomatic corrections

1. **Remove unrelated Japan prerequisites from earlier agreements.** In the compiled tree, `French Mirage Partnership` and `German Nuclear Accord` each require either `Japan Cerrado Research` or `Japan Steel Consortium`. The Mirage purchase was contracted in May 1970, well before the Japan-backed PRODECER programme. The Federal Republic nuclear agreement was signed in June 1975, before PRODECER's initial operational phase in 1979. Neither agreement depended on Japan cooperation. Move these offers to their own military/energy branches or make their precedents independent. The Japanese Cerrado and steel proposals can remain separate. [SIPRI Yearbook 1969/70, Brazil Mirage contract](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/SIPRI%20Yearbook%201969%E2%80%9370.pdf); [Itamaraty, Federal Republic nuclear agreement, 27 June 1975](https://concordia.itamaraty.gov.br/ApiConcordia/Acordo/integra-acordo-html/2273); [Embrapa Cerrados, programme history](https://www.embrapa.br/web/cerrados/historia).

2. **Reconcile the Itaipu royalty transfer with the treaty.** The compiled description calls the recurring Brazil-to-Paraguay transfer an RP royalty modeling the asymmetric bargain. The 1973 treaty instead provides that Itaipu itself pay equal royalties to both Brazil and Paraguay; it also allocates half the electricity to each country and provides that a party ceding unused energy is remunerated. The historically grounded asymmetry is in consumption, debt exposure, and purchase/offtake arrangements, not a simple unilateral Brazilian royalty paid directly to Paraguay. Keep the existing transfer only if it is clearly presented as an alternate RP concession. For a historical proposal, model equal project royalties to both governments plus separate compensation for Paraguay's ceded energy, with the off-taker and project entity stated. [Itaipu Treaty, Articles XIII-XV and Annex C](https://www.itaipu.gov.br/sites/default/files/u13/tratadoitaipu.pdf); [Itaipu Binacional, royalty allocation](https://www.itaipu.gov.py/institucional/transparencia/royalties).

3. **Make the French Mirage offer match a procurement contract.** Its access path currently follows Japanese agricultural or steel cooperation, even though the 1970 Mirage contract is a direct Brazil-France arms purchase. Its effect package charges both France and Brazil setup and recurring costs, while granting each research and productivity rewards. The historical buyer paid for aircraft and support; the supplier received export revenue and industrial work. Prefer a one-time Brazilian procurement cost plus a defined training/maintenance support fee, and show France export income or production value if the recipient side is meant to preview a real arms sale. Alternatively call it a separate bilateral training exchange and avoid presenting it as the 1970 acquisition itself. The chronology is sound; the current economics do not yet read like the cited deal.

4. **Itaipu's current transfer is at least less one-sided than the prior draft, but the agreement still understates the two-country energy bargain.** Both sides now pay GDP-scaled setup and weekly expenses, both receive a project asset, and Brazil's recurring transfer goes to Paraguay. That's a potentially fair RP offer, but it is not a literal restatement of the 1973 treaty. If the transfer stands, retain an explicit label such as negotiated royalty concession rather than implying the treaty's normal royalty allocation. Also retain Paraguay's half-share energy entitlement and Brazil's offtake/compensation responsibility in any text or actual proposal terms. [Itaipu Treaty, Articles XIII-XV](https://www.itaipu.gov.br/sites/default/files/u13/tratadoitaipu.pdf).

5. **The historical Itaipu, Embrapa, Proalcool, FRG nuclear, 1979 basin accord, and 1986 Argentina integration precedents are still executable without a date or founding-event condition.** Party identities now correctly require real registration, but these policy offers have no explicit date or event conditions in the compiled artifact. This permits an unlabelled 1970 historical run to complete institutions that arose later. The user requested non-arbitrary party access, not removal of all chronology. Keep focuses inspectable, and gate historical execution through the underlying 1973 treaty, 1973 Embrapa establishment, 1975 alcohol/nuclear programme, 1979 tripartite basin settlement, and 1986 integration process; mark earlier execution as alternate history. For the early 1970 Mirage procurement, no such later lock is needed.

6. **The PTB programme still conflates two distinct party identities.** `PTB Labourism` describes an old PTB restoration or a separately registered later PTB under one group identity. The 1979 naming dispute produced Ivete Vargas's PTB and Brizola's separate PDT; an old pre-1965 PTB restoration is only an alternate-history possibility. Keep the restored old PTB and Ivete's new PTB as distinct identities or explicitly state that the shared ten-focus content is provisional, while keeping PDT separate. The event's wording acknowledges separate registration, but it does not itself distinguish the two PTB cases.

7. **Municipal Networks remains gated by plural-party reform.** The Church and public service roots are now available under all governments, but `Municipal Networks` still requires `Plural Party Law`, which requires constitutional reform. If it represents physical infrastructure and local public service networks, this remains too late: municipalities and municipal services existed under the dictatorship, though elected authority and political competition were constrained. Either allow the administrative infrastructure focus earlier or rename and describe it specifically as an elected municipal autonomy reform.

8. **Federal Republic nuclear offer remains conditional on unrelated Japan cooperation.** This is covered by item 1 but is especially important because the Brazil-Federal Republic treaty was an independent bilateral negotiation with its own energy-security and technology-transfer rationale. Preserve the legitimate costs and the limits on technology transfer; remove Japan as a prerequisite.

9. **The Itaipu-Corpus precedent was a tripartite settlement.** `Parana Basin Accord` is offered only to Argentina after Brazil-Paraguay Itaipu is active, and its description says it cannot bind Paraguay. That means it cannot represent the historical 19 October 1979 Itaipu-Corpus agreement, which was reached by Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina. Make the historical precedent a three-country negotiation, including explicit Paraguayan consent, or label the current Brazil-Argentina offer as a separate counterfactual bilateral arrangement. [Treaty record, Argentina Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://tratados.cancilleria.gob.ar/tratado_multi_ficha.php?id=k52pmA); [ANTAQ study of the Plata Basin treaty](https://www.gov.br/antaq/pt-br/assuntos/atuacao-internacional/TratadodaBaciadoPrata.pdf).

10. **Separate economic integration from the nuclear confidence spirit, or use the broader 1985 political declaration as the shared parent.** In the compiled concept, the 1986 Argentine Industrial Protocol requires an active Argentine Nuclear Confidence agreement. The 1985 Iguazu Declaration launched broad bilateral coordination and was accompanied by a separate nuclear declaration; the 1986 integration protocols followed the broader integration programme. A nuclear agreement was a meaningful trust signal but was not a formal prerequisite for every industrial protocol. The present dependency overstates the institutional linkage and can make the industrial path disappear if the nuclear arrangement is inactive. [FUNAG, Brazil-Argentina relations and Iguazu Declaration](https://funag.gov.br/loja/download/1161-CADERNOS-DO-IPRI-N-3_30_08.pdf); [Itamaraty, 1986 integration programme](https://concordia.itamaraty.gov.br/ApiConcordia/Acordo/integra-acordo-html/3586).

## Initial structured-draft findings, superseded where noted above

1. **Separate the Lacerda opposition idea from the PRN programme.** Lacerda lost his political rights under the dictatorship and died in 1977. He cannot be the active 1970 leader of an electoral campaign or become a personal predecessor to Collor. The PRN began as the existing Party of Youth, which Collor renamed by TSE decision in May 1989; FGV's account says Collor launched its founding manifesto on 9 February 1989. Keep a 1970 Lacerda-associated opposition concept as explicitly counterfactual and contingent on political rehabilitation. Build a separate 1989 PRN programme around Collor's personal campaign, anti-corruption and anti-privilege appeal, and market-oriented modernization. Do not connect it as a direct successor. [FGV Atlas, 1989 election and PRN formation](https://atlas.fgv.br/verbete/1418); [TSE party glossary, PRN name change 11 May 1989](https://www.tse.jus.br/servicos-eleitorais/glossario/termos/partido-politico).

2. **Decouple Argentine nuclear cooperation from the Itaipu-Corpus settlement.** Brazil and Argentina signed their peaceful nuclear cooperation agreement on 17 May 1980, entering into force in 1983. Itaipu-Corpus was settled separately in 1979. The later 1985 Iguazu Declaration created a further confidence-building track, and the 1986 integration agreements created an industrial/economic programme. The current `arg_nuclear` requirement for an active Parana Basin Accord and `arg_integration` requirement for both the Federal Republic nuclear accord and Argentina nuclear agreement invent prerequisites. Make these distinct offers with appropriate periods and political context: the 1980 nuclear cooperation track, 1985 confidence-building, and 1986 economic integration. [Brazil Presidency, 1980 Brazil-Argentina nuclear agreement](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/atos/decretos/1983/d88946.html); [FUNAG, 1985 Iguazu Declaration and nuclear confidence building](https://funag.gov.br/loja/download/1161-CADERNOS-DO-IPRI-N-3_30_08.pdf); [Itamaraty, 1986 integration programme](https://concordia.itamaraty.gov.br/ApiConcordia/Acordo/integra-acordo-html/3586).

3. **Narrow the French aviation offer to documented instruments.** The French relationship clearly supports Mirage III procurement, with the first aircraft arriving in 1972 and French manufacturer technical staff assisting local assembly. A stronger industrial offset precedent was the CINDACTA I agreement, under which France purchased 41 Embraer Xingu aircraft. The AMX was an Italian-Brazilian programme, not a French-Brazilian aerospace pact. Rename `france_aircraft` to a specific Mirage procurement, training, or French offset offer. Do not imply a broad period aerospace R&D pact or AMX transfer. [Brazilian Air Force Academy, Mirage history](https://www2.fab.mil.br/afa/index.php/aeronaves/336-f-103e-dassault-mirage-iii-ebr-fab-4925); [FUNAG, offset agreement and Xingu purchase](https://www.funag.gov.br/biblioteca/download/1110_cadernos_do_ipri_n_1_ano_1.pdf).

4. **Treat 1982 as a foreign-credit and balance-of-payments crisis, not default.** The `debt_crisis` event name, description, and options generally fit an external credit squeeze, but keep any player-facing language precise: Brazil negotiated with the IMF in 1982 to 1983 and continued debt service. The suspension of interest payments came with the February 1987 moratorium. Do not make default or principal cancellation a 1982 event. [Banco Central, Pedro Malan interview on debt trajectory and 1982 crisis](https://www.bcb.gov.br/historiacontada/publicacoes/hc_bc_volume_19_pedro_malan.pdf); [Banco Central, 30 years of the Real](https://www.bcb.gov.br/controleinflacao/30anosreal?modalAberto=30anosreal_modal_jul94).

5. **Make the PTB split a genuine formation outcome.** Brizola sought the PTB name after the 1979 reform, but the TSE granted it to Ivete Vargas's group. Brizola's group founded the PDT in 1980. A pre-1965 PTB restoration can be an alternate-history party-formation route after a legal opening, but it should not be represented as the same identity as the later Ivete Vargas PTB. Trigger the PTB/PDT split through legalisation and the naming dispute, not by forcing both party identities or waiting mechanically until 1980. [FGV CPDOC, Leonel Brizola](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/leonel-brizola); [FGV CPDOC, Tancredo Neves on 1979 party reorganisation](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/biografias/tancredo-neves).

6. **Allow the pre-1979 institutional and social work to proceed without constitutional reform.** Requiring `constitutional` as the root of all Society programmes makes public services, CNBB dialogue, labour institutions, education, municipal networks, land administration, and party reform all impossible until AI-5 has already ended. Historically, CNBB advocacy, state service provision, and social movements existed under authoritarian rule, while reform was politically constrained and often repressed. Keep substantive civic and administrative programmes visible and condition their availability on the relevant programme or political opening. Make the two CNBB and secular-service nodes complementary, or present them as emphasis choices without claiming that the state and Church had to be mutually exclusive. Separate the genuinely later plural-party reform from that constitutional branch, and retain actual legalisation prerequisites for new parties. [CNBB on its human-rights role and period documents from 1973, 1976, and 1977](https://www.cnbb.org.br/ao-celebrar-30-anos-a-semana-social-brasileira-reafirma-sua-luta-pelos-direitos-humanos/).

## Party catalogue and chronology

The ten programme identities are conceptually distinct enough to retain. Keep all later programme content inspectable as provisional, but use actual organisation, legalisation, elections, and accession to government to control when a route can execute. A common party outlook slot must not erase identity. No programme should directly set the ruling party.

- **ARENA/PDS:** sound separation of the military command from the civilian governing bloc. ARENA existed in 1970; PDS followed the 1979 reorganisation. Its civilian conservative programme can be visible early, with the PDS label and successor link handled by the real party reform. Controlled opening should be decided by regime leadership and military faction politics rather than treated as a policy owned solely by ARENA.
- **MDB/PMDB:** the MDB is the legal opposition in 1970; PMDB is its successor after the 1979 reorganisation. The 1974 Senate breakthrough, 1978 amnesty and direct-election campaign, and 1985 Electoral College victory are important genuine waypoints. The concept's amnesty and constitutional choices are usable as policy alternatives, but the path must not describe a 1970 MDB as already governing.
- **PTB and PDT:** separate the historical 1979 to 1980 name dispute from an alternate return of the pre-1965 PTB. The source material supports the Brizola versus Ivete split. Distinct, conditional programme identities are appropriate.
- **PT:** founded in 1980 out of union, social movement, and Catholic grassroots contexts. The ABC labour mobilisations began before formal party creation. An early organisational route can develop through institutional legalisation without delaying every labour or civil society policy. Do not present later PT governance instruments, especially Porto Alegre participatory budgeting, as a 1970s institution. The concept correctly labels that focus as a later precedent and alternative.
- **PFL:** founded in 1985 through the Liberal Front break from PDS around the Tancredo candidacy. Keep it separate from a wholesale ARENA rename. Regional conservative networks, civilian coalition building, and market-oriented policies are plausible programme themes.
- **PSDB:** founded in 1988 by a PMDB split during the Constituent Assembly process. It can be an inspectable late identity, but it was not simply an established 1970 programme of social democracy. The focus descriptions already signal a late alternative; retain that distinction.
- **PCB and PCdoB:** both were distinct communist organisations and illegal in 1970. Both regained legality in 1985; the PCdoB registration process continued to definitive TSE registration in 1988. Do not treat PCdoB as a later name for PCB. PCdoB should not be restricted to the 1985 definitive registration date if political legalisation is the trigger, since it attained legality in 1985. The PCB route's Soviet relationship should remain a possible external tendency, not a defining institutional fact for every communist coalition. [FGV CPDOC, PCB historical entry and legalisation in 1985](https://cpdoc.fgv.br/sites/default/files/verbetes/primeira-republica/Partido%20Comunista%20Brasileiro%20%28PCB%29%20-%20Primeira%20Rep%C3%BAblica.pdf); [Brazilian Ministry of Justice oral history on PCdoB legality in 1985 and registration campaign](https://www.gov.br/mj/pt-br/central-de-conteudo_legado1/anistia/anexos/livro_internet_fmg_repressao_capa.pdf); [TSE registration date record for PCdoB](https://www.senado.gov.br/relatorios_sgm/RelPresi/2005/P1_C_REPRESENTACAO_DOS_PARTIDOS.pdf).
- **PRN:** formation and name change belong in 1989. The current route keeps that distinction in context, but the combined `Lacerdists / PRN` group and shared top-level route still create a false throughline. Separate the identities and reuse only general themes such as executive reform, not party continuity.

## Programme and starting-spirit review

- **Oil exposure:** the historical difficulty is strong. Brazil imported about 80 percent of its oil consumption around the first oil shock; road transport and industrial feedstock were exposed. Electricity was a different story because hydropower already supplied most generation. Avoid wording or effects that imply imported petroleum was the principal source of electricity or that Brazil simply lacked fuel for all activity. Treat the fuel shock primarily as an import bill, foreign exchange, transport, petrochemical, and inflation problem. Keep 1973 and 1979 shocks as later events. `fuel_gain_factor` is a broad production effect and may communicate domestic fuel scarcity rather than external price vulnerability; if retained, make sure its meaning matches the native mechanic and preview. [World Bank, 1973 energy outlook](https://whenthefedspeaks.com/doc/ifdp_1973-37); [IMF, Brazil energy and debt profile in 1980](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/022/0018/004/article-A007-en.xml).
- **Miracle:** appropriate for a 1970 start as an inherited 1968 to 1973 boom, but do not imply the entire 1970s were a boom. The high growth coexisted with sharp income concentration and wage compression. Label the growth and credit dynamics as inherited from the prior regime's economic programme; the wage squeeze also predates Médici. Its mechanical modifiers are balance hypotheses rather than documented historical quantities.
- **Wage compression and inequality:** the starting idea has a sound evidence base, but its consumer-goods modifier is not a direct representation of the wage policy. Keep the institutional story tied to wage restraint, real wage distribution, and unequal growth gains. Avoid presenting the nationwide land inequality spirit as removable through one focus without a sustained state programme; current paid extension, land title, and settlement branches give useful alternative mechanisms.
- **Agriculture and Embrapa:** Embrapa's 1973 foundation, Cerrado research, public credit, extension, and later PRODECER cooperation are appropriately staged. Preserve the distinction between a scientific institution and broad commercial frontier expansion. The contested land, ecological, and unequal credit effects are accurate design concerns.
- **Embraer:** founded in 1969, so the institutional path can exist at start. The company initially produced the Bandeirante, licensed military Xavante, and licensed light aircraft before later products. An `arms_factory` can imply a military-only institution; frame this as aircraft manufacturing if that is the available abstraction, and use limited early output or research, not the later mature export industry. [BNDES, Brazilian aerospace industry history](https://web.bndes.gov.br/bib/jspui/bitstream/1408/919/1/A%20ind%C3%BAstria%20aeron%C3%A1utica%20no%20Brasil_P-final_BD.pdf).
- **Nuclear programme:** Angra I and US-Westinghouse cooperation predate the 1975 agreement. The Federal Republic of Germany peaceful nuclear pact is a valid Geisel-era technology-transfer initiative, but it aimed at an extensive and costly integrated cycle and did not deliver all intended enrichment capabilities as planned. Keep it as a costly, negotiated civilian technology offer, not an instant reactor or guaranteed mastery. The current concept correctly reserves actual mines, buildings, and fuel supply for separate paid projects.
- **CNBB:** historically plausible as a separate social interlocutor, but it was not a state welfare substitute. Dialogue, social mediation, human rights advocacy, and pastoral activity can sit beside secular service provision. Avoid hard-wiring Church engagement exclusively to one route.
- **Foreign affairs and bilateral terms:**
  - **United States:** 1977 is not a general rupture of relations. Brazil rejected security assistance conditioned on human-rights reporting, then denounced/rescinded four military-area agreements. The existing conditional `us_rights` event is well framed if it represents this precise issue. Keep industrial licensing distinct from military instruments, as the event currently does.
  - **China:** recognition on 15 August 1974 is a genuine Geisel precedent. Its earlier completion should be marked as counterfactual. The current trade proposal is directionally plausible.
  - **Soviet Union:** the 1975 machinery/equipment and Brazilian goods agreement and 1978 extension support the proposal; this was pragmatic trade, not a communist political alignment. The effects can be negotiated, but do not make Soviet ties a universal prerequisite of PCB government.
  - **Paraguay and Itaipu:** the 1973 treaty created a binational project. The current costs and benefits should be negotiated as a binational infrastructure and energy purchase arrangement, not generic equal-benefit construction. Paraguay and Brazil jointly owned Itaipu, but the financing burden, energy consumption, and purchase terms were asymmetric. Show meaningful Paraguay revenue/energy rights and Brazilian demand/offtake commitments so the offer is not a one-sided Brazilian build presented as parity. Keep Paraguay's acceptance separate and real. [Itamaraty, Itaipu treaty](https://concordia.itamaraty.gov.br/ApiConcordia/Acordo/integra-acordo-html/3848).
  - **Japan:** PRODECER was a phased Cerrado development partnership with Japanese cooperation and finance, not only a research seminar. Make the offer's reciprocal value concrete, and retain land, public finance, and environmental tradeoffs. Separate it from a Japanese steel/mineral offtake or investment consortium. For the latter, name the actual steel or mineral project, public-company equity, purchase commitments, and industrial capacity involved rather than a generic steel pact. [Embrapa Cerrados, regional programmes and financing](https://www.embrapa.br/web/cerrados/historia); [FUNAG, Japanese project and technology cooperation](https://funag.gov.br/loja/download/498-ensaios_sobre_a_heranca_cultural_japonesa.pdf).
  - **Angola:** recognition at independence in November 1975 was a significant foreign policy move, and Brazil-Angola technical cooperation followed in 1977. The current offer correctly requires Angolan independence and recognition and does not transfer oil or resources. Keep a distinction between recognition and the later cooperation pact.
  - **Argentina:** keep the Itaipu-Corpus settlement as tripartite Brazil-Paraguay-Argentina diplomacy. The bilateral Brazil-Argentina agreement cannot bind Paraguay. Nuclear cooperation and democratic confidence building are separate later developments as noted above.
  - **Uruguay:** the proposed port protocol is honestly described as a plausible RP agreement, but no particular precedent is named. Keep it explicitly counterfactual or ground it in a dated Brazil-Uruguay transport, port, or trade agreement before calling it historical.

## Events and overall assessment

The oil-shock and US-human-rights event framing is generally good and conditional. The oil event should distinguish the October 1973 first shock from the 1979 second shock if they are separate triggers. Keep `Prices, Wages and Credit` as a later conditional inflation response, and do not imply that one 365-day choice historically describes every stabilisation attempt. The labourist naming dispute belongs in 1979 to 1980, and should create distinct PTB and PDT identities through registration and naming, not just change generic stability.

The content is strongest where it gives policy benefits paired with explicit budgets, service costs, coalition penalties, land conflict, and foreign consent. Several modifier values remain game-balance proposals, not historical measurements. Preserve GDP-scaled costs, keep foreign arrangements reciprocal and exact in preview, and avoid prose suggesting partner consent, costs, revenue, or resource rights that their actual effects do not show.
