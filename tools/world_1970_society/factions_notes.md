IMPERIA COLD WAR

Internal faction research for 1 January 1970

The machine-readable ledger is `factions.json`. `factions_build.py` rebuilds that ledger only and never edits a country history. All three selected groups use existing MD idea IDs and existing opinion variables. No callback, new faction type, event, scan or recurring calculation is introduced by this research package.

The 397 inventory records comprise 393 completed representative profiles and four unassigned technical/fictional tags pending the user's review: ALN, ZOM, C01 and ADS. These four have empty idea lists and must not be installed from this ledger. Research uncertainty is recorded here and in JSON only, not as player-facing disclaimers or faction names.

An MD faction is a broad institutional constituency. A three-group choice is not a measured distribution of power, a demographic statistic, an equal governing coalition or an opinion poll. The three slots in `common/idea_tags/00_idea.txt` establish the normal presentation, but no script-level hard maximum of three was demonstrated. Existing historically supported groups and national mechanics must not be removed solely to force every bespoke country down to three groups.

The default opinion of 50 comes from `setup_init_factions` in `common/scripted_effects/00_internal_faction_effects.txt`. It is a neutral game-balance fallback, not historical public approval. Preserve authored 1970 opinion values, including values assigned by called national initializers. For example, the Soviet authored setup already uses differentiated cadre, military, security, defence, farmer and union values. The JSON `opinions` field is an absent-value fallback only.

All 1,179 proposed native memberships passed the existing `allowed` original-tag predicates. No profile selects oligarchs, Donju, Iranian Quds Force or foreign jihadis. The first two would misrepresent the Soviet/North Korean economy in this setting; the latter two introduce institutions and movements from much later periods. Generic religious and labour groups describe social presence and institutional influence, not automatic regime support or freedom to organize.

Evidence is strongest where contemporary records identify the institutions directly: SOV party/KGB/military, CHI post-1969 party/PLA, Saudi royal/religious/oil interests, the new Libyan RCC, Iranian imperial military/oil, Pakistan's army and western elite structure, Nigerian wartime government, Suharto's New Order, Thanom's Thailand, Frei's Chile and the 1969 European employer/worker economies. Thirty-nine registered sources include official contemporary diplomatic and intelligence records, OECD surveys, ILO reporting and institutions' own histories for name chronology.

Territorial releases require more inference. Their profiles describe the actual territory's 1970 social economy, not the future organization named by the mod tag. Examples include ordinary Yemeni constituencies for AQY/HOU, Somali pastoral/trading/religious society for SHB, Mexico's rural/landholding/commercial society for cartel tags, Soviet party/worker/agricultural institutions for later separatist tags and Lebanese rural/religious society for HEZ. Independence dates and later armed-party institutions are not backdated.

`F_CIA73` is a near-period structural cross-check, with a material limitation: search-indexed PDF text was accessible, but the direct PDF URL redirected to the CIA reading-room front page. It is not presented as a complete directly downloaded country-by-country archival review. Its 1973 political chronology is not used to overwrite known 1969 governments. The broad territorial mappings carry medium or low confidence and should be treated as historically constrained native-category approximations. They are not claimed as the sole objectively correct set of three interest groups for every region.

The native map can supply misleading geography. The ledger corrects these cases explicitly: GLC is a US Great Lakes/Michigan tag, not African Great Lakes; OMG's capital is Inner Mongolia in the PRC despite its name; SAZ uses Iranian Azerbaijan rather than its erroneous Balochistan capital; TLS uses Talysh/Soviet Azerbaijan rather than its erroneous Scottish capital; PAP uses Australian-administered Papua and New Guinea despite the scenario map owner; RYU is US-administered Okinawa before 1972.

Valid native IDs can have invalid modern display names. `localisation_notes` records the exact selected or supported-existing keys, their current text and proposed English era names. Particularly important are Soviet KGB instead of FSB, Korean KCIA instead of NIS, petroleum concessionaires instead of ADNOC/Qatar Petroleum/NOC before their creation, Arabian American Oil Company instead of the 1988 Saudi Aramco company, pre-nationalization Venezuelan petroleum companies instead of PDVSA, and state trade unions instead of post-Soviet federations. The full `LABELS` table also covers optional authored groups not selected in the representative three.

Any display-name routing should be limited to the 1970 start-date condition and return the exact existing MD label outside that condition. It must not add a regular simulation callback or replace faction membership/opinion mechanics. Generic names such as Organised Labour deliberately avoid inventing a specific historical federation where the original native federation was created decades later.

Local evidence for pending technical/fictional classification:

* `history/countries/ALN - Alien.txt` disables the economic system, loads `ALN_2000`, and grants `EH_the_unstoppable_army_idea` and alien/chimera technology. It is an extraterrestrial gameplay tag, not an historical country.
* `history/countries/ZOM - Zombies.txt` loads `ZOM_2000`, grants `Zombie` and `zombietech`, and disables the economic system. It is a zombie gameplay tag.
* `history/countries/C01 - Custom_01.txt` begins with an upstream comment expressly identifying a utility tag. It is not proof of a specific historical society.
* `history/countries/ADS - Aegist.txt` starts its native profile in 2000, inherits ENG technology and uses dynamic-rebel/cosmetic tags. `localisation/english/countries_l_english.yml` labels its political variants Albionis and its people Albionis Constituent. This is a fictional/custom-polity candidate, not an evidenced January 1970 state. Its classification remains for user review rather than an invented replacement history.
