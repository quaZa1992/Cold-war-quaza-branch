# IMPERIA COLD WAR

## Religion research for 1 January 1970

This pass reviewed all 397 country-history tags. It supplies 393 original native-idea classifications. ALN, ZOM, C 01 and ADS have no assignment and remain pending the user's review. No country history or runtime provider was edited by this research task.

The principal quantitative check was Maoz and Henderson's World Religion Dataset version 1.1, using its 138 state observations for 1970. The original CSV was downloaded only to a private temporary research location. It is not copied into the repository because COW's terms restrict redistribution. No per-denomination numerical data are reproduced in the deliverable. The output consists of coarse native-game classifications and original explanations.

Period territorial checks came from the UN Demographic Yearbook 1971, table 17, printed pages 510-514, and 1973, table 31, printed pages 486-489. Those PDFs were also consulted privately and are not included. They are valuable for the Pacific islands, Brunei, Macau, Zanzibar, Suriname, the Seychelles and Palestinian Gaza. Later retrospective institutional histories are separately identified in the source catalogue. A current census result is never silently represented as a 1970 enumeration.

## Native interpretation

Religion is one mutually exclusive native MD cultural-institutional law. It is not the government's official religious policy, personal observance of every resident, or a complete demographic model. Communist rule therefore does not automatically grant a country-specific state-atheism reward spirit. Catholic and Protestant traditions use `christian`; Eastern Orthodox uses `orthodox_christian`; the Armenian Apostolic and Ethiopian Tewahedo traditions use their existing Oriental Orthodox base ideas. The native spelling of Buddhist is `buddism`.

The menu lacks generic Sikh, indigenous African, Siberian customary, Chinese folk, Taoist, Confucian, Druze, Alawite and secular categories. Mixed landscapes or a poor specific fit use `pluralist`. This is a native-menu approximation, not a researched claim of an explicit pluralist constitutional policy. No focus-upgraded church, pagan revival or harmony reward is granted.

Religion-specific national institutions can be important even when suppression, secularization or settlement means they are not an active-observance majority. Mongolia and the Buryat, Kalmyk and Tuvan territorial cases are explicitly marked cultural-institutional interpretations. Other regional releases without a period religious census are marked territorial proxies with reduced confidence. Such a proxy is historical interpretation, not an invented source observation.

## Material corrections

- `SER` represents the Yugoslav federation in 1970. Its Orthodox, Catholic and Muslim populations require a whole-federation mapping. It is not given a Serbia-only Orthodox baseline.
- `SOV` represents the USSR, including Muslim, Catholic, Jewish and Buddhist republics as well as Orthodox and nonreligious populations. It is not treated as only the Russian republic.
- `KOR` and `NKO` are not assigned Cheondo as a generic Korean religion. `cheondo` names a particular movement.
- `MAC` uses the actual 1970 Macau census evidence for a Buddhist baseline. Portuguese rule does not make its population Catholic.
- `BHR` uses the 1970 UN background describing Shia and Sunni communities as near equal. A modern Shia demographic estimate is not backdated.
- `TIM` preserves indigenous/customary and Catholic religious diversity before the mass growth of Catholic affiliation after 1975.
- `KAC` avoids importing a later Kachin Christian-majority narrative. Historical research specifically cautions against that claim before the late 1970 s.
- `NIG` uses native `fifty_fifty_divide` for comparably large Christian/Muslim institutions, with indigenous traditions explicitly acknowledged. This does not claim a literal 50/50 census result. The 1963 census is disputed.
- `LEB` uses the native confessional divide idea for its Christian, Sunni, Shia and Druze institutional landscape.
- `OMG` follows its Inner Mongolian territorial anchor rather than copying independent Mongolia from its misleading filename. `SAZ` follows Iranian East Azerbaijan and `TLS` the Azerbaijani Talysh territory rather than erroneous capitals.
- `GLC` is the North American Great Lakes territory, not an African confederation. `PAP` follows Australian-administered Papua and New Guinea; `RYU` follows Okinawan culture under US administration before 1972.
- Later real organizations use the religious culture of their 1970 territories; their modern ideology and organizational existence are not projected backward. They are not classified as fictional simply because they appeared later.

## Provenance and validation

`religion.json` contains the full source titles, authors, dates, direct URLs, access date, known/unknown reuse conditions, original rationale, confidence, status and native mapping limitations. `religion_build.py` is original project code that regenerates the same JSON without redistributing or requiring any protected source data. It validates all 397 inventory tags, every idea against the current native list and every source reference.

The four withheld records contain `idea: null`. ALN is a fantasy alien tag, ZOM a fantasy zombie tag, C 01 a generic utility slot, and ADS an authored Albionis state tag. No fallback or runtime idea is supplied for them. The underlying native files and localisation are listed as the evidence, with exact authors and permissions unknown where not recorded.
