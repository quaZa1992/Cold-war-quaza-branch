# France: constrained rewards and coercive patronage

Authorized by the user's 30 October 2026 requests to remove the Niger and
comparable bilateral corporate-tax penalties, reduce French land-doctrine
rewards, and make French neocolonial leverage playable. The approved focus
snapshots remain immutable. `deal_balance_revision.py` applies the subsequent
balance instruction after their integration.

## Reward corrections

- Remove all 22 negative corporate-tax modifier rows from 19 bilateral
  agreements, on either side. Domestic tax policy and positive tax effects
  retain their existing values. Existing setup charges, operating expenses,
  service payments and aid remain separate terms.
- Remove the French land-doctrine awards from Gabon Officer Schools, Chadian
  National Cadres, Sahel Field Services and Beirut Field Mission. These were
  five half-price doctrine purchases in programmes primarily intended to train
  the recipient. Their other already approved effects remain.
- Reduce Professional Core from 50% to 25% for one purchase, the five NATO
  land-doctrine awards from 25% to 15% for one purchase, and African Security
  from 25% to 10% for one purchase. Other French domestic doctrine awards remain
  at most 25%, each for one purchase. Recipient training is unchanged.
- Previously awarded limited-use doctrine discounts are stored in existing
  saves. No supported removal effect was found in the installed native effect
  documentation. The correction changes new awards and does not claim to
  revoke spent or already stored research bonuses.

## Asymmetric bargaining

The existing African finance and security entries unlock six country-specific
pressure decisions. They require a currently active signed concession or
support agreement, rather than giving France arbitrary control over a tag.
France spends 35 political power to deliver a demand; the recipient has 30
days to accept or refuse. Silence resolves as refusal only if the original
eligibility remains valid. A 365-day cooldown prevents repeated demands.

Acceptance establishes an indefinite Exclusive Procurement Charter:

- Host setup expenditure: 0.10% of its own current GDP. French setup
  expenditure: 0.04% of its own current GDP.
- Host payment to France: 1.00% of the host's current GDP per year, accrued
  weekly as `host gdp_total * 0.01000 / 52`. The two ledgers read one cached
  amount. France's GDP is not the price base for this payment.
- Host civilian-factory construction speed: -10%; military-factory
  construction speed: -15%; equipment cost: +5%. Regime support supplies
  army organisation +4% and stability +4%.
- France receives military access and guarantees the host. Its charter spirit
  adds +3% army personnel cost per active partner. Native relations that
  predate the charter must not be claimed or removed by it.

Refusal preserves base concessions, resource rights, access and host-paid
fees. Only actual French-paid aid transfers to that host are frozen for 180
days, on both ends of each payment. A 90-day Withdrawal of French Support
crisis adds civilian-factory construction speed -10%, research speed -5%,
equipment cost +8% and nominal debt interest +0.50 percentage points. The
interest term affects existing debt and creates no loan or principal.

Standing charter spirits suspend together if the qualifying base relationship
is inactive, either country disappears, the two countries go to war, either
embargoes the other, or any mirrored Imperia sanction class applies. Restoration
replays neither setup expenditure nor one-time rewards. The ordinary base
agreements retain their existing war and sanctions lifecycle.

## Historical basis and scope

French military interventions, paid logistical support, contractors, monetary
institutions and extractive concessions provide the historical basis for
asymmetric dependence. The specific ultimatum, GDP levy and modifier values
are alternate-history RP choices and balance proposals. They are not asserted
to be literal clauses imposed on all six historical states. In particular,
SOMAIR was created in 1968 and began uranium production in 1971; January 1970
rights do not create automatic mines or raw production.

Primary sources and native API traces are recorded in
`RESEARCH_MECHANICS.md`. Source/model checks and the generated inspector are
review evidence; they do not establish observed HOI4 or live multiplayer
behaviour.
