# IMPERIA COLD WAR — French pressure mechanics research

## Recommendation

Use a recipient-approved, fixed-country ultimatum as a counterfactual 1970 policy choice. The current pressure contract sets a 1.00% annual levy on the host's own GDP, paid weekly as `gdp_total × 0.01 / 52`, alongside separately specified setup costs. On refusal, freeze only the French-funded weekly cash-support streams explicitly attached to this feature's qualifying signed agreements for 180 days. Leave the underlying signed base agreement, host obligations, access, guarantees and resource rights intact. Do not revoke or regrant them, and do not replay setup expenditure, construction, research or other finite rewards when the freeze ends.

The recipient must receive a real accept/refuse decision. No focus completion should sign an unequal clause by itself. An accepted procurement or payment clause transfers 1.00% of the host's own current GDP per year, accrued as `own gdp_total × 0.01 / 52` weekly expense for the host and the same amount as weekly income to France. Keep both sides on the existing paired weekly-transfer stream and verify that France's receipt exactly matches the host's payment in the same signed lifecycle state. This is a designed pressure term, not a claim that the historical defense agreements imposed a general GDP tribute.

Keep the proposed host charter penalties distinct and visible: civilian construction speed −10%, military construction speed −15%, and equipment cost +5%; pair them with the stated host-side regime-support effects of army organisation +4% and stability +4%. These remain signed base concessions while the 180-day freeze affects only the French aid/cash contribution stream. The separate 90-day withdrawal-crisis modifiers are civilian construction speed −10%, research speed −5%, equipment cost +8%, and interest-rate modifier +0.50. These are concrete political-economic consequences, not control of the government. Do not turn the package into a subject relationship, an annexation, or a global sanction. Keep each French support stream and receipt attributable to its agreement.

Do not describe `interest_rate_multiplier_modifier` as a credit line. The current 90-day withdrawal-crisis package applies `+0.50` alongside temporary construction, research and equipment-cost penalties. In native calculation this is a +0.50 percentage-point borrowing premium, only while the host has debt; it neither creates principal nor pays the host’s weekly budget. This is borrowing-cost pressure, not emergency credit. A real credit bridge would require a separate, explicit principal transfer and matching repayment/liability contract. The engine clamps the final rate to a minimum of 0.8, and the modifier has no cash effect for a country with no debt.

## Native mechanics verified in the current mod

- **Debt interest:** `common/scripted_effects/00_money_system.txt:2597-2657` calculates `debt_interest_premium = debt / gdp_total × 10`, adds `modifier@interest_rate_multiplier_modifier` directly to that rate, clamps the final `interest_rate` to `0.8..50`, then computes weekly `debt_rate`. `common/modifier_definitions/money_modifier_definitions.txt:9-12` classifies the modifier as a numeric value, not a percentage. In this formula `+0.50` adds 0.50 percentage points to the nominal rate, subject to clamp. The native key `interest_rate_multiplier_modifier_tt` formats the direct numeric change. It is not “+50% interest” or a treasury transfer.
- **Weekly cash flow:** `common/scripted_effects/00_money_system.txt:3737-3755` resets and adds country-specific expenses to `additional_expenses_rate`; the existing France foreign-contract integration has its own weekly expensing hook. Use an expense in the host scope and equal income in French scope for the procurement clause. Avoid applying a rate or transfer in one scope twice when recipient decisions and France callbacks both run.
- **Sanctions:** `common/scripted_effects/IMP_sanctions_support_effects.txt:6-50` contains the four native `IMP_sanctions_add_{dip,fin,eco,mil}_from_prev` helpers. They update both payer and recipient arrays and, for the recipient, add the matching opinion modifier. `common/scripted_effects/imp_sanctions_effects.txt:126-178`, `184-224`, `230-280`, and `316-350` turn those arrays into broad diplomatic, financial, economic, or military effects. The system recalculates globally on monthly refresh through `IMP_sanctions_refresh_world` in `common/scripted_effects/IMP_sanctions_support_effects.txt:56-64`; the hooks are in `common/on_actions/zzzzzz_imp_sanctions_on_actions.txt`. Those are actual sanctions, not aid-withdrawal effects. Do not add a France sanction array entry just to simulate ending a French service contract. If a future design explicitly wants sanctions, use the native paired arrays, opinions and recalculation path, and label the resulting broad costs honestly.
- **Guarantees:** Native application is `give_guarantee = TAG` (examples include `events/05_australia_events.txt:1636` and `events/05_north_korea_events.txt:1155`); `has_guaranteed = TAG` checks for the live relation (for example `events/05_australia_events.txt:6902-6926`). Native removal examples use `diplomatic_relation = { country = TAG relation = guarantee active = no }` (`events/05_china.txt:1050, 5859-5860`). The trigger only says the guarantee exists; it does not identify who granted it. Record an agreement-owned flag when the France deal creates a guarantee, and remove it only when that receipt is present. Never remove an older guarantee or one supplied by another country.
- **Military access:** use native `give_military_access = TAG` and `has_military_access_to = TAG`; removal is shown with `diplomatic_relation = { country = TAG relation = military_access active = no }` (`events/Canada.txt:1141`; also `events/Tajikistan.txt:2208-2258`). As with guarantees, require a feature-owned grant receipt before removal. If no receipt exists, do not claim or revoke the pre-existing right.
- **Resource rights:** The mod’s existing Iraq scripts use the native `give_resource_rights = { receiver = TAG state = ID resources = { ... } }` form (`common/scripted_effects/IRQ_1985_scripted_effects.txt:666-696`). The current foreign-policy package models Niger uranium rights in State 329 and already requires explicit receipt tracking. Use the matching native `remove_resource_rights = STATE` only for a receipt-owned Niger grant, after checking its actual resource-rights trigger; it is state-wide. Do not generate raw uranium in state history: the approved scenario begins at zero raw uranium production.

## Historical grounds and limits

The precedents establish close military and financial interdependence, including asymmetric terms, but they do not establish that every state accepted under coercion or that a GDP tribute clause was historical. The 24 April 1961 defense agreement with Côte d’Ivoire, Dahomey and Niger was approved by French law in July 1961. A later official French government impact study summarizes the continuing Côte d’Ivoire technical-assistance terms: French military personnel for organisation, framing and training at host request, and equipment support; 1965 logistics support was on a paid basis. That is a solid basis for modeling named services and negotiated rights, not an automatic French power to cancel the host’s sovereignty.

The French National Assembly’s 1964 official record states that French troops entered Gabon in February 1964 under Article 3 of the 17 August 1960 defense agreement following a request from the Gabonese authorities, and describes the mission as restoring public order. The later parliamentary report also lists Chad’s 1960 defense agreement and Operation Limousin, launched in 1969 at Chad’s request against the FROLINAT insurgency. These are evidence of French intervention and military dependence; the claimed requests and the formal bilateral framework matter. They are not proof of a recurring financial tribute.

The Banque de France account of the franc-zone agreements describes fixed parities, transfer freedom, pooled foreign reserves and French convertibility guarantees, alongside French technical support and increased African central-bank participation after the 1972 and 1973 agreements. These arrangements show structural leverage and a real sovereignty trade-off. They should not be recast as a simple French tax or as all six countries being under identical terms. Cameroon’s 1960 defense record is described as secret in later French parliamentary material; avoid inventing clause text not available in the primary public sources.

SOMAÏR was created in 1968; its first uranium production began in January 1971. In January 1970, uranium extraction is therefore not an operating revenue stream that the game should grant. The approved Niger rights may represent a negotiated development or purchase option, but the game should still require paid mine development before it produces uranium. Make clear that the new ultimatum is an alternate-history escalation built on a real concession and defense relationship, not a claim that the historical Republic of Niger signed this exact coercive package.

## Scope

This is a read-only mechanics and historical-source audit for pressure design in NGR, GAB, SEN, CAM, CDI and CHA. No runtime code or generated game files were modified. Historical treaty language supports modeling actual services, access, and concessions, but the proposed GDP transfer, host penalties, refusal ultimatum and timed suspension are new alternate-history game terms.
