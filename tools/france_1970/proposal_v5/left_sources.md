IMPERIA COLD WAR — France 1970–1990 left-branch research

Research and design note for PS, PCF, Communist Old Guard and PSU. This is not game implementation. Historical positions, later developments, counterfactuals and balance proposals are labeled separately in `research_left.json`.

## Institutional and party evidence

- **French Socialist Party chronology** — https://parti-socialiste.fr/lhistoire-du-ps/ (official party history). The Epinay reorganization occurred in June 1971; the Common Programme followed in June 1972. The January 1970 start therefore cannot be described as a unified Mitterrand-led PS government. Mitterrand's later 1981 nuclear posture is sourced separately.
- **Common Programme, 1972** — https://archives.assemblee-nationale.fr/4/cri/1972-1973-ordinaire1/038.pdf (primary Assemblée nationale debate containing the programme text). The proposal includes six-month service, trained and regionally organized reserves, democratic rights/status for soldiers and cadres, and career protections/reconversion. It is a political programme, not proof of an enacted military reform.
- **PCF 22nd Congress, 1976** — https://projet.pcf.fr/65225 (PCF institutional retrospective by former senior party officials). It explicitly recounts Marchais's announcement abandoning the dictatorship-of-the-proletariat notion, and describes the French democratic road and continuing Soviet-model identification among some members. This partisan retrospective is useful for the distinction between the actual PCF and the proposed Old Guard. An archival record for the congress film is https://www.cinearchives.org/catalogue-22eme-congres-du-parti-communiste-francais-1104-325-1-0.html, but that catalogue entry alone does not establish the doctrinal details.
- **PS defense debate, 1978** — https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1978/01/09/bull-la-convention-du-p-s-le-parti-socialiste-definit-sa-politique-de-defense-l-imbroglio_3131846_1819218.html (contemporary report on the party convention). It records internal positions for retaining nuclear weapons and NATO membership as well as amendments to abandon Mirage IV, Albion and Pluton. This supports a real internal strategic dispute, not a unanimous PS platform.
- **Mitterrand at Île Longue, 24 July 1981** — https://www.elysee.fr/francois-mitterrand/1981/07/24/declaration-de-m-francois-mitterrand-president-de-la-republique-a-lissue-de-sa-visite-a-lile-longue-brest-vendredi-24-juillet-1981 (primary Élysée statement). Mitterrand defends deterrence as prevention of war and national independence. Treat as a later presidential decision, not evidence of the PS's 1970 position.
- **PSU and self-management** — https://histoire-sociale.cnrs.fr/amp/le-psu-et-lautogestion/ (CNRS/Paris 1 Centre d'histoire sociale). Records the PSU's founding and its post-1968 development of self-management as a distinct identity, formally adopted in 1972. The party's 1972 manifesto and contemporary explanation are also documented at https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1972/12/13/le-p-s-u-a-refait-son-unite-autour-de-m-rocard-et-sur-la-base-du-socialisme-autogestionnaire_3141481_1819218.html; it distinguishes workers' control from state nationalization and identifies technocratic capture as a risk.
- **PSU popular defence, 1978** — https://www.institut-tribune-socialiste.fr/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/78-02-02-APPEL-FRONT-AUTOGEST.pdf (primary appeal held by the Institut Tribune Socialiste archive). The appeal calls for self-management, demilitarized popular defence, and rejection of civil and military nuclear power. This is a later 1970s platform strand; applying it as a governing programme is explicit extrapolation.
- **France and NATO** — https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/nato-history/history-by-theme/my-country-and-nato/france-and-nato (official NATO history). France left NATO's integrated command in 1966 but remained in the Alliance; later military cooperation arrangements preserved French national command. All political routes can therefore debate alignment and deterrence while retaining sovereign command; leaving NATO should not be inferred.

## Country-specific cooperation precedents

- **Federal Republic of Germany / Airbus** — https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2022-10-28-october-50th-anniversary-of-the-first-flight-of-the-airbus-a300 (Airbus corporate history). Airbus Industrie GIE was established on 18 December 1970 with French SNIAS and West German Deutsche Airbus holding equal initial shares. Supports negotiated aerospace workshare and industrial standards; it does not establish a bilateral military treaty. A later joint tank/satellite announcement is in a 1980 presidential statement: https://www.vie-publique.fr/discours/133169-declaration-de-m-valery-giscard-destaing-lissue-du-35eme-sommet-fra.
- **Soviet Union** — https://www.elysee.fr/valery-giscard-destaing/1975/10/17/declaration-sur-le-developpement-de-lamitie-et-de-la-cooperation-entre-la-france-et-lunion-sovietique-moscou-le-17-octobre-1975 (primary French presidential declaration). It covers industrial, scientific, technical, environmental, cultural and educational exchange in the détente/Helsinki context. It supports reciprocal civilian/industrial offers; military staff or arms cooperation is separately labeled counterfactual.
- **Yugoslavia** — https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20764/v764.pdf (primary UN Treaty Series text). France and Yugoslavia's scientific and technical cooperation agreement entered into force in 1966; it encourages research-institution cooperation and periodic joint programmes. For self-management context, the 1974 Yugoslav Constitution text is reproduced at https://www.worldstatesmen.org/Yugoslavia-Constitution1974.pdf. It documents an institutional model, not proof that it could be transplanted without friction.
- **German Democratic Republic** — https://www.un-ilibrary.org/content/books/9789210594844s002-c013 (UN Treaty Series record). France and the GDR signed an economic, industrial and technical cooperation agreement in Paris on 19 July 1973. The source supports civilian machine-tool, maintenance and technical exchanges; it does not support an arms transfer claim.
- **People's Republic of China** — https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/13/rap-dian/dian003-2012.asp (National Assembly historical report). France recognized the PRC in 1964; a scientific and technical cooperation agreement followed in 1978. Use for voluntary civilian research contacts only; the source does not imply a shared military doctrine or Soviet alignment.
- **Algeria** — https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000000505851 (primary French legal publication) records a France–Algeria cartographic cooperation convention signed 2 August 1968. A UN Treaty Series record, https://treaties.un.org/doc/publication/unts/volume%20729/v729.pdf, records French expert, training and equipment support for Algerian agricultural education. Together these support mapping, engineering-school and technical-training offers, not combat cooperation.

## Design synthesis and limits

The PS fork uses the documented split over nuclear doctrine to oppose an expensive specialist deterrent/cadre posture to reserve depth, conventional territorial defence and civilian conversion. The PCF fork uses its Common Programme's proposed regional reserves against a hypothetical centralized public arms command. PSU's documented worker self-management and later demilitarized popular-defence platform inform a distinct citizen-defence choice against a limited professional force. The Old Guard choice is intentionally counterfactual and should never be rendered as documented policy of a separate real party.

Each compact in `research_left.json` names a fixed partner state and identifies the actual precedent. Benefits are reciprocal, topic-limited and designed as two-sided lasting effects. Each proposal requires the recipient player's explicit acceptance. Both parties' spirits end together on withdrawal, war or sanctions between the two states, or loss of a signatory. A government change does not end an accepted compact; it can invalidate only a pending proposal whose mandate no longer holds. No proposal changes a foreign government or imposes sanctions or mutual-war commitments. When only civilian or industrial cooperation is documented, a military framing is explicitly marked as extrapolation and should remain limited to training, maintenance or dual-use methods.

All technology rewards are design guidance, not executable assignments. Before implementation, verify exact local technology IDs, years, DLC gates and special-project requirements; use one-use bonuses only for named eligible research and do not complete a project or grant a generic military-category bonus.
