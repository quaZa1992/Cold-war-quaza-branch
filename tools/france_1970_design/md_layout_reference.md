## Geometry measurements and examples

The generic tree mixes independent top-row section heads with compact, anchored follow-ups. In its first row, political reform is at `(-7,0)`, international diplomacy `(4,0)`, roads `(6,0)`, ease of business `(14,0)`, air reform `(38,0)`, and army reform `(49,0)`. The broad root span is purposeful for a large generic tree; it is not a small-country target width. Standard child steps use one row per stage and often one grid column sideways. Example: `GENERIC_build_railways` is emitted `(1,1)` relative to roads and resolves to `(7,1)`, exactly one column right and one row down. `GENERIC_debt_refactoring` is `(-3,1)` from ease-of-business, resolving `(11,1)`. `GENERIC_fighter_planes` is `(-1,1)` under air reform, resolving `(37,1)`. Source: `00_generic.txt:1637-1695`, `:2412-2429`, `:5153-5218`.

A second pattern is a longer section spine rooted at a stable focus: Russia's Putin cluster uses `SOV_putin` (declared at `05_russia.txt:109`) as its anchor. `SOV_stabilisation_fund` resolves to `(75,3)` from `(0,2)` and costs 35 days; `SOV_putinism` resolves `(75,5)` from `(0,4)` and costs 35 days. Russia's far-right economic programme has its own anchor: `SOV_economic_slavneft` resolves to `(147,6)` from `SOV_economic_russian`, with `(-5,6)` emitted offset; `SOV_economic_sber_reorg` resolves `(175,3)` from `SOV_economic_mmvb`, with `(2,2)` offset. Both are 35 and 21 day focuses respectively. Source: `05_russia.txt:279-316`, `:389-415`, `:601-645`, `:34216-34252`, `:34514-34534`.

Spacing is a tree-specific visual rhythm, not a single mandated gap. A meaningful stage change usually advances `y` by one or two grid units; branch choices spread horizontally, while root-to-root gaps can be much larger to reserve their full downstream region. Do not copy Russia's absolute x coordinates or its superpower-wide canvas. Keep children anchored to earlier-declared IDs, preserve empty space for the entire visible subtree, and avoid tight horizontal rows when focus titles or icons approach adjacent cards. Grid units are not screen pixels. I did not derive pixel/card bounds or view a live game screenshot in this read-only source task.

## Prerequisite groups and choices

Separate `prerequisite` blocks are AND; repeated `focus` entries inside a single block are OR. The Russian `SOV_putinism` has two separate blocks, requiring both `SOV_young_guard` and `SOV_reform_sovfed` (`05_russia.txt:398-400`). `SOV_economic_slavneft` also requires both Gazprom Neft and Rosneft reform in separate blocks (`:34225-34227`). The generic sample `GENERIC_build_railways` has one predecessor and a visibly direct spine (`00_generic.txt:1677-1680`).

Exclusive alternatives remain present as cards. `SOV_oligarchic_republic` is gated by two independent prerequisites and has separate mutual exclusions against state corporatism and the combined economic system (`05_russia.txt:610-613`). Its emitted position is `(-1,7)` from `SOV_putin`; no inference that exclusivity hides the other card is appropriate. Preserve grouped logic and the full simultaneous-visible footprint in a France layout.

## Application to a new France concept

Use the donor geometry as visual grammar only: one stable entry per coherent policy section; one-step rows for simple progression; paired columns/rows for genuine alternatives; direct joins where branches truly reconverge; and enough lateral room for the full titles, icons and visible alternative subtree. Keep an economical tree sized to real January 1970 French institutions and political choices. Define absolute design positions first, then emit relative offsets against earlier stable anchors. Preserve exact AND/OR predecessor grouping and mutual-exclusion semantics. Sample costs, icons, reward bundles and flavour from main MD as relevant references, but calibrate mechanics and language to 1970 France rather than transferring Russian/modern scale. Preview readable branch detail as well as the whole footprint; the installed native game is the final authority for card fit, titlebars, camera and connector routing.
