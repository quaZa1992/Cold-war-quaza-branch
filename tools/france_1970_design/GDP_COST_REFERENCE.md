# France 1970 GDP-scaled costs and native reward reference

**Scope:** Read-only audit for the France 1970 concept and requested fiscal-policy redesign. The reviewed concept is `FRA2 concept 1.1: Gaullist NATO`, 152 focuses, SHA-256 `BBE435370FBFF890169652BA0A25A0E4244236E803B7761062A7FB5C014EFB22`. I read the project economy-stream skill and fiscal contract. No builder, generated design data, or loaded game code was changed. No tests or game runs were performed.

## Money units and payment paths

The native aggregate is `gdp_total`, MD's country GDP measure in billions of internal dollars. Its sector calculation sums civilian and military factories, dockyards, offices, agriculture, energy, transport and resource output (`common/scripted_effects/00_money_system.txt:6203-6220`; the formula describes GDP total as “in billions” at `:4795`). GDP is the annual economic base, not a weekly cash flow. In the 1970 setup, the internal price scale is year-2000 dollars: the project era-reference effect documents the `1970 GDP x 3.4` conversion and a separate historical-dollar display (`common/scripted_effects/mdcw_era_reference_effects.txt:3-18`). Use the internal `gdp_total` for script arithmetic. The displayed historical-dollar variable is not the money-system input.

`modify_treasury_effect` adds `treasury_change` to `treasury` and updates the money UI (`common/scripted_effects/00_budget_effects.txt:1482-1496`). For a one-time GDP-share fee, the native idiom is to set `treasury_change = gdp_total`, multiply by a negative share, and call `modify_treasury_effect`. There is no need to also subtract from `additional_expenses_rate`; that would turn one project cost into a second weekly debit. France has GDP-share focus payments at `common/national_focus/05_france.txt:16163-16164` (1%) and `:19694-19695` (0.5%). The UK has a 3% GDP focus payment at `common/national_focus/05_united_kingdom.txt:21270-21273`. Iraq's native focuses use a 1% payment at `common/national_focus/05_iraq.txt:8465-8471` and `:10653-10661`. Russia's inspected examples instead use flat MD-dollar treasury debits, such as `SOV_stabilisation_fund` at `common/national_focus/05_russia.txt:294-300` (-155), `SOV_exploit_natural_wealth` at `:933-936` (-7.5), and `SOV_mitigate_financial_crisis` at `:1686-1692` (-10). The native source therefore supports both fixed and GDP-relative prices; the requested redesign should consistently use the latter.

The current France design still encodes 67 focus treasury effects as flat costs from 0.05b to 0.50b. It also has 19 bilateral offers with fixed setup charges and flat weekly amounts. Its 8 direct building rewards each include a slot count equal to the level count. These counts were checked against the current concept hash above. Replace the flat treasury fields and agreement prices in the redesign; preserve the building slot quantities and show both charges and rewards in native previews.

## Recommended GDP-share price bands

These are proposed balance bands, not historical spending estimates. The payer's own January-1970 `gdp_total` is the base for every country's share. For a bilateral agreement, France pays `France gdp_total x French share`; the partner pays `partner gdp_total x partner share` inside that partner's country scope. Never charge a partner a percentage of French GDP or reuse France's computed invoice for the partner.

| Purpose | Suggested one-time cost | Suggested annual recurring budget | Design cue |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Cabinet study, diplomatic consultation, limited technical exchange | 0.05-0.15% of payer GDP | 0.02-0.08% of payer GDP/year | A narrow report or staff exchange with no building grant. |
| Public administration, education reform, survey, technical institute | 0.10-0.30% | 0.05-0.15%/year | A durable national capacity change or one limited research/doctrine reward. |
| One-state sector or transport programme, industrial tool-up, major bilateral exercise | 0.25-0.75% | 0.10-0.25%/year | A concrete building or sizeable persistent benefit. |
| Multi-state rail, major equipment, nuclear or multi-ministry plan | 0.75-1.50% | 0.15-0.40%/year | Reserve for large projects with a clear follow-up and high construction cost. |
| Exceptional national project | 1.50-3.00% | individually justified | Use rarely; show the funded asset, slot grant, and follow-on upkeep explicitly. |

For a standing programme whose policy is an annual appropriation, compute each signatory's weekly stream as `country gdp_total x annual_share / 52`. That converts a national annual budget share into the existing weekly MD unit. Example: a proposed 0.10% annual programme is `gdp_total x 0.001 / 52`, recalculated in each country's own scope. Keep the share literal to five decimal places or fewer; the runtime division avoids storing a long weekly constant. Choose the share with the smallest partner economy in mind and verify that the resulting bill and tooltip do not round to zero. The displayed numeric weekly bill must use enough precision for the actual value.

Use one clear price contract per project. Focus/decision one-time cost: calculate the GDP share when the effect executes and call `modify_treasury_effect` once. Bilateral acceptance: calculate and charge each signatory from its own GDP inside the same recipient-approved transaction, with separate country receipts. Standing service: store the signed agreement once, then recalculate a zero-initialized weekly stream from the current own-GDP share; suspend it whenever the agreement's real activation conditions fail. Setup charges and weekly appropriations are distinct and never charged through the same accumulator.

The Iraqi authoring utility demonstrates a frozen quote where deferred delivery or an old-save upgrade requires a stable invoice: `tools/iraq_1970/common.py:17-19` freezes `gdp_total x share` once behind a quoted flag, then `pay_var()` debits that stored invoice. Its compiler uses quote flags and a treasury-balance delivery guard at `tools/iraq_1970/compile.py:114-121,193-216`; the non-executing preview shows the stored quote after quoting and current GDP estimate before quoting in `tools/iraq_1970/presentation.py:91-108`. That is a deliberate deferred-transaction pattern, not a reason to add invoices to immediate France focus payments. This user request explicitly rules out new Treasury availability gates: do not copy the Iraq `treasury >= invoice` gate into France focus or offer availability. If a delayed transaction is actually needed, present the quote and retain its once-only receipt, but do not make Treasury balance an additional eligibility requirement.

Preview blocks must not execute the payment. Native MD shows variable arithmetic inside `effect_tooltip` for a numeric preview; the real debit belongs in the hidden, one-time transaction. Building helpers do not silently pay the Treasury cost: France grants offices/factories in focus effects and separately calls `modify_treasury_effect`, e.g. `common/national_focus/05_france.txt:1048-1064` and `:1497-1527`. Add exactly one explicit payment and do not attach a second charge to the slot or building helper.

## Weekly expense integration

The correct aggregate is `additional_expenses_rate` (plural), not a guessed singular alias. `calculate_additional_expense_rate` first resets it to zero and then adds active programme values (`common/scripted_effects/00_money_system.txt:3697-3706`); signed agreement lines enter at `:4587-4605`. `ingame_update_setup` calls the income and expense calculations during the native weekly refresh (`:247-269`), and the stream contributes to the displayed expense at `:4708-4717`. The expense block itself labels these variables weekly (`:3732` onward). The weekly stream is not an immediate Treasury transfer: it is included in the recurring MD expense/balance path.

For France programmes, add each stream only after setting its contribution to zero, then add the active annual-GDP-share conversion once. On suspension, expiry, parent loss, war or sanctions, a fresh recalculation must leave that contribution at zero. A restored treaty restarts the stream but does not replay either country's setup fee, buildings, XP, research, or other one-time awards. This is consistent with the project fiscal contract and the current NATO treaty design. Use at most five fractional digits in persisted/scripted budget shares, keep derived weekly math in runtime arithmetic, and ensure each stream enters the aggregate exactly once.

## Follow-up verification: research slots, faction support, and stockpiles

### Research slots

`add_research_slot` is a supported effect used by the native GDP slot decisions (`common/decisions/00_research_slot_decisions.txt:19-20,46-47`). It is already owned by a centralized GDP system: MD sets `BASE_RESEARCH_SLOTS = 2` (`common/defines/MD_defines.lua:177`), then `research_slot_init_for_country` resets the current slot count and grants GDP and GDP-per-capita tiers (`common/scripted_effects/00_research_slots_effects.txt:2-114`). The project 1970 threshold table is distinct (`common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_research_slot_thresholds.txt:2-22`); native decisions can later grant or remove slots as GDP crosses its thresholds (`common/decisions/00_research_slot_decisions.txt:1-107`). The scripted tier arrays allow up to nine GDP and six GDP-per-capita grants, and no additional `add_research_slot` cap was found in the MD scripts. The history file has no direct French `add_research_slot` call, but the actual January start count depends on GDP initialization and threshold processing; this source-only audit does not prove the live-game count. **Recommendation: do not add a permanent France focus slot.** It would compete with the economy-managed slot total and could be removed by the native GDP-loss decision because it is not included in that system's receipt counters. Use one-use category research or a limited doctrine reward instead.

### Internal faction opinion

The native opinion helper's caller input is `temp_opinion`, set to a signed value immediately before `change_<faction>_opinion = yes`. For example, France does `set_temp_variable = { temp_opinion = -7.5 }` then `change_labour_unions_opinion = yes` (`common/national_focus/05_france.txt:1108-1109`). The shared helpers are documented and implemented in `common/scripted_effects/00_internal_faction_effects.txt:1021-1060,1194-1266`: the helper copies the input into an internal `temp_opinion_change`, changes the relevant country variable, clamps opinion to 0-100, prints the native opinion tooltip, and reapplies the faction's dynamic modifier. Callers must use `temp_opinion`, not `temp_opinion_change`. The helper is a no-op unless the matching idea is present. Positive changes double only when `autocrats_opinion_change` recognizes one of its autocratic government checks (`:1001-1018`); France's UDR Western Conservatism trigger is not one of those checks. January-1970 France creates/retains the conglomerates, unions and farmers ideas, initializes each opinion to 50 if absent, and applies their dynamic modifiers (`history/countries/FRA - France.txt:89-119`), making these precise local policy consequences usable without inventing new opinion variables.

### Construction cost and stockpile boundaries

France focus examples build offices, infrastructure and factories with `add_building_construction` and add a separate, explicit Treasury debit through `modify_treasury_effect` (`common/national_focus/05_france.txt:1048-1064,1503-1527`). The native building helper has no MD Treasury charge parameter, and the inspected building-related on-actions/scripts do not attach a second generic flat Treasury bill to those direct focus grants. Therefore one explicit GDP-share `treasury_change` plus the slot/construction effects is not double-charging an MD helper. This conclusion concerns the MD Treasury path; it does not claim the engine's normal queued construction costs are Treasury payments. Keep slot, ownership, control and capacity checks in the grant transaction.

France's 1970 history deliberately uses empty land, naval and air OOBs (`history/countries/FRA - France.txt:54-61`), while the date-specific French infantry profile enables `infantry_weapons_1`, 1960 utility-vehicle research and support-engineer technologies (`common/scripted_effects/mdcw_1970_infantry_research_effects.txt:3287-3326`, applied by the startup hook `common/on_actions/mdcw_infantry_legacy_on_actions.txt:4-16`). `infantry_weapons_1` is a real, 1965 MD equipment type (`common/units/equipment/MD_infantry_equipment.txt:118-153`). It is the only low-risk example I verified as both specifically enabled for France in 1970 and an explicit stockpile type. If a small finite stockpile is necessary, consider that equipment with a deliberately modest count and show it in the focus preview. Prefer one-time army XP, a limited support-weapons/engineering research bonus, or a valid local facility when the intended OOB is still empty. Do not reuse the modern France 50-unit MBT/IFV grant: its tank/IFV technology and equipment availability are not established by the January-1970 profile. The evidence here verifies equipment keys and startup technology, not actual stockpile usability in an engine run.

## Design consequences for the current France concept

1. Replace every one-time flat `treasury` cost with a signed GDP-share cost, calculated from the payer's live `gdp_total` when it is charged. Do not add Treasury balance to focus/offer availability.
2. Replace every fixed bilateral setup payment with separate French and partner GDP-share amounts. Compute each inside the payer's own country scope and show both before recipient consent.
3. Convert each flat weekly programme amount into an annual GDP appropriation share divided by 52, recalculate from the signatory's own GDP in the weekly stream, and show the numeric resulting weekly amount. Keep stream state and finite awards separate.
4. The existing project concept's 8 buildings already carry equal level/slot counts; preserve those. For new rail, air and naval ideas, verify exact province/state and capacity before approving the reward, and apply the extra-slot project rule even where the native building does not use shared capacity.
5. Use specific sector productivity, efficiency/cap, finite research/doctrine, tax/influence, education, energy, logistics and equipment rewards with clear trade-offs. Track route ceilings and national starting spirits; avoid stacking broad factory output, research speed or manpower across every branch.
