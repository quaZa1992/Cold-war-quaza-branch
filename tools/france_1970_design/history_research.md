# France: historical design research, January 1970 to 1990

## Starting position in January 1970

**Fact.** Georges Pompidou had been president since June 1969, following de Gaulle’s resignation after losing the April referendum. He appointed Jacques Chaban-Delmas prime minister. The June 1968 parliamentary election had left a large Gaullist majority, so the Fifth Republic began this period with strong executive authority and a dependable Assembly majority. Chaban nevertheless advocated stronger parliamentary rights and a more open governing style. His 16 September 1969 programme, “Nouvelle Société,” diagnosed a fragile, under-industrialized and inflation-prone economy, an overcentralized state, and archaic social structures. It proposed faster education investment, technical training, university and broadcasting autonomy, administrative modernization, regional development and regular union dialogue. [French National Assembly, Chaban-Delmas’s programme](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/chaban1969.asp); [French National Assembly, Pompidou biography](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/gouv_parl/fiches_personnalites/Pompidou.asp).

**Fact.** May 1968 had not simply vanished after the June legislative victory. Its strikes exposed wage, workplace-authority and representation disputes: the OECD estimated roughly 9 to 10 million strikers, and Grenelle produced wage increases, shorter hours and improved benefits. University protest followed overcrowding, rigid administration and demands for student participation. The 12 November 1968 Faure law already created autonomous public scientific and cultural institutions and reorganized university units, so 1970 policy can plausibly test implementation, funding and order within an existing reform rather than inventing university autonomy from scratch. [French Ministry of Labour, Grenelle account](https://travail-emploi.gouv.fr/etudes-realisees-sur-les-syndicats); [OECD, France 1969 survey](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/1969/04/oecd-economic-surveys-france-1969_g1g16e17/eco_surveys-fra-1969-en.pdf); [French National Assembly, Edgar Faure and the 1968 law](https://www2.assemblee-nationale.fr/sycomore/fiche/2922).

**Fact.** Do not treat January 1970 as post-oil-shock crisis. France had devalued the franc by 11.11% in August 1969 and tightened credit and demand. The balance of payments improved and returned to surplus early in 1970. Industrial output remained high: official retrospective reporting puts 1970 industrial value-added growth at 6.9%, after 8.9% in 1969. Export demand and capital-goods demand benefited from the devaluation and ongoing investment, but inflation, capacity constraints, sectoral employment imbalances and disparities among regions persisted. The 1970 slowdown was temporary; policy eased later that year. Treat these as stabilization and distribution choices, not a preordained collapse. [IMF, 1969 franc adjustment](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781451971477/ch022.xml); [IMF, balance-of-payments recovery](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/book/9781616351830/ch005.xml); [INSEE historical industry report, 1970](https://www.persee.fr/doc/estat_0336-1454_1971_num_28_1_2143).

**Fact.** The modernization challenge was real. Chaban explicitly called for more industrialization and the state already had tools such as national planning, public enterprises, DATAR (created 1963), regional development and large projects. Yet modernization was uneven: northern and eastern industrial basins contrasted with agricultural and depopulating parts of the west and south; Paris dominated higher education and services. Three quarters of the population lived in just 12% of communes by 1968. Agricultural mechanization and rural departures improved productivity but hollowed out small farms and remote communities. Chaban’s regional planning, metropolitan development and local-government proposals were actual political remedies, not a generic fictional regionalism. [Peer-reviewed territorial history](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21681376.2015.1090887); [French National Archives, rural change 1961-1968](https://archives-nationales-travail.culture.gouv.fr/en/Decouvrir/Dossiers-du-mois/Ruralite-modernite-inegalites-la-France-agricole-a-l-epreuve-du-progres-social-1961-1968).

**Fact.** Algeria’s independence in 1962 and the end of empire remained politically charged. Repatriated pieds-noirs, harkis and officers carried distinct grievances; an official later parliamentary review describes the persistent political and bilateral weight of Algerian memories. Large immigration and postcolonial labor were also part of France’s industrial society. At the 1970 start, this is best represented as contested integration, labor and memory politics rather than a mature mass far-right electoral movement. [French National Assembly report on Algeria and memory](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/14/rap-info/i1501.asp); [Oxford, harki memory and activism, 1962-1991](https://academic.oup.com/fh/article-abstract/23/1/88/668266).

## Political landscape and plausible constituencies

Use date-specific organizations. The governing core was the **Union des démocrates pour la République (UDR)**, the Gaullist party, alongside the **Fédération nationale des républicains indépendants (RI)** around Valéry Giscard d’Estaing. The **Centre démocratie et progrès (CDP)** was formed in July 1969 by Jacques Duhamel and allies who joined Pompidou’s majority. The separate **Centre démocrate (CD)** under Jean Lecanuet remained a centrist formation; it was not yet the later CDS/UDF. On the left, use the **Parti communiste français (PCF)**, the **Parti socialiste (PS)** formed through the 1969 SFIO-led refoundation, the **Convention des institutions républicaines (CIR)** around François Mitterrand until its 1971 merger into the PS, and the **Parti socialiste unifié (PSU)** as a smaller, distinctive left current. The 1970 frame should not call the PS a later unified force under Mitterrand; that consolidation came at Épinay in 1971. [French National Assembly election records](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/histoire/leg5repnew.asp); [National Archives, CD and CDP institutional records](https://rdf.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/garance/entities/agent/050624/); [Larousse, CIR timeline](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/divers/Convention_des_institutions_r%C3%A9publicaines/114562).

The radical right needs restraint. **Ordre nouveau** existed from 1969 as a small, activist neo-fascist group; it helped found the Front national in 1972, which was not yet a 1970 mass electoral contender. An Action française or monarchist current is defensible only as a marginal intellectual/activist milieu, not as a major parliamentary party. [UQAM doctoral history of Ordre nouveau](https://archipel.uqam.ca/14434/); [Oxford, origins of the French far right and FN](https://academic.oup.com/book/34919/chapter-abstract/298502500).

## Real policy precedents through 1990

- **Social contract or conflict:** Chaban’s 1969-72 “Nouvelle Société” offered consultation, labor contracts, minimum-wage action and social reform within a market economy. This is a real alternative to a purely coercive answer to 1968, with cabinet and Gaullist resistance as the political cost. The 1970s also saw union and workplace bargaining as active questions.
- **State-led modernization and energy:** France’s industrial strategy already supported aerospace, computing, telecoms, civil nuclear research and later Airbus; Concorde began in 1962. The 1974 Messmer nuclear acceleration followed the 1973 oil shock, so nuclear independence can be foreshadowed as a strategic debate in 1970, but not justified by a shock that had not happened yet. [OECD 1969 survey](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/1969/04/oecd-economic-surveys-france-1969_g1g16e17/eco_surveys-fra-1969-en.pdf); [EDF institutional history](https://www.edf.fr/groupe-edf/le-groupe-edf/histoire).
- **Regional voice:** Chaban’s promised administrative decentralization and DATAR-style investment were available precedents. The 1972 regional public establishments and the 1982 Defferre laws provide a credible arc from planning under prefects to elected regional government. [French Interior Ministry, decentralization chronology](https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/fr/Archives/Archives-des-dossiers/2012-Dossiers/La-decentralisation-a-30-ans).
- **Economic direction:** The 1981 left government combined nationalization, higher social benefits, shorter work time and public investment, then shifted toward fiscal and monetary restraint in 1983 to hold the franc in the European Monetary System. This offers a historical policy swing with clear employment, inflation, trade and coalition trade-offs, not a frictionless ideology reward. [Citéco, 1981-83 economic policy](https://www.citeco.fr/10000-ans-histoire-economie/monde-contemporain/politique-economique-de-l-union-de-la-gauche-puis-le-tournant-de-la-rigueur).

## Design interpretation

**Plausible alternate history:** A stronger Chaban coalition could deepen labor bargaining, university reform, regional investment and parliamentary oversight, while facing Pompidou, UDR conservatives, inflation and employer resistance. A more technocratic Pompidou course could prioritize export competitiveness, industrial concentration and central planning, accepting weaker local and labor voice. A left route can credibly develop through PS-CIR convergence after 1971 and the common programme with PCF after 1972, with internal disagreements over ownership, Europe and fiscal limits.

**Balance proposals, not historical facts:** Present the central trade-offs as productivity versus regional equity; order versus participation after 1968; wage gains versus inflation and competitiveness; and strategic autonomy versus budgetary burden. Keep separate starting condition and later crisis: oil vulnerability exists in 1970, but the 1973 shock and Messmer plan are future events. Avoid workforce penalties as a proxy for labor tension; use bargaining pressure, industrial capacity, inflation or unrest mechanics if the tree needs costs.

## Additional constraints and social conditions

**Housing and migrant labor.** France’s labor market was tight by the standards of the 1970s: the OECD's historical series gives 1.6% unemployment in 1970, although measurement was imperfect and regionally uneven. Tight aggregate labor did not mean equal security. OECD identified shortages of skilled industrial labor alongside excess supply in some service work, and regional mismatch. Immigrant workers often had poor housing: National Archives labor history says precarious housing still affected more than 10% of people of foreign origin at the start of the 1970s. In Nanterre, departmental archives count 4,007 people in bidonvilles in 1970, mostly Algerian; the last were cleared in late 1970 or early 1971. A plausible policy conflict is labor demand and cheap housing versus urban clearance, discrimination, and long-term public housing provision. [OECD, France 1972 survey](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/1972/02/oecd-economic-surveys-france-1972_g1g16e93/eco_surveys-fra-1972-en.pdf); [French National Archives, immigrant workers and housing](https://archives-nationales-travail.culture.gouv.fr/Decouvrir/Expositions/Expositions-virtuelles/Ouvrieres-et-ouvriers-immigres-en-France-aux-19e-et-20e-siecles/3-Experiences-de-la-main-d-aeuvre-ouvriere-immigree/3-1-Une-vie-partagee-entre-le-travail-et-le-foyer/3-1-2-Le-reconfort-du-foyer-1-Le-temps-de-l-arrivee); [Hauts-de-Seine departmental archives, Nanterre bidonvilles](https://archives.hauts-de-seine.fr/n/bidonvilles/n%3A247).

**Skills and education.** Higher education reform was under way, but not enough technicians and qualified workers were being trained for the planned growth of industry. OECD reported that 1968-70 saw high capacity use, persistent vacancies and skills/geographic mismatch; its Sixth Plan forecasts expected shortfalls in technical-college output, reaching 49,000 to 61,000 per year by 1974 in its comparison. Chaban's extra investment in technical education therefore had a real, measurable rationale. [OECD, France 1972 survey](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/1972/02/oecd-economic-surveys-france-1972_g1g16e93/eco_surveys-fra-1972-en.pdf); [OECD, France 1973 survey](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/1973/02/oecd-economic-surveys-france-1973_g1g16ebb/eco_surveys-fra-1973-en.pdf).

**Women and family law.** Reform was incremental, and major inequities remained. By January 1970 married women could work without a husband's authorization and administer their own property, but he still administered common marital property; the 1972 equal-pay principle had not yet been enacted. The law replacing paternal authority with joint parental authority was adopted on 4 June 1970, after the scenario start. Contraception had been legalized in 1967, with access restrictions still in force; abortion remained criminalized until the 1975 Veil law. These are credible policy debates, rather than a fully emancipated starting position. [UN CEDAW, France's legal timeline](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/171360/files/CEDAW_C_FRA_2_Rev-1-EN.pdf); [French National Assembly, women's rights chronology](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/opendata/RINFANR5L15B1095.html); [French High Council for Equality, relevant laws](https://www.haut-conseil-egalite.gouv.fr/travaux-et-thematiques/sante-droits-sexuels-et-reproductifs/reperes-juridiques/principaux-textes?page=2&sort_by=field_ref_modification_date_value&sort_order=DESC).

**Industrial geography, energy and pollution.** Do not portray France in 1970 as already deindustrialized: industrial value added was growing strongly and industry was about 28.8% of GDP in 1970 on the cited parliamentary series. But older coal, textile, steel and shipbuilding basins were exposed to later structural decline. For example, the painful high-furnace closures in Longwy came in 1979; textile employment collapsed later, with the steepest reported losses from 1974 to 1983. The 1970 choice is how to renew old plants and skills before a crisis, not to pretend it has already happened. Energy import exposure was a serious latent weakness, but the figure needs careful definition: France's energy-independence ratio was only about 22% at the 1973 oil shock, meaning roughly 78% import dependence. The 60% figure in the cited INSEE review is the Western European average, not France's starting figure. This is vulnerability before the shock, not evidence that the shock had already occurred. [French National Assembly, energy independence from 1950 to 1973](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/rap-oecst/nucleaire/r1359-01.asp). Pollution and conservation had entered official policy by 1970: a first “100 Measures for the Environment” programme was adopted in June and a dedicated ministry followed in January 1971. [French National Assembly industrial report](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/opendata/RAPPANR5L17B1702.html); [INSEE, France's external trade since 1970](https://www.persee.fr/doc/estat_0336-1454_1977_num_94_1_6262); [France Mémoire, environmental ministry history](https://www.institutdefrance.fr/actualites/7-janvier-1971-creation-du-ministere-de-la-protection-de-la-nature-et-de-lenvironnement/).

**Defense burden.** France had a real, operational strategic deterrent by 1970, but force de frappe was expensive and did not make the conventional army limitless. In the 1970 budget debate, total defense appropriations were reported as 17.6% of the state budget and 3.4% of GNP. The National Assembly's retrospective account puts nuclear deterrence at 24% of the defense budget and half of defense investment over 1959-69. A 1971 National Archives/US intelligence memorandum records French defense minister Michel Debré's estimate that the force would absorb 25% of the 1970 military budget. These estimates use different methods, so avoid claiming one exact nuclear cost. The useful gameplay constraint is opportunity cost: strategic autonomy versus conventional readiness, conscript service, equipment and social spending. [French National Assembly, 1969 debate on 1970 defense credits](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1969/11/19/l-examen-des-credits-militaires-a-l-assemblee-nationale_3142275_1819218.html); [French National Assembly, deterrent cost and milestones](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/opendata/RINFANR5L15B1155.html); [declassified 1971 US assessment of French force de frappe costs](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP79R01012A025600040013-2.pdf).

**Church and secular state.** France was a secular republic under the 1905 separation law, with freedom of conscience and worship and a state that neither recognizes nor funds religious institutions. This does not mean religion or Catholic social influence had disappeared: Catholic practices, schools, associations and regional identities remained relevant, and the Centre démocrate drew on Christian-democratic currents. But no confessional party or faith-specific national branch is needed for France. The political tension is over public education, private religious schooling, social ethics and the boundaries of public neutrality. [French Government, historical construction of laïcité](https://www.info.gouv.fr/upload/media/organization/0001/01/sites_default_files_contenu_piece-jointe_2014_07_note_d_orientation_de_lobservatoire_de_la_laicite_3.pdf); [National Archives, Centre démocrate background](https://rdf.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/garance/entities/agent/050624/).

## Additional policy precedents and precise terminology

- **PSU and autogestion:** The PSU was not yet formally organized around autogestion in January 1970. Historians at the CNRS-affiliated Centre d'histoire sociale date its adoption as a defining PSU doctrine to December 1972, with the Lip watch-factory worker takeover and self-managed production in 1973 becoming a powerful precedent. Model this as a later selectable course with practical tensions over credit, markets, workers' control and party unity. [Centre d'histoire sociale, PSU and autogestion](https://histoire-sociale.cnrs.fr/amp/le-psu-et-lautogestion/); [Fondation Jean-Jaurès, Lip and self-management](https://www.jean-jaures.org/publication/juin-1975-le-parti-socialiste-et-lautogestion/).
- **Giscard and RI liberal reform:** From 1974, Giscard's presidency advanced social and institutional reforms including lowering the voting age, expanding constitutional review, contraception access/reimbursement, and the 1975 abortion and mutual-consent divorce laws. It was a reforming centre-right presidency, although the 1973 oil shock and economic crisis soon forced difficult trade-offs. [French government, Chirac 1974-76 government and reforms](https://www.info.gouv.fr/les-anciens-premiers-et-premieres-ministres-de-la-ve-republique/jacques-chirac-1974-1976); [French High Council for Equality, legal record](https://www.haut-conseil-egalite.gouv.fr/travaux-et-thematiques/sante-droits-sexuels-et-reproductifs/reperes-juridiques/principaux-textes?page=2&sort_by=field_ref_modification_date_value&sort_order=DESC).
- **Left unity and PCF:** The June 1972 Programme commun was signed by the PS and PCF, then by the left-radical grouping. It explicitly left some ideological disagreements visible, including the different PS and PCF approaches to autogestion/worker management; it did not make the parties identical. The PCF abandoned the formula “dictatorship of the proletariat” at its 22nd Congress in 1976 and publicly identified a divergence with the Soviet Communist Party over socialist democracy. A January 1970 party description should therefore show a pro-Soviet party that had criticized the 1968 Czechoslovakia invasion and was exploring a common left programme, not portray the 1976 doctrinal break as already accomplished. [The 1972 programme as published by Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1972/06/29/le-programme-commun-de-gouvernement-etabli-par-les-delegations-du-parti-socialiste-et-du-parti-communiste-francais_3141472_1819218.html); [Le Monde, Marchais and the 1976 doctrinal change](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1976/01/09/les-declarations-du-secretaire-general-du-p-c-f-a-antenne-2-france-m-georges-marchais-renonce-a-la-formule-de-la-dictature-du-proletariat_3123810_1819218.html).
- **Centrists and radicals:** CD and CDP remained separate organizations in 1970; they merged in 1976 to create the Centre des démocrates sociaux (CDS), one of the UDF's founding components in 1978. The Parti radical split on 4 October 1972 over left-union strategy. The breakaway was first called the Mouvement de la gauche radicale-socialiste; it took the name Mouvement des radicaux de gauche (MRG) in 1973 and was formally organized under that name in December. Keep the MRG label out of a January 1970 start. [French National Archives, CD/CDP and CDS dates](https://rdf.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/garance/entities/agent/050624/); [Larousse, radical party split](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/histoire_de_france/page/1023).

## January 1970 registry review: FRA.json

Read-only review of `tools/party_registry_1970/data/FRA.json`; no registry or mod code was changed. Recommended corrections for the author to consider:

1. **Slots 8-12 (FMI, Mo'talefeh, Grand Mosque, Hizb ut-Tahrir, AEIF) are not French national parties or institutions.** They describe Iranian opposition groups, transnational Islamists, and a Paris mosque/student association as if they were France-wide political constituencies. Their French presence, where any, was marginal or student/exile-based; they should not occupy major party-popularity slots in a French national registry. In particular, an Islamic coalition tied to Khomeini cannot be described as a French party with material leverage. Replace these slots with actual French parties, institutions or unions if those slots must be populated.
2. **The list omits the CIR**, the Convention des institutions républicaines around François Mitterrand, which still existed separately from the PS in January 1970. Conversely, avoid assigning it to a later merged PS branch until Épinay in June 1971.
3. **Slot 18 PSU over-dates autogestion.** Replace “preaches workers' self-management” at the January 1970 date with a more general left-socialist, anti-colonial and democratic socialist position. Its autogestion identity emerged in 1972; Lip is 1973.
4. **Slot 14 overstates CD's leverage.** The UDR held an absolute Assembly majority after the 1968 election, and Duhamel's CDP, not Lecanuet's CD, joined Pompidou's majority in 1969. The CD can be described as an opposition-centre party with local, Senate and public-profile influence, not a pivotal balance in a divided governing majority. Keep CD and CDP distinct through 1976.
5. **Slot 16 should not imply the MRG existed in 1970.** The radical split occurred on 4 October 1972, before the MRG name was adopted in 1973. At the 1970 date, identify Servan-Schreiber's reformist Parti radical and internal disagreement about strategy, not a settled later split.
6. **Slot 4 compresses the PCF's later programme and internal evolution.** It is reasonable to say it sought a common programme, but explain it as a proposal under negotiation; the agreement came in 1972. “Loyal to the Soviet Union” needs qualification alongside the party's public criticism of the 1968 invasion of Czechoslovakia. The formal end to the dictatorship-of-proletariat formula belongs to 1976, not the start date.

The registry's other major timeline anchors checked here are broadly plausible: UDR naming and Poujade leadership; Pompidou presidency and Chaban premiership; FNRI's position in the majority; PS as a 1969 formation with Savary first secretary and CIR outside; CDP as a distinct 1969 pro-Pompidou group; and Ordre nouveau as a small far-right activist current, with FN only in 1972. Treat claims about precise membership, geographic bases, factional leaders, or group influence as requiring additional specialist sourcing before using them as hard numerical mechanics.

### Clarification: 1969 franc devaluation

The devaluation was **11.11% in the franc's official gold parity** (0.180g to 0.160g fine gold per franc). The quoted exchange rate moved from 4.93706 to 5.55418 francs per US dollar, a **12.5% increase in francs per dollar**. Those percentages differ because the quoted rate is the inverse value. Use 11.11% when describing the devaluation of the franc itself; report 12.5% only when explicitly describing the francs-per-dollar quote. [IMF, 1969 franc and mark adjustments](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9781451971477/ch022.xml).

## Historically grounded foreign policy accords and agreements

These are real precedents available to a France tree. An alternate-history route can seek changed terms or new partners, but should label any new 1970 negotiation as a divergence from the historical baseline.

- **Algeria, hydrocarbons and industrial development (29 July 1965):** a bilateral agreement created joint Franco-Algerian bodies for exploration and industrial cooperation, including ASCOOP and OCI. This sits alongside Algeria's assertion of control over its resources; later politics included renegotiation and nationalization. An RP branch could bargain over revenue shares, investment, technology transfer or guarantees, but should not frame the historical arrangement as uncomplicated French control. [UN, Agreement terms and background](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/1302147/files/A_8058-EN.pdf); [Cambridge, 1965 Algeria-France Oil Accord](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-legal-materials/article/abs/algeriafrance-oil-accord/DBCA0D9CF5DA1EA1C1AF38BB96628EF2).
- **Algeria, migration (27 December 1968):** the agreement specifically regulated circulation, work and residence of Algerians and their families; contemporary later records describe an initial annual worker quota of 35,000 and a special residence-status regime. This reflected negotiated control of labor movement after independence, not fully equal common-market mobility. A new RP settlement could renegotiate recruitment, family rights and protections. [UN Treaty Series, full agreement record](https://www.un-ilibrary.org/content/books/9789210453929s003-c006); [French Interior Ministry, 1968 agreement](https://www.immigration.interieur.gouv.fr/limmigration-en-france/accords-internationaux/accords-bilateraux-en-matiere-de-circulation-de-sejour-et-demploi).
- **European industrial cooperation:** the Anglo-French Concorde agreement of 29 November 1962 explicitly rested on equal work and cost sharing; the Anglo-French 17 May 1965 memorandum launched joint development of trainer/light-strike aircraft and the Jaguar followed. A Franco-German agreement signed 29 May 1969 launched joint A300 development. The A300 is a near-term project in January 1970, but the Airbus Industrie consortium was only formed in December 1970. These are strong historical precedents for high-cost, shared industrial programmes with delivery and market risks. [Concorde agreement text](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/sspp/assets/icbh-witness/concorde.pdf); [UK Parliament, Jaguar and aircraft cooperation statement](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1965/may/17/anglo-french-collaboration-on-aircraft); [Airbus, 1969 agreement and 1970 consortium timeline](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2009-05-airbus-celebrates-the-40th-anniversary-of-its-first-aircraft).
- **Soviet Union, technical and economic cooperation:** France and the USSR signed a scientific, technical and economic cooperation agreement on 30 June 1966; a 1970 protocol added political dialogue and consultation. This supports a détente and trade route without implying that France left the Western alliance. [UN Treaty record](https://treaties.un.org/pages/treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/Monthly%20Statement/1990/10/monstate.pdf); [peer-reviewed study of Franco-Soviet détente](https://www.persee.fr/doc/etudo_1241-3496_1993_num_5_1_1040).
- **Senegal and postcolonial defense relations:** there was not a 1963 defense treaty. Senegal succeeded to the 22 June 1960 cooperation agreements signed with the Mali Federation after its breakup; these provided French help organizing, equipping and training Senegal's armed forces, including French officers and NCOs. A formal defense cooperation agreement was signed in 1974 and expressly framed cooperation in terms of equality and mutual respect. A plausible 1970 RP alternative is to renegotiate the inherited 1960 arrangement earlier, clarify Senegalese sovereignty and move toward reciprocal or multilateral security; do not present the later 1974 treaty as already in force. [1964 French International Law Yearbook on 1960 agreements](https://www.persee.fr/doc/afdi_0066-3085_1964_num_10_1_1796?pageId=T1_840); [UN Treaty Series, 1974 defense agreement](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%201061/v1061.pdf); [Senegalese Embassy, bilateral agreements chronology](https://www.ambasenparis.gouv.sn/accords-entre-le-senegal-et-la-france/).
- **European Community, Hague Summit (1-2 December 1969):** the Six adopted the political formula “completion, deepening and enlargement,” opening the path to Community own resources, foreign-policy cooperation through the Davignon process and accession negotiations with the UK, Ireland and Denmark. A 1970 French choice can be about the scope and terms of European integration: use the summit as the existing baseline, not as a future hypothetical. [Council of the EU, institutional history and Hague Summit](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/media/29975/qc0415219enn.pdf).
