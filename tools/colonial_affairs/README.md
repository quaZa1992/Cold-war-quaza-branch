# IMPERIA COLD WAR — Security Affairs: territories, insurgency and terrorism

<!-- IMPERIA CW — Cold War: approved shared colonial administration and cabinet dossier. -->

The cabinet-file design is implemented in **Decisions → Security Affairs**. Its three tabs are **Territories**, **Internal Files** and **Foreign Files**. The category remains visible for human players in the 1970 and 1985 scenarios, including countries without colonies or discovered contacts. Empty files explain access requirements. The regional popup can also be opened from the selected state. A native intelligence agency and a network **strictly above 50% in that exact state** are required for every foreign organisation file and transaction. The contact browser cycles only through currently authorised states. Native occupation resistance is also eligible outside the historical colonial registry. Existing deliveries expire normally after network access is lost; further payments are blocked.

**Internal Files** replaces the former IRA seven-slider simulation and its separate interface. The British IRA dossier shows Northern Ireland's organisation strength, aid, policing mandates and weekly appropriation. The German RAF dossier reads the existing cadre, milieu, generation and foreign aid values, with a direct button to the German countermeasure window. Before **14 May 1970**, Germany instead receives a preliminary-observation notice; the organisation is not activated early. RAF contact files require the existing German RAF system to be active. Both interfaces call the same German actions and programme providers; no second RAF incident or budget simulation is added. The new IRA uses regional strength, police appropriations and incidents; it does not turn core territory into a military occupation. Each has an emblem in the shared file. The IRA donor emblem is retained; other distinct archive badges are stylised identifiers, not purported reproductions of historical arms.

All ten registry powers have complete 90×104 portraits derived from the leaders and existing picture paths in their 1970/1985 country-history snapshots. Each portrait requires its named leader to be in power. Complete archival flag cards cover unpictured successors, and a neutral institutional image covers new administrators. This replaces the empty portrait frame and tiny engine-flag fallback. `authority_images.json` records portrait coverage, leader guards and source textures.

**Internal Files → Measures** contains two pages per organisation. Six new British measures cover evidence-led casework, fair employment/complaints, emergency internment, detention review, confidential ceasefire contacts and constitutional power-sharing talks. Community tension is tracked separately from organisation strength: internment can create recruitment backlash; reform works gradually; talks can fail or collapse. Three new German programmes fund federal-state case coordination, judicial review and independent prison medical review. Four additional buttons call the existing German wanted-notice, dragnet, crown-witness and exit-programme actions with their original unlocks, costs and cooldowns. New commitments enter the same weekly expense account. No measure automatically dissolves an organisation. Historical precedents, alternate-history interpretations and source links are recorded in [MEASURES_HISTORY.md](MEASURES_HISTORY.md).

German **BKA Expansion**, **Data Protection** and **The Stammheim Trial** respectively unlock the three programmes in both interfaces. The programme catalogue is shared in `raf_programmes.json`; the German monthly dispatcher alone applies RAF aid growth and ongoing programme changes. Closed cases stop recruiting, planning and incurring programme costs, while custody cards follow aggregate prisoner releases. See [SECURITY_CONTRACT.md](../west_germany_1970/SECURITY_CONTRACT.md) for bounded build commands, old-save compatibility and integration checks.

Colonial standing budgets are doubled against the initial implementation. Contested and armed administrations maintain resistance floors where native occupation resistance exists, with only rare, paid, temporary relaxation. A disclosure roll follows actual successful foreign aid: **10% political, 25% funding, 40% arms**. Disclosure notifies the responsible government and broadcasts a major news event using event-local sponsor and region identities; covert success does not automatically incur a public diplomatic penalty.

The registry covers **44 administrations, 94 map states and ten historical powers**: Australia, Denmark, France, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Portugal, South Africa, Spain, the United Kingdom and the United States. Associated territories, protectorates and integral overseas departments retain their existing status and receive no fabricated liberation war. Explicit historical and map limitations are documented in [HISTORY.md](HISTORY.md); this feature does not silently redraw the scenario.

## Player access guide

The three tab tooltips explain each section's available actions and entry conditions. **Territories** covers the player's active overseas administrations, mandates, weekly budgets and petitions. **Internal Files** covers the active British IRA and German RAF cases, policing and countermeasures; it does not require a foreign network. The RAF case remains conditional on the existing German simulation and its formation date.

For **Foreign Files**, create an agency, assign an operative to build a network in the target area, and obtain **more than 50% network strength in the exact state** containing an eligible movement. Then use **Next authorised contact**, or select the state on the map and choose **Regional file** in its state panel. No eligible state means the browser is disabled. A network does not create a movement; states controlled by the player, their subjects or their overlord are excluded. Open files show the movement and its current strength/aid, with cadres/milieu for the RAF. The **Support** tooltips retain the authoritative action-specific costs, effects, cooldowns, aid limits and disclosure risks. Network loss hides a file and blocks new deliveries; existing aid can complete its term while the recipient remains eligible.

Empty registers display dedicated cabinet briefing sheets rather than assistance forms. Each has an introductory filing panel and three ruled sections explaining the institution's remit, the circumstances under which dossiers reach cabinet, and the commitments ministers may authorise. The English prose uses political and intelligence-service language; necessary thresholds and terms remain explicit. Foreign liaison explains the regional network threshold, eligible movements, the contact browser, map dossiers and assistance. Domestic and territorial briefings cover their own jurisdiction and available policy responses. Full tab tooltips provide the supporting detail.

The three body blocks begin at y=290, 416 and 538, above the foreign contact browser at y=625. Actual organisation records and paid-action providers are unchanged. `security.py` owns the briefing art and native widgets; its generator, the installed GUI, visibility providers, English localisation and UI catalogues remain in sync. Artwork provenance is recorded in [BRIEFING_NOTICE.md](../../gfx/interface/coldwar_colonial/BRIEFING_NOTICE.md).

## Rebuild and verification

```powershell
python tools/colonial_affairs/compile.py
python tools/colonial_affairs/validate.py
python tools/colonial_affairs/test_runtime.py
```

`compile.py --no-art` rebuilds runtime scripts and merges existing UI localisation without redrawing DDS assets. Canonical sources are `registry.json`, `runtime.py`, `contacts.py`, `retire_ira.py`, `ui.py`, `security.py` and `measures.py`. The last two compose the existing territory and contact providers into one native category and supply the domestic measures, preserving transaction guards. Full UI rebuild uses Pillow and local Windows fonts. No global fonts or Soviet donor files are modified. Script files are UTF-8 without BOM; English localisation uses UTF-8 with BOM.

The compiler owns its named Colonial Affairs outputs, two bounded money/Sahara integrations, and the authorised old IRA retirement. `retired_ira/` preserves original custom sources outside game-loading folders. Unrelated British political institutions and the British national focus graph remain. Remaining historical focus/event rewards consume the new Northern Ireland organisation strength; old foreign-action duplicates, the two obsolete Libyan IRA delivery focuses, and the old countermeasure UI are removed. Legacy modifier/idea identifiers have inert migration definitions so old saves can remove them without restoring their effects. Other feature generators must not restore the retired modules.

`manifest.json`, `validation.json`, `runtime_tests.json` and `ui_geometry_review.json` record source/model checks. `ui_preview/security_*.png` contains the current composites rendered from parsed native GUI coordinates. These are approximate previews, not screenshots of a running HOI4 session. Geometry checks account for the mutually exclusive tab and domestic-case predicates; runtime tests verify those predicates, browser access, strict network thresholds and image coverage.

## Runtime

See [RUNTIME.md](RUNTIME.md) for exact policy multipliers, local modifiers, resistance stages, aid prices, cleanup and provider contracts. Recurring budgets use the existing MD weekly expense account. Grants and funding use the actual treasury effect; arms consume MD infantry and command-and-control equipment. Foreign resistance assistance has a shared 15-point maximum per affected state and blocked payments at the cap.

The daily observer initializes existing 1970 saves lazily, but a **fresh January 1970 campaign is the intended complete start**. In particular, state-history ownership corrections are not retroactively imposed on existing saves. Startup receipts prevent reopening a save from decrementing a second policy/concern day through this feature's startup callback.

No native HOI4 load, hover, save/reload or multiplayer synchronization test has been performed for this revision. The user's screenshot of the previous design exposed the empty portrait fallback. Engine checks should cover the 510×740 category envelope, tab visibility, the RAF countermeasure shortcut, adjacent state-view controls, complete authority pictures, an exact-cost aid package, a petition left open beyond its expiry, partial independence and the weekly economy breakdown. Restart HOI4 to reload native GUI and DDS assets. The source model deliberately does not simulate native occupation evolution, AI decisions or networking.

## Recorded limits

- Papua/New Guinea's mainland and Diego Garcia have pre-existing map/status mismatches; the registry records them explicitly instead of imposing a new ownership settlement.
- One grouped administration simultaneously divided between unrelated foreign powers currently follows one administrator and only counts its retained eligible states. Normal native independence, protected-state independence and a complete foreign takeover are covered.
- Numerical budgets are authored gameplay values, not claims about historical annual accounts. Performance and financial balance still require a running campaign check.
