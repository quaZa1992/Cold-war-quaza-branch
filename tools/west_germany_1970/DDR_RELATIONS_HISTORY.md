# DDR policy precedents for Inner-German Relations

This note records historical precedents for the DDR-side options in the multiplayer rivalry. Historical facts below describe real institutions and choices from 1970–90. Any numeric game costs, meter changes, cooldowns, and crisis modifiers are design calibration; they are not historical quantities.

## Policy precedents and gameplay use

| DDR policy | Historical precedent | Possible gameplay trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compulsory exchange and visitor controls | On 5 November 1973, the GDR doubled the minimum exchange requirement to DM 10 per day in East Berlin and DM 20 elsewhere. Willy Brandt protested the measure; visitor numbers fell. [LeMO, 1973 chronology](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/jahreschronik/1973/1000); [bpb, Brandt and the compulsory exchange increase](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutschlandarchiv/345318/eine-beziehung-besonderer-art/) | Reduce the hard-currency-shortage meter while worsening climate and reducing Western contact. `ddr_raise_exchange` already captures this choice.
| KoKo, Intershops, and consumer imports | The Area for Commercial Coordination (KoKo), led by Alexander Schalck-Golodkowski, pursued convertible-currency income through trade and special operations. The GDR's 1982 export drive strained domestic supply. Intershops also converted West German currency into access to Western goods. [bpb, the 1969–75 turn](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/izpb/548474/der-fluechtige-zauber-des-neuanfangs-1969-1975/); [bpb, KoKo and the credit crisis](https://www.bpb.de/themen/deutschlandarchiv/209006/ein-kommunistischer-kapitalist-zum-tode-von-alexander-schalck-golodkowski/) | Ease the shortage or exit pressure, with greater dependence on Western money and potential domestic supply strain. Existing `ddr_intershop`, `ddr_koko`, and `ddr_consumer_imports` decisions cover the core institutions.
| Credit in exchange for border and family concessions | West German bank loans of DM 1.95 billion in 1983–84 restored the GDR's credit standing. The 1983 loan was accompanied by dismantling border SM-70 devices and mines and by promised family-reunion relief. By 1984 the spring guns had been removed; the mines were cleared by 1985. [bpb, 1982–90 financial crisis](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/izpb/geschichte-der-ddr-312/48560/auf-dem-weg-in-den-zusammenbruch-1982-bis-1990/) | Relieve the currency shortage and soften the border lever, while increasing dependence on Bonn and imposing a political cost on the SED. Existing `ddr_ask_credit`, Bonn's `credit`, and `ddr_goodwill` options express this trade.
| Diplomatic recognition | Both German states joined the United Nations on 18 September 1973. [LeMO, 1973 chronology](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/jahreschronik/1973/1000) | Raise GDR recognition while worsening climate if Bonn continues to assert its legal position. Existing `ddr_recognition_drive` is an appropriate representation.
| Security control over civic groups | The Ministry for State Security monitored churches, clergy, and church-linked peace, environmental, and human-rights groups. Its November 1987 raid on the East Berlin Umweltbibliothek prompted solidarity protests and helped publicize the group and the Initiative for Peace and Human Rights. [Bundesarchiv, church surveillance](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/glossar/detail/kirchen-bekaempfung-und-infiltration-der/); [Bundesarchiv, Umweltbibliothek](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/themenbeitraege/die-umweltbibliothek/) | A sweep may temporarily reduce opposition and reinforce SED control while increasing Western media attention and damaging recognition or climate. Existing `ddr_stasi_sweep` covers this policy choice.
| Tolerated church forums and social accommodation | Churches provided a degree of protected space for independent groups in the 1970s and 1980s. The state sometimes sought to contain criticism without provoking a wider confrontation. [Bundesarchiv, opposition and freedoms](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/vor-ort-entdecken/ausstellungen/dauerausstellung-rastatt/virtuelle-fuehrung-og-raum-4); [Bundesarchiv, church surveillance](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/glossar/detail/kirchen-bekaempfung-und-infiltration-der/) | A bounded opening could improve climate and reduce tension while allowing civic opposition and group networks to grow. This is a useful alternative to a security sweep.
| Travel and family-reunion concessions | After signing the 1975 Helsinki Final Act, the GDR formally accepted freedom-of-movement principles but continued to restrict emigration. By 1989, applications, embassy occupations, and departures via Hungary created mounting political pressure; the leadership eventually allowed embassy refugees to leave by special trains. [Bundesarchiv, suppression of the emigration movement](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/stasi-unterlagen-einsehen/hinweise-zum-mfs/mfs-lexikon/detail/ausreisebewegung-bekaempfung-der/); [Bundesarchiv, 1989 Stasi reports](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/assets/bundesarchiv/de/Publikationen/ZAIG_1989_Auswahledition_Auflage-06_barrierearm_reduziert.pdf) | Improve climate and soften border or exchange levers, with exit pressure potentially rising as restrictions ease. Existing `ddr_goodwill` is a suitable calibrated gesture.
| Reliance on Moscow's guarantee | The GDR's security position depended on Soviet backing. In July 1989, Gorbachev affirmed that each socialist state could choose its own development, ending the Brezhnev Doctrine's intervention guarantee. [LeMO, July 1989](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/jahreschronik/1989.html); [LeMO, Glasnost and the Brezhnev Doctrine](https://www.hdg.de/lemo/kapitel/deutsche-einheit/wandel-im-osten/glasnost-und-perestroika.html) | Calling Moscow can strengthen deterrence while the guarantee holds, but creates strategic dependence. Crisis decisions should lose reliability as Soviet backing recedes.

## Existing DDR actions

The current decision set already offers the main institutional choices: expand Intershops, use KoKo, tighten the border regime, act against independent groups, import consumer goods, pursue Socialist Glasnost, raise compulsory exchange, harass transit, increase NVA readiness, conduct an HVA offensive, pursue recognition, offer goodwill, request credit, and close transit. These actions should be retained where possible; additional choices can fill the church-accommodation gap and make credit-for-concession bargaining more explicit.

The shared rivalry's crisis answers—back down, stall, escalate, call on Moscow, or make a counter-offer—are abstractions for multiplayer roleplay. Their outcomes are game-authored and should be balanced against initiative, climate, escalation pressure, DDR domestic meters, and the Bonn levers. No historical source establishes their numeric costs or meter changes.
