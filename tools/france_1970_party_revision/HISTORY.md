IMPERIA COLD WAR

# France party programmes: historical precedents

**Phase:** research and preview only. This note proposes historical anchors and
trade-offs for revision of the existing party programmes. It does not authorize
runtime edits or assert that the alternate-history options occurred.

## PCF: make Soviet cooperation reciprocal and specific

The current `soviet_friendship` concept is a strong fit for French-Soviet
intergovernmental cooperation, but its player-facing choice can be sharper. The
real bilateral framework already included the 1966 scientific, technical and
economic agreement, the 1971 industrial cooperation agreement, and a 1975 energy
agreement. That 1975 accord expressly covered gas, oil, coal, electrical and
nuclear power, energy equipment, rational use, expert exchanges, joint studies,
industrial supply and joint projects. A Soviet-led partnership chosen by a PCF
government in 1970 is counterfactual; its additional scale and political
conditions must be labelled as the French government's choice to deepen existing
frameworks, not a historical PCF treaty.

| Distinct proposal | French return | Soviet return | Real cost or tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pipeline and industrial production compact** | Long-term gas supply; French contracts for compressors, pipe, electrical controls and plant equipment; specialist orders and industrial output. | Reliable equipment, engineering and financing for energy infrastructure; long-term gas export revenue. | Dependence on one supplier and long contracts, credit exposure and domestic capacity tied to export orders. Show the share of imports or debt exposure as a real penalty if the design uses it. |
| **Civilian energy engineering and research exchange** | Access to Soviet fuel-cycle, grid and resource expertise; joint applied research and technician training. | French reactor, grid, efficiency and instrumentation expertise; trained specialists and reciprocal technology access. | Constrain the benefit to civilian research and expertise. Do not grant future nuclear technologies for free; use limited-use research rewards and explicit contract costs. |
| **Commercial maritime and technical exchange** | French shipbuilding and port-service contracts, freight access, maritime training and container transport opportunities. | Better access to French ports and commercial carriers, container vessels, shipping expertise and civilian industrial services. | Competition for freight and port capacity; require balanced cargo access or a joint commission. This is a commercial and technical pact, not naval basing or an alliance. |
| **Independent French communist course** | More domestic legitimacy for a pluralist programme, control over investment priorities and room for European or non-aligned technical partners. | Continued normal trade and diplomacy, but no exclusive technology or political alignment. | Forfeits the preferential Soviet package and some Soviet support. Preserve the PCF's own freedom to choose a French democratic road; do not force NATO accession or withdrawal. |

The PCF's 22nd Congress in 1976 abandoned the dictatorship-of-the-proletariat
formula and argued for a democratic French road. That did not amount to an
automatic break with Moscow: the line's relative autonomy was contested and
short-lived, with a harder Soviet-friendly course later. An appealing design
should therefore make **access and autonomy the choice**: Soviet cooperation can
offer useful supply, research or production, while the autonomous line keeps
political control and broader partnerships. Avoid a binary “good Soviet / bad
West” faction reward.

## Party-specific domestic directions

These suggestions extend the current runtime nodes rather than replace their
party identities. A “real precedent” describes a policy already debated or
attempted; the concrete focus reward remains an alternate-history proposal.

| Party programme | Precedent to build on | Distinct playable reform and credible cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UDR / Gaullist** | Chaban-Delmas's New Society and the existing participation reforms; Gaullist emphasis on national industrial capacity. | A negotiated industrial participation compact: co-finance technical training and employee savings, then improve productivity and labour cooperation. Price it in treasury and employer concessions; do not repeat generic stability rewards. |
| **Independent Republicans (RI)** | Giscardian liberal modernisation and the Barre government's 1976-77 anti-inflation redress. | Choose between disciplined-franc adjustment and targeted modernisation credit. The first improves monetary confidence but limits demand; the second upgrades firms but strains the budget and increases exposure to inflation. Keep their Atlantic trade and European-market focuses civilian and commercial. |
| **Centre démocrate / CDS** | Christian-democratic social responsibility, mutual societies, and the 1976 merger into the CDS; regional and institutional reform. | A mutual-insurance and local-care compact paired with elected regional investment. It improves local service access and consent, while transferring budget discretion away from Paris and requiring a durable coalition. |
| **Radicals** | Radical modernisation, secular public education and the republican-left tradition; MRG participation in the 1972 Common Programme shows a distinct route into left cooperation. | Fund a civilian engineering and technical-school consortium, with regional firms competing for procurement. Offer research and firm productivity in return for substantial public cost and open competition against protected incumbents. Do not turn this into automatic US alignment. |
| **Socialists (PS)** | The 1972 Common Programme proposed expanded public ownership, democratic planning, regional investment and social rights. Later governing experience exposed monetary and balance-of-payments constraints. | Offer a real fork between household-demand reflation and a European monetary compact. Reflation raises demand and public employment but risks deficits and import pressure; discipline can protect currency and market access but slows consumption and employment. Keep public-credit and labour institutions substantive. |
| **Unified Socialists (PSU)** | The PSU's 1972-73 programme made worker and popular control central; the 1973 Lip factory occupation and attempted restart is a concrete industrial precedent. | Give workers a binding role in a limited cooperative production pilot with credit and technical support. Pay for training and slower initial throughput; reward workplace autonomy and output only if the commercial plan succeeds. The PSU was electorally small, so keep the pilot focused rather than giving it a national command economy. |
| **Ecologists** | René Dumont's 1974 presidential campaign connected resource limits, pollution, social justice and participation; early ecology was a movement, not yet a mass party. | Fund efficiency, insulation, public transport and local generation before new supply. Reduce energy demand and pollution while accepting slower heavy-industry throughput and higher up-front costs. Pair conversion aid with worker retraining so “green policy” is not merely a factory penalty. |
| **Nationalist right** | The FN began as a small and internally heterogeneous coalition in 1972; its 1980s proposals emphasised national defence and sovereignty. A governing FN in this scenario is explicit alternate history. | Offer an export-and-defence industrial programme or a protected domestic market. Export contracts raise production but conflict with autarkic rhetoric and expose firms to foreign markets; protection can shelter selected producers but risks retaliation, shortages and reduced competition. Do not grant ethnic or religious exclusion as a gameplay reward. |

## Design cautions

- Treat the specific 1970 governing programme as an alternate-history choice,
  especially for the PCF, PSU and FN branches. Keep the historical names, party
  requirements and incumbents intact.
- Keep agreements reciprocal and fixed-partner. Display each country's own
  complete costs and effects before consent; use a recipient-owned accept/decline
  decision and a live activation/suspension check.
- Use existing national treaties as precedents for practical cooperation, not as
  justification for automatic alliance membership, NATO entry/exit or military
  basing. The proposed Soviet packages are civilian and commercial.
- Make setup costs and recurring costs belong to the country paying them. Show
  actual assets, research, contract costs and trade-offs in effect previews.
- Distinguish institutional records from policy claims. Historical sources below
  are paraphrased; no archival wording, logo or art is copied. Page and document
  licences are unknown unless specified by the custodian.
