# Reserve realignment and presidential dollar briefings

The user approved an uncapped, one-time cost for voluntary reserve-law changes: **10% of annual GDP plus 10% of outstanding public debt**. This is a deliberate multiplayer balance choice, not an estimate of historically observed transaction fees. At GDP 100 and debt 500, the payment is 60 billion in MD treasury units. Debt remains outstanding; GDP is not reduced. Existing political-power prices remain in force. No additional cooldown or economic penalties were approved or added.

All 18 existing laws in the reserve-currency category use the same requirement and payment, including generic domestic reserves, the DDR Mark, French Franc, Italian Lira and the UAE Dirham. The existing country, date, issuer and Eurozone restrictions remain intact. The native law selector calculates its quote directly from current `gdp_total` and raw `debt`; it does not use the native, capped `debt_ratio`. Both the quote and the payment use the same expression, with negative invalid input balances floored at zero and no upper bound. Sufficient treasury is required by the actual law availability trigger. Execution recalculates the live amount and pays through MD's existing `modify_treasury_effect`.

History-file grants do not execute native `on_add`. Existing authored focus rewards, initializers, faction alignment changes, Euro conversion and Yuan tier upgrades retain their original financial treatment. Each explicit scripted reserve grant/removal is bracketed with a short depth counter so it cannot acquire a second, unintended law-selector charge. The counter handles the euro's nested domestic fallback and is cleared when the conversion returns. Tooltip-only previews are left untouched. Initializers suppress presidential notices as well. There is no new startup pass, daily/weekly/monthly pulse, country scan, cached quote, debt ledger or scheduled monitor. Existing saves can use the feature without a new initialization flag.

The US Dollar's existing `on_remove` reports a departure to USA after one hour. The event uses its own `FROM` country, rather than a shared global country target. Multiple countries leaving at once therefore retain their separate identities. The event rechecks that its sender still exists and no longer holds the Dollar. A country removed from the map, an initializer, the United States itself, or a temporary removal followed by immediate restoration produces no displayed briefing. The event recommends a Treasury and State response in English RP prose; its acknowledgement does not automatically impose sanctions, spend money or create a new modifier. Dollar adoption counts and their existing economic effects continue to use native MD code.

## Historical and institutional precedents

- Douglas Dillon, memorandum to President Lyndon Johnson, 17 March 1965: the Treasury briefed the President on gold losses and French reductions of dollar balances. This directly supports the institutional voice and country-specific notice. France reduced dollar holdings; this was not a literal withdrawal from MD's binary category. Source: [US Department of State, FRUS 1964-1968, volume VIII, document 53](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v08/d53). Original document author: Douglas Dillon. Publisher: Office of the Historian. Document-level redistribution terms: not established here. No source text or image is reproduced.
- Lyndon Johnson, memorandum to Treasury Secretary Henry Fowler, 16 June 1965: the President requested analysis of US vulnerability to political and economic pressure through gold conversion of official dollar balances. This supports the recommendation to assess countermeasures, without granting unapproved automatic retaliation. Source: [FRUS, document 64](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v08/d64). Original author: Lyndon Johnson. Publisher: Office of the Historian. Document-level redistribution terms: not established here.
- Robert McCauley and Tracy Chan, *Currency movements drive reserve composition*, BIS Quarterly Review, December 2014: reserve composition is related to exchange-rate anchoring and the dollar zone; it also discusses Heller and Knight's 1978 findings. This supports treating a move between MD reserve categories as an external monetary realignment, not a replacement of all domestic cash. Source: [Bank for International Settlements](https://www.bis.org/publications/currency-movements-drive-reserve-composition). Underlying publication remains subject to BIS rights; no full article or data are bundled.
- IMF, *Guidelines for Foreign Exchange Reserve Management*, 2001, and the IMF's capital-flow framework discuss liquidity, currency and asset/liability mismatch risks. These justify the direction of the debt-related gameplay burden. They do not substantiate the chosen 10% and 10% rates or imply all domestic public debt is foreign-currency debt. Sources: [IMF guidelines](https://www.imf.org/external/np/mae/ferm/eng/index.htm), [IMF framework FAQ](https://www.imf.org/en/about/faq/capital-flows). Author: IMF institution. Reproduction permission for full publications: not established here. Guidelines landing page was search-index accessible but direct access returned HTTP 403 during this review. Access date for these records: 23 October 2026.

Native `country_event`, `check_variable`, existing MD treasury effects and existing law lifecycle callbacks are reused.
