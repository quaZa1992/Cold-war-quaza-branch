IMPERIA COLD WAR: African starting equipment variants, 1 January 1970

This record supports `africa_variants.json`. It records imported models separately from the operator country. The JSON contains all 89 African tags in the regional research manifest. Empty lists are intentional pending specific pre-cutoff model evidence, especially colonial and non-state releases. No division, stockpile, ship, air wing, factory, or production quantity is created by a design record.

The data currently contains 69 designs and 127 country assignments across 33 evidenced operator packages. It contains original early tanks, reconnaissance cars, wheeled APCs, towed artillery, fighters, utility helicopters, and Egyptian conventional warships. Native MD uses the medium tank destroyer family for reconnaissance vehicles, including ENG Saladin/Ferret, FRA AMX-13, SAF Eland, and SIA M41 in the 2000 donors. The earlier 1955 derivative follows this convention. APCs follow native MD's Walid amphibious-chassis family with wheels, without claiming actual amphibious performance. Gasoline engines and steel armor correct misleading diesel/aluminum shortcuts in several 2000 donors. No donor MIO or modernized component is copied. Optional aircraft gun fits use neutral MG,20/23 mm,30 mm or mixed23/37 mm modules, selected by actual armament. Impala Mk I and MB-326 represent optional MG-pod combat configurations, with no later integral Mk II cannon installation. The final physical gun check uses the native 100/105 mm band for T-54/T-55, the WWII 75/85 mm band for 75/76 mm light tanks and the original 83.4 mm Centurion 20-pounder, and the dedicated 90 mm module for Eland. It does not substitute a later larger gun merely because its slot fits.

## Deliberate omissions and unresolved native representation

Canberra, Il-28, Su-7BMK and Buccaneer are now fitted with the dedicated early conventional bomb module. Early fighter hulls have no auxiliary weapon slot, so gun-only and bomb/cannon ground-attack configurations are separate legal designs. No guided weapon is inferred. Jet trainers/light attack aircraft use the low-performance early chassis where possible. The non-BBA DRC T-6/T-28 fallback is the dedicated early piston attack model, not a jet fighter. [Air Force Magazine 1993](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/app/uploads/1993/12/1293_Dec1993.pdf) independently identifies the early Ghanaian two-seat MB-326F variant.

Egyptian and Algerian Komar/Osa boats are deliberately not represented with modern oversized corvettes. South Africa has no submarine on 1 January: its first Daphne commissioned in July 1970. President-class steam frigates use the dedicated early compatible steam module, with conservative 100 mm gun and early ASW equipment. W-class ASW refits use the native light helipad for their two Wasp helicopters and omit the former heavy anti-ship torpedo outfit. The [original W-class history](https://journals.ufs.ac.za/index.php/jch/article/view/412) dates these conversions to 1962-66. The Dutch-built Nigerian frigate uses its actual diesel propulsion. No later Sea Cat, CIWS or anti-ship missile upgrade is assumed. Egyptian Romeo transfers in 1966-69 are specifically supported by [original CNS/NTI submarine export research](https://www.nti.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/russian_submarine_exports.pdf), cross-checked against [USNI original fleet research](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1985/march/international-submarine-forces). Tu-16 strategic bombers are omitted until a faithful large-airframe/fallback mapping is checked, rather than hidden in a tactical fighter frame.

The native light helicopter chassis permits only gunship layouts. Unarmed Alouette/JetRanger designs therefore use the native medium chassis to satisfy the actual transport-layout rules. This is an explicit categorization/payload abstraction, not a claim of historically increased payload or medium size. No weapons, armor, mast radar or active defense is installed. Mi-4 and Whirlwind retain piston engines; the UH-1 and Alouette/JetRanger use turbines.

No named rifle or truck is invented for an operator merely because the same producer supplied its tanks. Unresolved exact-model assignments remain empty. Existing generic research baselines remain separately documented in the African profiles, including older military technology for small states.

The `service_by` field is a conservative latest proven bound for the listed operators, not a claim that every operator received the vehicle on that exact day. Sources dated early 1970 are accepted only for clearly existing pre-cutoff inventories; orders and later deliveries are excluded. Low-confidence uncertain equipment is omitted instead of filling all 89 tags with a generic catalog. Empty lists for Angola, Mozambique, Namibia and later releases do not create independent 1970 military procurement institutions.

All linked third-party texts and images remain credited to their authors/publishers. This data records historical facts and original game mappings, without copying external images or long source passages. Applicable licensing for external source text is unknown unless stated by its publisher; no ownership claim is made.

## Native protection and mobility calibration

All thirteen African armored designs have explicit native `tank_nsb_armor_upgrade` and `tank_nsb_engine_upgrade` levels. Shared model families use the same levels and early steel baselines as the other regions: T-34-85 1/2; T-54 and T-55 1/1; original Meteor Centurion 2/0; M41 1/3; AMX-13 0/3; Saladin 1/2; Ferret 0/1; Saracen 1/1; BTR-152 0/2. Early Eland uses 0/2. These compare native MD bands, suspension, actual powerplant family and protection class. They do not convert real armor millimetres directly into game points. The native armor upgrade changes speed, reliability, cost and defense as well as armor; engine upgrades also change fuel and reliability. Those consequences are retained. The fixed legacy fallback has a different upgrade system and remains its documented coarse type rather than copying NSB factors.

The [Saumur T-54 collection record](https://museedesblindes.fr/les_chars/t54/) gives 520 hp, 36 tonnes, 50 km/h and 200 mm maximum armor. The [Victory Museum T-55 record](https://victorymuseum.ru/encyclopedia/technic/bronetankovaya-tekhnika/sredniy-tank-t-55-sssr/) gives 580 hp, 36-36.5 tonnes, 50 km/h and frontal turret/hull armor of 200/100 mm. These original engines share a coarse designer mobility band because extra horsepower did not imply a higher maximum road speed. The [Tank Museum T-34/85 record](https://tankmuseum.org/tank-nuts/tank-collection/t-34-85) provides its distinct 55 km/h and 90 mm maximum armor.

The [US Army 1953 Armor journal](https://www.benning.army.mil/armor/eARMOR/content/issues/1953/JAN_FEB/1953JanuaryFebruary.pdf) identifies the original M41 500 hp engine, and [its 1997 historical account](https://www.benning.army.mil/armor/eARMOR/content/issues/1997/MAY_JUN/ArmorMayJune1997web.pdf) gives 44 mph maximum. The [Tank Museum AMX-13 record](https://tankmuseum.org/tank-nuts/tank-collection/amx-13) gives 37 mph and 10-40 mm steel protection; the [Dutch National Military Museum magazine](https://assets.nmm.nl/app/uploads/2026/01/09-02-NMMagazine_Jaargang-9-nummer-2.pdf) confirms the original 250 hp SOFAM petrol engine. M41 and AMX-13 use the early thin-steel module, not the full MBT steel baseline. The [Tank Museum Centurion page](https://tankmuseum.org/tank-nuts/tank-collection/centurion/?tpage=1) explicitly distinguishes marks and labels its numerical table Mk 3. It supports the slow Meteor-family road mobility but does not justify copying one mark's full armor or engine table to every African Mk 5.

The [Museum of American Armor Ferret exhibit](https://www.museumofamericanarmor.com/exhibits) specifies a 129 hp petrol B60 and 58 mph; the [Air Combat Museum Saladin exhibit](https://aircombatmuseum.com/?page_id=80) specifies the 170 hp petrol engine. [Yorkshire Air Museum's Saracen](https://yorkshireairmuseum.org/alvis-saracen/) has 160 hp, 45 mph and 16 mm steel. [Saumur's BTR-152V](https://museedesblindes.fr/les_chars/btr-152/) has 110 hp, 65 km/h and 13 mm steel. These are scout/APC protection classes, not tank armor. The [South African Armour Museum Eland chronology](https://www.saarmourmuseum.com/eland-90) specifically places Chevrolet repowering in 1972 and the Mk 7 in 1979. Its later exhibit's engine figures are therefore excluded from the early 1970 Eland record; uncertain early numerical specifications remain unspecified.

## Exact imported component knowledge and DLC availability

The compiler keeps imported designs separate from broad producer research. Before creating a variant, it enables the minimum actual native bare type and only its documented fitted modules. The independent interpreter checks both each technology's `allow_branch` and its native research folder's DLC availability. A legacy transport research ID in the unavailable bomber folder cannot silently unlock the type under By Blood Alone; likewise a legacy artillery ID cannot stand in for an active No Step Back enabler. Mirrored native artillery nodes are selected in their respective branches. Negative controls verify that both formerly missed cases are rejected.

An explicit narrow compiler policy allows one common exact-type service receipt for a shared static design when at least one applicable DLC configuration has no usable ordinary pre-1970 unlock. This is relevant to the An-2's static transport-role abstraction. In branches where an ordinary unlock already exposes that same bare type, the duplicate availability adds no further capability. The exception cannot add another type, a new stockpile, production competence, bonuses or future hardware. Fully complementary ordinary native unlocks, such as the two early artillery branches, are used instead of this exception. The original rule for a genuinely dead/unlinked evidenced frame remains separate. Component knowledge bundles are non-researchable, hidden from normal folders and limited to exact fitted modules and independently justified bare types; they grant no future research family.

## Native role and inheritance corrections

The native startup test exposed a real distinction between a legal module slot and a buildable equipment role. MD's aircraft archetypes use `type_override = fixed_main_weapon_slot`; the conventional bomb module adds the CAS category. All sixteen African bomb-load designs now select the matching native CAS derivative of the same early base instead of an interceptor or tactical-bomber base. Actual aircraft armament, engine count, planform, date and performance generation remain unchanged. The prefix-correct bases load before MD's actual `duplicate_archetypes` definitions so the engine, rather than just the catalogue, creates the intended role types. [Role-frame provenance](role_frames_notice.md) records native authorship and the original donor copies.

The utility helicopter frame now inherits designer slots from the real native generated transport-helicopter type, not the static legacy model. The static fallback remains separate. Validation resolves explicit `module_slots = inherit` through the actual parent; a static parent's shared archetype cannot manufacture missing slots. Exact service knowledge may include the selected role and its one immediate prefix-correct copied base when native role initialization requires both. This does not authorize generic parent families, other roles, later research, quantities or production assets. The independent validator checks active selected-model evidence for that precise related base.

Focused regressions also verify actual archetype/parent source order, original enum preservation and legal native mission tokens from the existing MD CAS/bomber definitions. The malformed old mission aliases are rejected explicitly. Static checks pass after these corrections; only the next native run can establish that the engine accepts the complete revised design package.
