# Americas and Oceania: January 1970 historical design provenance

Cutoff: 1 January 1970. This regional input covers all 81 non-US history tags in the Americas/Oceania profile. USA is maintained separately in `usa_variants.json`. The companion JSON records original producers, local operators, bounded service dates, physical equipment and evidence. This is a representative set of service designs, not a complete inventory or order of battle.

Research and native module mapping: Codex for IMPERIA COLD WAR, using the linked originating government, armed-forces, museum, manufacturer, parliamentary and archival sources. Canonical physical module configurations were reused from the European, Asian and African regional manifests where appropriate, with separate local-operator evidence. Original source authors retain their rights. Individual authors, licences and reuse permission are unknown where the source does not identify them. No source photographs, diagrams or substantial passages were copied into the mod. A project identifier establishes no ownership of third-party material.

## Evidence and cutoff method

A service date ending 31 December bounds evidence stated only to a year; it is not an invented acceptance-ceremony date. For a shared family, the notes identify different national entry dates and the bound covers every assigned operator. A contract, proposed acquisition, foreign deployment, prototype flight or demonstration does not by itself establish local operational service.

The country arrays are exhaustive for this region, while design packages are deliberately selective. An empty array means no separately evidenced model package in this pass, not that inhabitants possessed no basic arms. Hypothetical releases, offshore dependencies and regional breakaways receive no invented independent armour, aircraft, shipyard, missile or strategic programme. The separate research baseline remains sector-specific and conservative.

Imports record the original design producer independently of local assembly and service. Minimal native bare equipment and exact historical service-component knowledge are compiler prerequisites, not an entire supplier-country technology package. No MIO, design team, experience premium, production efficiency, troops, stockpiles, aircraft, naval hulls, embarked air group or completed special project is spawned.

## Canada

The package distinguishes the original gunless CF-104 strike configuration from its later cannon restoration. It records licensed CF-5 deliveries from 1968, the original 1961 ex-USAF CF-101B and pre-1970 Hercules B/E variants. Voodoo receives no Genie nuclear rocket or later Peace Wings missile-control update. Conventional strike stores do not create nuclear warheads.

The Royal Canadian Armoured Corps museum's history records 105 mm Centurion conversions for the NATO force in the early 1960s and continued service through 1970. The variant retains its original Meteor petrol engine, cast turret, steel armour and mechanical Horstmann-system abstraction. Canadian M113A1 records use Defence's documented 1964-65 purchases and original diesel configuration, with C5/.30-inch armament represented by the native generic machine-gun category. No 1980s A2 or post-2004 A3 rebuilding is included.

The Artillery Museum explicitly dates first Canadian M109 service and first round to 1968. The design uses the original short 155 mm barrel and manual conventional ammunition, not the museum vehicle's eventual A4+ configuration. C1 rifle and towed howitzer, plus M38A1CDN2 Jeep, are separately evidenced.

Bonaventure remains valid on the cutoff: commissioning in January 1957 preceded retirement on 3 July 1970. Canadian Oberons use the already commissioned 1960s conventional configuration, excluding the 1979 SOUP sensor/fire-control programme.

## Australia and New Zealand

Australia receives the licensed delta-wing Mirage IIIO, Avon-powered CAC Sabre and original Canberra B.20. Official RAAF history explicitly dates Sabre Sidewinder issue to February 1960 and retirement to July 1971. Australian Hercules A delivery began on 13 December 1958, UH-1B service in 1962 and P-3B operation in 1968.

The Australian Centurion keeps its original 83.4 mm 20-pounder, represented by the nearest native historical 75/85 mm category, rather than the stronger 100/105 mm class. The original M113A1 open machine-gun carrier is distinguished from later T50 and fire-support conversions. The unarmoured Series IIA Land Rover is evidenced by dated War Memorial artifacts and photographs without copying their armed or ambulance configuration.

Australian naval designs are bounded to Perth's original 1965 Tartar/127 mm steam-destroyer fit, the Type 12 River gun/ASW configuration, already commissioned Oberons and Melbourne. No 1974 digital combat system, Standard, Harpoon, CIWS, modern VLS or later submarine weapons update is added. Seacat/Ikara equipment is omitted where the available native bundle cannot isolate the original component faithfully.

Australian A-4G deliveries in 1967 support a true early carrier airframe and carrier-attack fallback. The first ten aircraft are included as a design family; the 1971 second order is excluded. The original single J52 and two 20 mm guns remain. Separate Sidewinder carriage is omitted because the early native carrier frame has no legal auxiliary slot.

New Zealand receives the original Vampire, Canberra B.20, 1965 Hercules H, 1966 UH-1D, P-3B, M41A1, L1A1 and L5 pack howitzer. The dated 1965 defence film directly shows New Zealand M41 operation and rebuilding of its petrol engine. Original gun/ASW Type 12 and Leander frigates are supported by the contemporary Naval Board report. The later UH-1H order and Skyhawks delivered during 1970 are excluded. No New Zealand M113, Scorpion, Strikemaster or modern fighter is inferred from later service.

P-3B is a static native maritime-patrol family in both countries. The original four turboprops, early ASW sensors and torpedo/depth-charge role are documented, while the local modular air categories lack a faithful early aerial ASW-torpedo module. A modern Harpoon carrier is not substituted.

## Latin America

- Argentina: government defence-equipment research places original M113A1 in 1968, C-130H in 1968 and AMX-13 acquisition in 1969. AMX-13/105 retains its original FL-12 oscillating turret, petrol engine and mechanical magazine. Official Air Force chronology dates first A-4B arrival to 31 October 1966; the national museum specifically distinguishes its original 20 mm guns from a later 30 mm conversion. Naval chronology supports Veinticinco de Mayo's 1969 incorporation. No operational Pulqui II/Pucara prototype, 1970 Canberra, 1971 naval A-4Q, 1972 Mirage, later TAM or foreign Harrier demonstration is granted.
- Brazil: Air Force and military-academy histories support the 1953 twin-Derwent Meteor, original C-130E in 1964-65, and the absence of later H-model deliveries. Parliamentary evidence dates M41 service from 1960. It retains the 76 mm gun and petrol engine, excluding Scania diesel and 90 mm Caxias conversions from the later 1970s/1980s. IMBEL and parliamentary records date the original M964 FAL programme to 1964. Navy histories establish Minas Gerais in December 1960 and US Navy DANFS establishes Para/Fletcher transfer in June 1959. No Niteroi, Mirage III or modern Brazilian armoured-vehicle programme is backdated.
- Cuba: contemporary 1962 weapon inventories identify T-34, T-54, BTR-152, BTR-50 and 122 mm howitzer families. December 1963 DDCI notes separately establish Cuban takeover of former Soviet armour and MiG-21 operation, distinguishing Soviet deployment from local service. MiG-17, original F-13 delta-wing MiG-21 and 1967 SA-2 practice firing are documented. Il-28s and strategic missiles withdrawn in 1962 are excluded. No warhead, missile stockpile, later SAM, mobile Kub or invented nuclear programme is granted.
- Peru: official newspaper and military-college service-pilot evidence place Mirage 5P arrival in June 1968 and local flight on 18 July. It is a radarless strike Mirage with delta wing and 30 mm guns, not a radar-equipped Mirage III or later P4. The earlier gun-only Hunter, conventional Canberra and 75 mm FL-10 AMX-13 are documented separately. The later 105 mm/missile AMX conversions are excluded.
- Chile: the Air Force's institutional history records Hunter assembly and local flights in late 1967, with operational aircraft during 1968. A brief supersonic dive does not make Hunter a Mach-2 interceptor. No Mirage/Elkan, later Shafrir package or modern fighter is added.
- Colombia and Venezuela: Colombia's military-college history and preserved aircraft record support its 1956 Canadair Sabre Mk.6; Smithsonian's physical-aircraft provenance records an F-86F transfer to Venezuela in 1955. Both remain original gun-only single-engine families. Venezuela does not receive Canadian CF-5s transferred in 1972 or a later Mirage.
- Mexico, Ecuador and Uruguay: exact official operator dates support early T-33s. Ecuador also operated Meteor, F-80 and Canberra before 1970. Uruguay's institutional history explicitly retires its F-80s during 1969, leaving T-33s in 1970; F-80 and old Uruguayan Mustangs are therefore excluded. Original C-47 and piston H-13 are independently documented.
- Guatemala, Bolivia, El Salvador, Honduras and the Dominican Republic: first-person ferry-pilot testimony and an Air Force archive photograph support Guatemala's 1954 F-51. US Army ARSOF history identifies the Bolivian Mustang as an operational mainstay during 1967; its first T-33 jets in 1973 are excluded. Salvadoran and Honduran official histories identify the actual piston types used during July 1969, while contemporary 1965 congressional and UN records document Dominican Mustang, Vampire and armed Texan activity. No later Fouga, Ouragan, F-86, A-37 or supersonic package is inferred.
- Nicaragua: further delivery research revealed disposal of most Mustangs during 1963-64. They were not assigned merely because an earlier acquisition was documented. Unverified exact Guard aircraft or armoured-vehicle packages remain unassigned. Other small states and colonial tags receive no invented national force from a foreign garrison.

## Native fitting limits

The manifests use the exact local equipment and module identifiers. Aircraft guns use the neutral historical HMG, 20 mm, 30 mm or mixed-cannon families, avoiding misleading later country-specific gun names. MiG-21, Mirage III and Mirage 5 use true delta-wing modules. Gun-only fighters retain the mandatory original gun-weapon main module; optional fittings do not introduce a modern missile.

The native 100/105 mm category represents actual 100/105 mm tanks. The 115/120/125 mm category is not substituted. Original 75/76/83.4/85 mm guns use the historical band; original 90 mm designs use narrow 90 mm hardware where applicable. Cast/welded turret and petrol/diesel distinctions are retained. No later suspension, ERA, composite armour, laser, thermal sight or automated digital fire control is smuggled into a service design.

Imported engines, conventional steel/aluminium, mechanical magazines, early radar/analogue directors and utility layouts occasionally predate the coarse module's native tree year. `module_exceptions` records their historical physical justification and source. It does not justify actual post-cutoff components. Towed artillery, rifles, Jeeps and unarmoured trucks remain static native equipment without fabricated designer slots.

The carrier airframe has a legal carrier-attack fallback. Other legacy fallbacks are intentionally conservative role abstractions where the static tree cannot reproduce an exact design. The gunless CF-104 and radarless Mirage 5P use a conventional attack/bombing fallback, so their exact Mach performance is not claimed for that legacy family. Piston-engine modules also provide broad family performance rather than exact horsepower or trainer speed.

Static validation checks dates, exact IDs, real slots, required modules, type restrictions, DLC routing and fallbacks. It cannot establish live designer acceptance, balance, naval air-group behavior or multiplayer/save-load stability. No native game result is claimed in this provenance record.

## Native MD mobility and protection calibration

The original land designs now use explicit native `tank_nsb_armor_upgrade` and `tank_nsb_engine_upgrade` levels. These are balance-scale choices within the existing MD designer. An armor level adds 7.5% aggregate game armor and costs speed and reliability; an engine level adds 3.5% game speed and costs reliability, fuel and IC. Neither level is a millimetre or horsepower conversion. Chassis, turret, engine and armor modules also contribute to the final result. Real specifications remain in each model's `historical_specs`, with an explicit `native_calibration` rationale.

Shared family levels are T-54/Type 59 armor 1 / engine 1, Meteor Centurion 2 / 0, original petrol M47/M48 3 / 6, T-34-85 1 / 2, M41 1 / 3 and AMX-13 0 / 3. The higher petrol Patton engine level compensates for the native first-generation petrol module's lower initial speed than the diesel module. It does not install a later engine. Existing MD historical variants supply conservative comparison anchors, including Centurion armor 2, T-55 armor 1 and early Leopard armor 1. Existing fallback models use their separate coarse legacy equipment scale; NSB upgrade identifiers are not copied into that unrelated scale.

Physical reference evidence added for this calibration:

- [Saumur T-54 museum record](https://museedesblindes.fr/les_chars/t54/) records the original 520 PS, 36 tonne, 50 km/h family and 200 mm maximum armor reference. Those physical dimensions are not a direct game-armor formula.
- [JGSDF Seventh Division equipment table](https://www.mod.go.jp/gsdf/nae/7d/siryoukan.html) gives Type 61 at approximately 35 tonnes, 570 PS and 45 km/h. Armor 1 / engine 0 is a conservative comparison; no exact thickness is invented from the official table's missing numerical armor data.
- [Taiwan Armour School engine study](https://www.mnd.gov.tw/File/13360), page 7, identifies the original Type 59 12150 diesel at 520 hp. Later 580 hp Type 69 and turbocharged successor engines remain excluded.
- [Bovington's M47 restoration history](https://tankmuseum.org/article/history-of-the-m47) gives the original 810 hp AV-1790 petrol, 46 tonnes and 30 mph, with 4 inch hull/turret fronts and a 4.5 inch gun shield.
- [First Division Museum M48 record](https://www.fdmuseum.org/exhibit/m48-patton-tank/) distinguishes early petrol tanks from the later diesel M48A3. Its original-engine technical table gives 810 hp and 28 mph. Its family-wide overview is not treated as proof of an exact M48A1 maximum armor thickness or local diesel refit.
- [Bendigo RSL's own Australian Mk5/1](https://bendigorsl.com.au/centurion-tank/) identifies its 83.4 mm gun, 650 bhp Meteor Mk4B, 54 tonnes, 35 km/h and 152 mm maximum cast-turret armor. These are specific to that Australian example; its exact weight and protection are not silently copied onto every Centurion mark.
- [Swiss Centurion owner's technical sheet](https://centurion-verein.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Centurion-Datasheet.pdf) corroborates the original Meteor family's 35 km/h mobility. It is used only for the shared mobility band where a different operator's mark is represented.
- [Contemporary US Army ARMOR, January-February 1953](https://www.benning.army.mil/armor/eARMOR/content/issues/1953/JAN_FEB/1953JanuaryFebruary.pdf) describes the original Walker Bulldog's 500 hp engine and road speed exceeding 40 mph. No later Brazilian diesel or 90 mm upgrade is fitted.
- [Saumur T-34-85 record](https://museedesblindes.fr/les_chars/t34_85/) gives 500 PS, 32 tonnes and 55 km/h for the original family. Museum maximum armor figures differ between examples; no exact maximum thickness is asserted for the Cuban tank.

Vijayanta remains a clearly marked conservative armor 1 / engine 1 baseline. The manufacturer establishes the original Vickers family and period. Exact numerical protection and mobility have not been established from accessible primary material in this pass, so no unsupported precision or later Bison conversion is added. The shared native bands provide differentiated starting designs, while their final combat values remain an MD game abstraction requiring native inspection and playtesting.


## Additional real-state coverage

- **Guyana:** The [CIA National Intelligence Survey](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP01-00707R000200070011-6.pdf) identifies the date and origin of individual equipment categories: UK 7.62 mm SLRs acquired in 1969 and unarmored Land Rover transports in 1966. The SLR maps to the original L1A1 family. The utility model is deliberately named without an unproven exact Land Rover subseries; the separate 1967 armored Land Rovers are not silently substituted for modern APCs. No military air wing is backdated from its later formation. The CIA endpoint intermittently returns maintenance, but the indexed full inventory text preserves those explicit dates.
- **Haiti:** [CIA's February 20, 1969 assessment](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve10/d380) records US 105 mm howitzers and C-47 transports. The fixed M2/M101 family is a conservative representative for the unspecified US 105 mm howitzer. The source reports that only five transports and three trainers were flyable. Grounded F-51s and uncertain armed T-28 subvariants are therefore excluded from the added operational package. No stockpile quantities or operational readiness figures are changed by adding the design.
- **Paraguay:** [DINAC's aviation history](https://www.dinac.gov.py/v3/index.php/biblioteca-tecnica-virtual/item/1558-evoluci%C3%B3n) dates the military transport organization to March 1954 and identifies the initial Dakota/DC-3/C-47 family. The existing unarmed C-47 design is reused. Later CASA replacements and jet combat aircraft are excluded.
- **Nicaragua:** Ricardo Ramon Boza Paiz's [aviation historical narrative](https://enriquebolanos.org/media/publicacion/RevistaTemasNicaraguenses132abril2019.pdf), printed page 214, dates six T-33 arrivals to 1962. Its neighbouring Bay of Pigs paragraph misdates that episode to 1960, so that paragraph is not used for a precise B-26 transfer date. The [Nicaraguan Army's institutional history](https://www.ejercito.mil.ni/contenido/relaciones-publicas/publicaciones/docs/memoria-1979-2009-107-116.pdf) corroborates the T-33 family remaining in the former National Guard inventory in 1979. The earlier dated T-33 family is represented with original gun-only fittings; the entire 1979 inventory is not backdated.
- **Jamaica:** The [official Air Wing history](https://www.jdfweb.com/jdf-air-wing/) is intermittently unavailable. The dated [Bell airframe register](https://www.helis.com/database/model/623/) identifies H-1 serial 2901 and H-2 serial 2907, while the [Air Wing service narrative](https://irp.cdn-website.com/02989753/files/uploaded/JDFAW.pdf) dates the first Bell 47G-3B-1 to October 1963. These support an original piston utility helicopter. The native designer's smallest unarmed transport-compatible chassis is an acknowledged category mismatch for this light helicopter. Later Bell 206s, Bell 212s and Hueys are excluded. Confidence in this dated family is moderate because the full official unit page could not currently be re-read.
- **Trinidad and Tobago:** The detailed [English-speaking Caribbean armed-forces history](https://pdfcoffee.com/26-armed-forces-of-the-english-speaking-caribbean-pdf-free.html), printed page 54, dates the first Australian 7.62 mm SLR batch to 1966. This secondary operator-history evidence is used with the original museum-supported L1A1 physical family. It does not justify later Galil weapons or helicopters introduced after 1970. The known small Vosper patrol boats are not forced into an oversized frigate or missile-corvette abstraction in this pass.
