# IMPERIA COLD WAR - Cuban foreign policy mechanics review

Scope: source review only. No runtime or generator changes are part of this note. The installed MD reference is `E:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/workshop/content/394360/2777392649`.

## Volunteer deployment API

The volunteer modifiers are real MD-supported effects, not prose-only keys. Cuba already has dynamic modifier readers: `COLDWAR_CUB_defence_programme` maps `send_volunteer_size` to `COLDWAR_CUB_defence_send_volunteer_size`, and `COLDWAR_CUB_guevarist_programme` maps it to the Guevarist variable (`common/dynamic_modifiers/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_dynamic_modifiers.txt:28-40,91-109`). The current Expeditionary Forces focus adds `+2` volunteer divisions and separately exposes `+6%` army organization, `+12%` army personnel expense, and `+5%` supply consumption (`common/national_focus/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_focus.txt:1128-1146`). The Guevarist Solidarity Profit focus adds `+1` volunteer division and `+5%` army personnel expense (`...focus.txt:2348-2366`). Their apply effects initialize and refresh the variables, so new focus rewards should use the existing volunteer variable family and refresh path rather than invent a new field (`common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_effects.txt:1397-1435,1742-1816`).

The supported fields and displayed units are:

| Field | Native MD presentation | Meaning shown by source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `send_volunteer_size` | Flat divisions, `+=0` | Maximum volunteer force divisions. Cuba already reads it. |
| `send_volunteer_factor` | Percent, `+=%1` | Factor on the volunteer force cap. Not currently wired into Cuba's dynamic modifiers. |
| `send_volunteer_divisions_required` | Percent, `-=%1` | Modifies the required division threshold. Not currently wired into Cuba's dynamic modifiers. |
| `send_volunteers_tension` | Percent reduction to the Send Volunteers tension limit | Lowers the world-tension gate; not currently wired into Cuba's dynamic modifiers. |
| `air_volunteer_cap` | Flat cap, `+=0` | MD uses it for air volunteer allowance. Cuba has no current air-volunteer variable reader. |

The formatter evidence is in MD `localisation/english/MD_dm_modifiers_l_english.yml:73-74,272,286,371`. Russia's `SOV_aviatcia` gives `+20` air volunteer cap, `+1` land volunteer size, and `-5%` divisions required (`common/national_focus/05_russia.txt:22013-22019`); its later aviation focus uses `+20` air cap and `-15%` divisions required (`...05_russia.txt:22772-22779`). MD idea examples include `-20%` required and `-15%` tension for `SOV_idea_moscow_faction` (`common/ideas/Russian.txt:721-722`), and Warsaw Pact member effects of `+5` volunteer size, `-25%` required, `-15%` tension, and `+25` air cap (`common/ideas/Factions.txt:304-306`). These establish precedent and scale, not Cuba balance targets.

The native rule `set_rule = { can_send_volunteers = yes }` is used by the generic Militarism focus (`common/national_focus/00_generic.txt:1554-1562`). Cuba's 1970 starting leader uses `communist_state`, whose `communism` ideology already includes `can_send_volunteers = yes` (`history/countries/CUB - Cuba.txt:5-57`; `common/ideologies/00_ideologies.txt:57-95`). A new permission reward would therefore be redundant at the approved start. More importantly, MD changes the base minimum from vanilla's 30 divisions to 7 (`common/defines/MD_defines.lua:29-31`; vanilla `common/defines/00_defines.lua:136-140`). The Cuba 1970 history explicitly loads empty land, air, and naval OOBs (`history/countries/CUB - Cuba.txt:42-59`; `history/units/mdcw_1970_empty_land.txt`, `...empty_air.txt`). A volunteer-size bonus raises only the deployment cap; it does not create divisions, manpower, equipment, or an operational army. A threshold reduction should be modest and justified against the live army size, not advertised as producing forces. The relevant 1970 outcome is limited deployment capacity after the player builds units.

Air volunteer support is a plausible later addition only if needed. MD Russia's dynamic modifier maps `air_volunteer_cap` to a variable (`common/dynamic_modifiers/99_SOV_dynamic_modifiers.txt:282-289`), and MD changes the air volunteer ratios to 25% of the source air force and 20% of recipient airbase capacity (`common/defines/MD_defines.lua:211-212`). Cuba currently has an empty air OOB and no `air_volunteer_cap` mapping. The field is supported, but no existing Cuban reader or starting aircraft make it an immediate payoff.

For an overseas policy focus, a truthful native package can show `+1` volunteer size, optionally `-5%` tension limit or `-5%` required threshold, and a concrete army logistics or personnel cost. Avoid `available_workforce` and any implication that a signed treaty automatically deploys troops. Volunteer rules are country-wide, not target-specific: the paired agreement can represent Cuban readiness and partner consent, but the native cap itself does not restrict dispatch to that signatory. Both players still use the actual volunteer diplomacy action. Exact effect evaluation is engine behavior and requires an in-game test.

## Permanent paired spirits and alternatives

The current data source has eight alternate commercial packages, one term per SOV, MEX, SPR, or CAN partner, plus four standing relationships: Soviet assistance, DDR scientific cooperation, the medical mission, and reciprocal Interests Sections. Each package receipt charges one-time Treasury setup costs on acceptance; paired spirits are then permanent while both parties retain the signed flags and the relationship is eligible. The reconciler suspends both sides during bilateral war, native embargo, or any of the eight Imperia mutual sanction entries, and restores them on return to eligibility (`tools/cuba_1970/bilateral.py:50-99,118-219`; emitted `common/scripted_effects/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_bilateral.txt`). It grants ideas and agreement records only; it does not create resource flows, buildings, or a volunteer deployment.

The approved eight package values are concise enough to audit here; percentages below are the literal modifier values in `tools/cuba_1970/bilateral.py:8-44` and `common/ideas/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_bilateral_ideas.txt:4-19`:

The four standing spirits add: CUB and SOV technical assistance gives Cuba +15% sanction resistance and -15% foreign-influence defence, while the Soviet counterpart gets +5% foreign influence and +2% bureaucracy cost (`common/ideas/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_diplomacy_ideas.txt:9-14`; paired idea at `common/ideas/COLDWAR_CUB_1970_bilateral_ideas.txt:21`); DDR cooperation gives both sides +2% research and +3% education cost (`...diplomacy_ideas.txt:21-26`, paired idea line 22); medical cooperation gives Cuba +3% foreign influence and +2% education cost, while each accepted recipient gets -5% health cost (`...bilateral_ideas.txt:23-24`); Interests Sections gives both +3% foreign influence, with Cuba -2% foreign-influence defence and the USA +1% bureaucracy cost (`...bilateral_ideas.txt:25-26`).

Country size matters for these permanent, stackable spirits. Each partner permits only one active term, but Cuba can hold one with each of SOV, MEX, SPR, and CAN at once. The literal CUB idea values sum to +26% civilian-factory productivity from SOV industry, MEX energy, SPR nickel, and CAN industry together; construction speed sums to +19% from SOV industry, SPR training, and CAN rail. Those totals exclude the separate medical, SOV, DDR, and USA institutions, and are not a tested projection of final in-game output. The +10% MEX sanction-resistance term is read by Imperia's sanctions system as a reduction to sanction multipliers, including blowback (`common/scripted_effects/imp_sanctions_effects.txt:131-148,192,244,318,389`). That is a sizeable possible standing package for a small country even without any workforce increase. Keep alternatives institutionally distinct, show costs, and check the all-partner stack in the intended RP. Do not make a flat bonus from every partner the only meaningful outcome. The named agreements describe policy packages; current modifiers do not represent literal shipments.

## Focus selection, counterpart consent, and refusal

For the requested flow, the Cuban focus should itself initiate one recipient-owned consent event or decision, and the recipient's acceptance should set the durable bilateral signature before reconciliation adds both spirits. A later war or sanction suspends the benefits without erasing the signed agreement; either signatory can terminate it. This matches the current bilateral lifecycle. It avoids a separate CUB proposal decision after the focus.

Native focus completion is not repeatable in this tree. No `repeatable = yes` national focus example was found in the local or installed MD focus sources, and `cancel_if_invalid` controls invalidity before completion rather than proving that a completed focus can run again. Therefore, after the focus is completed and the counterpart refuses, the same focus cannot be selected again through a native retry. The clean focus-only choice is to treat refusal as a final rejection of that specific offer. If retry after refusal is required, it needs another mechanism: a second separately available focus, an automatically reissued recipient-owned consent after a cooldown, or a reoffer interaction outside focus completion. The last two choices are custom policy mechanics, not native focus replay. Keep any renewed ask subject to fresh recipient consent.

Focus mutual exclusions are suitable for choosing the permanent strategic direction once. They lock the branch choice, including when the counterpart declines; cancellation of a signed spirit cannot make the already completed sibling focus selectable. If the policy should remain renegotiable after acceptance or cancellation, put the term choice in the recipient's consent interaction and keep a single focus as the initial mandate. The recipient's alternatives must be term-specific to the actual Cuban offer, rather than allowing an unsolicited, unapproved counter-package.
