# January 1970 recognition catalogue

The recognition tab now lists the twelve represented January 1970 cases: West Germany, East Germany, the PRC, the ROC, North Korea, South Korea, North Vietnam, South Vietnam, Israel, Mongolia, Biafra and Rhodesia. It also retains eligible existing MD breakaway states. The user explicitly chose missing historical recognition cases rather than an unrestricted list of every country. Countries that do not currently exist are excluded. Mere absence from the UN is not a reason for inclusion: Switzerland and later independent states are not automatically labelled disputed.

The previous filter exposed only `lacks_international_recognition` plus GER and DDR. A country could therefore have an unresolved bilateral recognition question and still be missing from the picker. In particular, UN membership did not establish universal bilateral recognition of Mongolia, Israel or the ROC government.

`global.ICW_recognition_candidates` is a display catalogue, separate from MD's `global.Unrecognised_States`. Opening the recognition tab copies the small existing MD registry and appends twelve existence-guarded country tags, deduplicated. Existing saves gain the catalogue when the tab is reopened. The existing country-initialization provider registers newly appearing candidates without scanning the world. There is no new recurring callback, country scan, membership rebuild or diplomatic AI pass. Existing non-1970 campaigns retain the native eligible breakaway list.

The additions do not grant an isolation spirit, declare a UN member unrecognised, assign sanctions, grant UN membership, rewrite `recognised_countries`, or invent an exhaustive bilateral 1970 recognition table. **Existing recorded recognitions remain authoritative.** This is an eligibility and presentation correction, not a new historical seeding pass. The original German baseline and later player choices are retained. There is no automatic removal of recognition of the other German state. The normal native grant, rescind, opinion and campaign paths are retained. No automatic One-China or One-Korea exclusivity rule is introduced by listing their competing governments.

Compact English labels identify the divided states without changing their country names elsewhere. Official current names remain in native tooltips and in the selected-country heading. German and Korean/Vietnamese directional labels fall back to the native name if the other state no longer exists. The PRC abbreviation is tied to the authored `CHI_PRC_1970` cosmetic identity. Rhodesia uses the existing `ZIM_RHODESIA_1970` name through the native fallback.

North Korea (`NKO`) and South Korea (`KOR`) may both be recognised by the same country. Recognition of either government does not remove recognition of the other. Withdrawing and subsequently restoring either recognition remains independent; the additional source regression exercises both grant orders and withdrawal of each state through the actual native button effects and eligibility checks.

The complete country-list container, grid and button template were checked. Each 250-by-35 slot holds its existing 148-by-34 button at x=40. Two columns remain within the 515-pixel container. The list now has 4 pixels of top padding instead of 50 and ends before the selected-country heading. The existing scrollbar accommodates the extra rows. New short labels were measured using the installed `hoi_16mbs` glyph advances and kerning, reserving 16 pixels for a flag and 12 pixels for button padding. This is source and font-metric verification, not a live game screenshot.

## Historical basis for inclusion

- **The two German states:** Henry Kissinger's 25 March 1970 memorandum to President Nixon describes the recognition dispute and the distinction between acceptance of two German states and recognition under international law. This is evidence of the ongoing 1970 issue, not a claim that all acts discussed were already complete on 1 January. [Office of the Historian, FRUS 1969-1976, volume XL, document 71](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v40/d71).
- **PRC and ROC:** The PRC Foreign Ministry's history records the expansion of bilateral ties before the end of 1969, followed by a further wave in the 1970s. The October 1970 Canada-PRC communique explicitly links recognition and establishment of relations, so that later change must not be assumed at the January start. The original UN roster continues to represent China through TAI. [PRC Foreign Ministry, second wave of diplomatic relations](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/mfa_eng/zy/wjls/3604_665547/202405/t20240531_11367577.html), [Canada-PRC communique](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/mfa_eng/gjhdq_665435/3376_665447/3382_664830/3383_664832/202406/t20240612_11425180.html).
- **The two Koreas:** Contemporary Canadian diplomatic papers discuss the Republic of Korea's recognition campaign and its rival northern government. A declassified CIA retrospective describes their later competition for recognition. No numerical global recognition count is derived from that retrospective for 1 January 1970. [Canadian diplomatic memorandum, 13 July 1949, Library and Archives Canada](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/206/301/faitc-aecic/history/2013-05-03/www.international.gc.ca/department/history-histoire/dcer/details-en.asp%40intRefid%3D9674?nodisclaimer=1), [CIA report, diplomatic rivalry appendix](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP84S00553R000300010002-4.pdf).
- **The two Vietnamese governments:** The State Department's recognition history distinguishes US relations with Saigon from non-recognition of Hanoi after partition. Its contemporary 1950 memorandum records Chinese and Soviet recognition of Ho Chi Minh's government. The native SVN tag represents Saigon, not the separate southern Provisional Revolutionary Government. [US recognition history](https://history.state.gov/countries/vietnam), [FRUS 1950, volume VI, document 480](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1950v06/d480).
- **Israel:** Contemporary US policy papers treated Arab recognition of Israel as a separate diplomatic issue. UN membership alone therefore cannot determine the available bilateral-recognition action. This provides contextual evidence of the continuing dispute rather than a new table of every state's January position. [FRUS 1969-1976, volume XXIII, document 121](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76v23/d121).
- **Mongolia:** The official US recognition history explicitly says Mongolia's 1961 UN admission did not constitute US recognition; that occurred in 1987. This is a direct precedent for keeping recognition available after UN membership. [Office of the Historian, Mongolia](https://history.state.gov/countries/mongolia/1000).
- **Biafra:** Contemporary congressional discussion identifies African states that recognised Biafra, while January 1970 diplomatic records describe the end of the conflict. The BIA tag exists at this mod's January start and remains selectable while it exists. There is no forced calendar-date annexation or destruction of a surviving RP Biafra. [Congressional Record, 19 January 1970](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1970-pt1/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1970-pt1-1-1.pdf), [FRUS, Nigerian relief report, January 1970](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1969-76ve05p1/d170).
- **Rhodesia:** The contemporary US position explicitly distinguished consular contacts and assistance from recognition of Rhodesian independence. Listing it must not automatically grant recognition by South Africa or Portugal. [FRUS 1964-1968, volume XXIV, document 553](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v24/d553).

These sources justify which disputes the catalogue can represent. They are not used to fabricate unrecorded bilateral decisions. Primary official sources can reflect their issuing governments' political views; the game additions identify the represented country tags and allow RP decisions without adopting those sources' legitimacy claims. Sources accessed 23 October 2026.
